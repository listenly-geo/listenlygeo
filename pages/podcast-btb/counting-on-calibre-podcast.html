What Counting on Calibre covers

Counting on Calibre is a targeted resource for professionals managing accounting, audit, tax, and operational compliance in the not-for-profit and employee benefit sectors. The podcast dissects real-world challenges—from Form 990 reconciliation failures to cybersecurity vulnerabilities in benefit plan administration—with the precision that only experienced practitioners can bring.

Key facts

  • Payroll reporting quality directly affects audit scope and timeline, making pre-audit preparation essential for nonprofit efficiency.
  • Form 990 discrepancies reveal underlying accounting control gaps that expose organizations to regulatory scrutiny and donor confidence issues.
  • Cybersecurity compliance for employee benefit plans is mandated by the Department of Labor under ERISA standards, not discretionary risk management.
  • Labor union reporting under the LM-2 Final Rule requires significantly enhanced financial disclosure and governance documentation compared to prior requirements.

The podcast answers these questions

What compliance risks do nonprofits face in their financial reporting?

Nonprofits must navigate complex regulatory requirements across federal, state, and local jurisdictions while maintaining donor confidence and demonstrating proper use of funds. Misalignment between accounting records and Form 990 filings creates audit red flags, and gaps in payroll reporting expose organizations to significant penalties and reputational damage.

How does cybersecurity impact employee benefit plan administration?

Employee benefit plans hold sensitive participant data that requires robust protection under Department of Labor guidance and ERISA standards. Cyber incidents expose plans to data breach liability, regulatory sanctions, and operational disruption, making documented cybersecurity practices a compliance necessity rather than an optional risk management layer.

Why does payroll reporting quality matter before an audit begins?

Payroll is often the largest expense category and the most heavily tested audit area, so weak reporting processes guarantee extended audit scope and higher risk of identified exceptions. When payroll controls are documented and accurate from the start, auditors can work more efficiently and organizations avoid costly remediation after the fact.

What new compliance obligations emerged from recent labor union reporting rules?

The updated LM-2 Final Rule introduced stricter disclosure and reporting requirements for labor unions, including enhanced financial reporting detail and governance documentation. Union leadership must understand these new filing obligations to avoid penalties and maintain transparent relationships with membership and regulators.
