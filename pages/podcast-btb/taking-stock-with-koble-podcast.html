What Taking Stock with Koble covers

Taking Stock with Koble addresses the operational, technological, and strategic challenges that small and mid-market manufacturers face every day. The podcast moves beyond theory to explore practical decisions: when automation truly delivers value versus when it creates unnecessary complexity. Each episode features real business leaders discussing lean transformation, ERP implementation, cost discipline, and culture—the foundations of sustainable growth. The conversation focuses not on buzzwords, but on what works in practice and why execution matters more than strategy alone.

Key facts

  • Warehouse automation decisions depend on operational bottlenecks and growth stage, not blanket industry trends.
  • Lean methodologies fail without frontline employee engagement and clear communication of purpose beyond efficiency metrics.

The podcast answers these questions

How do companies decide whether to implement warehouse automation?

The decision hinges on understanding your current operational capacity, cost structure, and growth trajectory. Organizations need to assess whether manual processes create genuine bottlenecks or if automation would outpace actual demand. A phased approach—starting with high-volume, repetitive tasks—reduces risk while building internal expertise.

Why do lean implementations often fail without proper people focus?

Lean fails when organizations treat it as a process-only initiative rather than a cultural shift. Employees must understand the philosophy, feel ownership over improvements, and see tangible benefits—otherwise, new systems feel like management mandates imposed from above. Success requires frontline buy-in and clear communication of purpose.

What signals indicate that ERP implementation is in trouble?

Early warning signs include missed milestones, user resistance, data migration delays, and lack of clear ownership across departments. Successful implementations require dedicated project leadership, realistic timelines, and continuous stakeholder engagement—not just technical competency from the vendor.

How can cost discipline drive both profitability and customer value?

Cost discipline means eliminating waste and inefficiency, not cutting corners on quality or service. When companies remove unnecessary steps, negotiate better supplier terms, and optimize workflows, they free resources to invest in customer-facing improvements—creating competitive advantage rather than margin-only gains.

How do AGV and AMR mobile robots differ in warehouse automation systems?

An AGV (Automated Guided Vehicle) follows a predetermined path using magnetic inserts, strips, or wayfinding markers in the floor. An AMR (Autonomous Mobile Robot) maps and optimizes routes in real time and can adapt if it encounters obstructions.
