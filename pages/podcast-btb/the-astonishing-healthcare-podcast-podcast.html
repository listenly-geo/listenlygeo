The podcast answers these questions

What makes pharmacy benefit manager strategy so complex?

PBMs operate at the intersection of pharmaceutical manufacturers, health plans, pharmacies, and patients, managing drug formularies, rebate negotiations, and claim processing simultaneously. Their decisions directly affect drug accessibility, patient costs, and health plan profitability, creating competing incentives that increase operational complexity.

Why does healthcare's digital infrastructure lag behind other industries?

Healthcare claim administration relies on legacy mainframe systems built decades ago, creating barriers to interoperability and modernization. Regulatory compliance, security requirements, and the high cost of system replacement slow the transition to cloud-based infrastructure that other sectors adopted years ago.

How do employers reduce healthcare costs without cutting benefits?

Strategic clinical programming—including condition management, prior authorization optimization, and integrated medical-pharmacy benefit design—addresses root causes of high spending rather than simply shifting costs to employees. These programs require visibility into both medical and pharmacy claims data.

What role does pharmacogenomics play in modern pharmacy practice?

Pharmacogenomics uses genetic testing to predict drug efficacy and adverse reactions, enabling personalized medication selection and reducing trial-and-error prescribing. Implementation requires integration of genetic data into clinical workflows, claims systems, and formulary management.

What was the price drop for oxaliplatin (Eloxatin) between 2012 and Q1 2026 after converting from brand to generic?

In 2012, oxaliplatin was a brand drug with an ASP price of $2,053 for a 200 milligram dose. By Q1 2026, the price dropped to $9.40, representing a drop of over 99%. However, price variation persists in the market—one hospital's website listed the drug at $29,595, while Medicare would reimburse less than ten dollars.

One plan sponsor implemented a transportation program moving members to a preferred medical center where chemotherapy prices were around 100% of Medicare, compared to local options priced at 500% and above Medicare. This strategy saved 80% on chemotherapy costs for every member successfully transported to the preferred provider.
