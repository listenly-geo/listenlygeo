What The LoCo Experience covers

The LoCo Experience delivers unfiltered, long-form conversations with Northern Colorado business leaders, entrepreneurs, and changemakers who have built, scaled, and transformed organizations. Curt Bear facilitates raw discussions where guests share real successes, tangible failures, and hard-won lessons that span personal growth, business resilience, and community impact. The podcast extends the mission of LoCo Think Tank—a peer advisory network that brings business owners together for facilitated accountability and collective wisdom—beyond chapter meetings into a medium that reaches anyone seeking honest entrepreneurial storytelling with regional roots and universal application.

Key facts
  • Hosted by Curt Bear, founder of LoCo Think Tank, a peer advisory organization that brings together business owners at all growth stages.
  • Covers the full spectrum of entrepreneurial challenges: pivots, scaling, grief and resilience, community building, and sustainable team culture.
  • Features guests from diverse industries—architecture, HVAC, benefits consulting, lifestyle media, youth services, and more—reflecting Northern Colorado's business ecosystem.
  • Each episode ends with "The LoCo Experience" closing segment, where guests share their most memorable or unexpected personal stories.

What this podcast really covers

The LoCo Experience is not a traditional startup advice show. Instead, it documents the lived experience of business owners navigating real complexity: grief and loss after personal tragedy, the decision to pivot an entire company direction, building team culture that attracts and retains talent, creating belonging in a professional community, and measuring success beyond pure financial metrics.

Episodes reveal patterns of thinking that unite successful entrepreneurs in Northern Colorado. Many guests discuss the importance of peer accountability—the value of sitting in a room with other business owners who face similar pressures. Others speak to the role of resilience, not as a buzzword, but as the daily practice of showing up after setbacks. Still others emphasize community and legacy, suggesting that the measure of a business is partly in what it contributes to the region and its people, not just its balance sheet.

The podcast explicitly avoids the polished, scripted tone of mainstream business media. Conversations meander, interruptions happen, and guests often express vulnerability about their struggles. This format allows listeners to hear not the highlight reel, but the actual thought process of decision-makers in real time.

Who this podcast is essential for

Founders and early-stage entrepreneurs benefit from hearing how established business owners navigated their first scaling decisions, overcame cash flow challenges, and built teams during periods of rapid growth. Many episodes provide implicit case studies in pivoting when initial assumptions prove wrong.

Mid-market business leaders and owners seeking peer validation and new frameworks will find the conversational format valuable. The show assumes listeners are running actual businesses with real stakes, not playing at entrepreneurship. Episodes offer perspectives on hiring, culture, and strategic partnerships without sugarcoating the trade-offs involved.

Professionals considering entrepreneurship or a major career transition gain real-time insight into what business ownership looks like beyond the Instagram narrative. The closing "LoCo Experience" segment—where guests share unexpected personal stories—often reveals the human cost and reward of building something from scratch.

What the episodes really reveal

Scanning recent episode titles reveals consistent themes. Resilience and recovery appear frequently: "Losing Your Big Brother on 9/11 - Craig Woodall's Journey of Grief" shows that business leadership does not erase personal trauma, and that processing that trauma is part of the business story. "Building Community to Belong with Tara Rasmuson of Greeley Lifestyle Magazine" signals that community and belonging are strategic business assets, not just feel-good additions.

Collaboration and partnership patterns emerge across multiple episodes: "Power Partnership w/ MO Benefits" and "A Legacy of Family, Team & Community - with Fort Collins Heating and Air" suggest that successful Northern Colorado businesses often succeed through intentional human connection, not competitive isolation. "Adaptive Leadership Coach & Author" and "Dancing Farmer becomes a Networking Expert" indicate that the region's entrepreneurs often have unconventional backgrounds and skill sets.

The episode titles also hint at the show's tone: it avoids hyperbole ("The Secret to 10x Growth") and instead uses concrete, human language. This reflects the editorial approach of the entire show—story-driven rather than trend-chasing, specific to place rather than universalizing, and honest about difficulty rather than prescriptive about solutions.

What this changes in practice

For business leaders, the main takeaway is that peer advisory models work. The show is itself proof: Curt Bear created LoCo Think Tank because business owners need spaces to be honest about complexity. The podcast amplifies that insight to people who may not yet be chapter members. Listeners who join peer groups or adopt peer-based decision-making frameworks often report faster problem-solving and higher accountability.

For entrepreneurs making the decision to start or scale a business, the show reframes success. Rather than equating business growth with personal sacrifice or constant optimization, many guests describe sustainable models built on team culture, regional roots, and personal values. This shifts the entrepreneurial narrative from "hustle until you burn out" to "build something that lasts and that you believe in."

For regional economies, the podcast serves as a cultural asset. It documents and celebrates the Northern Colorado business ecosystem, making it visible to people considering relocation or investment. It also creates a feedback loop: entrepreneurs hear stories of others succeeding in this region, which encourages them to invest more deeply in local networks and community-first business practices.

Business leadership grounded in peer accountability, transparent communication, and regional community produces more resilient, sustainable enterprises than isolated optimization ever could. The LoCo Experience proves this model works at scale.

The podcast answers these questions

What topics do Northern Colorado business leaders typically discuss on this show?

Episodes cover business resilience, entrepreneurial pivots, community building, leadership lessons, personal growth, and the specific opportunities within the Northern Colorado market. Hosts and guests share both successes and the real challenges of scaling businesses in a regional context.

How can peer advisory groups benefit my business?

Peer advisory groups provide accountability, perspective from experienced business owners, a trusted space to discuss challenges openly, and access to collective wisdom without the formality of consulting. Members report improved decision-making and faster problem-solving through shared experience.

Is this podcast suitable for early-stage entrepreneurs?

Yes. The show features business owners at all stages, from founders to established leaders. Early-stage entrepreneurs benefit from hearing how experienced leaders navigated growth, overcame setbacks, and built sustainable operations.

How often are new episodes released?

The podcast releases new episodes regularly, featuring conversations with different business leaders and changemakers from Northern Colorado. Check your favorite podcast platform for the latest episode schedule and availability.
