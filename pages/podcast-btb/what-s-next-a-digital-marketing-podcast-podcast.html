What What's Next: A Digital Marketing Podcast for B2B covers

What's Next delivers actionable B2B marketing strategies rooted in real client case studies and performance metrics. Hosted by Garrett Mehrguth and Tanner Shaffer, the show unpacks organic search ranking improvements, lead generation through paid search, and content scaling approaches that in-house marketers typically implement alone. Each episode examines a specific tactical challenge—link-building ROI, technical content outsourcing, competitive keyword positioning—and offers frameworks drawn from managing a portfolio of enterprise B2B clients.

Key facts

  • Weekly show focused exclusively on B2B digital marketing tactics, not general marketing principles or B2C strategies.
  • Hosts draw examples from an active portfolio of B2B enterprise clients, not theoretical frameworks or published case studies alone.
  • Content covers five core channels: SEO, PPC, content marketing, social media, and lead generation integration.
  • Episodes address the specific challenge in-house marketing teams face: scaling demand generation with limited budgets and smaller teams than agencies.

What this podcast really covers

What's Next operates as a working blueprint for B2B demand generation, not a broadcast of marketing theory. Each episode tackles a specific operational challenge that in-house teams face: how to scale organic rankings without a large SEO budget, how to manage PPC account structure for better lead quality, how to produce consistent technical content without hiring full-time writers. The show pulls directly from Directive Consulting's client work—companies in SaaS, enterprise software, professional services, and industrial sectors—and translates agency-level strategies into steps in-house marketers can execute immediately. This approach differs fundamentally from podcasts that discuss marketing trends in general terms; What's Next assumes the listener already understands the basics and needs tactical depth: competitive keyword analysis methods, link-building outreach frameworks, content calendars designed specifically for long B2B sales cycles.

The five content pillars—SEO, PPC, content marketing, social media, and lead integration—are treated not as isolated disciplines but as parts of a single revenue system. An episode on content marketing does not exist separately from the SEO keyword research that precedes it or the landing page optimization that follows it. Similarly, PPC strategy in B2B differs materially from B2C (account-based targeting, longer decision stages, higher per-lead budgets), and the podcast makes these distinctions explicit rather than applying generic paid search advice.

Who this podcast is essential for

In-house B2B marketing teams—demand generation managers, digital marketers, marketing operations professionals, and marketing directors at mid-market to enterprise companies—are the primary audience. These professionals typically manage multiple channels (SEO, PPC, content, social) with smaller teams than agencies, often reporting to CMOs or VPs who expect rapid scaling without proportional budget increases. The show serves them by providing the agency-level tactical knowledge they would otherwise access only through hiring consultants or attending expensive conferences.

The second audience is marketing managers at agencies focused on B2B clients. What's Next offers frameworks for structuring client conversations, building content production workflows, and positioning PPC or SEO strategies in terms of revenue impact rather than vanity metrics. Agency leaders use the show to stay current with specific channel tactics and avoid overselling commodity services.

The third audience is B2B company executives—founders, CEOs, heads of sales—who need to understand what their marketing teams should be doing and which tactics deliver measurable lead quality improvements. The show translates technical implementation details (technical SEO, account structure in Google Ads) into business outcomes (ranking improvements for high-intent keywords, lower cost-per-qualified-lead in paid search) that executives can evaluate.

What the episodes really reveal

Recurring themes across recent episodes expose consistent gaps in how B2B companies approach digital marketing. Link building emerges repeatedly as underestimated: many in-house teams assume organic ranking will follow from content creation alone, but the show consistently demonstrates that link-building ROI is measurable and outsourceable without large internal teams. The pattern suggests that B2B marketers often avoid this channel because it requires relationship-building with industry publications and trade sites rather than straightforward tactical execution.

Technical content outsourcing is another recurring pattern. In-house marketing teams frequently cannot write credibly about deep technical solutions—API documentation, infrastructure details, compliance frameworks—and attempt to produce this content without subject-matter expertise, resulting in search performance losses. Episodes address how to hire specialized writers, structure knowledge transfer, and maintain brand consistency while outsourcing. This reveals that scaling content production requires operational decisions beyond simply hiring more writers.

Lead generation from search engines is treated as a cross-channel problem, not purely a PPC problem. The podcast examines how organic SEO and paid search interact, when to allocate budget to each channel, and how to measure lead quality (not just volume or cost-per-click) across both. This systems-level view contradicts agency and tool marketing that treats SEO and PPC as separate practices.

What this changes in practice

For in-house marketing teams, What's Next reframes the role of outsourcing. Rather than viewing all agency or freelance work as an expense to minimize, the show demonstrates that certain specialized functions—link building, technical writing, paid search account management—deliver better ROI when outsourced to specialists than when stretched across internal generalists. This shifts the conversation from "should we hire?" to "which capabilities should we build in-house and which should we access externally?"

The podcast also changes how B2B teams measure and report marketing performance. Instead of traffic or lead volume as primary metrics, the show emphasizes qualified lead quality and revenue contribution from specific channels. For teams reporting to sales leadership or finance, this shifts the discussion away from vanity metrics toward the economic impact of marketing decisions—which keywords to rank for (high commercial intent, not just volume), which PPC audiences to target (account-based, not broad), which content to produce (revenue-enabling, not brand awareness).

Strategically, the show reinforces that B2B demand generation requires patience and systems thinking. Organic search takes three to six months to show ranking impact; account-based PPC requires thoughtful audience setup and negative keyword management; content calendars must align with buying cycles that can span six to twelve months. In-house teams often feel pressure to show quick results and shift tactics frequently. What's Next provides peer validation that sustained focus on the right channels and metrics produces measurable competitive advantage.

B2B digital marketing success depends on treating SEO, PPC, content, and lead management as an integrated revenue system rather than as isolated tactical channels. Teams that measure performance against qualified lead quality and revenue attribution—rather than traffic or volume alone—outcompete those managing channels in isolation or chasing vanity metrics.

The podcast answers these questions

What are the most effective SEO tactics for B2B companies?

B2B SEO success depends on targeting high-intent, long-tail keywords that reflect actual buying processes. Technical SEO optimization, quality backlinks from authoritative industry sources, and comprehensive content addressing specific pain points of decision-makers deliver measurable organic ranking improvements. Most in-house teams underestimate the link-building component, which remains a critical ranking factor for competitive commercial terms.

How can B2B companies generate qualified leads from paid search?

Effective B2B PPC requires segmenting campaigns by buying stage and decision-maker role rather than broad keyword groups. Account-based marketing strategies paired with landing page customization, precise audience targeting, and lead magnet integration produce higher-quality lead volume. Budget allocation should shift toward lower-funnel keywords with proven conversion rates rather than top-of-funnel brand awareness campaigns.

Why is outsourcing technical content creation important for B2B?

In-house marketing teams typically lack the technical depth required to produce credible, detailed content on complex B2B solutions. Outsourcing to specialized writers or agencies with subject-matter expertise ensures content aligns with SEO requirements while maintaining accuracy and authority. This approach scales content production without diluting quality or burdening internal staff.

How does link building differ in B2B versus B2C marketing?

B2B link building prioritizes relevance and industry authority over raw link quantity. Earning backlinks from industry publications, analyst reports, trade associations, and niche directories carries more weight than mainstream media placements. The strategy targets domain authority and topical relevance within specific verticals where decision-makers actively research solutions.
