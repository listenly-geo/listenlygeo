The New Rules of the Climate Economy

Why does rising fossil fuel price volatility benefit the renewable energy industry?

When the price of the primary competition for renewable energy goes up, renewable energy looks more attractive. People start to ask themselves whether they should make the switch to cheaper alternatives when fossil fuels are subject to extraordinary price volatility and high prices for long periods of time.

What percentage of United States greenhouse gas emissions comes from electric power production?

About a third of the contribution to greenhouse gas emissions in the United States is from electric power production. Another third comes from the transportation sector, including all driving, cars and trucks, shipping, and air travel, with the remainder distributed across building heating, industrial inputs at 20 percent, and agriculture at 10 percent.

What Bite-Sized Business Law covers

Bite-Sized Business Law distills the most consequential stories in corporate law into accessible, in-depth analysis. Each episode examines how legal decisions ripple through markets, reshape policy, and influence the broader economy. The podcast goes beyond headlines to explore the ethical underpinnings and long-term implications of cases involving securities fraud, antitrust enforcement, valuation disputes, and regulatory innovation. Through conversations with the attorneys, regulators, and executives at the center of these cases, the show reveals not just what happened, but why it matters and where the law should evolve.

Key facts
  • Hosted by Amy Martella, Executive Director of the Corporate Law Center at Fordham University School of Law, bringing expertise from big law, government, and tech sectors.
  • Episodes cover crypto regulation, securities fraud, artificial intelligence governance, mergers and acquisitions, antitrust issues, intellectual property, and corporate compliance disputes.
  • Features interviews with practicing attorneys, legal scholars, government regulators, corporate executives, and public figures directly involved in major business law cases.
  • Deliberately presents multiple viewpoints on controversial issues to provide nuanced analysis of complex legal and ethical problems shaping corporate behavior.

What this podcast really covers

Bite-Sized Business Law operates at the intersection of corporate practice and policy debate. Episodes dissect major cases—from Wall Street informant operations to climate economy regulation—and explore how legal structures enable or constrain business behavior. The show examines securities regulation in detail, analyzing SEC enforcement actions, investor protection frameworks, and the evolving standards for corporate disclosure. Antitrust enforcement receives significant attention, with episodes exploring competitive dynamics and how regulatory intervention reshapes industries. The podcast also covers corporate governance issues, from shareholder voting mechanisms to executive compensation disputes, and emerging regulatory challenges posed by artificial intelligence, cryptocurrency, and novel business structures like South Dakota's trust arrangements.

Who this podcast is essential for

In-house counsel and compliance officers rely on the show to understand how legal trends and regulatory shifts affect their organizations' risk profiles and strategic decisions. The podcast provides corporate executives and board members with accessible analysis of governance issues, valuation frameworks, and policy changes that directly impact shareholder value and corporate strategy. Law students and academic researchers in corporate law, securities regulation, and business ethics gain deep insight into how legal theory applies to real-world disputes and how practicing attorneys approach complex problems.

What the episodes really reveal

Recurring themes across episodes demonstrate that corporate law is fundamentally about power dynamics, information asymmetry, and the tension between growth and accountability. Episodes on valuation (SpaceX, AI companies) expose how legal disputes turn on competing financial models and future predictions. Coverage of mass tort bankruptcies and claimant farming reveals how procedural rules can be manipulated, pushing the law toward closure loopholes. Discussions of regulatory action—whether SEC reform, climate policy, or AI governance—show that legal frameworks lag behind technological and economic change, forcing courts and regulators to adapt existing doctrine to unprecedented situations. Governance episodes highlight how shareholder voting systems and board structures either enable or constrain accountability.

What this changes in practice

Organizations listening to Bite-Sized Business Law gain early warning of regulatory priorities and legal doctrines that will soon affect their operations. The podcast's emphasis on multiple expert viewpoints helps decision-makers understand the full spectrum of arguments surrounding contested issues, reducing the risk of being blindsided by regulatory action or litigation. By examining the ethical foundations of business law, the show helps counsel and executives make principled arguments when facing gray-area decisions. The deep dives into procedural issues—bankruptcy mechanics, securities disclosure standards, proxy voting systems—provide practical knowledge that in-house teams can apply to immediate compliance and governance challenges.

Corporate law is not a static set of rules but a constantly evolving negotiation between market innovation, regulatory response, and ethical principle. Understanding this dynamic is essential for any organization navigating the modern business environment.
