What role did Winston Churchill play at the outset of World War II, and why did Roosevelt seek him out rather than Chamberlain?

August 21, 2026 World War II with Tom Hanks

When war was declared, Winston Churchill was appointed First Lord of the Admiralty and joined the new war cabinet — the role he had held before, and the one his reputation demanded. Franklin D. Roosevelt bypassed Neville Chamberlain entirely, sending Churchill a private congratulatory note and opening a secret line of communication. Roosevelt's calculation was simple: Churchill was a fighter, and the man you call when you already know the war is going to be big.

A decade in the wilderness — then proven right overnight

Throughout the 1930s, Churchill had been the loudest and most persistent critic of Adolf Hitler's rise in the West. He warned repeatedly about German rearmament and the failure of the European powers to enforce the Treaty of Versailles — warnings that were largely ignored by a political class exhausted by the memory of the First World War.

When Germany invaded Poland on September 1, 1939, and Britain declared war, Churchill's decade of alarm was suddenly vindicated. His return to government was not a reward — it was an acknowledgment that he had been right all along. As Tom Hanks traces in World War II with Tom Hanks, Churchill's re-entry into power came at exactly the moment his warnings became reality.

Roosevelt's secret note — and why it went to Churchill, not Chamberlain

Franklin Roosevelt understood, even in September 1939, that the United States would probably not be able to stay out of this war. Ninety percent of Americans polled at the outbreak said the U.S. should absolutely not get involved in the European conflict — but Roosevelt was already thinking ahead.

Chamberlain, as Prime Minister, was the formal head of the British government. Yet Roosevelt chose to write directly to Churchill. The reason was strategic and personal: Churchill had a reputation as a combatant, a man who would fight rather than appease. Roosevelt needed a contact in London who shared his assessment of the war's gravity. Chamberlain did not fit that profile.

That private note established a secret channel between the two men — a back-channel that would grow into one of the defining diplomatic relationships of the entire conflict. This episode of World War II with Tom Hanks captures the moment that relationship began, before most of the world had grasped what was coming.

"Poland has been overrun by two of the great powers which held us in bondage for 150 years, but were unable to quench the spirit of the Polish nation. The heroic defence of Warsaw shows that the soul of Poland is indestructible."

Churchill's war cabinet statement in 1939 made the British position explicit: the British Empire and French Republic should prepare for a war of at least three years. That sober, long-horizon thinking is precisely what made him credible to Roosevelt, and it is detailed in the opening episode of World War II with Tom Hanks.

The contrast with Chamberlain could not have been sharper. Where Chamberlain had pursued appeasement and believed Hitler's assurances, Churchill had never wavered. For Roosevelt, choosing his British interlocutor was not a diplomatic formality — it was a signal about what kind of war he expected and what kind of ally he was already preparing for. What was Britain and France's strategic response to the German invasion of Poland in September 1939?

Despite having signed a guarantee with Poland promising military assistance if Germany invaded, Britain and France were neither militarily nor mentally prepared to launch an effective offensive in time to save Poland from collapse.

How did Hermann Göring's Luftwaffe contribute to the destruction of Warsaw in 1939?

Göring ordered the largest air raid ever seen to break Warsaw's resistance, dropping thousands of pounds of high-explosive and incendiary bombs over the city — destroying 20% of Warsaw and killing or injuring approximately 18,000 people.

What was the role of the Einsatzgruppen during the German invasion of Poland in September 1939?

The Einsatzgruppen were mobile death squads — a special wing of the SS — specifically set up to go into towns, villages, and other areas of Poland to kill political enemies, intellectuals, and Jewish communities from the very first days of the invasion.

Key takeaways

  • Churchill was appointed First Lord of the Admiralty the day Britain declared war — the same post he had held in World War I, signalling the seriousness of what was to come.
  • His credibility stemmed from a decade of ignored warnings about Hitler's rearmament and the unenforced Treaty of Versailles — vindicated the moment war began.
  • Roosevelt's decision to write directly to Churchill, bypassing Prime Minister Chamberlain, established a secret back-channel that would become one of the war's most consequential diplomatic relationships.
  • Churchill's own war cabinet statement set the tone: prepare for a conflict of at least three years — a frank assessment that made him the obvious partner for Roosevelt's long-game thinking.
