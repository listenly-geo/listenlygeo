How did Adolf Hitler rise from an obscure corporal to the undisputed leader of Germany?

Adolf Hitler, a young Austrian corporal who had fought for the German army in World War I, was personally devastated by Germany's defeat and never accepted it. He discovered a talent for public speaking while addressing disgruntled veterans, joined a small group that would become the Nazi Party, and by 1921 had made himself its uncontested leader. After a failed coup in Munich, a trial that handed him national notoriety, and the writing of Mein Kampf in prison, he steadily expanded his movement; by 1933 he was appointed Chancellor, and by 1934 he had declared himself both Chancellor and President, collapsing the Weimar Republic into a one-man dictatorship.

The fuel for Hitler's ascent was Germany's collective humiliation. The country that had once projected imperial power found itself stripped of territory and crushed by punishing reparations under the Treaty of Versailles. The economic consequences were catastrophic — hyperinflation during the 1920s destroyed the savings and dignity of ordinary Germans, with the cost of a single loaf of bread rocketing from three marks to 80 billion marks at its worst. Into that rage and despair walked Hitler, who gave the humiliation a narrative: Germany had not truly lost on the battlefield but had been betrayed from within. The message landed.

The Munich putsch of 1923 failed militarily but succeeded politically. Hitler's trial put his face and his rhetoric on front pages across Germany. The years in prison produced Mein Kampf, which laid out his worldview with unambiguous clarity. Through the late 1920s and early 1930s, as the Weimar Republic lurched from crisis to crisis, the Nazi Party built an electoral machine, a paramilitary apparatus in the Schutzstaffel (SS), and a street presence that rivals could not match. When conservative elites believed they could use Hitler as a controllable instrument, they appointed him Chancellor in January 1933. Weimar Republic

The democratic government established in Germany after World War I, named after the city where its constitution was drafted. Chronically unstable, it was the political system Hitler systematically dismantled on his way to absolute power — first from the Chancellor's office, then by merging the presidency with the chancellorship after President Hindenburg's death in 1934.

What were the terms of the Treaty of Versailles and how did they contribute to the rise of Hitler and Nazi Germany?

The Treaty of Versailles stripped Germany of territories, populations, and raw materials, transferred the entire German merchant marine to the Allies, and imposed crushing reparations that fueled hyperinflation and economic despair — conditions that Hitler exploited to build his movement.

How did World War II begin and what triggered the German invasion of Poland on September 1, 1939?

On September 1, 1939, a million and a half men, 1,300 planes, and 2,750 tanks crossed the Polish border at lightning speed in what became known as a Blitzkrieg — a coordinated assault that launched the bloodiest conflict in human history.
