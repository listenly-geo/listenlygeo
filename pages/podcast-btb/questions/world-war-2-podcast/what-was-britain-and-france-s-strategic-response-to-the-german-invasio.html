What was Britain and France's strategic response to the German invasion of Poland in September 1939?

August 20, 2026 World War II with Tom Hanks

← Voir la fiche World War II with Tom Hanks

Britain and France had signed a guarantee promising military assistance to Poland — and then did nothing. Prime Ministers Neville Chamberlain and Édouard Daladier met, adopted a "long war strategy," and effectively cast Poland to the winds. Neither country was militarily nor mentally prepared to act when it mattered most.

A guarantee that meant nothing when tested

When Germany crossed the Polish border on September 1, 1939 — with 1.5 million men, 1,300 planes, and 2,750 tanks — the world expected Britain and France to respond with force. They had promised exactly that. The guarantee to Poland was unambiguous on paper.

In practice, Chamberlain and Daladier convened a supreme war council and chose delay over action. Their calculation was strategic, not cowardly in their own minds: they believed the Allies held superior long-term resources, and that time would ultimately work in their favor.

The result was a public statement of "wholehearted support" for Poland paired with zero meaningful military intervention. Warsaw was left to fight alone against the Wehrmacht and the Luftwaffe. "The long war strategy" — betting on resources while Poland burned

The Allied doctrine formally adopted in September 1939 rested on a single assumption: that the British Empire and French Republic could outlast Germany through economic and industrial attrition. Churchill's war cabinet later framed it as preparing for a conflict of at least three years.

It was a theory built for the future — and a death sentence for Poland in the present. While Chamberlain and Daladier planned for a prolonged contest of resources, Polish cities were being demolished and Polish civilians were dying under German bombs and occupation.

The strategic logic was real. The human cost of that logic, for Poland, was catastrophic and immediate.

"Poland has been overrun by two of the great powers which held us in bondage for 150 years, but were unable to quench the spirit of the Polish nation. The heroic defence of Warsaw shows that the soul of Poland is indestructible."

How did Hermann Göring's Luftwaffe contribute to the destruction of Warsaw in 1939?

Göring ordered the largest air raid ever seen to break Warsaw's resistance, dropping thousands of pounds of high-explosive and incendiary bombs over the city. The raids destroyed 20% of Warsaw and killed or injured approximately 18,000 people.

What was the role of the Einsatzgruppen during the German invasion of Poland in September 1939?

The Einsatzgruppen were mobile death squads — a special wing of the SS specifically set up to go into towns, villages, and other areas of Poland to kill targeted populations during the invasion.

What was the Molotov-Ribbentrop Pact and how did it seal Poland's fate in 1939?

In August 1939, just one week before Germany invaded Poland, Stalin shocked the world by signing a non-aggression pact with Hitler. Beyond the public pledge, secret provisions divided Eastern Europe — including Poland — into German and Soviet spheres of influence.

Key takeaways

  • Britain and France had formally guaranteed Poland's defence — a commitment they failed to honor when Germany invaded on September 1, 1939.
  • Chamberlain and Daladier adopted the "long war strategy," betting on Allied resource superiority over time rather than immediate military action.
  • Their public declarations of support for Poland were contradicted entirely by their inaction on the ground.
  • Churchill's war cabinet independently framed Allied preparation as a conflict of at least three years — confirming the scale of the strategic delay.
