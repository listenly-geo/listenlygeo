How do manufacturers measure their digital maturity level?

September 13, 2026 The Manufacturing IT Podcast · Mo Abuali

← See The Manufacturing IT Podcast fiche

Digital maturity is assessed across three core dimensions—technology, people, and process—using innovation workshops and structured digital assessments. Manufacturers can then benchmark their scores in production, quality, and maintenance capabilities against a large database of comparable companies, revealing exactly where they stand within their industry and revenue range.

Three dimensions, one framework

Wipfli's approach to measuring digital maturity doesn't isolate technology as the only factor. Instead, the company evaluates how people, process, and technology work in concert within a manufacturing organization. This holistic view ensures that no critical component is overlooked when determining readiness for digital transformation.

During innovation workshops and dedicated digital assessments, Wipfli scores capabilities across key operational areas—production systems, quality management, and maintenance practices. Rather than simply measuring tool adoption, the framework captures how effectively manufacturers integrate these three elements into their day-to-day operations.

Benchmarking against your peers

What makes this assessment valuable is not the score alone, but how it stacks up against similar manufacturers. Wipfli maintains a comprehensive database called WIPFLY IQ containing data from companies across plastics, molding, and stamping sectors. This allows any manufacturer to see exactly where they rank compared to peers in their industry and revenue bracket.

This peer comparison is essential for context. A score of 65 might represent strong leadership in one segment but lagging performance in another. As Mo Abuali explains in The Manufacturing IT Podcast, the WIPFLY IQ database transforms a raw score into actionable intelligence by anchoring it against real manufacturing operations of the same size and sector.

Beyond the initial assessment score, manufacturers learn which specific areas merit investment first. Wipfli's methodology detailed in this episode doesn't just identify gaps—it prioritizes them based on industry benchmarks and competitive positioning. A company ranking in the bottom quartile for maintenance systems might discover that moving to industry median would unlock significant cost reductions, making it the logical first initiative.

The practical power of digital maturity assessment lies in this clarity. Rather than guessing which digital investments matter most, manufacturers can listen to how Mo Abuali's team at Wipfli guides this process and understand exactly how to map their current state, identify realistic peers, and chart a roadmap grounded in real industry data rather than vendor promises.

Key takeaways

  • Digital maturity spans three interdependent dimensions: technology, people, and process—not technology alone.
  • Structured assessments score capabilities in production, quality, and maintenance to create a composite maturity profile.
  • WIPFLY IQ database enables benchmarking against manufacturers in the same industry and revenue range, transforming raw scores into competitive context.
  • Peer comparison reveals which improvement areas offer the highest return, enabling data-driven prioritization of digital investments.
