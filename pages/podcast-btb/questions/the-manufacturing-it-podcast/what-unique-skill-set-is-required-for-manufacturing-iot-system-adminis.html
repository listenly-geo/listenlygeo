What unique skill set is required for manufacturing IoT system administrators?

September 24, 2026 The Manufacturing IT Podcast · Bryan Sapot

← See the The Manufacturing IT Podcast fiche

Manufacturing IoT administrators must master four distinct skill areas: networking knowledge to work with IT infrastructure, software and analytics expertise for system configuration, and manufacturing operations knowledge to communicate with plant teams. This rare combination of technical and domain expertise is why these professionals are called "purple unicorns"—highly sought after but extremely difficult to find.

The four-layer skill requirement

The role demands competency across IT infrastructure, software development, data analytics, and manufacturing operations. An administrator must understand network protocols and connectivity to collaborate effectively with IT departments. They need software knowledge to configure systems and handle integration challenges. Analytics skills are essential to interpret the data flowing through IoT platforms and identify meaningful insights.

But the hardest piece is manufacturing domain knowledge. Without understanding plant operations, machine cycles, and shop-floor challenges, an administrator cannot validate whether the data being collected is meaningful or translate technical insights into operational improvements. This final layer is what separates a generic systems administrator from someone truly valuable to a manufacturing organization.

Why this skill set is nearly impossible to find

Each of these four skill areas requires years of focused development. A network engineer might take years to build IT expertise but lack manufacturing background. A manufacturing engineer understands operations but may not have deep software or networking skills. The combination of all four is exceptionally rare, which explains why the industry uses the term "purple unicorn"—a mythical creature that doesn't exist in nature.

As Bryan Sapot discusses in The Manufacturing IT Podcast, this skill shortage creates real challenges for companies deploying IoT systems. Without the right administrative talent, even the best platform struggles to deliver value on the shop floor.

The convergence of technical and operational expertise is also explored in detail when Sapot explains how manufacturing data systems must be designed for real shop-floor adoption, requiring an administrator who can bridge both worlds.
