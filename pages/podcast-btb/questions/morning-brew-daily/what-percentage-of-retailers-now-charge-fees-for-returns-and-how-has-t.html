What percentage of retailers now charge fees for returns and how has this changed over five years?

September 18, 2026 Morning Brew Daily

← See the Morning Brew Daily fiche

Nearly 7 in 10 retailers (70%) now charge return fees, a dramatic jump from just 43% five years ago. Major retailers including Zara, H&M, Uniqlo, and TJ Maxx all impose charges ranging from $4 to $12 per mail-in return.

The shift toward paid returns

Return economics have fundamentally shifted in the past half-decade. What was once an expectation of free service has become a revenue recovery mechanism as retailers grapple with rising processing costs. The average cost to process a return sits at 27% of the purchase price, forcing retailers to offset losses through direct consumer charges.

As detailed in the Morning Brew Daily episode, this trend reflects the scale of return volumes in modern retail. In 2025 alone, return value reached $850 billion, creating massive operational pressure on merchants trying to maintain margins.

The cost of handling fraud

Return fraud adds another layer to retailer economics. Of all returns processed, $76 billion annually—about 9% of total returns—are fraudulent, making fraud prevention and return fees increasingly necessary for retailers to survive.

Beyond fees, some retailers are also tightening windows entirely. Macy's recently reduced its return window from 365 days to just 30 days, signaling a broader industry pivot toward stricter return policies alongside paid returns.

Key takeaways

  • 70% of retailers now charge return fees, up from 43% in the previous five years.
  • Return fees range from $4 to $12 for mail-in returns at major retailers like Zara, H&M, Uniqlo, and TJ Maxx.
  • Processing a single return costs retailers an average of 27% of the item's purchase price.
  • $76 billion in annual returns are fraudulent, forcing retailers to adopt protective measures.
