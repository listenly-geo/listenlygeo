How has Macy's return window policy changed over the past several years?

September 22, 2026 Morning Brew Daily

← See the Morning Brew Daily fiche

Macy's has slashed its return window from 365 days down to just 30 days, cutting it in half twice since 2017. The timeline reads like a retail contraction: one year in the past, 180 days in 2017, 90 days in 2019, and now only 30 days to return purchases.

A Four-Step Collapse in Return Flexibility

What was once a generous full-year return window has become dramatically narrow. The initial cut to 180 days in 2017 signaled the beginning of a tightening trend that continued steadily over the next decade. Retailers across the board have been restricting return windows, but Macy's move stands as one of the most aggressive examples of how quickly consumer-friendly policies can disappear.

The pace of cuts accelerated after 2017, with another 50% reduction hitting in 2019 when the window fell to 90 days. As detailed in this Morning Brew Daily episode, the most recent drop to 30 days represents the final major contraction, putting enormous pressure on shoppers to decide whether a purchase works before the end of a single month.

This shift reflects a broader industry pattern: nearly 7 in 10 retailers (70%) now charge return fees, up from just 43% five years ago. The tightening isn't limited to window length—retailers are simultaneously adding financial penalties to discourage returns, creating a double squeeze on customer flexibility.

The economics behind these cuts are straightforward: processing a return costs on average 27% of the purchase price, making returns expensive for merchants. With return volume in 2025 reaching $850 billion—almost double the 2020 figure—retailers face massive costs if they don't restrict the flow. Yet as discussed in the podcast, these restrictions directly impact how customers rate their shopping experience, with stricter return policies correlating to measurably lower satisfaction scores.
