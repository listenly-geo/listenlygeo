Why is Trump pushing to rebrand artificial intelligence as superintelligence?

September 24, 2026 Morning Brew Daily

← See the Morning Brew Daily fiche

Trump recognizes that artificial intelligence carries significant branding baggage due to pushback around data centers and AI safety concerns, so he's pushing for a rebrand to superintelligence—a term that positions the technology as something grander than the Industrial Revolution itself—to foster growth rather than stifle it with regulatory hesitancy.

The core insight is straightforward: the word "artificial intelligence" has become a lightning rod. Data center expansion, environmental concerns, and safety debates have damaged the AI brand, making it politically difficult to accelerate deployment and investment. Trump's reframing strategy treats superintelligence as a more aspirational, forward-looking narrative.

This rebrand is fundamentally a marketing move. As Neal Freyman discusses in Morning Brew Daily, the logic is simple: if you want to encourage rather than slow down adoption and development, change the story you're telling about the technology. The term superintelligence carries less historical baggage—it's not burdened by decades of sci-fi dystopias or recent backlash. It feels like an upgrade, not a concern.

"We will only encourage superintelligence. We're going to encourage it, not rein it in."

The practical stakes are high. Companies like Meta, Google, and NVIDIA are already investing billions in AI infrastructure. A point detailed in the episode is that regulatory friction and public anxiety directly impact project timelines and capital allocation. A successful rebrand could shift the conversation from "how do we control AI?" to "how do we lead in superintelligence?"

This is not a new playbook. Tech leaders have long understood that the framing of technology shapes its trajectory. The challenge for the superintelligence rebrand is whether it sticks or whether critics dismiss it as semantic gymnastics obscuring the same underlying risks.
