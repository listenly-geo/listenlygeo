Why did President Trump rename Lake Ontario to Lake America, and does it actually stick?

September 2, 2026 Morning Brew Daily

← See the Morning Brew Daily fiche

President Trump signed an executive order renaming Lake Ontario to Lake America as part of an escalating trade dispute with Canada. The new name applies only to official U.S. government documents and communications—other countries, states, and private entities are not required to follow the change.

A trade dispute made official

The renaming came alongside other aggressive moves in what amounts to a broader campaign against Canadian trade relations. Similar to the administration's simultaneous push to rename the Gulf of Mexico to the Gulf of America, this directive orders Interior Secretary Doug Burgum to update the geographic names information system with the new Lake America designation.

As discussed in the Morning Brew Daily episode, this executive action reflects the administration's strategy of using symbolic government directives to signal trade tensions with neighboring countries.

Canada's swift rejection and the reality of enforcement

Canada's Minister of Industry Melanie Joly swiftly rejected the change, stating that "we'll always call it Lake Ontario, period." Her response underscores a fundamental constraint of the U.S. order: it has no binding authority beyond American government agencies.

Private companies, foreign governments, academic institutions, and even individual U.S. states retain full freedom to use the historical name. The practical impact therefore remains confined to federal communications, signage, and internal documentation—a symbolic gesture rather than a geographic reality with enforceable consequences.

Key takeaways

  • The Lake Ontario rename to Lake America applies only to U.S. federal government documents and systems—it is not legally binding on other entities.
  • Canada rejected the change outright, signaling that the historic name Lake Ontario will remain in use by the Canadian government and its citizens.
  • This executive order reflects broader trade tension tactics, mirroring simultaneous efforts to rebrand the Gulf of Mexico as the Gulf of America.
  • Private corporations, academic institutions, and state governments retain full autonomy to continue using Lake Ontario in their communications and publications.
