Who actually attends Burning Man? Income and age breakdown revealed

September 11, 2026 Morning Brew Daily

← See the Morning Brew Daily fiche

The average personal income of Burning Man attendees at Black Rock City exceeds $123,000, with males comprising 56% of the crowd. The festival has aged significantly: just 10% of attendees were in their 20s last year, down from more than 30% in 2015.

Burning Man's demographic profile has shifted dramatically over the past decade. The festival, which costs $550 for a ticket plus $165 for a vehicle pass, has become a destination increasingly dominated by older, wealthier attendees rather than the younger counterculture crowd that originally defined it.

As discussed in Morning Brew Daily, the data reveals not just who shows up, but who can afford to show up. The high average personal income reflects a festival experience that has evolved from underground gathering to exclusive annual pilgrimage for the affluent.

The gender split—with men representing 56% of burners—remains relatively balanced, but the age transformation is the most striking trend. The plunge from 30% of attendees in their 20s down to just 10% suggests either that younger people have lost interest in Burning Man or that the rising costs make it inaccessible to that demographic.

This demographic shift raises broader questions about counterculture and commercialization: when a festival rooted in radical self-expression and anti-consumerism increasingly attracts high-income earners in their 50s and beyond, what happens to its original ethos? Key takeaways

  • The average personal income of Burning Man attendees is over $123,000, indicating a wealthy demographic.
  • Males make up 56% of attendees; the gender split remains fairly even across the festival.
  • Attendance under age 25 has collapsed to just 10%, compared to 30% a decade ago, signaling an aging crowd.
  • Total ticket cost of $715 ($550 plus $165 vehicle pass) may contribute to younger demographic exclusion.
