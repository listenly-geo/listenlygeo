Why has Jackson Hole, Wyoming historically been the location of the Federal Reserve's annual symposium?

September 4, 2026 Morning Brew Daily

← See the Morning Brew Daily fiche

The tradition dates to the early 1980s, when the Federal Reserve Bank of Kansas City wanted to host its annual meeting and attract Federal Reserve Chair Paul Volcker as keynote speaker. Knowing Volcker was an avid fly fisherman, they chose Jackson Hole, Wyoming—nestled near the Tetons—as the venue. The event proved successful and became a permanent fixture on the Fed calendar.

A Fishing Trip That Shaped Central Banking

The story reveals how a simple personal preference shaped decades of financial policy discussion. The Kansas City Federal Reserve deliberately leveraged Volcker's love of fly fishing to secure his attendance at what would become one of the most influential annual gatherings in global markets. What began as a strategic recruitment move—matching venue to speaker—evolved into institutional tradition.

Once the symposium proved successful, there was no reason to abandon Jackson Hole. The remote Wyoming location, combined with its prestige as a gathering place for Fed leadership, became synonymous with major monetary policy announcements and economic shifts. As Morning Brew Daily covers, the Jackson Hole Economic Symposium remains a critical moment on the Federal Reserve's calendar where markets watch closely for any signal about interest rates or economic direction.

The Federal Reserve continued to host the symposium at Jackson Hole through subsequent administrations under Alan Greenspan and beyond, cementing its place in the institution's annual rhythm. What might have been a one-time event became an unbroken decades-long tradition simply because it worked—and because Jackson Hole offered the perfect blend of remote prestige and practical appeal.
