What makes placemaking the real magic behind retail survival?

September 6, 2026 The Crexi Commercial Real Estate Podcast · Paul Kurzawa

← See The Crexi Commercial Real Estate Podcast fiche

Placemaking is the emotional and experiential bond that visitors form with a specific retail destination—far beyond branding or merchandise mix. It's the human connection that helped retail survive the pandemic, transforming shopping centers into genuine community gathering places rather than transactional spaces.

The distinction matters because placemaking addresses what distinguishes a thriving retail destination from a struggling one. It's not primarily about what's sold or how the mall is marketed; it's about the intangible feeling a person experiences upon arrival. As Paul Kurzawa explains in the episode, this emotional anchor proved decisive during industry disruption.

Destinations like Century City, the Americana at Brand, and the Summit Birmingham succeeded not because they had perfect tenants or cutting-edge promotions alone, but because they created spaces where people genuinely wanted to spend time. This sense of belonging and community is what separates a destination from a mere collection of stores.

The difference between branding and placemaking

Branding communicates a promise through logos, advertising, and positioning. Placemaking manifests that promise through the entire sensory and emotional experience on the ground. A visitor walks in and immediately feels whether they belong there, whether the space reflects their values, and whether they want to return.

This layering of experience—curated retail, design, community programming, and genuine hospitality—creates what Kurzawa calls the true magic that allowed retail to endure through unprecedented challenges. Without authentic placemaking, even well-known brands and premium retailers cannot sustain visitor loyalty long-term.

The pandemic accelerated this realization. As discussed at length in this podcast, retail destinations that had invested in genuine community bonds survived and recovered faster than those relying solely on transactional models.

"It means the connection that a person makes with that place. It's probably the magic that helped retail survive the pandemic."

Beyond the definition itself, the episode delves into specific case studies showing how different properties achieved placemaking success—practical lessons in how to build experiential depth into commercial real estate.

How did the Dining Terrace at Westfield Century City redefine food court experiences in American shopping malls?

Rather than a traditional food court, Westfield created what it called the Dining Terrace at Century City with staff busing tables and proper cutlery, elevating the standard food court experience into fine dining-adjacent hospitality.

What distinguishes Westfield's branding strategy from competitor approaches in shopping center development?

Westfield created shopping destinations with the brand prominently displayed and experiences built around the brand promise, including a curated retail mix that reflected and reinforced the brand identity at every touchpoint.
