How did Cushman & Wakefield's Knoxville office launch in 2010?

September 25, 2026 The Crexi Commercial Real Estate Podcast · Justin Kazana

← See The Crexi Commercial Real Estate Podcast fiche

The Cushman & Wakefield Knoxville office opened in 2010 with seven experienced brokers on the first day, all deal-makers ready to build a presence in the market. The office has since grown to 25 brokers and now manages nearly three million square feet of space while handling some of the biggest transactions in Knoxville.

Opening a commercial real estate office requires more than just hiring bodies—it demands brokers with proven track records and market acumen. Each of the seven founding brokers brought deal-making experience, which became the foundation for sustainable growth in a competitive market. This strategy of starting lean with talent rather than scale shaped how the office would evolve over the next 15 years.

The trajectory from seven to 25 brokers reflects the market's expansion and Knoxville's growing importance as a commercial real estate hub. As Justin Kazana explains in The Crexi Commercial Real Estate Podcast, managing nearly three million square feet of space across office, industrial, and retail assets required not just hiring power, but a deliberate approach to building a strong local network and market presence.

"You've got to build. If you're going to grow of any scale, you've got to build that base so we can play the long game."

The founding team's approach to building the office reflected a philosophy of long-term market development rather than quick expansion. This patience and strategic growth mindset became visible in how the firm tackled the largest deals in Knoxville—the kind of transactions that require both local credibility and deep market relationships. Starting with experienced brokers meant the office could compete immediately and build reputation-based client relationships from day one.

When Kazana discusses the office's growth trajectory on the podcast, he emphasizes that hiring decisions were driven by competence and fit rather than headcount targets. This selectivity in building the team allowed the seven initial brokers to establish strong footholds in their respective specialties—office, industrial, and retail—before scaling.

From Launch to Market Leadership

The 15-year journey from 2010 to 2025 tells a story of strategic patience and market timing. Growing from seven to 25 brokers while managing nearly three million square feet places Cushman & Wakefield (and later Avis & Young) at the center of Knoxville's commercial real estate activity. This wasn't accidental—it was built on the foundation those original seven brokers established.
