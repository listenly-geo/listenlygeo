How does Dreamplay transform vacant department store space into family entertainment?

September 8, 2026 The Crexi Commercial Real Estate Podcast · Paul Kurzawa

← See the The Crexi Commercial Real Estate Podcast fiche

Dreamplay is a 50,000 square foot kinetic interactive family entertainment center featuring DreamWorks properties like Kung Fu Panda and Shrek, with climbing facilities and hands-on experiences where families can learn martial arts. The first facility opened in Manila at City of Dreams and proved so successful that it created new opportunities for family entertainment centers to repurpose vacant department store boxes in malls across North America.

Repurposing the "Dark Box" Problem

Shopping malls across North America faced a persistent challenge: department store closures left behind massive vacant spaces—what the industry calls "dark boxes." These 50,000 square foot voids consumed valuable mall real estate and disrupted foot traffic. Traditional retailers couldn't easily fill spaces of that scale.

Dreamplay emerged as a direct solution to this problem. As Paul Kurzawa explains in the episode, the model proved its viability in Manila before being adapted for the North American market. The kinetic entertainment concept—combining branded intellectual property with interactive physical experiences—provided a fundamentally different tenant model than traditional retail.

What makes Dreamplay distinctive is its integration of movement and learning within a branded universe. Families don't simply watch or consume; they climb, train, and engage physically with DreamWorks characters and narratives. This experiential layer transforms an empty retail box into a destination venue that drives consistent foot traffic and dwell time across the mall.

DreamWorks Animation's Strategic Role

DreamWorks Animation played an active role in developing the Dreamplay concept. The podcast details how Kurzawa's work with DreamWorks extended beyond traditional holiday experiences into this entirely new category of entertainment venue. The partnership leveraged DreamWorks' visual assets, character IP, and storytelling expertise to create immersive spaces.

The Manila opening was the proof of concept that validated the business model. Once successful, it opened doors for other developers and mall owners to consider similar entertainment-anchored approaches to their vacant department store footprints. This represents a meaningful pivot from passive retail tenancy toward active, experiential venue development.

Key takeaways

  • Dreamplay is a 50,000 square foot kinetic family entertainment center anchored by DreamWorks intellectual property and interactive physical experiences.
  • The first location in Manila at City of Dreams proved the business model's viability before expansion into North American malls.
  • Dreamplay directly addresses the "dark box" problem—vacant department store space that traditional retail cannot fill.
  • The model represents a shift from passive retail tenancy toward experiential, destination-driven venue development as a solution for mall vacancy.
