What subsea engineering modules does TechnipFMC's program include?

24 September 2026 The Namibia Oil and Gas Podcast

← See The Namibia Oil and Gas Podcast fiche

TechnipFMC's expanded subsea engineering program delivers five expert modules combining in-person and virtual training, covering offshore safety, subsea umbilicals risers and flow lines, subsea production systems services, and Life of Field operations. This comprehensive curriculum equips engineers with practical, hands-on expertise across the full subsea lifecycle.

The program structure reflects the growing demand for skilled subsea professionals in Namibia's expanding offshore sector. As the episode details, TechnipFMC has positioned this initiative to prepare local talent for real-world subsea challenges.

A structured approach to subsea competency

The five dedicated modules form a progressive learning pathway from foundational offshore safety to advanced Life of Field management. Each module builds on core technical knowledge, ensuring participants develop both theoretical understanding and practical application skills across critical subsea disciplines.

The hybrid delivery model—blending in-person workshops with virtual sessions—allows flexibility for working professionals and institutions like the Namibia University of Science and Technology to integrate the training into existing schedules. This approach also reduces barriers to participation across Namibia's distributed workforce.

Specifics about subsea umbilicals, risers, and flowlines (commonly abbreviated as SURF systems) form a major component, addressing one of the most technically demanding aspects of offshore operations. Similarly, the podcast emphasizes how production systems services training equips engineers to manage complex subsea infrastructure once fields are operational.

Building Namibia's subsea workforce

The expansion of TechnipFMC's curriculum directly supports Namibia's local content development agenda, a central theme of the Namibia Local Content Conference and Exhibition 2026 (scheduled for 19–23 October in Windhoek). By developing in-country expertise, the program reduces reliance on international contractors and builds sustainable capability within Namibia's energy sector.

Life of Field modules, in particular, prepare engineers to manage extended field operations—a critical capability as Namibia's offshore projects mature beyond initial production startup phases. This long-term thinking ensures the curriculum remains relevant as the sector evolves.

Key takeaways

  • The program comprises five distinct expert modules delivered through both in-person and virtual formats.
  • Offshore safety is positioned as a dedicated module, reflecting regulatory priorities in subsea operations.
  • SURF systems (subsea umbilicals, risers, and flowlines) and production systems services form core technical components.
  • Life of Field training prepares engineers to manage extended offshore operations over project lifecycles.
