What should happen when a lawn care owner consistently cannot meet their budgeted hours despite trying to improve efficiency?

September 7, 2026 LCR Media Podcast

← See the LCR Media Podcast fiche

If you've optimized efficiency—invested in better equipment, improved routes, eliminated wasted time—and still cannot hit your budgeted hours consistently, you should adjust the budget itself to match reality. This prevents perpetually undershooting your target man-hour rate and allows you to price accurately based on actual achievable efficiency rather than aspirational targets.

The problem: aspirational budgets versus actual performance

Many lawn care owners set budgets based on ideal conditions or best-case scenarios, then struggle when reality consistently falls short. For example, if you budgeted 45 minutes for a property but it consistently takes 50–55 minutes despite best efforts, the issue is not your execution—it's your budget. Continuing to chase an unrealistic target exhausts your team and distorts your pricing.

When you get paid hourly, as discussed in the LCR Media Podcast, every minute compounds across your entire operation. Undershooting your budgeted hours means undershooting your profitability, job after job.

The solution: reset your budget to achievable reality

The fix is straightforward: raise the budgeted hours to 50 or 55 minutes if that's what the property genuinely requires. This new budget becomes your baseline for pricing and scheduling. You are no longer chasing an impossible target; you are pricing based on what you can actually deliver.

This approach protects your margin and your team's morale. As Cornell Mack explains in the podcast episode, the difference between struggling business owners and those "crushing it" often comes down to whether they price and plan around reality or fantasy. Adjusting your budget is the bridge between the two.

Why this matters for your man-hour rate

If you're targeting a $100 per man-hour rate (a common baseline in the industry), pricing work based on false budgets means you're actually collecting far less than $100 per hour. Resetting your budget to match reality ensures that your actual collected rate aligns with your pricing strategy.

This isn't about lowering your standards—it's about building a business model that works. Once you've optimized equipment, routes, and process, your realistic budget *is* your best performance. Price from that truth, not from wishful thinking.

When should lawn care owners invest in equipment upgrades like zero-turn mowers to improve efficiency?

Equipment upgrades like zero-turn or stand-on mowers are worthwhile when your current equipment prevents you from hitting your budgeted man-hour targets consistently.

What inefficiencies should lawn care owners eliminate to improve job profitability?

Key inefficiencies include excessive drive time between properties (solved by route density—clustering properties geographically), poor equipment maintenance, wasted motion on site, and unoptimized job sequencing.

How do material costs and service-specific man-hour rates affect pricing for landscaping projects beyond mowing?

For projects involving materials—planting flowers, bushes, trees, mulch, fertilizer, or weed control—pricing includes both material costs with markup plus a service-specific man-hour rate that accounts for labor complexity.
