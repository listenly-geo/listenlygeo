How should lawn care business owners calculate their pricing based on man-hour rate?

September 2, 2026 LCR Media Podcast

← See the LCR Media Podcast fiche

Start by defining your target man-hour rate—the amount you want to earn per person working per hour. Multiply that rate by the total number of man-hours required for a job to find your baseline charge. But you must add a buffer above this break-even number to cover overhead costs and operational inefficiencies; the final price should always exceed the basic man-hour calculation.

The Man-Hour Formula: From Theory to Job Pricing

A man-hour is one person working for one hour. If three crew members work one hour on a project, that job consumes three man-hours of labor. The math is straightforward: if your target rate is $100 per man-hour and a job requires three man-hours, your minimum charge is $300.

This approach works even if you never tell your clients you charge by the hour, as discussed in the episode. Many lawn care owners quote flat fees per project instead—but the underlying economics still run on hourly rates.

Why Your Quote Must Exceed the Break-Even Number

The $300 figure in our three-person example is only the bare minimum to cover labor costs. It doesn't account for your business overhead: truck fuel, equipment maintenance, insurance, office staff, scheduling inefficiencies, or the time you spend managing jobs instead of working on them.

Real-world jobs also lose time to hidden inefficiencies—walking between job areas, retrieving supplies, transitioning between projects. These minutes vanish from billable time but still cost you money, as explained in this podcast episode. Your final price must cushion for these gaps.

Cornell Mack emphasizes that choosing the right man-hour rate target is foundational to building a profitable business. Many struggling lawn care owners underestimate this rate and end up working long hours for minimal profit. Setting a clear, defensible rate—whether $100, $95, or higher—gives you a framework for every quote you create.

In lawn care and landscaping, professionals are fundamentally paid by the hour, even if they don't explicitly bill that way. Every minute matters because inefficiencies and wasted time directly reduce profitability—five minutes of wasted time per job compounds across your entire schedule and cuts into your earnings.

Key takeaways

  • Define your man-hour rate first—this is the amount you want to earn per person per hour worked on a job.
  • Multiply man-hours by your rate to find the baseline charge, but always add a buffer for overhead and inefficiencies.
  • Time losses from inefficient scheduling, travel, and transitions directly eat into profitability and must be factored into your final price.
  • Setting a clear, confident man-hour rate is the foundation of moving from struggling self-employment to a profitable business model.
