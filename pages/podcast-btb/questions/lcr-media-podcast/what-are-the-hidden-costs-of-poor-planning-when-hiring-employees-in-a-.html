What are the hidden costs of poor planning when hiring employees in a landscape business?

September 23, 2026 LCR Media Podcast · Naylor Taliaferro

← See the LCR Media Podcast fiche

Without clear planning before hiring, you waste significant resources on wasted travel to wrong locations, purchasing forgotten equipment, and paying employees for non-billable shopping trips and equipment runs. This inefficiency directly erodes profitability and makes scaling harder, which is why establishing your ideal customer, service area, and service offerings before hiring employees is essential.

Many landscape business owners rush into hiring without first clarifying their operational foundation. The result is chaotic workflows that cost both time and money. Employees end up sitting idle or traveling to incorrect job sites because the business owner never defined the service area or customer profile upfront.

The hidden toll extends beyond just wasted miles. When you lack proper planning, you discover missing tools and equipment only once crews are on site. Employees then spend billable hours helping you pick up forgotten gear from suppliers instead of working on revenue-generating tasks. This transforms your payroll into overhead that doesn't move the business forward.

Why planning comes before payroll

As explained in this episode of the LCR Media Podcast, the successful landscape business owners structure their growth around clarity first. They identify who their ideal customer is, map out the neighborhoods where those customers live, and nail down their service offerings. Only after this foundation is solid do they bring on employees.

Poor planning forces you to make expensive decisions on the fly. Without a clear service area, you might hire workers who spend half their day driving between distant jobs that don't cluster efficiently. Route inefficiency alone can eliminate your profit margin before you even account for the wasted downtime and equipment runs.

"I knew that I would have to grow my business to achieve a certain total revenue so that my portion would be enough to replace my retail salary and hopefully then some."

What makes this quote relevant is that Taliaferro had a specific revenue target before scaling with employees. This intentionality prevented him from hiring prematurely and discovering costly operational gaps later. A business owner without clear financial and operational targets is far more likely to hire reactively, then struggle with the hidden costs of that reactive decision.

The concrete hidden costs include excessive fuel and vehicle wear from inefficient routing, employee wages paid for non-productive shopping and equipment retrieval, and the management time spent solving operational crises instead of growing the business. Each of these compounds the longer poor planning persists.

To avoid these traps, define your ideal customer profile before hiring. Identify the neighborhoods where they cluster so your service area is tight and efficient. Lock down your core service offerings—what you will and will not do—so crews know what tools and equipment they need. This foundational work, discussed in detail in the episode, turns payroll from a risk into an asset.
