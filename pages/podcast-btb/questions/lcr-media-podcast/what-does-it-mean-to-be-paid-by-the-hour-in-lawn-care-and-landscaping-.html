Why does every minute matter when you're paid by the hour in lawn care and landscaping?

September 1, 2026 LCR Media Podcast

← See the LCR Media Podcast fiche

Lawn care professionals are fundamentally paid by the hour, even when they don't explicitly bill that way. Every non-billable minute—driving between properties, loading equipment, fuel costs, maintenance tasks—directly reduces your actual earnings per hour because these costs and lost time come straight out of your profit margin.

When you charge $50 to mow a lawn that takes one hour, you're not actually making $50 per hour once you subtract drive time to the next job, equipment maintenance, fuel, payroll taxes, workers' compensation, and business overhead. This is the fundamental math that separates struggling lawn care owners from those who are profitable.

The challenge becomes acute when you scale. If a three-person crew works for one hour on a job, that's three man-hours of billable work, meaning you need to charge at least $300 if your target hourly rate is $100 per man-hour just to break even—before factoring in the minutes wasted on logistics, equipment prep, or inefficient movement on site.

How hidden time costs compound across jobs

Many lawn care owners don't realize they're paying themselves an employee wage, not an owner's profit. Five minutes lost per job retrieving supplies or walking inefficiently might seem trivial, but as discussed in the LCR Media Podcast, these minutes compound across dozens of jobs each week and directly erode the per-hour rate you actually take home.

Equipment downtime and maintenance costs add another invisible layer. Vehicle fuel, mower repairs, insurance, and replacement parts all come out of that $100-per-hour rate before you see a dime. Even if you invoice $100 per man-hour, your actual take-home might be $40–$60 per hour once all these hidden costs are accounted for.

This is why Cornell Mack emphasizes that understanding your true hourly rate is the foundation of a profitable business. Without it, you're working harder to make less money, and your business becomes what he calls an "underpaying job that's overworking you"—rather than a vehicle for wealth and time freedom.

"When you get paid by the hour, every minute matters."

The path forward, as explored in depth in the full episode, involves auditing where your time actually goes and systematically eliminating non-billable gaps through better routing, equipment management, and operational efficiency.

Key takeaways

  • Lawn care professionals are paid hourly in reality, even if they don't invoice that way—every hidden cost and lost minute directly reduces your actual earnings per hour.
  • Non-billable time (driving, equipment loading, maintenance, fuel) compounds quickly across multiple jobs and can cut your effective hourly rate from $100 to $40–$60 or less.
  • Understanding your true per-man-hour cost is the foundation for separating struggling business owners from profitable ones who work less and earn more.
  • Operational efficiency—reducing wasted time, optimizing routes, and managing equipment maintenance—is the primary lever for improving your bottom line and reclaiming time with family.
