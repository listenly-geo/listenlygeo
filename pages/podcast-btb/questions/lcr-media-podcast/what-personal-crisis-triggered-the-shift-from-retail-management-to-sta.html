What personal crisis triggered the shift from retail management to starting a lawn care business?

September 17, 2026 LCR Media Podcast · Caleb Allman

← See the LCR Media Podcast fiche

Retail management had become unsustainable—Caleb Allman's life was deteriorating under the demands of the industry, with work bleeding into every waking hour. Starting a lawn care business offered a concrete escape plan: finish work by 6 PM, skip weekends, and rebuild both his business and his relationships. It wasn't just an entrepreneurial venture; it was a lifeline to sanity.

The reality of retail management had worn him down completely. Long, unpredictable hours meant sacrificing evenings and weekends with family. The work wasn't just consuming his time—it was consuming his life. When Allman realized that the traditional retail career path would never give him back what he'd lost, he made the decision to leave.

The lawn care business became the vehicle for radical change. Unlike retail, where management decisions ripple through entire weeks of overtime, a lawn care operation has natural boundaries. The work schedule could be structured around client needs during daylight hours, leaving evenings and weekends genuinely free. This wasn't a theoretical improvement—it was a concrete system Allman could design himself.

Beyond the schedule, the lawn care business offered something equally powerful: direct ownership of outcomes. In retail, managers navigate corporate policies, inventory systems, and staffing challenges beyond their control. In lawn care, Allman controlled pricing, client selection, and operational efficiency. This autonomy transformed work from a burden into a project he could shape intentionally.

Rebuilding his relationships became the real measure of success. As Allman explains in the episode, the shift wasn't about chasing maximum profit margins—it was about having dinner with his family most nights and actually being present on weekends. The business was the mechanism; the life he wanted to live was the goal.

What makes Allman's story instructive is that the LCR Media Podcast explores not just his personal journey, but the systemic principles he's developed to help other landscape professionals escape the same trap. The personal crisis was real, but the business principles that emerged from it have proven applicable far beyond his own story.

Today, this work-life balance philosophy is central to his coaching approach at LCR Summit, an annual gathering in Louisville, Kentucky where landscape business owners learn from peers who've successfully rebuilt their lives and businesses simultaneously.

How does route density impact profitability and work-life balance in lawn care operations?

Route density is about not driving all over town wasting drive time. When you're scattered geographically, you work past dinner, work weekends, and can't achieve the work-life balance you're seeking.

What are the two core metrics lawn care business owners must focus on to achieve profitability from day one?

The two key numbers are man hour rate and budgeted hours. Man hour rate is what you need to make per hour as a business, accounting for all expenses and profit margins.

Why is making price increases and policy changes about customer benefit more effective than explaining business financial needs?

Customers prioritize their own needs and concerns over a contractor's financial situation. When communicating changes like price increases, framing them around customer benefit resonates far more effectively.
