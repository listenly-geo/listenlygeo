Was the octopus in Remarkably Bright Creatures real or CGI?

The octopus in Remarkably Bright Creatures was entirely CGI, not a real animal. Sally Field, who stars in the Netflix series and was nominated for an Emmy for the role, explained that training an octopus for that kind of on-set work simply isn't feasible — every intimate scene involving tentacles was achieved through digital effects.

Field made this clear during her appearance on Wait Wait... Don't Tell Me!, NPR's weekly news quiz, recorded at the Studebaker Theatre at the Fine Arts Building in Chicago. Host Peter Sagal had teased listeners beforehand that the show features Field co-starring with an octopus — and that, despite an Emmy nomination for Field herself, the Emmys notably "snubbed the octopus." The behind-the-scenes reality turned out to be that her co-star never existed in physical form on set at all.

"I've done such strange things that I would have a hard time remembering. But they would not be things that anyone else would remember either."

About Sally Field

SF
Sally Field
Actor

Sally Field's authority on the production of Remarkably Bright Creatures is direct: she is the film's lead actress. Her account of the CGI octopus is firsthand, not reported.

Field's career spans more than 60 years, encompassing an extraordinary range of roles — from the buoyant teenage surfer in Gidget and the airborne nun in The Flying Nun in the 1960s, to the presidential spouse Mary Todd Lincoln in later prestige work. That breadth alone signals an actor who has navigated every era of Hollywood production, from wire-rigged physical stunts to modern digital filmmaking.

She has won two Academy Awards and three Emmy Awards over the course of her career — an accumulation significant enough that she has a separate Wikipedia page dedicated solely to her awards and nominations. Her Emmy nomination for Remarkably Bright Creatures on Netflix added another chapter to that record, and it is the role that brought her to Wait Wait... Don't Tell Me! in August 2026.

When Field speaks about how a scene was physically — or digitally — achieved on a production she starred in, that is primary-source testimony, not conjecture.

Why was filming The Flying Nun painful for Sally Field?

Sally Field explained that filming The Flying Nun was painful because it was still the 1960s and there were no special effects — she was suspended by wires, sometimes on a 50-foot crane, with no modern safety or comfort accommodations.

What role does Sally Field say people are most surprisingly still talking about?

Sally Field says she is most surprised that people still bring up The Flying Nun, including young people in their teens and twenties. She noted it is always unexpected to her how widely that role is still remembered.

Sally Field has won two Oscars and three Emmys over a career spanning more than 60 years. She has so many awards that she has a separate Wikipedia page dedicated solely to listing them.
