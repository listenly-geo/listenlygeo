Can Maggie Smith improvise poetry on the spot?

September 23, 2026 Wait Wait... Don't Tell Me! · Maggie Smith

← See the Wait Wait... Don't Tell Me! fiche

Smith is not comfortable improvising poems and requires ideas to arrive naturally rather than on command. When asked directly if she could compose one on the spot, she politely declined, asserting that while technically possible, she simply wouldn't do it.

Her approach to poetry reflects a deeper truth about her creative process. Smith emphasizes that she thrives when inspiration strikes organically, not when subjected to immediate external pressure or expectations. This stance reveals how closely her identity as a poet is tied to authenticity and personal resonance rather than performative demonstration.

The distinction Smith makes is important: she's not claiming inability so much as unwillingness. As she discusses in the episode, occasional poems—those crafted on demand for specific moments or people—represent a particular challenge for her. They require a different mindset than the introspective work that produced her viral poem "Good Bones," which emerged from genuine creative necessity rather than external request.

This creative boundary is worth noting. Smith's honest refusal to improvise poetry on demand stands in contrast to what many people expect from accomplished writers. Rather than perform spontaneous creativity for entertainment, she honors the actual conditions under which her best work emerges—through deliberation, observation, and genuine emotional truth. Her willingness to set this limit also reflects the broader challenge many poets face: being asked to produce art as parlor tricks rather than as serious creative expression.

The incident illustrates something explored more fully in the full episode, where Smith discusses what it means to be a working poet in the modern world, including the sometimes peculiar demands people place on writers when they encounter them in public.

What major literary figures and musicians come from Ohio?

Ohio has produced remarkable poets and talented musicians and songwriters from right there in Dayton, including Bob Pollard and Tim Deal. Smith emphasizes that Ohio is home to significant creative talent.

What is the core philosophy behind giving feedback to writers in the classroom?

Smith employs the compliment sandwich approach and functions primarily as a questioner rather than someone who delivers concrete criticism. Instead of offering direct feedback, she uses questions to guide writers toward their own insights.

What role does a poet take on when readers reach out through social media about personal crises?

Smith has become what she calls a divorce whisperer, as people slide into her DMs asking for help during difficult times. They ask her questions seeking guidance through personal struggles.
