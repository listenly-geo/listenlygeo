How did a stand-up comedian transition from playing college basketball to winning their first comedy competition?

September 16, 2026 Wait Wait... Don't Tell Me! · Leslie Jones

← See the Wait Wait... Don't Tell Me! fiche

Leslie Jones was a basketball player at Colorado State University more focused on partying than academics—her GPA was 1.7—when a friend recognized her natural comedic talent and signed her up for a campus competition without permission. She put together a 10-minute set, tested it with friends for feedback, performed it, and won, launching her comedy career in a single night.

The turning point came when a perceptive friend saw something in Jones that she hadn't yet recognized in herself. Rather than simply encouraging her to try stand-up, this friend took direct action by entering her into the funniest person on campus competition without asking. It was a gamble that paid off.

Jones approached her first performance methodically. She crafted a 10-minute set and performed it for her friends before taking the stage at the competition. Their feedback shaped the material, giving her a tested foundation for her debut. This preparation, combined with her natural ability to make people laugh, led her to victory in that first contest.

From the basketball court to the comedy stage

The shift from athlete to comedian wasn't premeditated. Jones had been pursuing basketball at Colorado State University, but her priorities—and her grades—reflected that partying ranked higher than studying. It took someone else's faith in her comedic instinct to redirect her entirely. A point explored further in the full episode on Listenly, where Jones discusses how that single competition changed everything.

What made this first win significant wasn't just the victory itself—it was the validation. Winning immediately proved that her comedic talent was real and marketable. From that moment, Jones committed fully to stand-up comedy. She went on to become a cast member on Saturday Night Live, personally recommended by Chris Rock, and has since hosted shows on HGTV, including Roast My Rental.

The entire journey—from a college basketball player with a mediocre GPA to a comedy competition winner—happened because someone believed in her potential even when she didn't yet see it herself.
