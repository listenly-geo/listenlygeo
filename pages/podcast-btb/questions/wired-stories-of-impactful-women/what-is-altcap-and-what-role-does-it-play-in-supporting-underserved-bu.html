What is AltCap and what role does it play in supporting underserved business owners?

September 16, 2026 WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women · Kesi Milligan

← See the WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women fiche

AltCap is a Community Development Financial Institution (CDFI) designed to deliver capital to disinvested communities and early-stage startups that traditional banks routinely reject. The organization provides small business loans up to $350,000 to entrepreneurs who cannot find financing elsewhere, filling a critical gap in access to credit for underserved and underprivileged business owners, particularly those in their first three years of operation.

Filling the lending gap for zero-to-three-year startups

Traditional banking institutions have strict lending criteria that exclude many promising entrepreneurs. AltCap steps into this gap by serving startups and small businesses during their most vulnerable stage—the first three years—when conventional lenders see too much risk. This timing is critical: founders need capital precisely when traditional banks won't touch them.

The organization's mission extends beyond loans. By operating as a CDFI, AltCap explicitly targets disinvested communities—neighborhoods and populations historically starved of financial resources. As explored in detail on the podcast, this approach addresses a structural inequality in American finance where geography and historical disinvestment determine access to capital.

Why AltCap matters in commercial real estate and entrepreneurship

Kesi Milligan's involvement with AltCap revealed a deeper problem: minority entrepreneurs and small business owners seeking commercial real estate guidance had virtually no representation in the brokerage industry. This gap motivated her shift from residential real estate to commercial brokerage, where she could directly connect underserved founders to both financing and property solutions.

The $350,000 lending ceiling at AltCap may seem modest compared to conventional commercial loans, but it's precisely the size needed for bootstrapping entrepreneurs to establish operations, purchase inventory, or secure their first commercial space. As detailed in this episode, access to this tier of capital is transformative when it's otherwise unavailable.

"When it gets right at the difficult moments when you think you just can't take it anymore, that's when the breakthrough comes."

The breakthrough Milligan references applies directly to AltCap's clients: when an entrepreneur secures capital they've been denied elsewhere, it often arrives at the exact moment they're ready to give up. That's when the real work begins—and AltCap ensures they have the resources to push forward.

To understand how Milligan's own career transitions informed her approach to supporting underserved founders, read about her shift from residential to commercial real estate, where these lessons became actionable.

Key takeaways

  • AltCap is a CDFI explicitly designed to serve startups and small businesses in disinvested communities during their zero-to-three-year lifecycle.
  • The organization provides loans up to $350,000 to entrepreneurs who cannot access traditional bank financing.
  • A critical gap exists in representation for minority and underserved business owners seeking commercial real estate guidance alongside capital.
  • AltCap's lending model addresses structural inequalities in American finance by targeting both capital-starved geographies and underrepresented founders.
