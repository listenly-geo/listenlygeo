What local community service projects have Rotary clubs in central Missouri implemented in recent years?

September 10, 2026 WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women · Jodi Sundrum

← See the WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women fiche

Rotary clubs across central Missouri have built 12 community gardens at Ruthmore Gardens in Liberty, constructed dog parks and pavilions in public spaces, and partnered with Feed Northland Kids and Feed Lee Summit to combat food insecurity. The Liberty Rotary Club alone distributes $20,000 annually in scholarships, including a matching program with William Jewell College offering $5,000 per year for all four years of study.

Gardens, Parks, and Meals on Wheels

Beyond gardens and green spaces, Rotary clubs have invested in practical infrastructure that strengthens neighborhood gathering spaces. Dog parks and pavilions built by club members provide accessible venues for families and seniors to connect and enjoy outdoor time together.

The organization also supports Meals on Wheels programs that deliver nutritious food to elderly residents who face mobility challenges. As Jodi Sundrum explains in the episode, this work reflects a core principle: meeting immediate community needs while building sustainable local systems.

Addressing Food Insecurity and Education

Partnerships with Feed Northland Kids and Feed Lee Summit show how Rotary clubs collaborate with existing nonprofits to maximize impact. These alliances ensure that children and families facing food insecurity receive consistent support without duplication of effort.

Scholarship programs represent another pillar of Rotary's educational commitment. The Liberty club's $20,000 annual scholarship fund, reinforced by matching grants from William Jewell College, removes financial barriers for local students pursuing higher education. This work is detailed further in the podcast, where Sundrum describes how sustained funding creates pathways to opportunity.

"We have so much here in the U.S. that we're fortunate to feel that way and we just feel like there's a pull for us to just do more because we have more."

Key takeaways

  • Rotary clubs in central Missouri have established 12 community gardens at Ruthmore Gardens in Liberty, providing fresh produce access and green community spaces.
  • Partnerships with Feed Northland Kids and Feed Lee Summit directly address childhood and family food insecurity across the region.
  • The Liberty Rotary Club awards $20,000 in scholarships annually, including $5,000 per year for four years through a matching grant program with William Jewell College.
  • Dog parks, pavilions, and Meals on Wheels support create physical and nutritional infrastructure that strengthens elderly care and neighborhood connectivity.

What responsibilities does a district governor hold within Rotary's organizational structure?

A district governor visits all clubs in their district to establish personal connection and assess community work, overseeing district operations and ensuring alignment with Rotary's mission across multiple locations.

What are the four pillars of the Rotary Four-Way Test used to guide decision-making?

The Four-Way Test asks: Is it the truth? Is it fair to all concerned? Will it build goodwill and better friendships? And will it be beneficial to all concerned?

What is the history and mission of Rotary International as a service organization?

Rotary was founded by Paul Harris in 1905 in Chicago as a gathering of business people—bankers, attorneys, real estate professionals—dedicated to community service and international collaboration.
