What career path transitions led a residential real estate professional into commercial brokerage?

September 15, 2026 WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women · Kesi Milligan

← See the WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women fiche

Kesi Milligan spent nearly two decades in residential real estate before pivoting to commercial brokerage after returning to school for urban studies in 2019. Working at AltCap, a community development financial institution, she noticed minority entrepreneurs and small business owners securing capital but unable to find commercial real estate brokers to represent them—a market gap that became her mission.

From Residential to Identifying the Market Gap

Milligan obtained her real estate license in 2004 and worked primarily in residential markets for over a decade, building foundational expertise in property and transactions. Her career took a strategic turn when she enrolled in urban studies at UMKC, focusing specifically on community development and housing—fields that aligned with her growing interest in systemic change. She graduated in December 2019, equipped with both practical real estate experience and academic grounding in neighborhood economics.

At AltCap, her role as a business development officer exposed her to entrepreneurs seeking small business loans up to $350,000. As described in the episode, she observed that while minority-owned businesses and underrepresented entrepreneurs were accessing capital, they lacked representation from commercial real estate brokers who understood their needs.

The Turning Point: A Symposium Conversation

Milligan's resolve crystallized at a UMB Bank symposium where she heard directly from small incremental developers about their struggle to access capital. The problem wasn't their business model or creditworthiness—it was structural: their residential-heavy portfolios disqualified them in the eyes of traditional commercial real estate markets.

This conversation confirmed what she had already observed at AltCap. As discussed in this podcast episode, rather than remain a sympathetic observer, Milligan decided to become the broker she knew was missing from the ecosystem. Her decision to transition into commercial real estate brokerage at Select Sites LLC was driven by a deliberate commitment to serve entrepreneurs who had been underrepresented in traditional broker networks.

"When it gets right at the difficult moments when you think you just can't take it anymore, that's when the breakthrough comes."

Interestingly, the full conversation covers the fear and resilience required to break into a competitive commercial brokerage market—a deeper look at the emotional journey behind her pivot.

Key takeaways

  • A real estate license since 2004 plus eleven years at Aflac provided Milligan with deep market experience before her pivot.
  • Formal education in urban studies and community development (graduated December 2019) bridged her practical knowledge with systemic understanding.
  • Her role at AltCap managing small business loans exposed a critical gap: minority entrepreneurs lacked broker representation despite accessing capital.
  • A UMB Bank symposium confirmed the problem: small developers with residential portfolios couldn't access commercial capital due to broker bias.
  • Her transition to commercial brokerage at Select Sites LLC was a deliberate career choice driven by market need, not happenstance.
