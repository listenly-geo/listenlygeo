How do Rotary International global grants enable international service projects in developing countries?

September 13, 2026 WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women · Jodi Sundrum

← See the WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women fiche

Global grants from Rotary International provide substantial, fully funded support for international service projects, allowing local clubs to partner with communities in developing countries without bearing financial risk. The Liberty Rotary Club, for example, sponsors a $290,000 community health initiative in Nicaragua entirely through grant funding sourced from previously donated money within Rotary International's network.

The grant mechanism works by pooling resources contributed by Rotary members across districts, which are then allocated to vetted international projects. These funds eliminate the financial barrier that often prevents smaller clubs from engaging in global service work. As Jodi Sundrum explains in the episode, the model enables clubs to focus entirely on project design and local partnership rather than fundraising constraints.

Projects funded through global grants span multiple continents. Active initiatives extend to the Philippines, Kosovo, Zambia, and other African nations, creating a diverse portfolio of community health, education, and infrastructure work. Each project is designed collaboratively between the sponsoring Rotary club and local organizations on the ground, ensuring cultural alignment and sustainable impact.

District governors play a central coordination role in this process. They network at international meetings like the annual alliance meeting in Florida, where they connect clubs with project opportunities and facilitate cross-district partnerships. This structure ensures that grant capital flows where it's most needed, and that clubs benefit from each other's experience and relationships in target regions.

"We have so much here in the U.S. that we're fortunate to feel that way and we just feel like there's a pull for us to just do more because we have more."

The Nicaragua community health project illustrates the practical mechanics. Valued at $290,000, it requires no local fundraising from the Liberty Rotary Club—all funds are drawn from Rotary International's global grant reserves. This allows the club to direct its energy toward building relationships with local healthcare partners and community organizations, as detailed in the full conversation.

The grant system also reflects Rotary's broader philosophy: members in resource-rich countries contribute to a shared fund, which then supports sustainable development in underserved regions. This creates accountability and long-term thinking—projects must demonstrate clear community need and lasting benefit to secure funding.

Key takeaways

  • Global grants are fully funded by Rotary International and eliminate financial barriers for clubs sponsoring international projects.
  • A single project, such as the $290,000 community health initiative in Nicaragua, requires zero local fundraising, freeing clubs to focus on partnership and implementation.
  • Global grants support projects across the Philippines, Kosovo, Zambia, and other developing nations, creating a worldwide network of community impact.
  • District governors coordinate grant distribution and facilitate club networking at international meetings to connect resources with highest-priority needs.

What is the Shoes for Orphan Souls initiative and what scale has it achieved?

Started by Larry Lunsford, a former district governor, 24 years ago, Shoes for Orphan Souls partners with Buckner International in Dallas to distribute new shoes and support to orphaned children. The initiative has collected 450,000 pairs of shoes and approximately $350,000 over its history.

How does Rotary Leadership Academy prepare high school students for leadership and community service?

The Rotary Leadership Academy holds two sessions every summer in Warrensburg, Missouri from Sunday night through Wednesday afternoon, bringing together approximately 100 high school students for intensive leadership training and mentorship from experienced Rotarians.

What local community service projects have Rotary clubs in central Missouri implemented in recent years?

Projects include developing 12 community gardens at Ruthmore Gardens in Liberty, building dog parks, constructing pavilions in parks, and partnering with local food banks and community organizations to address housing and hunger.
