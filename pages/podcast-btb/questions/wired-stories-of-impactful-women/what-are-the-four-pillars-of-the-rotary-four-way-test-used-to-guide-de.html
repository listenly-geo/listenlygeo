How does the Rotary Four-Way Test guide decision-making?

September 8, 2026 WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women · Jodi Sundrum

← See the WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women fiche

The Four-Way Test poses four fundamental questions to evaluate any decision: Is it the truth? Is it fair to all concerned? Will it build goodwill and better friendships? And will it be beneficial to all concerned? This framework encourages leaders and members to look beyond personal benefit and consider how their actions ripple through the entire community.

At its core, the Four-Way Test challenges decision-makers to move beyond self-interest. Rather than asking only "What's good for me?" Rotarians apply a lens that encompasses truthfulness, fairness, relationship-building, and collective benefit. This systematic approach transforms individual choices into communal ones, embedding ethical thinking into everyday professional and personal decisions.

The test's power lies in its simplicity and its applicability. Whether navigating a business transaction, resolving a conflict within a club, or planning a community service project, Rotary members across the organization's 35,000 clubs worldwide use these four questions as a compass. Each pillar builds on the last: truthfulness establishes the foundation, fairness ensures no one bears an unfair burden, goodwill strengthens relationships, and universal benefit ensures sustainable outcomes.

The Four-Way Test originated as Rotary's ethical framework and has become a trusted tool for leaders far beyond the organization. In practice, it shifts the conversation from "Can we do this?" to "Should we do this—and in a way that honors everyone involved?" Sundrum explains how this mindset shapes Rotary's approach to decision-making at every level, from club governance to the organization's global service projects in over a dozen countries.

For professionals and entrepreneurs, the Four-Way Test offers a practical way to align business decisions with personal values. It creates accountability—not just to shareholders or stakeholders, but to a broader ethical standard. When applied consistently, it fosters trust, strengthens relationships, and builds the kind of goodwill that sustains organizations over decades.

Key takeaways

  • The Four-Way Test asks: Is it the truth? Is it fair? Will it build goodwill? Will it benefit all?
  • This framework shifts decision-making from self-benefit to collective impact.
  • The test is used by Rotary's 1.2 million members across 35,000 clubs to guide both personal and organizational choices.
  • Applying the Four-Way Test builds trust, accountability, and sustainable relationships.

What is the history and mission of Rotary International as a service organization?

Rotary was founded by Paul Harris in 1905 in Chicago as a gathering of business people who came together to serve their community. Today, Rotary International has grown to more than 1.2 million members across 35,000 clubs worldwide, united by a mission of service above self.
