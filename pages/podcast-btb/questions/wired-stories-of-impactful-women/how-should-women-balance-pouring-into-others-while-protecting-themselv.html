How should women balance pouring into others while protecting themselves from burnout?

September 25, 2026 · WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women · Cyndi Garza

← See the WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women fiche

The key is to establish clear boundaries and protect dedicated rest days, even when it feels uncomfortable at first. Building time away from work—whether for family, exercise, or personal care—actually increases your productivity and makes you more effective, not less.

Protect your non-negotiable rest days

Setting aside specific days where you don't enter your business is not a luxury—it's essential maintenance. As Cyndi Garza explains in her discussion on the podcast, she protects Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Sundays completely. These are non-negotiable time blocks reserved for family, long walks, workouts, and meal planning—activities that refill the well.

The resistance to taking these breaks is real. When you're driven to help others and see opportunity everywhere, stepping away can feel selfish. But enforcing this discipline returns enormous benefits: higher productivity during working hours, a better attitude, and genuine sustainability in what you do.

"We can't put ourselves into one box. We have a lot of things going on. We're mom, we're Mimi, we're leader, all of those things."

Rest is not laziness—it's fuel

One of the biggest myths holding women back is the belief that constant availability equals dedication. The opposite is true: rest days directly improve your effectiveness and your relationships. When you're well-rested and mentally clear, you're more present with people, better at problem-solving, and genuinely more enjoyable to be around.

This isn't about working less overall. It's about creating rhythm—periods of intense focus and giving, followed by periods of genuine restoration. The coaching philosophy explored throughout this episode emphasizes that sustainable leadership requires bringing all dimensions of life into balance, not compartmentalizing work as separate from wellbeing.

For women in leadership and entrepreneurship, the challenge is particularly acute. You're managing multiple roles—professional, personal, family, community—and the pressure to pour into all of them simultaneously is relentless. But the full conversation reveals how building these boundaries early becomes your competitive advantage, not your limitation.

Why discomfort at the start is normal

If protecting rest days feels awkward initially, that's actually a sign you need them. The discomfort often signals you've been running on empty for longer than you realized. The first week of enforcing boundaries might feel strange—you might check emails, feel guilty, or wonder if you're letting people down.

That passes. What remains is the clarity that you can't pour from an empty cup, and that protecting your own wellbeing is not selfish—it's a prerequisite for being your best self in all your roles.

Key takeaways

  • Designate specific days (at minimum weekly) when you completely step away from work—no exceptions.
  • Use rest days for genuine restoration: exercise, family time, personal projects, meal planning—activities that refill you.
  • Expect initial discomfort when setting boundaries; this feeling will fade as you experience the benefits.
  • Rest directly increases productivity and improves your presence with others; it's the opposite of laziness.
