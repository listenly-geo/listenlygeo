What responsibilities does a district governor hold within Rotary's organizational structure?

September 9, 2026 WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women · Jodi Sundrum

← See the WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women fiche

A district governor serves as the operational and strategic leader for all Rotary clubs within their district, visiting every club to build personal connections while assessing local community work. They oversee district operations through monthly board meetings with 12 assistant governors, manage the foundation dinner fundraiser, organize the April district conference, and ensure all clubs meet Rotary International's expectations and goals.

The role represents a five-year leadership commitment that builds progressively through club presidency, assistant governorship, and eventually the district governor position itself. This structured pathway ensures leaders bring proven experience to the role. As Jodi Sundrum describes in the episode, she moved through these positions naturally within her own Rotary journey, having joined in 2017 and advanced to district governor after serving her club and district at multiple levels.

Managing a district spanning 52 clubs across Missouri

The district governor role demands hands-on oversight of a large geographic area and numerous organizations. Sundrum's district encompasses 52 clubs across central and northern Missouri, requiring regular personal visits to maintain relationships and assess how each club implements Rotary's service mission. These visits serve a dual purpose: building the personal connections that strengthen the organization's culture, while also ensuring accountability to Rotary's global standards.

Monthly board meetings with 12 assistant governors provide the operational structure needed to coordinate this network. Each assistant governor supports multiple clubs, creating a clear line of communication and delegation. The podcast details how this structure enables effective governance of diverse community initiatives, from local scholarship programs to international service projects. The district governor ultimately anchors this system, ensuring coherent strategy and accountability across the entire district.

Major events and fundraising responsibilities

Beyond governance, the district governor oversees the district's signature fundraising event—the foundation dinner, which serves as the biggest annual fundraiser for the district's philanthropic work. This event generates critical funding for projects like scholarships, global service initiatives, and local community support. The district governor's leadership is essential to making this event successful and meaningful.

The April district conference represents another major responsibility, bringing together delegates from all clubs to celebrate achievements, share best practices, and reaffirm Rotary's values. One particularly striking dimension of the district governor role that emerges throughout the podcast episode is how Sundrum balances strategic oversight with genuine personal engagement—visiting all 52 clubs isn't just a box-check exercise, but a reflection of Rotary's core value that leaders know and support their communities directly.

Key takeaways

  • The district governor visits all clubs in their district to establish personal connections and assess community work aligned with Rotary's mission.
  • Operational oversight happens through monthly board meetings with 12 assistant governors, creating a clear chain of command and accountability.
  • The district governor runs the foundation dinner, the largest annual fundraiser that supports district and global service projects.
  • The April district conference is organized by the district governor to bring all clubs together and reinforce Rotary's values and goals.
  • The role ensures all clubs within the district meet Rotary International's expectations and contribute to the organization's global vision.

What are the four pillars of the Rotary Four-Way Test used to guide decision-making?

The Four-Way Test asks: Is it the truth? Is it fair to all concerned? Will it build goodwill and better friendships? And will it be beneficial to all concerned? These four questions guide Rotarians in their professional and personal decisions.

What is the history and mission of Rotary International as a service organization?

Rotary was started by Paul Harris in 1905 in Chicago as a gathering of business people including bankers, attorneys, and real estate professionals who met to combine their business interests with community service. Today, it is a global organization with 1.2 million members across 35,000 clubs dedicated to service and fellowship.
