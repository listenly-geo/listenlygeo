When does education become an excuse to avoid action?

September 21, 2026 WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women · Kesi Milligan

← See the WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women fiche

Continuous education can become a crutch to delay entrepreneurial action rather than a path to readiness. Kesi Milligan found herself enrolling in training after training—real estate forums, urban development programs, neighborhood initiatives—before realizing she already possessed the knowledge to execute. A mentor's direct advice shifted her mindset: stop learning and start doing.

The trap is subtle. Each new course, certification, or program feels productive, like progress toward your goal. But when you pause to examine honestly whether you're building skills or building excuses, the distinction becomes clear. Knowledge gaps rarely justify inaction the way we tell ourselves they do. Most entrepreneurs discover they knew far more than they believed when forced to move forward anyway.

Kesi Milligan's experience illustrates this perfectly. She had already decided what she wanted to do—transition into commercial real estate brokerage—yet kept returning to more training programs. In her interview on WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women, she describes recognizing the pattern: education had shifted from enabler to obstacle. The real barrier was fear masquerading as preparation.

A crucial reframe emerges from her story: courage doesn't come from perfect knowledge. As discussed in the episode, courage is the willingness to act despite uncertainty. You don't need to be courageous to execute tasks you already master—courage is required precisely when you move forward with what you know while accepting gaps you'll fill along the way.

"When it gets right at the difficult moments when you think you just can't take it anymore, that's when the breakthrough comes."

The mindset shift requires honesty. Ask whether the next training solves a real gap or postpones a difficult decision. Most founders discover they were ready months before they admitted it to themselves. The knowledge was there; the permission to be imperfect while executing was missing.

If you find yourself accumulating credentials without launching, consider whether a mentor's direct feedback might serve you better than another course. Kesi Milligan's full story on the podcast explores not just how she broke through this pattern, but how she channeled her diverse background into her mission—and what cost the delays had imposed on her vision.

What role does fear play in identifying proximity to meaningful goals?

Fear should be treated as a north star because once fear starts to set in, that means you are getting closer to what you are trying to accomplish. The body registers this as fear, but it's actually a signal of proximity to meaningful growth.
