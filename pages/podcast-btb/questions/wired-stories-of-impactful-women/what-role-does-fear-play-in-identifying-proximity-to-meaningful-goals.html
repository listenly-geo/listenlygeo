What role does fear play in identifying proximity to meaningful goals?

September 20, 2026 WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women · Kesi Milligan

← See the WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women fiche

Fear should be treated as a north star guiding you toward your real goals — the moment fear starts to intensify, you're actually getting closer to what you're trying to accomplish. When the negative voice tells you that you don't belong or that you're not capable enough, that's a signal something meaningful is just ahead, not a reason to turn back.

The body's natural protective mechanism generates fear signals to keep us safe, but this same mechanism can become a trap if we misinterpret it. When you're stepping into unfamiliar territory—launching a business, changing careers, or pursuing something you've never done before—fear is inevitable. This discomfort is the evidence you're moving toward growth, not away from it.

As explored in the WIRED episode with Kesi Milligan, the breakthrough moment often arrives at the exact point when you feel you cannot continue. You're broke. You're scared. You don't know what you're doing. That's precisely when transformation happens. The key is recognizing that courage isn't the absence of fear—it's doing what scares you anyway.

"When it gets right at the difficult moments when you think you just can't take it anymore, that's when the breakthrough comes."

Never be afraid of success itself. Instead, let fear become your internal compass. When you hear doubt creeping in—whether it's imposter syndrome, financial anxiety, or the voice saying you don't fit the mold—treat it as confirmation that you're on the right path. The fear is proof that something important is happening.
