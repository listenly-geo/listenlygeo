How did Rotary International begin, and what drives its global mission today?

September 7, 2026 WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women · Jodi Sundrum

← See the WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women fiche

Rotary International was founded in 1905 by Paul Harris in Chicago as a gathering of business professionals—bankers, attorneys, and real estate specialists—who met weekly at lunchtime and rotated meeting locations, which inspired the organization's name. Today, Rotary operates as a global service organization with 1.2 million members across 35,000 clubs worldwide, though membership growth is concentrated in Asia and parts of Europe while the United States has experienced decline like other traditional service organizations.

From Chicago Networking to Global Service

When Paul Harris established Rotary in 1905, he brought together a small circle of Chicago business leaders seeking both professional connection and a purpose beyond profit. The rotating meeting locations quickly became emblematic of the organization's name and philosophy—keeping members engaged across different neighborhoods and perspectives.

The organization's expansion from a local Chicago group to an international network reflects a fundamental shift in how Rotary approaches community impact. As explained in the episode, what began as informal business lunches evolved into structured service programs with measurable global outcomes.

Where Growth Happens—And Where Challenges Lie

The geographic distribution of Rotary's membership today tells a revealing story. While Asia and select European nations are experiencing robust growth, the United States—Rotary's birthplace—has seen membership decline, a trend mirrored across many long-established volunteer organizations.

Jodi Sundrum, who joined Rotary in 2017 after a casual invitation to a networking meeting, has witnessed firsthand how the organization attracts new members through a combination of professional networking and service opportunity. In the podcast conversation, Sundrum shares how she moved from new member to club president within two years, then into leadership roles spanning multiple states—a trajectory that illustrates how Rotary creates pathways for members to deepen their impact.

"We have so much here in the U.S. that we're fortunate to feel that way and we just feel like there's a pull for us to just do more because we have more."

Sundrum's reflection underscores Rotary's core mission: to mobilize business professionals and community leaders not simply to meet and network, but to translate their resources and influence into sustained, measurable change. The episode also explores how individual Rotary clubs execute this mission through local scholarships, food security programs, and international health initiatives.

Key takeaways

  • Rotary International was founded in 1905 in Chicago by Paul Harris as a weekly gathering of business leaders who rotated meeting locations—the origin of the organization's name.
  • The organization now spans 35,000 clubs with 1.2 million members globally, making it one of the largest service networks in the world.
  • Membership growth is strongest in Asia and parts of Europe, while the United States has experienced decline similar to other traditional volunteer organizations.
  • Rotary's mission combines professional networking with structured community service, creating clear pathways for members to advance into leadership roles and amplify their impact.
