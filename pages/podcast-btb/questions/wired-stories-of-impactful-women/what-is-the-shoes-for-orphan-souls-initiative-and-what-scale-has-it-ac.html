How Shoes for Orphan Souls Distributed 450,000 Pairs in 24 Years

September 12, 2026 WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women · Jodi Sundrum

← See the WIRED: Stories of Impactful Women fiche

Shoes for Orphan Souls, started by Larry Lunsford 24 years ago, partners with Buckner International in Dallas to distribute new shoes to children in need across Guatemala, the Dominican Republic, and other countries globally. Over its two decades, the initiative has collected 450,000 pairs of shoes and approximately $350,000, plus socks and shoelaces, with ambitious plans to reach half a million pairs by its 25th anniversary in April.

The program operates through a straightforward model: Rotary clubs and community members donate new shoes, which are then distributed through Buckner International's established networks in developing regions. As Jodi Sundrum discusses in the episode, the initiative combines the practical need for footwear with a deeper commitment to supporting vulnerable children—those living in orphanages or extreme poverty who would otherwise have no access to basic shoes.

The scale of impact speaks to the power of sustained, collaborative effort. The 25th anniversary goal of collecting 25,000 new pairs in April 2026 reflects Rotary's strategy to accelerate impact in milestone years. By partnering with corporate sponsors, the initiative aims to transform awareness into action, making the final push toward half a million pairs a tangible, achievable milestone rather than a distant dream.

What makes Shoes for Orphan Souls distinctive is its focus on dignity—shoes aren't charity; they're a foundation for school attendance, health, and participation in daily life. The money collected supplements the shoe donations, helping cover shipping costs to international partners and supporting related needs in those communities. In the WIRED podcast episode, Sundrum emphasizes how Rotary's global reach and local club network allow programs like this to scale without losing their personal, human-centered mission.

"We have so much here in the U.S. that we're fortunate to feel that way and we just feel like there's a pull for us to just do more because we have more."

The program also reflects a broader philosophy within Rotary: that access to basic resources—in this case, shoes—can unlock opportunity for the world's most vulnerable children. Learn more about how Rotary clubs coordinate these global initiatives and the personal stories behind the donations in the full episode.

A 24-Year Journey Toward Half a Million Pairs

Larry Lunsford's vision began two decades ago with a simple observation: children in developing nations often lack even basic footwear. What started as a modest initiative within Rotary has grown into a structured, measurable program with clear goals and corporate partnerships. The partnership with Buckner International, a Dallas-based organization with deep roots in international child welfare, ensures that donations reach verified organizations managing orphanages and child welfare programs in target countries.

The 25th anniversary campaign signals a shift toward mobilizing larger donations and corporate sponsorships. By setting an ambitious one-year goal of 25,000 pairs—up from the historical annual rate—Rotary is using a milestone moment to inspire larger commitments. This strategy transforms individual giving into collective momentum, turning what might feel like an overwhelming target into an achievable campaign that can galvanize entire communities and businesses.

Key takeaways

  • Shoes for Orphan Souls has collected 450,000 pairs of shoes and $350,000 over 24 years through Rotary clubs and community donors.
  • The program partners with Buckner International to distribute shoes to children in need in Guatemala, the Dominican Republic, and other developing countries.
  • The 25th anniversary goal is to collect 25,000 new pairs in April 2026, with corporate sponsorships aimed at reaching 500,000 total pairs.
  • Footwear donations remove barriers to school attendance and daily participation for vulnerable children in poverty and orphanages.

How does Rotary Leadership Academy prepare high school students for leadership and community service?

The Rotary Leadership Academy holds two sessions every summer in Warrensburg, Missouri from Sunday night through Wednesday afternoon, bringing in approximately 100 students to develop leadership skills and learn about community service initiatives.

What local community service projects have Rotary clubs in central Missouri implemented in recent years?

Projects include developing 12 community gardens at Ruthmore Gardens in Liberty, building dog parks, constructing pavilions in parks, and partnering with Feed Northland Kids and Feed Lee Summit to combat food insecurity.

What responsibilities does a district governor hold within Rotary's organizational structure?

A district governor visits all clubs in their district to establish personal connection and assess community work, oversees district operations, and serves a five-year commitment to support Rotary's mission across their region.
