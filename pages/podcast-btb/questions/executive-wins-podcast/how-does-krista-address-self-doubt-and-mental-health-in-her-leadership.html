How does Krista manage self-doubt and mental health as a leader?

September 6, 2026 Executive Wins Podcast · Krista Rabideau

← See the Executive Wins Podcast fiche

Krista acknowledges that self-doubt and limiting beliefs are universal among entrepreneurs, but she overcomes them by programming her brain through daily affirmations like "everything's OK and it's going to be OK." Rather than pursuing work-life balance, she focuses on work-life integration, recognizing that the brain is highly programmable and that internal alignment is where real success begins.

Every leader faces moments of uncertainty—wondering if they're making the right decisions or whether they'll ultimately succeed. For Krista Rabideau, Managing Director at Anderson, this was no exception. What sets her apart is her deliberate approach to managing these doubts rather than pretending they don't exist.

The breakthrough came when Krista shifted her perspective from trying to achieve perfect work-life balance to embracing work-life integration. This distinction matters profoundly. Balance implies a constant, exhausting negotiation between two competing worlds. Integration means viewing work and personal life as interconnected parts of a whole self that shows up authentically in both domains. When you integrate rather than segregate, the mental load lightens significantly.

Brain programming as the foundation of leadership resilience

At the heart of Krista's mental health strategy lies a powerful insight: the brain is programmable. Just as you might update software or retrain a system, your mind can be trained to respond differently to stress, uncertainty, and self-doubt. Affirmations like "everything's OK and it's going to be OK" aren't empty mantras—they're deliberate rewiring exercises that change how your nervous system responds to pressure.

This practice became especially critical when Krista joined Anderson seven years ago as Managing Director with a mission to rebuild and scale the boutique accounting firm. The organization was owner-managed, with roughly 23–24 people, and faced significant transformation challenges. As discussed at length in this episode of the Executive Wins Podcast, Krista didn't just bring operational expertise—she brought a commitment to leading authentically, which required her to first master her own inner dialogue.

The practical application is simple but demanding: when self-doubt surfaces (and it will), Krista interrupts the spiral by consciously reinforcing a grounded, compassionate message. Over time, this rewiring becomes automatic. The brain learns to default to resilience rather than catastrophizing.

"You can receive success, but you will never feel successful until you're actually yourself."

What is Krista's philosophy on authentic leadership and employee engagement?

Krista believes that people can achieve external success but will never feel successful until they show up authentically. She tells her team that showing your true self at work and at home is not just better for wellbeing—it drives better business results.

How does Krista distinguish her advisory approach from the Big Four accounting firm model?

At Big Four firms, Krista worked in a rigid environment focused on returns and compliance. At Anderson's boutique model, she brings a more personalized, relationship-focused advisory approach that allows for authentic leadership and deeper client partnerships.

What role does adaptability play in Krista's work planning and strategy execution?

Krista emphasizes that plans must be vague enough to pivot while remaining purposeful in direction. Rather than rigid roadmaps, she creates flexible plans that allow Anderson to stay responsive to market changes without losing strategic focus.

Key takeaways

  • Self-doubt is universal among entrepreneurs; the difference lies in how you manage and reprogram your response to it.
  • Work-life integration—viewing personal and professional life as one authentic whole—reduces mental friction and increases resilience.
  • Daily affirmations and intentional brain programming create automatic neural pathways that default to resilience under stress.
  • Internal mental alignment is the foundation upon which external business success is built.
