How did Spark and Pony redefine success to build lasting growth?

September 10, 2026 Executive Wins Podcast · Cass Laboucane

Instead of pursuing relentless growth, Cass and Michelle shifted their definition of success to stability over the last 12 months, focusing on strengthening foundational structures before scaling. They rejected the conventional push-harder mentality to create a sustainable business capable of bearing the weight of future expansion.

From growth pressure to foundational strength

Most business advice pushes founders toward one directive: grow faster, push harder, expand relentlessly. Cass Laboucane and Michelle Scully, co-founders of Spark and Pony Creative, discovered this mindset was unsustainable for their seven-year-old creative agency. They pivoted to defining success as stability—a deliberate choice to stop chasing metrics and start building durable systems.

This shift wasn't a retreat from ambition. Rather, it was a strategic repositioning. Over their four years as a two-person partnership before hiring team members, then expanding further, the founders realized their rapid growth had outpaced their internal infrastructure. Strong foundational structures—including team processes, delegation frameworks, and operational clarity—became the priority before taking on additional revenue or clients.

As discussed in the Executive Wins Podcast episode, this approach applies directly to creative businesses where burnout is endemic. By locking in stability first, Cass and Michelle created a platform from which sustainable, controlled growth could happen without sacrificing the well-being of their team or their own sanity.

"A lot of conventional business advice is always telling you to grow, grow, push your team harder. And in the last 12 months we've been redefining success as stability."

The episode also dives into how Cass and Michelle learned to say no to opportunities that didn't align with their foundational work—a tactical skill rarely taught in typical entrepreneurship guides, explored in depth in this conversation.

Key takeaways

  • Stability is a valid strategic goal, especially for creative agencies prone to burnout and unsustainable growth cycles.
  • Foundational structures—team processes, delegation, and operational clarity—must be strengthened before scaling further.
  • Rejecting conventional growth-at-all-costs advice is a competitive advantage for long-term sustainability and team retention.
  • Saying no to misaligned opportunities protects the stability you've worked to establish and preserves team capacity.
