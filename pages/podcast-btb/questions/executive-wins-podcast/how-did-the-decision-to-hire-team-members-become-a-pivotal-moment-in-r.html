How did the decision to hire team members become a pivotal moment in redefining business success?

12 September 2026 Executive Wins Podcast · Cass Laboucane

← See the Executive Wins Podcast fiche

After four years of operating as a two-person team, Cass and Michelle hit burnout and hired their first support staff—a part-time designer and full-time content marketer—doubling their team overnight. This moment forced them to confront a fundamental truth: success doesn't mean doing everything yourself, and sustainable business requires delegation.

For many creative founders, the path starts lean. Both co-founders were managing design, strategy, content, client relationships, and operations simultaneously. The exhaustion was inevitable. What changed everything was their willingness to hire—not because growth demanded it, but because sustainability required it.

Bringing in a full-time content marketer to support Cass's side and a part-time designer to support Michelle's work wasn't just about dividing labor. It was about redefining what winning actually means. Instead of measuring success through burnout endurance or how many hours they logged, they began measuring it through business stability and team empowerment.

"A lot of conventional business advice is always telling you to grow, grow, push your team harder. And in the last 12 months we've been redefining success as stability."

This shift is rarely discussed in startup culture, where "doing more with less" remains the default narrative. Yet Cass and Michelle's experience reveals something deeper: the real win is recognizing when your team's limits become your business's limits. Hiring those first team members didn't accelerate growth—it protected it.

The decision also shifted how they thought about mentorship and systems. Rather than being the only people who understood how work got done, they could now invest in training, documentation, and processes that outlived their personal effort. This is how founders move from being the business to building a business that can thrive without their constant presence.

What makes this moment even more significant is that it didn't feel like scaling up—it felt like survival. And that's precisely why it mattered. Cass and Michelle's journey details exactly how to put in place the systems and processes that prevent burnout while building a sustainable creative agency.

From burnout to breathing room

The first hire isn't just about capacity—it's about permission. For four years, Cass and Michelle carried the entire weight. Hiring someone meant admitting they couldn't do it alone. But more importantly, it meant trusting someone else with their vision. That trust, once extended, fundamentally changed their relationship to work.

With support staff in place, they could finally think strategically instead of tactically. They could ask: "What should we be doing?" instead of "What can we possibly squeeze in today?" That mental shift is where real business transformation happens—not in the hiring announcement, but in the space that hiring creates.

Key takeaways

  • A two-person team operating for four years without support eventually faces burnout—a signal that hiring isn't optional, it's essential for survival.
  • Redefining success from growth to stability requires abandoning conventional business narratives that celebrate overwork.
  • The first hire creates mental and operational space for systems and delegation, transforming how founders think about their business.
  • Sustainable growth demands that founders trust others with their vision, moving from doing all the work to building a team that can.
