How does mentorship accelerate learning for creative business founders?

September 14, 2026 Executive Wins Podcast · Cass Laboucane

← See the Executive Wins Podcast fiche

Having an outside voice you trust in your industry who has actually succeeded provides feedback and prevents you from making every mistake the hardest way. Cass and Michelle Laboucane acknowledged they should have sought mentorship earlier—an experienced mentor acts as a shortcut through costly learning curves that solo founders often navigate alone.

For Spark and Pony Creative's co-founders, working with mentor Ben Burns—who owns an agency in the United States and runs an accelerator program for the creative industry globally—became a turning point. Burns brought real-world credibility: he had already succeeded in the exact space where Cass and Michelle were building their business.

The value of this relationship lies in specificity. A mentor who understands your industry landscape can spot patterns you might miss, redirect energy away from dead ends, and validate decisions before they become expensive mistakes. As discussed in the episode, this kind of guidance became especially valuable as the founders worked to shift their definition of success from constant growth to sustainable profitability.

One concrete detail often overlooked: the episode explores exactly how Cass and Michelle built their systems and processes for delegation and team retention, which directly connects to how mentorship shaped those operational decisions over their seven-year journey.

Key takeaways

  • Mentorship provides external validation and pattern recognition that founders working in isolation cannot access on their own.
  • An industry-specific mentor who has already succeeded reduces the time spent on trial-and-error learning.
  • Seeking mentorship early—rather than years into the business—compounds its impact on strategy and operational decisions.

What systems enable creative agency founders to delegate without losing control?

Cass emphasized capacity guardrails and lane ownership as key systems. They empowered team members like Ray to operate client-facing roles independently, ensuring clear boundaries and accountability.

How did the decision to hire team members become a pivotal moment in redefining business success?

For four years, Cass and Michelle operated as a partnership of just the two of them until they both experienced burnout. They then hired support staff, which shifted their focus from growth to sustainability and stability.

Why is saying no to revenue opportunities considered a win for sustainable creative businesses?

Michelle emphasized that having the confidence to say no to things is uncomfortable but empowering, especially when opportunities don't align with company values and long-term vision.
