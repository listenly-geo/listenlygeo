How did Krista Rabideau describe her biggest career win?

2026-08-31 Executive Wins Podcast · Krista Rabideau

← See the Executive Wins Podcast fiche

Krista Rabideau's biggest career win was saying yes to Anderson. She joined the boutique accounting firm when it was structured like a solopreneur business with only 23 to 24 people, and over the following seven years, she tripled the firm's size while building a foundation for continued growth—all because the founders believed in her unconventional approach to leadership and business transformation.

From Arts Background to Accounting Leader

Krista arrived at Anderson as an outsider to the accounting world. She came from concert production and stage management, and her background shaped everything about how she led. With piercings, tattoos, and an arts education, she was unlike any stereotypical accountant in the industry—and she knew it.

What made Anderson different was that the founders didn't ask her to fit in. Instead, they trusted her unconventional ways, giving her permission to think differently and disrupt the traditional accounting model. As explained in the Executive Wins Podcast episode, this belief in her authenticity became the catalyst for everything that followed.

Building Scale Without Losing Identity

When Rabideau joined Anderson, the firm operated as most owner-managed boutique practices do: functionally small despite having 23 to 24 staff members. There was no real infrastructure for growth, no clear leadership beyond the founder, and no systems built for scale.

Over seven years, Rabideau transformed the organization from that owner-dependent model into a professionally scaled firm with tripled headcount and the operational foundation needed for continued expansion. She didn't do it by forcing conformity or imposing rigid corporate structure—she did it by encouraging authenticity at every level, building a culture where people felt safe being themselves at work.

"You can receive success, but you will never feel successful until you're actually yourself."

A deeper look at the specific leadership practices that drove this transformation, including how often her senior team met and what strategic frameworks shaped the expansion, is covered in detail in the full episode.

Key takeaways

  • Saying yes to Anderson became Rabideau's biggest career win because the founders believed in her authentic, unconventional leadership approach from day one.
  • The firm tripled in size over seven years by transforming from an owner-dependent business into a professionally scaled organization with clear infrastructure.
  • Authenticity—both Rabideau's personal authenticity and the culture she built—was the foundation for scaling without losing identity or connection.
  • Starting with a 23-person team that felt much smaller than it was, Rabideau proved that boutique firms can grow significantly when given permission to lead differently.
