How did Krista transition from an arts background to becoming an accountant?

2026-08-30 Executive Wins Podcast · Krista Rabideau

← See the Executive Wins Podcast fiche

Krista Rabideau grew up immersed in the arts and began her career in concert production and stage management. When a professor at Grant MacEwan University recognized her natural aptitude for accounting, he encouraged her to pursue it as a more stable path—a single intervention that fundamentally redirected her professional trajectory.

Krista's career path mirrors a story many professionals never tell: the power of a single mentor's belief. While working in the performing arts world, she was already developing the organizational and analytical skills that would serve her later. But it took one professor's intervention at Grant MacEwan University to help her see a different possibility for herself.

That professor, Grant McEwen, didn't simply offer generic career advice. He recognized something concrete in Krista's work and thinking that aligned with accounting principles—a perception she might not have had of herself at the time. His encouragement planted the seed for a career transition that would eventually lead her to join Anderson as Managing Director, where she would later rebuild and scale the firm over seven years.

What made this transition particularly significant is that Krista didn't abandon her arts background—she carried its values and her unconventional thinking into the accounting profession. As she reflects in the episode, her unique perspective, shaped by years in concert production, became her greatest strength, not something to overcome.

"You can receive success, but you will never feel successful until you're actually yourself."

The deeper work Krista later undertook—learning to bring her full, authentic self to her professional role, piercings, tattoos, and unconventional thinking included—proved far more transformative than the technical skills of accounting alone. Discover how her commitment to authentic leadership has reshaped her firm's culture and results by listening to the full episode.

Key takeaways

  • A single mentor's recognition of hidden potential can redirect an entire career, even when the initial path seemed secure and fulfilling.
  • Arts background and unconventional thinking are assets in accounting, not liabilities—they bring problem-solving creativity to technical work.
  • Career success stems from bringing your authentic self to your professional role, not conforming to industry stereotypes.
  • Mentorship works best when the mentor sees concrete capability, not just potential, and encourages the mentee to pursue it actively.
