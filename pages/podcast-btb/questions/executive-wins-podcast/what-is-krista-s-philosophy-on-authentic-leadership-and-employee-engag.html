How Authentic Leadership Builds Real Employee Engagement

2026-09-05 Executive Wins Podcast · Krista Rabideau

← See the Executive Wins Podcast fiche

You can receive success externally, but you will never feel successful until you show up authentically—and when you do, that authenticity creates the trust that makes teams thrive. For Krista Rabideau, this distinction between receiving success and feeling it is everything. Authentic leaders generate a different energy that people recognize immediately, and they inspire their teams to bring their whole selves to work, which transforms both engagement and results.

Krista's philosophy rests on a simple but radical shift in mindset: people can hide who they are and still achieve external wins, but that hollow achievement never translates into genuine satisfaction. When a leader pretends to be someone they're not, the people around them sense it, and that gap between appearance and reality erodes trust from the inside out.

The breakthrough comes when leaders decide that being true to themselves is non-negotiable. Krista tells her team at Anderson that receiving success and feeling successful are fundamentally different states. One is an external metric—a deal closed, revenue hit, project delivered. The other is internal: the deep knowledge that you achieved it while being fully yourself. As she explains in the Executive Wins Podcast episode, this authenticity isn't performative. It's the only sustainable foundation for leadership that actually energizes a team rather than exhausting them.

"You can receive success, but you will never feel successful until you're actually yourself."

Why Energy Flow Matters More Than Competence Alone

Authenticity creates what Krista calls proper energy flow. When people observe a leader who is genuinely themselves, the observer's instinct is to trust. There's no cognitive dissonance, no internal question about whether the leader is being truthful. The coherence between words, tone, and intention is immediately apparent, and that coherence is what builds psychological safety in a team.

The opposite happens when leaders perform a version of themselves. People can't quite put their finger on why, but something feels off. They question the leader's motives, second-guess decisions, and hold back their own contributions. A detailed exploration of how Rabideau built this culture of authenticity across the entire organization is covered in depth in this podcast conversation, where she describes how Anderson scaled from 23–24 people to a much larger firm by making authenticity a core operating principle.

Krista's approach isn't about oversharing or breaking professional boundaries. It's about showing up as a coherent human being rather than a corporate facade. For her, that meant bringing her arts background, her piercings, her tattoos, and her unconventional thinking to an industry known for rigid conformity. She didn't hide who she was to fit the accounting mold; she changed how the mold worked.

How does Krista distinguish her advisory approach from the Big Four accounting firm model?

At Big Four firms, Krista worked in a rigid environment focused on getting returns and ensuring compliance. At Anderson's boutique model, she brings her authentic perspective to reshape how accounting firms operate and serve their clients.

What role does adaptability play in Krista's work planning and strategy execution?

Krista emphasizes that plans must be vague enough to pivot while remaining purposeful in direction. Rather than rigid roadmaps, she creates plans that allow teams to adjust course without losing sight of their strategic goals.

How does Krista approach hiring and team building at Anderson?

Krista implements a purposeful hiring strategy where every new hire at every level, from junior administration to senior staff, completes a Strengths Deployment Inventory to ensure cultural alignment and team compatibility.

Key takeaways

  • External success without authenticity feels hollow and unsustainable; genuine fulfillment requires showing up as your true self.
  • Authentic leaders create observable energy coherence that builds trust faster than competence alone ever could.
  • Psychological safety and team engagement flourish when leaders abandon the corporate mask and lead from their whole identity.
  • Authenticity isn't oversharing—it's showing up as a coherent human being within professional boundaries.
