How can creative agencies bridge expertise and online presence?

September 9, 2026 Executive Wins Podcast · Cass Laboucane

← See the Executive Wins Podcast fiche

The key is to combine messaging and visual skills to make client expertise visible online. Many businesses excel at their core work but fail to articulate that talent on their website or social media, appearing amateur or outdated. A creative agency's role is to translate what clients do exceptionally well into compelling online presentation that matches their actual capability.

Spark and Pony Creative's approach starts with a simple observation: the gap between what businesses do and how they present themselves online is enormous. A skilled service provider with outdated messaging, poor visual branding, or scattered social media presence looks less credible than they actually are—and potential clients often can't see past the surface to understand the real depth of their expertise.

This is where the creative agency's core job becomes clear. As Cass Laboucane explains in the Executive Wins Podcast, it's about bringing two distinct skill sets together: the words that communicate value and the visual design that builds trust. When those elements align, a client's actual talent and systems suddenly become visible to their market.

Aligning systems with storytelling

The businesses Spark and Pony works with often have great systems and refined service offerings, but their digital footprint doesn't reflect that investment. A business might have spent years perfecting their process, training their team, and developing proprietary methods—yet their website looks like it was built a decade ago, or their social media posts don't convey any of that sophistication.

The fix isn't about reinventing the business; it's about making the existing excellence visible. This involves auditing what the client actually does well, extracting the core value propositions that matter most, and then rebuilding the messaging and visual presence around those truths. The business doesn't change—its story does.

For founders learning to scale and delegate, there's an additional dimension: as discussed in this episode, redefining success means building sustainable systems rather than chasing constant growth. A well-articulated brand story supports that stability by attracting the right clients, reducing the need to constantly sell, and building trust through consistent, professional presentation.

"A lot of conventional business advice is always telling you to grow, grow, push your team harder. And in the last 12 months we've been redefining success as stability."

The broader lesson here applies to any service business: expertise without visibility is invisible. The creative agency's job is to fix that visibility equation, not by exaggerating what clients do, but by finally letting what they genuinely do shine through. To hear more about how Spark and Pony built this approach over seven years as a two-person operation before growing their team, listen to the full conversation with Cass Laboucane and Michelle Scully.

Key takeaways

  • Many skilled businesses fail to communicate their expertise online, appearing less professional than they actually are.
  • Creative agencies solve this by aligning messaging and visual design to make client talent and systems visible.
  • The work is about articulation, not reinvention—taking what works and ensuring it's presented professionally.
  • Strong online branding supports sustainable business by attracting better-fit clients and reducing constant sales pressure.
