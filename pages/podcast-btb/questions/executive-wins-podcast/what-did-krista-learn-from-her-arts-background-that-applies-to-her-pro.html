What did Krista learn from her arts background that applies to her professional leadership?

7 September 2026 · Executive Wins Podcast · Krista Rabideau

← See the Executive Wins Podcast fiche

The cardinal lesson from her stage management days is this: the show must go on, regardless of what happens backstage or what goes wrong. This mindset translates directly to professional leadership—when obstacles arise or circumstances spiral, the business continues, and your performance depends on how you show up despite the challenges, not on whether external conditions are perfect.

Before Krista Rabideau became Managing Director at Anderson, she worked in concert production and stage management. That world taught her something visceral about resilience: the audience is seated, the lights are on, and the curtain is rising. Costume malfunctions, lighting cues that miss, or performers running late do not stop the show. You adapt, you execute, and you deliver.

That discipline translates to the modern accounting and advisory firm. When staff members quit unexpectedly, or a major client relationship falters, or market conditions shift, the business does not pause for perfect circumstances. Your team looks to you to demonstrate that the organization moves forward. The resilience Rabideau learned on stage became the psychological foundation for how she leads through uncertainty and change.

In a boutique advisory firm rebuilding itself from owner-managed operations into a scaled organization—work Rabideau has been doing for seven years at Anderson—that mindset becomes essential. There is no luxury of postponement. There is only the choice to show up, adapt, and keep moving.

"You can receive success, but you will never feel successful until you're actually yourself."

Authenticity and resilience are not separate virtues in Rabideau's philosophy—they are inseparable. The same arts training that taught her "the show must go on" also allowed her to bring her whole self to her work: piercings, tattoos, unconventional thinking and all. She did not feel truly successful until she found an environment—Anderson—where her unconventional approach was not just tolerated but believed in and encouraged.

For leaders building high-performing teams, the insight here is practical. When you model the willingness to move forward despite setbacks, and when you do so as your authentic self rather than as a sanitized corporate persona, your team sees permission to do the same. Resilience becomes contagious. Authenticity stops being a risk and becomes a competitive advantage.

The Arts as a Foundation for Leadership Under Pressure

Rabideau's journey from the arts to accounting is not a sidetrack—it is the core of her leadership identity. Her background in stage management gave her exposure to high-pressure environments where decisions are made in real time, under the gaze of an audience, with no option to rehearse again or redo the take. That training in executing under live conditions prepared her for the uncertainty of growing a firm.

When you are managing a stage, you learn that perfection is not the goal. Execution is. You learn to troubleshoot on the fly, to keep multiple stakeholders aligned under pressure, and to take responsibility for the whole system—not just your department. Those are the exact skills that translate to scaling a professional services firm.

Key takeaways

  • Stage management taught Rabideau that performance depends on how you show up despite obstacles, not on perfect external circumstances.
  • The "show must go on" mindset from live performance translates to professional resilience when managing business challenges and uncertainty.
  • Authenticity and resilience work together: showing up as your whole self while moving forward through difficulty gives your team permission to do the same.
  • Arts training builds real-time decision-making and stakeholder management skills that directly apply to scaling a professional services firm.
