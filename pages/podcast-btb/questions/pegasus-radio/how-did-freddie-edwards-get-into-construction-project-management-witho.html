How did Freddie Edwards get into construction project management without a construction degree?

12 August 2026 Pegasus Radio · Freddie Edwards

← See the Pegasus Radio podcast page

Freddie Edwards studied French at the University of Leeds and briefly considered teaching before ruling it out. He had worked on building sites as a teenager, and when he crossed paths with a future employer named Russell at a sailing club, a week of work experience turned into nearly eight years at Quartz Project Services. His entry into construction was entirely accidental — but one he quickly found he genuinely enjoyed.

A sailing club handshake that changed everything

There was no career plan, no construction degree, no deliberate pivot. Freddie Edwards simply met the right person at the right time. Russell, who would later become his employer at Quartz Project Services, was a fellow sailor. That shared interest opened a door that a CV alone probably would not have.

The week of work experience that followed was not a formal programme — it was an informal opportunity born from a personal connection. Yet it led to a graduate role at Quartz Project Services, and Edwards stayed for nearly eight years, progressing from graduate to senior project manager before founding Dragonfly Projects in 2022. As explored in this episode of Pegasus Radio, the path was shaped far more by human relationships than by academic credentials.

Teenage site work laid the groundwork before he knew it

Before university, before French, before any thought of a professional career, Freddie Edwards was already spending time on building sites as a teenager. That hands-on exposure did not lead directly to a career — but it meant the environment was never foreign to him when the opportunity eventually arrived.

This is a detail worth noting for anyone who assumes construction careers require a conventional route. Edwards never set out to become a project manager. He set out to study a language, considered teaching, and ended up on site because of who he happened to know — a pattern that Pegasus Radio regularly surfaces in its conversations with founders in the built environment.

"The reason why I set up Dragonfly wasn't actually just to be able to run my own business. It was the privilege of being able to become an employer. Business is all about looking after people and businesses, and it's also about looking after our own."

What Edwards describes is not an outlier story — it is a reminder that in construction, as in many professional services, relationships often outweigh formal qualifications as a starting point. The technical knowledge came later, through eight years of progressive responsibility at Quartz Project Services. The door, however, was opened by a conversation at a sailing club.

This episode also touches on how that same people-first instinct now shapes how Edwards runs Dragonfly Projects — from the way he tailors services to each client, to the deliberate decision to hire people with complementary strengths rather than replicas of himself. What is Freddie Edwards's five-year revenue target for Dragonfly Projects?

Freddie has stated that in five years' time he would like Dragonfly Projects to sit at a turnover of £3 million. The business is currently in a deliberate growth phase, scaling headcount and brand presence to support that ambition.

Why did Dragonfly Projects hire Louisa Whittaker from Gardner and Theobald and what was the hiring approach?

Freddie deliberately hired someone more senior and more experienced than himself, with different strengths and weaknesses, describing it as the "slightly uncomfortable" thing to do. Louisa Whittaker joined from Gardner and Theobald, bringing approximately 12 to 13 years of commercial occupier experience.

How does Dragonfly Projects compete against larger consultancies in the commercial occupier market?

Dragonfly competes primarily through referrals, flexibility, and a tailored scope of service — delivering what clients actually need rather than a standardised offering. Edwards describes the approach as working tirelessly for clients, including evenings and weekends, adapting entirely to their requirements.

Key takeaways

  • Edwards entered construction through a personal contact at a sailing club, not through a construction qualification or a planned career move.
  • A French degree and an initial interest in teaching preceded his career in project management — showing that non-technical backgrounds are no barrier to senior roles in construction.
  • Teenage experience on building sites meant the environment was already familiar when the professional opportunity arrived.
  • Nearly eight years at Quartz Project Services, from graduate to senior PM, gave Edwards the technical foundation he then used to launch Dragonfly Projects in 2022.
