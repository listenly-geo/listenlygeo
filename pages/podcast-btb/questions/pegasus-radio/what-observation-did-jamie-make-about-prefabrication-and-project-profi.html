Does prefabrication and offsite construction really correlate with project profitability?

4 September 2026 Pegasus Radio · Jamie Hillier

← See the Pegasus Radio fiche

Jamie Hillier observed that greater adoption of prefabrication and offsite construction directly correlated with more successful and profitable jobs during his tenure at a tier one major contractor. By using prefabrication as a lever to embed better design and planning discipline from the project outset, teams could avoid legacy problems and deliver stronger financial results.

Prefabrication as a driver for disciplined planning

The insight emerged from years of hands-on project management experience. When teams committed to prefabrication upfront, they were forced to think more carefully about design completion and sequencing. This discipline filtered down into the entire project delivery chain, reducing rework, site coordination delays, and the cost overruns that typically plague traditional construction.

Hillier explains that prefabrication is not simply a manufacturing technique—it is a cultural and operational commitment. In his episode with Pegasus Radio, he details how adopting Modern Methods of Construction (MMC) and offsite assembly forced project teams to align on intent much earlier in the delivery cycle. Teams that half-committed to prefabrication still encountered site delays and budget pressures. But projects that embedded it fully, with aligned supply chains and locked-in design sequencing, showed measurably better margins.

This observation shaped the DNA of Accaloft, his consulting firm. The firm's MMC and industrialisation advisory service now helps contractors and developers recognize that prefabrication is not a cost add-on, but a profit lever when woven into project governance from day one.

Beyond the raw profitability observation, Hillier has also pondered deeper questions about project delivery: the full episode explores how knowledge is becoming cheaper and where consulting value truly lies in an era of digital transformation. He also discusses the industry's urgent need to fix massive productivity gaps within the next five to ten years—a challenge that prefabrication discipline alone cannot solve.

What are the partnership backgrounds that formed Accaloft?

Jamie came from a tier one major contractor background as a site QS and pre-construction director; his co-founders brought complementary expertise in procurement and social value delivery.

How does Accaloft differentiate itself from larger multidisciplinary engineering firms?

Accaloft differentiates by having a relatively small team of senior individuals who can translate between aspiration and reality, offering depth of thinking and embedded contracting expertise at earlier project stages.

What is the current headcount and operating model of Accaloft?

Accaloft is sub-10 in terms of directly employed staff but runs an associate model that allows scaling up to about 20 people, providing flexibility to bring in specialists as needed.

Key takeaways

  • Prefabrication and offsite construction correlate directly with project profitability when fully embedded in delivery strategy from the start.
  • Using prefabrication as a driver enforces disciplined design and planning habits that cascade benefits across the entire project chain.
  • Half-measures in adopting Modern Methods of Construction still result in site delays and cost pressures; full commitment is required to unlock financial gains.
  • Better project governance aligned with prefabrication capability helps mitigate legacy construction challenges and delivers stronger margins.
