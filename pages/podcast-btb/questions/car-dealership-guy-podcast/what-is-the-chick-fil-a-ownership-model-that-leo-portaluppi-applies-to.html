What is the Chick-fil-A ownership model that Leo Portaluppi applies to his dealership group?

August 21, 2026 Car Dealership Guy Podcast · Leo Portaluppi

← See the Car Dealership Guy Podcast page

Leo Portaluppi structures store leadership at P4 Automotive the way Chick-fil-A requires owner-operators: no one runs a location without real skin in the game. He built a leadership team — what he calls the P4 team — with partners in certain stores, prioritizing people who grew up within the group and thinking long-term rather than optimizing for individual store gains.

Why Chick-fil-A — and Not McDonald's

The comparison isn't random. Portaluppi points directly to the customer service gap between Chick-fil-A and McDonald's as evidence that ownership structure drives culture. Chick-fil-A requires every location to be run by an owner-operator — someone who has a genuine stake in the outcome. McDonald's does not impose the same standard, and the results show.

With nine rooftops across five different cities, Portaluppi recognized he could no longer be everywhere at once. The Chick-fil-A model became his answer to scaling without losing accountability. Each store partner carries real responsibility — not just a title. This is a point Portaluppi develops in detail on the Car Dealership Guy Podcast.

Growing Leaders From Within — "We Don't Hire Bad Habits"

Portaluppi's preference is to develop store leaders internally rather than recruit them from outside. His reasoning is direct: external hires bring habits already formed elsewhere, and those habits are harder to correct than gaps in knowledge.

This philosophy applies on both the sales and management sides of the business. P4 Automotive targets partners who have lived the group's processes from the inside — people whose instincts were shaped by the same culture Portaluppi built. As he explains on this episode, the priority is long-term thinking at the group level, not short-term wins at any single store.

"We don't want to hire bad habits, we want to train bad habits."

The logic behind promoting from within is also practical. Someone who has sold cars, managed a service drive, or run fixed ops inside P4 Automotive already understands the group's specific targets — including the "Road to 500" goal of 500 cars sold per month and the "Road to 700" target of $700,000 in fixed operations gross for the Indianapolis stores. An outside hire has to learn all of that from scratch, and they arrive with habits already set. How did Leo Portaluppi's fixed operations background give him an advantage as a dealer group owner?

Leo Portaluppi grew up working as a technician, service advisor, and service manager, which allows him to connect directly with those employees and bridge the gap between fixed and variable operations — a perspective most dealer group owners simply don't have.

Key takeaways

  • Portaluppi uses the Chick-fil-A owner-operator model as a direct blueprint: no store leadership without genuine skin in the game.
  • The P4 team structure gives select partners a stake in specific stores, creating accountability at scale across nine rooftops in five cities.
  • Internal promotion is the default — growing leaders within the group protects culture and avoids importing outside habits.
  • The model is explicitly long-term: individual store performance is subordinate to the health of the group as a whole.
