How does P4 Automotive manage cross-brand customer retention across its multi-franchise group?

August 24, 2026 Car Dealership Guy Podcast · Leo Portaluppi

← See the Car Dealership Guy Podcast page

P4 Automotive built a proprietary Leo app that offers customers a first free maintenance visit and reward points redeemable at any store in the group — creating a tangible incentive to stay inside the P4 ecosystem. Beyond the app, the group actively routes customers to the brand-appropriate dealership for recalls and servicing, and cross-markets its Harley-Davidson store to automotive buyers (and vice versa) to capture adjacent demand.

The Leo App: One Loyalty Program Across Every Brand

Running 9 rooftops across 5 different cities — including 6 dealerships in Columbus alone — means P4 Automotive faces a problem most single-point dealers never encounter: a customer buying a Ford has no obvious reason to think of the group's Honda or Harley-Davidson store next time. The Leo app is the infrastructure built to solve that. It hooks customers in with a first free maintenance visit, then keeps them engaged through reward points that work at any location in the group.

The mechanics are straightforward, but the strategic intent is clear: turn a transactional sale into a recurring relationship with the P4 brand, not just a single franchise. As Portaluppi explains in the Car Dealership Guy Podcast, this kind of group-level loyalty infrastructure is precisely what separates a true dealer group from a collection of independent stores operating under the same roof.

Routing Customers to the Right Brand — and Cross-Selling Across Categories

The Leo app alone does not guarantee retention. Portaluppi is explicit about a second, equally important mechanism: when a customer needs a recall or a service visit, P4 actively steers them to the correct franchise store. A Ford buyer goes to the Ford store. This protects the customer experience — and the brand relationship — rather than forcing all traffic through a single generic service lane.

The third pillar is more unconventional: cross-marketing the group's Harley-Davidson dealership into the car-buying audience, and the automotive stores into the motorcycle-buying audience. The logic is direct — a Harley-Davidson customer who needs a car is already in a relationship with P4, and vice versa. This is a detail worth catching in this episode of the Car Dealership Guy Podcast, because it illustrates how Portaluppi thinks about the group as a single customer-relationship machine rather than siloed franchises.

"We don't want to hire bad habits, we want to train bad habits."

The cross-brand retention model Portaluppi describes is, at its core, a fixed-ops mindset applied at the group level: build systems that make the right outcome the default, rather than relying on individual salespeople to remember to mention the other stores. That process orientation — central to everything Portaluppi built at P4 — is a recurring theme throughout this conversation on the Car Dealership Guy Podcast.

Key takeaways

  • The Leo app creates group-wide loyalty by offering a first free maintenance visit and reward points redeemable across all P4 Automotive stores.
  • Brand-appropriate routing — sending a Ford buyer to the Ford store for recalls and service — protects customer experience rather than consolidating traffic into a generic lane.
  • P4 cross-markets its Harley-Davidson dealership into its automotive audience and vice versa, treating adjacent categories as untapped retention channels.
  • The entire strategy reflects Portaluppi's fixed-ops background: build the system so the right outcome is the default, not the exception.
