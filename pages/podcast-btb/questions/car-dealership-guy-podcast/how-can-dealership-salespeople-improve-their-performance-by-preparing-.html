How can dealership salespeople improve their performance by preparing mentally before customer interactions?

September 23, 2026 Car Dealership Guy Podcast · David Zuniga

← See the Car Dealership Guy Podcast fiche

What happens between opportunities is critical—preparation builds the confidence that customers are actually looking for. Most sales professionals jump into customer interactions without mental preparation, even though athletes across all sports spend significant time preparing mentally before competition.

David Zuniga draws a direct parallel to how football players mentally prepare before a game. They visualize plays, review strategy, and align themselves mentally—yet in car sales, many representatives skip this essential step entirely. The moment a customer walks onto the lot, that salesperson must already be mentally ready.

This preparation isn't complicated, but it is deliberate. As Zuniga explains in the episode, the first person a salesperson has to close every day is themselves. Self-closure—mental alignment and intention-setting—directly determines how they show up with customers.

"Every day the first person we have to close is ourselves. What did we come here to do today?"

The logic is straightforward: customer confidence mirrors salesperson confidence. If a salesperson arrives at the customer interaction already mentally prepared—clear on the day's goals, emotionally centered, and intentional—that state transmits to the customer. It's not about manipulation; it's about showing up as a grounded, focused professional rather than reactive and scattered.

A curious aspect explored in the full conversation is how Zuniga applies these principles across a 9-person sales team managing 130 to 140 units per month at the Denville location—a rhythm that demands consistent mental discipline from every rep.

Why salespeople skip preparation and what breaks the cycle

The gap between sports and sales is partly cultural. In professional athletics, mental preparation is non-negotiable; it's budgeted, scheduled, and respected as part of the job. In retail car sales, the pressure to immediately engage customers often displaces preparation time. Salespeople move straight from admin work or a break into a customer interaction, carrying whatever mental state they happened to have.

Closing this gap requires a deliberate habit—treating mental preparation as mandatory, not optional. Even two minutes of intentional focus—reviewing the day's purpose, settling the nervous system, reaffirming what you want to accomplish—shifts the energy a customer feels the moment they meet you. Sales floors that normalize this practice see salespeople who feel more confident and customers who respond more positively, as discussed at length in this podcast episode.

Key takeaways

  • What happens before customer interactions—mental preparation—is as critical as the interaction itself.
  • Confidence is the primary signal customers respond to; preparation directly builds that confidence.
  • Sales professionals can adopt the mental preparation discipline that athletes use routinely before competition.
  • Closing yourself mentally each day—clarifying intention and emotional state—is the foundation of closing customers consistently.
