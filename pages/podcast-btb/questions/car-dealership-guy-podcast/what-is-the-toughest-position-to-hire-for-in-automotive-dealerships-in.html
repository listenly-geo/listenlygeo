Why are sales professionals the toughest position to hire for in automotive dealerships?

September 24, 2026 Car Dealership Guy Podcast · David Zuniga

← See the Car Dealership Guy Podcast fiche

Sales professionals are the hardest role to fill because dealerships remain locked in outdated coaching and training methods that no longer work for today's salespeople. The industry continues applying approaches that succeeded in the past, but new hires need fundamentally different development strategies to thrive.

The core challenge isn't finding candidates—it's adapting the organization itself. As David Zuniga explains in the episode, dealerships must fundamentally shift how they coach, develop, and train incoming sales talent.

The mismatch occurs because veteran managers often default to the methods that got them to their current position. What worked for them in an earlier era of dealership sales simply doesn't resonate with new hires entering an industry that has transformed. This generational gap in training philosophy is at the heart of the sales hiring crisis.

Adjusting Your Approach to Set New Hires Up to Win

Zuniga, who manages nine salespeople at Performance Auto Group's Kia dealership in Denville, New Jersey, emphasizes that dealers must actively redesign their coaching style to match how new salespeople learn and perform best. The old playbook no longer guarantees success.

This isn't about lowering standards or abandoning proven sales fundamentals. Rather, it's about recognizing that the delivery mechanism, the motivation triggers, and the feedback loop all need to evolve with the workforce. Dealerships that continue forcing yesterday's training model onto today's recruits inevitably watch them leave for competitors or industries where their development feels more relevant.

"Every day the first person we have to close is ourselves. What did we come here to do today?"

Interestingly, Zuniga also reveals specific tactics his dealership uses to retain and develop salespeople effectively once they're hired, a practical complement to solving the initial recruitment challenge.

Key takeaways

  • Sales is the hardest position to hire for because dealerships apply outdated coaching methods to new talent.
  • New salespeople require different training methodologies, coaching styles, and development approaches than past generations.
  • Dealerships must actively adjust their internal systems to set incoming hires up for success, not force them into legacy processes.
  • The mismatch between old-school sales management and modern salespeople directly drives turnover and hiring difficulty.
