How does P4 Automotive build management talent from within?

August 30, 2026 Car Dealership Guy Podcast · Leo Portaluppi

← See the Car Dealership Guy Podcast fiche

Leo Portaluppi's preference is to grow managers internally from within P4 Automotive, developing people on both the sales and management side rather than bringing in external hires. However, the rapid acquisition of four stores in the last 18 months has forced him to hire externally when the pace of internal grooming cannot keep up with growth demands.

This philosophy reflects Portaluppi's deep belief in organizational continuity and culture preservation. As he describes it in the Car Dealership Guy Podcast, he looks to the success models of major brands like Chick-fil-A and McDonald's to understand why certain organizations maintain superior customer service and operational discipline.

Portaluppi modeled P4 Automotive's structure on the Chick-fil-A owner-operator model, where accountability is genuine because managers have real financial skin in the game. When he operated as a sole proprietor with fewer locations, this system worked flawlessly—he could directly oversee every decision and cultivate talent from the ground up, much as he had done in his own career progression from parts runner to technician to service advisor to fixed ops manager.

The tension between growth and internal development

With P4 Automotive now operating nine rooftops across five different cities, Portaluppi acknowledges the constraints. He cannot replicate the hands-on mentorship model at scale without compromising the speed needed to integrate newly acquired dealerships. As discussed in this episode, the expansion has revealed a hard truth: internal grooming takes time, and external hiring becomes a pragmatic necessity.

His philosophy on hiring remains distinctive: rather than recruit people who already carry "bad habits" from other dealerships, he prefers to hire people whose foundational character and work ethic are sound, then shape their dealership-specific behaviors through P4 Automotive's training systems. This distinction reflects his belief that habits can be taught, but fundamental integrity and hunger cannot.

"We don't want to hire bad habits, we want to train bad habits."

The real constraint that emerges from the podcast discussion is that Portaluppi is caught between his stated preference for internal development and the commercial reality of growth. He cannot slow acquisition to preserve his training culture; instead, he must selectively hire external talent while maintaining a clear pipeline of internal successors for core management positions.

Key takeaways

  • Internal development is Portaluppi's stated preference, grounded in his belief that character and work ethic cannot be hired—only habits and dealership processes can be trained.
  • Rapid acquisition growth forces selective external hiring when the pace of internal grooming cannot keep pace with adding new locations.
  • The Chick-fil-A owner-operator model—where managers have genuine financial accountability—underpins P4 Automotive's management structure.
  • Portaluppi's own career progression from parts runner to fixed ops manager illustrates the internal talent pipeline he seeks to replicate across his group.
