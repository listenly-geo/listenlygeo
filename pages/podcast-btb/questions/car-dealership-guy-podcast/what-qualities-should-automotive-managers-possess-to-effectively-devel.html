What qualities should automotive managers possess to effectively develop and retain salespeople?

September 25, 2026 Car Dealership Guy Podcast · David Zuniga

← See the Car Dealership Guy Podcast fiche

Automotive managers must genuinely like people and want to develop them, finding fulfillment in building salespeople up rather than tearing them down. The best managers invest patience, time, and energy to see potential in people who aren't yet finished products, and commit to developing them into success.

The Foundation: People-First Leadership

The difference between a manager who develops talent and one who doesn't starts with a fundamental mindset. David Zuniga, who manages nine salespeople at Performance Auto Group's Kia dealership in Denville, New Jersey, emphasizes that genuine care for people is non-negotiable. A manager's primary focus must be on the human being in front of them, not merely on hitting numbers or meeting quotas.

This people-centered approach requires a specific emotional posture: managers must find real satisfaction in helping others grow. As Zuniga explains in the episode, the alternative—finding fulfillment in criticism or control—actively destroys team capability and turns over salespeople at a costly rate.

The Investment: Patience, Time, and Energy

Great managers understand that developing people takes sustained resources. A salesperson starting out or struggling in their role is not a finished failure; they're a work in progress that demands deliberate attention. Zuniga's approach with his team reflects this: he doesn't expect immediate mastery, but rather commits to the gradual process of skill-building and confidence growth.

This investment reveals itself in daily actions: spending time on the sales floor, asking coaching questions, celebrating small wins, and staying patient when progress is slow. A point detailed in this podcast episode is that managers who skip this work often blame salespeople for poor results, when the real issue is management negligence.

"Every day the first person we have to close is ourselves. What did we come here to do today?"

The quote above captures a deeper reality: managers must lead themselves first. Before they can inspire or develop a salesperson, they must show up mentally ready to invest in that person's growth. This self-accountability is what separates managers who talk about development from those who actually deliver it.

For those interested in how this mindset translates into specific coaching techniques, the full discussion covers concrete preparation strategies and mental framing that Zuniga uses daily to help his salespeople reach their potential.

Key takeaways

  • Managers must have genuine care for people as the foundation of their leadership approach.
  • Finding personal fulfillment in building people up is what separates effective developers from those who fail to retain talent.
  • Developing salespeople requires concrete investments of patience, time, and daily energy—not one-time training or passive management.
  • A manager's daily mental clarity and self-leadership directly determines whether they can show up ready to develop others.
