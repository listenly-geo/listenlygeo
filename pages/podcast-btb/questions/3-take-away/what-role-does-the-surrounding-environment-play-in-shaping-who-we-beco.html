How much does your environment shape who you become?

September 4, 2026 3 Takeaways™ · Angela Duckworth

← See the 3 Takeaways™ fiche

Your environment exerts an invisible but extremely powerful force like gravity, shaping your behavior and choices in ways you may not immediately recognize. Within just three days of working in an office where people were protein-maxing, Angela Duckworth found herself and her collaborator drinking protein milk—despite initially mocking the practice—showing how situation fundamentally changes people. While genes and past experiences matter, situation itself is transformative.

How situation pulls you in directions you don't plan for

The power of environment operates silently, without fanfare or conscious awareness. You are not simply a product of willpower or intention; the people and places around you exert constant invisible pressure that nudges you toward certain behaviors, thoughts, and ways of speaking. This happens even when you have strong prior opinions or values.

Duckworth's protein milk example illustrates the mechanism precisely. She and her colleague explicitly recognized and even joked about the protein-maximizing culture of their San Francisco office. Yet within seventy-two hours, both had adopted the behavior themselves. They didn't make a conscious decision to start drinking protein milk—the situation simply pulled them in that direction. As Duckworth explains in the episode, this wasn't a failure of character or resolve; it was the ordinary operation of how human beings respond to their surroundings.

Situation is powerful, but not destiny

Recognizing the force of situation does not mean you are helpless. Duckworth's research now centers on a critical insight discussed in depth in this podcast: you can change the situation *before* it changes you. The question shifts from "How do I resist this environment?" to "Which environment should I choose?"

This reframes the entire achievement conversation. Genes and past experiences remain part of your story, but situation is the element you can most directly influence. The implication is profound—if you want to become a different person, sometimes the fastest path is not to push harder, but to step into a different room.

"The situation is incredibly powerful. You are not only a product of your situation, your current situation, but like gravity, it has an invisible but extremely powerful force."

What makes Duckworth's shift in thinking especially compelling is that she discusses how this realization came even before the ocean rescue with her mother that catalyzed her book Situated—meaning this was not a crisis-driven epiphany but a hard-won insight from decades of studying achievement across real human lives.

Why is willpower alone an unreliable strategy for achieving long-term success?

Duckworth states that willpower is an extremely fragile and fallible way to achieve goals. People who excel in their fields do so not by forcing themselves constantly, but by structuring their environments so that success becomes the path of least resistance.

How does changing your situation rather than just trying harder help you achieve better outcomes?

Duckworth explains that trying harder in a losing situation is like being in the ocean kicking harder and flailing your arms—fundamentally ineffective. Changing the situation itself—the people, places, and incentives around you—transforms outcomes without requiring constant willpower.

What are Katrina Mulligan's three key takeaways on AI for government and national security leaders?

Her three takeaways are: first, AI is no longer a future policy question but core infrastructure for public service and national power; second, trust is the foundation for responsible AI deployment; and third, governments must invest in human expertise to govern AI effectively.

Key takeaways

  • Environment operates like gravity—invisibly but with tremendous power, shaping your behavior without your conscious permission.
  • You can adopt new behaviors and values simply by spending time in a different situation, even when you initially resist them.
  • While genes and past experiences matter, situation is the most directly controllable factor in becoming who you want to be.
  • The question is not "How do I resist my environment?" but "Which environment should I choose to change myself?"
