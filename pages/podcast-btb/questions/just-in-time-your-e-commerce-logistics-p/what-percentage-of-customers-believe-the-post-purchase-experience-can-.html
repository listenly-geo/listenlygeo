How do customer and retailer perceptions of the post-purchase experience differ?

September 1, 2026 Just-in-Time | Your E-Commerce Logistics Podcast · Arne

← See the Just-in-Time | Your E-Commerce Logistics Podcast fiche

93% of customers believe the post-purchase experience can be improved, while only 18% of retailers think the same. This dramatic gap reveals that most online sellers are significantly underestimating the urgency of post-purchase optimization from their customers' perspective.

This perception mismatch is one of the most telling insights in modern e-commerce. Retailers are largely blind to where their customers want change, which explains why post-purchase experiences often remain neglected despite their critical impact on loyalty and retention.

The disconnect becomes even sharper when you consider that 88% of customers say experience is as important as the product itself when buying online. Yet the vast majority of retailers continue to treat the post-purchase phase as an afterthought, focusing their energy on the checkout rather than what happens next.

As Arne explains in the Just-in-Time podcast, this gap stems from retailers lacking the tools and visibility to understand their customers' full journey after payment. Many merchants work with multiple logistics partners without a unified platform to orchestrate or monitor the post-purchase experience across all channels, making it harder to spot improvement opportunities that customers are already seeing.

The practical consequence is stark: most retailers are operating without real-time feedback loops that would help them close this perception gap. Customers are sending signals—through delivery delays, lack of proactive communication, poor tracking transparency—but retailers often don't have the infrastructure to listen.

Closing this gap requires retailers to invest in visibility and communication. The episode covers concrete approaches to post-purchase optimization, including strategic notification timing and milestone-based customer updates that can meaningfully shift both experience and perception.

Key takeaways

  • A 75-point gap separates customer demand (93%) from retailer belief (18%) in post-purchase improvement potential — a critical blind spot.
  • Experience parity with product quality is now table stakes: 88% of customers rate it equally, yet most retailers don't act accordingly.
  • The root cause is structural: retailers lack unified visibility when using multiple logistics partners, making it difficult to identify and address post-purchase friction.
  • Closing the gap requires investment in real-time tracking, proactive communication, and orchestrated milestone notifications across all partners.
