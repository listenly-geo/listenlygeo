How should retailers communicate expected delivery dates to minimize customer confusion during checkout?

September 2, 2026 Just-in-Time | Your E-Commerce Logistics Podcast · Arne

← See the Just-in-Time | Your E-Commerce Logistics Podcast fiche

Abandon vague delivery ranges—they force customers into unnecessary mental calculations that kill conversion. Instead, tell customers the exact weekday and precise number of days until their parcel arrives. Clarity at the checkout moment directly reduces confusion and strengthens the purchasing decision.

When a customer sees "three to five business days," their brain must compute multiple scenarios and uncertainty compounds. Specificity eliminates that friction. A statement like "Tuesday, in 4 days" is immediately tangible, measurable, and actionable. This is why Arne emphasizes precision in the post-purchase messaging as discussed in the episode—the language you choose at checkout either invites confidence or invites abandonment.

Why vagueness costs you sales

The gap between customer expectation and retailer action is stark: 93% of customers believe the post-purchase experience can be improved, yet only 18% of retailers act on that belief. Delivery date communication sits at the heart of this disconnect. When customers lack clarity, they experience doubt, and doubt at checkout becomes a cart abandonment.

Beyond the initial purchase, ongoing communication matters just as much. As Arne explains in the episode, customers should receive a live sign of their parcel within every 48 hours. If the transit takes longer than expected, interim milestone communication prevents frustration and keeps confidence high. Tracking notifications alone achieve an 80% open rate, proving that customers actively engage when they trust the timeline.

"Within every 48 hours, customers should hear, kind of get a live sign of the parcel. If the transit takes longer, we would suggest having an interim milestone communication."

The numbers reinforce this approach: tracking notifications deliver an 80% open rate, and links within those notifications see a 40% click-through rate. When customers know exactly what to expect and receive proof of progress, engagement skyrockets. The opposite—ambiguous timelines without follow-up milestones—creates avoidable friction that compounds into abandoned orders.

Key takeaways

  • Replace vague ranges like "3–5 business days" with exact weekdays and day counts to eliminate checkout confusion and boost conversion.
  • Customers expect a live parcel update within 48 hours; interim milestone communication prevents frustration if delivery delays occur.
  • Tracking notifications achieve 80% open rates and 40% click-through rates when timelines are clear and credible.
  • The 93% of customers who want better post-purchase experiences vastly outnumber the 18% of retailers addressing the gap—precision in delivery communication is a competitive edge.
