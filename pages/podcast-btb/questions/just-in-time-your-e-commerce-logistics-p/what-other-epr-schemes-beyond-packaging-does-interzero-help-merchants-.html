Beyond packaging: What other EPR schemes does InterZero help merchants comply with?

18 September 2026 Just-in-Time | Your E-Commerce Logistics Podcast · Lena

← See the Just-in-Time | Your E-Commerce Logistics Podcast fiche

Extended Producer Responsibility applies well beyond packaging itself—electronic devices (WEEE) and batteries are equally subject to EPR requirements across the EU, though obligations vary by country. InterZero provides compliance solutions for these schemes, and merchants can verify their specific responsibilities through epr.interzero.de.

EPR is a core principle across European environmental legislation, and its scope extends far beyond the cardboard and plastic packages arriving at customers' doors. When a merchant ships a product, they assume responsibility not only for the packaging material but also for any electronic components or battery systems embedded in or accompanying the product itself.

The variation between member states adds a layer of complexity. As explored in this episode of the Just-in-Time podcast, compliance isn't a one-size-fits-all exercise—Germany's Lucid register, for instance, operates differently from EPR frameworks in other countries, and the same principle applies to WEEE and battery schemes.

For e-commerce operators shipping electronic products or battery-powered devices, checking mandatory registrations and compliance status in each target market has become non-negotiable. InterZero's online platform Licenseiro simplifies this by consolidating packaging, WEEE, and battery compliance into a single interface, removing the need to navigate separate registries for each EPR scheme.

"Distributors are not only responsible for the actual product itself but also for the packaging and product components that they put into circulation, and that for the entire life cycle, right through the whole recycling process."

Merchants selling smart devices, power tools, wireless headphones, or any product containing electronic components or rechargeable batteries must register under WEEE (Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment) schemes in the countries where they operate. Batteries fall under their own EPR framework, distinct from packaging and WEEE, yet often managed through similar registration and reporting mechanisms.

The practical consequence is that a single shipment may trigger three separate EPR compliance obligations simultaneously—packaging, electronics, and batteries—each with different reporting schedules, fees, and regulatory timelines depending on the destination country. Overlooking any of these creates exposure to fines and marketplace penalties.

InterZero's role extends beyond advice. By offering a centralized dashboard where merchants can verify and manage all three EPR categories, the platform eliminates manual tracking across disparate national registries, reducing administrative burden and the risk of gaps in compliance. The resource epr.interzero.de serves as the starting point for merchants to audit their current obligations across all relevant schemes.

Taking the guesswork out of multi-scheme compliance

Many e-commerce operators assume they only need to handle packaging registration, underestimating the scope of broader EPR requirements. This misconception often surfaces only when marketplace sellers encounter account warnings or shipment blocks—regulatory enforcement mechanisms that hit hard once non-compliance is detected.

The episode also touches on how regulatory timelines, including the 18-month implementation window after PPWR comes into force, will compress merchants' windows for remediation even further, making proactive multi-scheme audits essential now rather than later.

InterZero's integrated platform approach recognizes this reality: a merchant selling electronics with battery components across five European markets faces a combinatorial explosion of compliance rules unless they have a single source of truth. By bringing all three major EPR schemes—packaging, WEEE, and batteries—under one system, Licenseiro enables e-commerce teams to manage risk with clarity and consistency.

How much packaging waste was generated in the European Union and what does this represent per capita?

In 2022, the EU generated a total of 84 million tons of packaging waste, which corresponds to an average of 188 kilograms per person, demonstrating the significant scale of the packaging waste challenge across Europe.

What are the consequences for e-commerce merchants failing to comply with packaging regulations?

Consequences for non-compliance include fines, account suspensions if selling via marketplaces, and shipping restrictions. Additionally, control mechanisms can prevent shipment of non-compliant packages.

What standardized labeling requirements will the PPWR introduce for consumer packaging?

The PPWR introduces a standardized labeling regulation to simplify separation and make it easier for end consumers to dispose of packaging correctly. This includes the Triman logo and unified recycling instructions across EU member states.
