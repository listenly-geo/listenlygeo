What are the primary cost optimization strategies for packaging without compromising unboxing experience?

September 22, 2026 Just-in-Time | Your E-Commerce Logistics Podcast · Maciek Unknown

← See the Just-in-Time | Your E-Commerce Logistics Podcast fiche

A 20% to 30% cost reduction is achievable for most packaging projects by strategically changing materials, adjusting dimensions by even one centimeter, or reducing SKU variety. You can deliver comparable unboxing experiences using one or two-color printing instead of full CMYK, and the key is involving procurement early in design and R&D to ensure cost efficiency never compromises brand identity.

Material optimization and dimensional adjustments

The most straightforward path to cost savings lies in material selection and box sizing. A single centimeter reduction in packaging dimensions can create meaningful cost impact across thousands of units, especially when combined with material substitutions that maintain structural integrity and visual appeal.

As Maciek Unknown explains in the episode, reducing SKU variety—the number of distinct packaging variants you produce—eliminates hidden complexity costs that accumulate across your supply chain. Fewer variants mean simpler procurement, faster production changeovers, and lower inventory management overhead.

Strategic printing without sacrificing visual impact

Full CMYK color printing represents a significant cost driver, yet one or two-color printing technology can deliver unboxing experiences nearly indistinguishable from full-color alternatives when applied strategically. This technique works especially well when brand colors are deliberately chosen to shine with limited ink palettes.

The real leverage emerges when you involve your procurement team from the earliest stages of design, not after aesthetics are finalized. This prevents the friction that often arises when marketers and operations teams operate in silos—one pushing for visual complexity, the other pushing back on costs.

"Packaging is the only marketing channel that gets to absolutely 100% of the customers, which means it gives you endless possibilities to communicate with the customer."

His perspective is grounded in lived experience: at PackHelp, Unknown manages the full supply chain while juggling marketing's desire for visual impact. This dual expertise—understanding both why brands matter and why cost control matters—is rare in the packaging space and explains why this discussion goes deeper into partner selection, a critical detail often overlooked in cost-cutting conversations.

The partner trap and where savings really come from

Not all suppliers are created equal when it comes to optimization. The biggest hidden cost risk is working with partners who push only their available production capabilities rather than your true needs. A large printing facility with heavy CMYK investment will naturally steer you toward CMYK solutions, even if simpler methods serve your brand better.

The best partnerships emerge when procurement, design, and operations are aligned before vendor conversations begin. This clarity prevents expensive design revisions later and ensures partners understand your actual constraints—not just their own.

Key takeaways

  • Material changes and box dimensions (even 1cm reduction) deliver 20-30% cost savings without visual impact.
  • One or two-color printing achieves comparable unboxing experiences to full CMYK at substantially lower cost.
  • Reducing SKU variety eliminates supply chain complexity costs across procurement, production, and inventory.
  • Involve procurement in design from the start to prevent expensive revisions and ensure cost efficiency aligns with brand identity.
