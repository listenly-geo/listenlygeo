How should retailers handle parcel delivery exceptions such as packages left with neighbors?

September 7, 2026 Just-in-Time | Your E-Commerce Logistics Podcast · Arne

← See the Just-in-Time | Your E-Commerce Logistics Podcast fiche

Instead of simply sending a delivery confirmation, retailers should communicate specifically that the parcel was delivered to a neighbor and ask the customer to collect it when convenient. This targeted message reduces friction and eliminates unnecessary customer service calls that arise from confusion about missing packages.

Turning a delivery exception into a clear customer directive

When a carrier leaves a parcel with a neighbor, the standard response—a generic "delivered" notification—leaves customers confused and creates support burden. Instead, retailers need to proactively address the exception by naming the delivery exception explicitly in their outbound message. This shifts the customer's mental model from "Where is my package?" to "When can I pick it up from my neighbor?"

The goal is to transform what feels like a problem into a solved transaction. By adding a single line—"Your package has been left with Mrs. Johnson at 42 Oak Street, just next door"—a retailer removes ambiguity and gives the customer agency. As Arne explains in the episode, this kind of clarity is part of the broader post-purchase experience strategy that reduces friction at every step.

Moreover, this communication should invite action without pressure. The message "Please collect at your convenience" respects the customer's timeline and acknowledges that neighbor deliveries are not emergencies. The result: fewer frustrated customers calling support, fewer follow-up messages, and a smoother end-to-end experience.

"Within every 48 hours, customers should hear, kind of get a live sign of the parcel, if the transit takes longer, we would suggest having an interim milestone communication."

The principle behind neighbor delivery communication mirrors this broader messaging cadence: timely, specific updates prevent customer anxiety. A customer who knows exactly where their package is—even if it's temporarily with a neighbor—remains confident. A customer in the dark triggers a support inquiry within hours.

One practical angle worth exploring further in the full episode is how merchants can integrate neighbor delivery tracking data into their notification platforms, ensuring that this critical piece of information flows from carrier to retailer to customer without delay or loss.

Why this matters at scale: The broader post-purchase picture

Neighbor deliveries are just one flavor of the 20% of orders that experience delivery exceptions across the industry. Each exception—delayed delivery, failed attempt, customer absence, neighbor drop-off—creates a moment of customer doubt. Retailers who handle these moments with clarity win loyalty; those who default to silence lose it.

The data supports this urgency: 93% of customers believe the post-purchase experience can be improved, yet only 18% of retailers are actively managing it with this level of specificity. Neighbor delivery communication is a concrete, implementable way to close that gap and demonstrate the retailer's commitment to transparency, as explored in this episode.

Key takeaways

  • Replace generic delivery confirmations with specific neighbor delivery details: address, name, and timing expectation.
  • Frame neighbor delivery as a resolved status, not a problem, by giving the customer clear next-step instructions.
  • Reduce support friction by preemptively answering the question "Where is my package?" before customers need to ask.
  • Align neighbor delivery communication with your standard 48-hour update cadence to maintain consistency in post-purchase messaging.

What percentage of orders experience delivery pitfalls or exceptions requiring special handling?

Approximately 20% of orders experience delivery pitfalls such as delays, failed delivery attempts, or customer absence, with around 25 identified potential delivery exceptions that retailers must actively manage to maintain customer satisfaction.

What are the key differences in handling post-purchase experience for time-sensitive versus non-urgent products?

Delivery timing urgency depends on product type: sofas must arrive exactly on the promised day, T-shirts can arrive a day earlier without issue, and the post-purchase messaging strategy should reflect these fundamentally different customer expectations.

How many notifications does a typical customer receive from multiple carriers before their parcel is delivered?

In one customer analysis, the typical customer was receiving eight notifications before parcel delivery, with only three from the merchant and five from the carrier, highlighting excessive notification fatigue that undermines rather than supports the post-purchase experience.
