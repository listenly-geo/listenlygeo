How much packaging waste was generated in the European Union and what does this represent per capita?

17 September 2026 Just-in-Time | Your E-Commerce Logistics Podcast · Lena

← See the Just-in-Time | Your E-Commerce Logistics Podcast fiche

In 2022, the EU generated 84 million tons of packaging waste, equivalent to 188 kilograms per person per year. This staggering volume underscores why Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) regulations now require e-commerce distributors to manage packaging through its entire lifecycle—from production through recycling.

These figures reveal the scale of Europe's packaging challenge. To put 188 kilograms per capita into perspective: that's roughly the weight of two adult humans in packaging waste alone, generated per person annually across the European Union.

The responsibility for managing this waste falls directly on distributors—companies sending packages to consumers. As Lena explains in the episode, producers and distributors cannot simply hand off accountability once a product ships. They remain liable for collection, sorting, and the entire recycling process.

This obligation is driving real change in e-commerce operations. Companies must now register their packaging in national databases, track material flows, and ensure compliance with increasingly stringent recycling targets. The upcoming Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) will tighten these requirements further, mandating that all packaging be at least 70 percent recyclable by 2030.

"Distributors are not only responsible for the actual product itself but also for the packaging and product components that they put into circulation, and that for the entire life cycle, right through the whole recycling process."

For e-commerce merchants, these statistics translate into compliance urgency. Ignoring packaging regulations carries real penalties: fines, marketplace account suspensions, and shipping restrictions. The 84 million tons figure isn't abstract—it represents the regulatory pressure already shaping how online retailers package and ship goods across Europe.

The deeper insight worth exploring is how Germany's central packaging register (Lucid) tracks every ton of material, creating a enforcement mechanism that makes compliance visible and verifiable across entire supply chains.

What are the consequences for e-commerce merchants failing to comply with packaging regulations?

Consequences for non-compliance include fines, account suspensions if selling via marketplaces, and shipping restrictions. Control mechanisms ensure compliance across the supply chain.

What standardized labeling requirements will the PPWR introduce for consumer packaging?

The PPWR introduces a standardized labeling regulation to simplify separation and make it easier for end consumers to dispose of packaging correctly.

What is the timeline for PPWR implementation and when will it take effect?

The final approval of the definite version of the PPWR by the new European Parliament is planned for autumn 2024, and the regulations are set to take effect 18 months after adoption.
