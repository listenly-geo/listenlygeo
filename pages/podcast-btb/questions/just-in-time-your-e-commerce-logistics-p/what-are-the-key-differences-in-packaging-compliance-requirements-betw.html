What are the key differences in packaging compliance requirements between European countries?

September 11, 2026 Just-in-Time | Your E-Commerce Logistics Podcast · Lena

← See the Just-in-Time | Your E-Commerce Logistics Podcast fiche

Packaging compliance requirements vary significantly by country in Europe, with no universal threshold applying across all nations. France mandates licensing from the first kilogram of packaging without quantity thresholds, requires the Triman logo labeling, and has required a prevention plan since 2023. Austria also has no quantity thresholds but since 2023 requires appointing an authorized representative if you do not have a branch in the country.

Country-Specific Regulatory Frameworks

The complexity of European packaging compliance stems from Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) regulations that apply across the continent but are implemented differently in each nation. When shipping packaged goods to private consumers, distributors must navigate distinct licensing and regulatory requirements country by country—there is no "one-size-fits-all" approach.

In France, the framework is particularly strict: licensing is mandatory from your very first kilogram of packaging with no exemption thresholds whatsoever. This triggers immediate obligations including the mandatory Triman logo (the distinctive visual symbol indicating recyclable packaging) on product labels, plus the requirement to file a prevention plan—a formal document outlining how you will manage your packaging waste and environmental impact. As Lena explains in the podcast episode, these rules apply the moment you start distributing packaged products into the French market.

Austria follows a similarly stringent approach regarding quantity thresholds—there is no minimum quantity that exempts you from compliance—but the specific compliance mechanism differs. Since 2023, appointing an authorized representative is mandatory if your company does not already maintain a physical branch or office within Austria. This representative must be designated to handle all packaging-related regulatory communications and filings on your behalf.

The key takeaway is that European e-commerce companies must treat each country as a distinct compliance jurisdiction, not as part of a single market—each nation has its own entry points, licensing procedures, and documentation requirements.

"Distributors are not only responsible for the actual product itself but also for the packaging and product components that they put into circulation, and that for the entire life cycle, right through the whole recycling process."
