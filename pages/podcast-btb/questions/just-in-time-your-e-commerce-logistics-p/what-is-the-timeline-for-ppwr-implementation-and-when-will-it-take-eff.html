When does PPWR take effect, and what is the implementation timeline?

14 September 2026 Just-in-Time | Your E-Commerce Logistics Podcast · Lena

← See the Just-in-Time | Your E-Commerce Logistics Podcast fiche

The European Parliament plans to finalize approval of the PPWR in autumn 2024, after which the regulation will take effect 18 months later—giving e-commerce merchants a concrete preparation window before they must comply with the new packaging standards.

This timeline is critical for any business shipping products across the European Union. The 18-month implementation window is not arbitrary; it's designed to give distributors and e-commerce companies sufficient time to audit their packaging systems, register with national authorities, and adjust their supply chains to meet the new Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) requirements.

A Phased Approach to Compliance Readiness

The two-step timeline—approval first, then the delayed enforcement period—reflects a deliberate regulatory strategy. As discussed in the episode, companies cannot ignore the autumn 2024 approval date as a "future problem." Instead, businesses should treat the announcement itself as a trigger for action, since the 18-month countdown begins immediately after formal entry into force.

This means that merchants must begin mapping their packaging obligations now, even before the regulation's final approval. Different European countries already have varying packaging compliance requirements, and the PPWR will layer additional harmonized standards on top of existing national rules. Starting early ensures you're not scrambling to comply when the 18-month window opens.

"Distributors are not only responsible for the actual product itself but also for the packaging and product components that they put into circulation, and that for the entire life cycle, right through the whole recycling process."

To understand what you'll need to do after the 18-month implementation period expires, the episode also covers the three main obligations for e-commerce merchants, which include dual system licensing, packaging data registration, and full Extended Producer Responsibility compliance across the entire product lifecycle.

The takeaway is simple: autumn 2024 is when the countdown begins, not when compliance suddenly becomes urgent. Every month between now and that 18-month mark is preparation time you cannot recover.

What is the Packaging and Packaging Waste Regulation (PPWR) and what are its key environmental goals?

The PPWR is a proposed new regulation aiming to improve sustainability of packaging, reduce packaging waste, and harmonize regulations across EU member states with concrete targets like 70% recyclability by 2030.

What are the three main obligations for e-commerce merchants placing packaging on the German market?

The three packaging act obligations in Germany are: first, completing licensing via a dual system through Licenseiro.de; second, registering packaging data; and third, maintaining full Extended Producer Responsibility compliance throughout the recycling cycle.

What are the key differences in packaging compliance requirements between European countries?

Regulations differ significantly country by country. In France, licensing is mandatory from the first kilogram of packaging with no quantity thresholds, while other EU countries have different registration systems and participation rules.
