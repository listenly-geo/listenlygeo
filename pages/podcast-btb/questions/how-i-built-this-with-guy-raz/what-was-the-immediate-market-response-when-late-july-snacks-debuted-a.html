How did Late July Snacks become a national brand overnight at the Natural Products Expo?

September 4, 2026 How I Built This with Guy Raz · Nicole Bernard Dawes

← See the How I Built This with Guy Raz fiche

Late July Snacks debuted at the 2003 Natural Products Expo in Baltimore and left the show already operating as a national brand, with signed contracts from Whole Foods nationwide and major distributors. At that moment, Late July was the only organic cracker brand available in natural food stores.

From unknown startup to national presence in one trade show

The Natural Products Expo in Baltimore became a turning point for Late July Snacks. Nicole Bernard Dawes and her team walked in as an emerging brand and walked out with national retail placement secured—a result that would have taken most food companies years to achieve through traditional sales channels.

The timing aligned perfectly with a market gap. Natural food stores had evolved dramatically in the produce and dairy sections with certified organic options, yet the snack aisles remained stagnant, dominated by conventional mass-market brands. Late July's entirely organic cracker line filled a void that had no competition at the natural retailer level.

What happened at that Baltimore expo tells a larger story about market readiness and product-market fit. As Nicole Bernard Dawes explains in the episode, the immediate buyer enthusiasm wasn't coincidental—it reflected years of industry evolution and consumer demand that had been waiting for exactly this kind of offering.

"We debuted the company in 2003 at the Natural Products Expo in Baltimore. And we basically left that show a national brand."

The success at Baltimore had immediate consequences. Within approximately two months of that expo, a bank loan worth $3 million was called in—a financial pressure that would test the young company's resolve but also provided capital to meet the sudden, massive demand from national retailers.

For anyone building a consumer brand, the full story on How I Built This with Guy Raz reveals how Late July navigated the pressure of explosive growth, the challenges of maintaining organic certification while scaling production, and the critical decisions that followed that transformative weekend in Baltimore.

How did the passage of the Organic Food Production Act impact Late July Snacks' market entry?

The Organic Food Production Act established the USDA Organic Seal with strict federal rules and regulations, making it a federal crime not to follow them. This gave Late July credibility and created a regulatory barrier that protected the brand from non-compliant competitors in early-stage organic snacking.

What inspired the creation of an organic cracker brand in the early 2000s?

Nicole Bernard Dawes noticed that while the perimeter of natural food stores had evolved with organic produce and dairy, the snack section remained unchanged and full of conventional options. This gap between consumer demand and available products became the seed for Late July Snacks.

What is Raised Nutrition and what makes its protein bar different from competitors?

Raised Nutrition, founded by Darcy and his wife Jess and based in Ballina, New South Wales, Australia, makes whole food protein bars free from artificial ingredients. The brand emphasizes real, recognizable ingredients as its core differentiator in a crowded functional food market.

Key takeaways

  • Late July Snacks achieved national retail placement in a single trade show appearance—an exceptionally rare outcome for emerging food brands.
  • The company entered a retail gap: natural food stores had no certified organic cracker options available at the time of launch.
  • Whole Foods and multiple major distributors signed contracts immediately, validating the product-market fit and creating rapid scaling pressure.
  • The expo success triggered a $3 million bank loan call within two months, requiring swift operational scaling to meet national demand.
