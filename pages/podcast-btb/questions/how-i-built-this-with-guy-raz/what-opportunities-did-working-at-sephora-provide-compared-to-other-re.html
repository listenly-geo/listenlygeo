Why did Sephora training give Karissa Bodnar an edge that other beauty retailers couldn't offer?

September 18, 2026 How I Built This with Guy Raz · Karissa Bodnar

← See the How I Built This with Guy Raz fiche

Sephora provided comprehensive training across every brand in the store, plus certifications in artistry, skincare, fragrance, and hair care, whereas other beauty retailers rejected candidates who lacked these credentials. Unlike department store beauty counters, Sephora's structured education became the foundation of Bodnar's professional expertise before she ever stepped into entrepreneurship.

When Bodnar applied to beauty counter positions at other retailers early in her career, she encountered a hard ceiling: they wanted people who already possessed the skill set she didn't yet have. Sephora, by contrast, didn't just hire her—they invested in her education. This systematic approach to training set her apart from peers who pursued beauty retail elsewhere.

The breadth of Sephora's curriculum was unusual for retail at the time. Rather than specializing in a single brand or product category, Bodnar was trained on the entire Sephora portfolio, giving her a 360-degree view of the beauty industry that spanned multiple disciplines: makeup artistry, skincare chemistry, fragrance families, and hair care science. This knowledge wasn't theoretical—it was grounded in hands-on product expertise and consumer interaction every single shift.

"I love this industry. I love what I get to do every day."

This foundation proved invaluable when she later moved to product development at L'Oreal. The multi-disciplinary knowledge Sephora had instilled meant she could speak the language of chemistry, artistry, and consumer behavior—skills that would have been fragmented or missing entirely if she'd worked at a traditional department store counter. As explained in this episode of How I Built This with Guy Raz, that education became the bedrock for everything that followed in her entrepreneurial journey.

Bodnar has since credited Sephora with "raising her" and providing her "real education in beauty"—language that underscores how rare this kind of structured, multi-brand training was in retail beauty at that moment. The opportunity wasn't just a job; it was an apprenticeship that other retailers weren't offering to people without prior credentials. She got hired precisely because Sephora was willing to teach, while others demanded proof of existing mastery.
