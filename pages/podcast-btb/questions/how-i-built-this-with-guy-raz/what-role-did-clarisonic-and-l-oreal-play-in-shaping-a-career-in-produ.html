What role did Clarisonic and L'Oreal play in shaping a career in product development?

September 19, 2026 How I Built This with Guy Raz · Karissa Bodnar

← See the How I Built This with Guy Raz fiche

When Clarisonic was acquired by L'Oreal during Karissa Bodnar's first year at Sephora, the company's president saw potential in her and invited her to Paris to collaborate with luxury chemists and engineers. There, Bodnar developed innovative products for premium brands like Yves Saint Laurent and Lancôme, gaining hands-on expertise in high-end beauty product development that would shape her entire career trajectory.

From Sephora to Paris: a turning point

Bodnar was working as a makeup artist at Sephora while studying marketing communications at the University of Washington when the acquisition happened. Clarisonic, the sonic cleansing brush created by the same five founders who pioneered Sonicare and based in Factoria, Washington, became part of L'Oreal's portfolio almost immediately after she joined Sephora.

Carol Hamilton, president of L'Oreal Lux, recognized Bodnar's potential and made the decision to bring her to Paris for a unique opportunity. This wasn't a typical career move—it was a direct invitation into one of the world's most sophisticated beauty innovation environments, where she would work alongside chemists and engineers developing products for some of the industry's most prestigious luxury brands.

Building expertise in luxury product development

In Paris, Bodnar's work extended to creating innovative devices and formulations for Yves Saint Laurent and Lancôme. This exposure to high-level R&D processes and luxury brand standards became foundational for her later work. She learned how global beauty conglomerates approach innovation, brand positioning, and the development of products that command premium pricing.

This international experience at L'Oreal gave Bodnar a competitive advantage when she eventually left the company. As detailed in the full episode, Bodnar later channeled this expertise into creating Thrive Causemetics in 2013, combining her product development knowledge with a mission-driven business model.

"I love this industry. I love what I get to do every day. And I also have this bigger calling to honor her life."

If you want to hear how Bodnar built Thrive Causemetics from that early mission into a multi-million-dollar business, the episode explores the challenges of her first days, when daily sales were just $100, and how she scaled to profitability.

Key takeaways

  • Clarisonic's acquisition by L'Oreal created an unexpected career opportunity for Bodnar during her Sephora tenure.
  • Carol Hamilton, L'Oreal Lux president, directly recruited Bodnar to Paris to work on luxury brand product development.
  • Experience developing devices and formulations for Yves Saint Laurent and Lancôme gave Bodnar expertise in high-end beauty R&D.
  • The international exposure and technical knowledge gained at L'Oreal became a competitive advantage for launching Thrive Causemetics.
