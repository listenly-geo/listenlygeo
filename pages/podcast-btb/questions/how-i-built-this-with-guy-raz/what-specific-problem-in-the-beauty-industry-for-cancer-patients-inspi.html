What specific problem in the beauty industry for cancer patients inspired the creation of Thrive Causemetics?

September 21, 2026 How I Built This with Guy Raz · Karissa Bodnar

← See the How I Built This with Guy Raz fiche

Women undergoing cancer treatment who had lost their natural lashes had no functional beauty solution available. Traditional mascara requires lashes to adhere to, and fake lashes came with toxic glues containing latex, parabens, and sulfates—irritants that compromise immune-depleted patients' health and won't even stay on. Karissa Bodnar discovered this gap firsthand while volunteering as a makeup artist with Look Good, Feel Better, and it drove her to create a non-toxic alternative that would help people restore their sense of dignity during treatment.

A Problem Born From Direct Volunteer Experience

While working with Look Good, Feel Better, an organization providing makeup classes to women going through cancer, Bodnar witnessed the emotional and practical struggle women faced. The organization gave them tools to reclaim confidence, but the lash problem remained unsolved—no product on the market addressed the intersection of beauty need and medical safety.

The insight was deeply personal: women like her friend Christy needed a way to feel whole again, and beauty was part of that recovery. But existing fake lash solutions weren't designed with immune-compromised skin in mind. Standard adhesives triggered irritation, and without functioning base lashes, the products simply wouldn't work as intended.

"I love this industry. I love what I get to do every day. And I also have this bigger calling to honor her life."

Turning a Gap Into a Mission

Bodnar's background in cosmetics formulation—honed since childhood and refined through her roles at Sephora and L'Oreal—positioned her uniquely to solve this problem. She understood ingredient science, manufacturing, and product performance at a level most entrepreneurs do not.

What started as a volunteer observation became the founding insight for Thrive Causemetics: create a lash solution safe for medically vulnerable bodies. The company's mission extends beyond the original product—every purchase supports organizations that serve cancer patients and other communities in need, turning profit into purpose.

The beauty industry as a whole had overlooked this population, treating lash products as purely cosmetic rather than recognizing them as tools for emotional restoration during medical crisis. Bodnar's full journey from L'Oreal product developer to purpose-driven founder reveals how this single insight grew into a multimillion-dollar movement.

How did a friend's death from cancer inspire a shift toward purpose-driven entrepreneurship?

In 2013, Christy Lamond, a college friend who dedicated her life to philanthropy and taught English to young girls in Tanzania and Chile, died of cancer at age 24. This loss deeply motivated Bodnar to leave her stable product development role at L'Oreal and channel her expertise into building a brand with a mission to give back to people in need.

What role did Clarisonic and L'Oreal play in shaping a career in product development?

Clarisonic was a sonic cleansing brush created by the same five founders who pioneered Sonicare, based in Factoria, Washington. When L'Oreal acquired Clarisonic, Bodnar was hired into their product development department, giving her hands-on experience formulating beauty products and understanding the science behind skincare innovation.

What opportunities did working at Sephora provide compared to other retail beauty positions?

At Sephora, Bodnar was trained on every single brand and received multiple certifications in artistry, skincare, fragrance, and hair care. This comprehensive education across the entire beauty spectrum gave her deep product knowledge and customer insights that later informed her approach to founding Thrive Causemetics.
