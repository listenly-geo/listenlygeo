Why did the name 'Late July' resonate with the founder's vision for the brand?

September 6, 2026 How I Built This with Guy Raz · Nicole Bernard Dawes

← See the How I Built This with Guy Raz fiche

Nicole Bernard Dawes chose Late July to capture Cape Cod's most magical moment—when summer is in full swing, tomatoes and corn are emerging, and there's a special, almost enchanted feeling in the air. Her personal connection deepened the choice: her birthday falls on July 30th, a date that tied her emotionally to that precise window of the year.

The name wasn't arbitrary. Dawes was building a brand rooted in place and seasonality, values that would define Late July Snacks from its founding in 2003. As she explains in the episode, the name encapsulated not just a product, but a philosophy about when things taste best—when ingredients are at their peak and nature offers abundance.

The late-July window on Cape Cod holds a particular resonance: vacation season is in full momentum, families are gathered, tables are laden with fresh summer produce. It was a moment Dawes knew intimately from her childhood and family legacy. Her father, Steve Bernard, had founded Cape Cod Potato Chips in the early 1980s, rooting that business in the same geographic identity and seasonal tradition.

This naming choice became more than branding—it was a commitment to authenticity and locality. When she later launched Late July with organic crackers as the centerpiece, the name reinforced a promise: these were products designed for that perfect slice of summer, made with care and premium ingredients. The emotional resonance of the name aligned perfectly with what consumers were beginning to seek in the early 2000s: food brands with real stories, real places, and real values behind them.

"We debuted the company in 2003 at the Natural Products Expo in Baltimore. And we basically left that show a national brand."

The surprising part of this origin story—detailed further in the full episode—is how quickly retailers embraced the brand after that Baltimore debut, and the manufacturing challenges that came with that sudden national scale.

Key takeaways

  • Late July was chosen to represent Cape Cod's most perfect moment in time—mid-summer when vacation is in full swing and fresh produce is abundant.
  • Nicole Bernard Dawes's personal connection to July 30th (her birthday) added emotional depth to the brand name choice.
  • The name reinforced the brand's commitment to authenticity, locality, and seasonal thinking—values tied to her family's Cape Cod heritage.
  • Late July Snacks debuted in 2003 as one of the first brands to launch entirely under the USDA Organic Seal, with the name reflecting a philosophy about peak-season, premium ingredients.

What challenge emerged after securing major retail orders for the newly launched organic cracker line?

While the company could manage the capital requirements for production, many retailers were excited about the organic concept but their customers did not take to the crackers as expected.

What was the immediate market response when Late July Snacks debuted at the Natural Products Expo in Baltimore?

The company left the 2003 Natural Products Expo in Baltimore as a national brand, signing up with Whole Foods nationally and obtaining contracts with two major retailers.

How did the passage of the Organic Food Production Act impact Late July Snacks' market entry?

The Organic Food Production Act established the USDA Organic Seal with strict federal rules and regulations, making it a federal crime not to follow them and giving certified organic brands immediate credibility.
