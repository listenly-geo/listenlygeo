How did a friend's death from cancer inspire a shift toward purpose-driven entrepreneurship?

September 20, 2026 How I Built This with Guy Raz · Karissa Bodnar

← See the How I Built This with Guy Raz fiche

In 2013, Karissa Bodnar's close friend Christy Lamond died of cancer at age 24. Christy had dedicated her life to philanthropy, teaching English to young girls in Tanzania and Chile, and her blog "Wake Up and Live" explored dying successfully. This loss moved Bodnar to leave L'Oreal, where she was working in product development, and instead create Thrive Causemetics—a beauty brand built from the ground up with a mission to give back.

From grief to mission: A moment of clarity

When Christy called Bodnar from her hospital bed while Karissa was presenting to L'Oreal's CEO at their annual conference in Paris, the moment felt like a turning point. Nine months later, after Christy passed away, Bodnar's sense of purpose shifted entirely. She had spent years building products for one of the world's largest beauty conglomerates, but her friend's life—and death—showed her a different way to measure success.

Christy's blog and her relentless commitment to helping others despite her illness crystallized something for Bodnar. As she explains in the episode, leaving a stable corporate position to start a business is a difficult decision—but honoring a friend's legacy and creating a brand with a mission made it inevitable.

"I love this industry. I love what I get to do every day. And I also have this bigger calling to honor her life."

The founding of Thrive Causemetics was not a gradual transition—it was a deliberate break from the conventional career path. Bodnar invested approximately $100,000 of her life savings into the company, launching with early-day sales that sometimes reached only $100. But the difference was clear: every product sold would directly support charitable causes, creating a direct line between beauty and philanthropy.

What makes this shift meaningful is that it wasn't driven by market research or a business opportunity—it was driven by genuine loss and the desire to continue her friend's work in a new form. As detailed in this podcast episode, Bodnar's journey from product developer to founder was inseparable from her decision to build a company that would honor Christy's belief in giving back.

Key takeaways

  • Christy Lamond's death from cancer at age 24 became the pivotal moment that shifted Bodnar's priorities from corporate advancement to purpose-driven entrepreneurship.
  • Bodnar left a stable product development role at L'Oreal to build Thrive Causemetics, investing her life savings and committing to a mission-first model.
  • The brand was built explicitly to honor her friend's legacy of giving back and helping others, turning grief into a sustainable business model.

What role did Clarisonic and L'Oreal play in shaping a career in product development?

Clarisonic was a sonic cleansing brush created by the same five founders who pioneered Sonicare, based in Factoria, Washington. When L'Oreal acquired the company, Bodnar was hired for product development, gaining deep expertise in skincare innovation and formulation that would later inform Thrive Causemetics.

What opportunities did working at Sephora provide compared to other retail beauty positions?

At Sephora, Bodnar was trained on every single brand and received multiple certifications in artistry, skincare, fragrance, and hair care. This comprehensive training became foundational to her later work in product development and brand building.

How did growing up on a farm influence early interest in cosmetic formulation and chemistry?

Bodnar started formulating makeup in her parents' kitchen at age 10, learning chemistry through imagination and experimentation. She ground down sunflower petals and mixed household ingredients to create products, developing a hands-on understanding of formulation from an early age.
