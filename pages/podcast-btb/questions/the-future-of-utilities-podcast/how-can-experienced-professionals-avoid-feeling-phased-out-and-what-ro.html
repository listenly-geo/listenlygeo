How can experienced professionals avoid feeling phased out, and what role does mentoring play?

29 August 2026 The Future of Utilities Podcast · Karen Anderson

← See the The Future of Utilities Podcast fiche

Mentoring forces experienced professionals to reflect deeply on where they are and where they want to go—something that rarely happens in the rush of daily work. Working with younger colleagues is refreshing and prevents expertise from becoming static; it keeps accumulated knowledge alive and dynamic.

The fear of obsolescence is real for many seasoned professionals, but Karen Anderson emphasizes that this feeling can be transformed through intentional action. Rather than watching from the sidelines, experienced professionals who take on mentoring roles gain renewed purpose and direction. The Future of Utilities Podcast explores this dynamic through conversations with industry leaders who've discovered that mentoring isn't a step backward—it's a powerful way forward.

Reflection as the gateway to staying engaged

Mentoring creates a structured space for career reflection that busy professionals rarely carve out on their own. The act of guiding someone else forces you to examine your own path, values, and ambitions. This self-awareness alone can reignite motivation and help experienced professionals see new possibilities within their existing roles or beyond.

The process also counters the invisibility trap—where senior talent feels overlooked or bypassed by younger, faster-moving colleagues. By formalizing a mentoring relationship, experienced professionals reclaim visibility and become recognized as knowledge holders and developers of talent. As discussed in this episode, the Women's Utilities Network has witnessed this transformation firsthand across the utilities sector.

"Mentoring, for me, has given me a purpose. Seeing people build confidence, overcoming obstacles, they've set goals that they thought were impossible. Seeing them achieve those goals has been rewarding."

Expertise doesn't have to be static

A common misconception is that accumulated experience has a shelf life—that what worked yesterday automatically becomes irrelevant today. In reality, seasoned professionals bring irreplaceable perspective, especially when they engage actively with emerging talent and evolving industry challenges.

Mentoring relationships are bidirectional. While mentors guide mentees through obstacles and help them set and achieve ambitious goals, mentees often bring fresh ideas, new technologies, and contemporary perspectives that keep experienced professionals sharp and connected. This cross-generational exchange is what the One Mentoring Programme facilitates across the utilities industry—creating structured pathways for this renewal to happen systematically.

Key takeaways

  • Mentoring forces career reflection and helps experienced professionals reconnect with their own direction and purpose.
  • Working with younger colleagues keeps accumulated expertise dynamic rather than static, and prevents knowledge from becoming obsolete.
  • Formal mentoring roles restore visibility and recognition for senior talent, countering the invisibility that can come with being sidelined.
  • The reciprocal nature of mentoring relationships—where both mentor and mentee benefit—ensures that experience remains relevant and evolving.
