How will Robinhood's new social trading platform work?

September 22, 2026 Sorry, We're Closed with Pat Light · TiVo

← See the Sorry, We're Closed with Pat Light fiche

Robinhood is launching a social media platform for traders scheduled to release in early 2026 that will display real-time trades and allow users to track major investors like Warren Buffett and Nancy Pelosi. To grow influence and be tracked on the platform, traders must execute their trades directly through Robinhood's app.

Real-time tracking and investor visibility

The platform will function as a live trading feed where users can follow the trade execution of prominent investors in real time. This directly echoes the appeal that made Wall Street Bets and GameStop such phenomena on Robinhood, but now formalized into an official social layer built into the trading platform itself.

Users will be able to see complete trade execution history—when trades were made, how they were executed, and what the returns were. This transparency creates a competitive dynamic where traders can build personal brands by demonstrating their investment acumen and profitability directly within the Robinhood ecosystem.

The Robinhood-only requirement

While Robinhood will track big-name investors like Warren Buffett and Nancy Pelosi regardless of where they trade, individual traders who want to build their own platforms and grow influence on Robinhood's social app must make their trades directly on Robinhood. This is the mechanism that ties trading activity to social media presence.

The strategy mirrors what happened organically during the GameStop era—traders sharing their positions and results—but now creates a built-in incentive structure where Robinhood's platform becomes the native venue for both executing trades and broadcasting them socially. As discussed in the episode, this directly benefits Robinhood by increasing trading volume from users seeking to grow their social profiles.

One detail worth exploring further: the episode goes deeper into how Robinhood is competing with Silicon Valley giants like OpenAI, Microsoft, and Google to recruit top engineering talent to build this platform—a revealing insight into the scale and ambition behind the project.
