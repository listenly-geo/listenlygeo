Why do coaches believe they don't have a framework when they actually do?

September 22, 2026 Best Damn Coach Podcast: Conversations for coaches, mentors, and service providers to grow a profitable coaching business · Amanda Walker

← See the Best Damn Coach Podcast: Conversations for coaches, mentors, and service providers to grow a profitable coaching business fiche

Most coaches experience insider blindness—they are so close to their own process that the work feels natural and effortless, making them invisible to their own eyes. When the framework exists as a repeatable pattern across dozens or hundreds of sessions but stays unwritten, unnamed, and unrecognized, both the coach and their potential clients miss it entirely.

The paradox is this: when a coach says "it just comes to me" or "I don't really have a framework, I just know what to do," that very statement signals they are already operating from a framework. What they're describing is actually their zone of genius at work—the place where expertise becomes so internalized that it feels invisible.

The framework is there. It's in every session, every client interaction, every decision they make during the coaching process. But because it lives inside the coach's head rather than on paper, with a name, and a structure that can be taught, it remains hidden from view. This invisibility creates a real problem: potential clients can't see what makes this coach different, and the coach themselves can't scale or systematize what they do.

"Your lived experience is the very thing that sets your process apart from somebody else's."

Interestingly, Amanda Walker found that a coach's framework typically emerges within the first 20 minutes of the discovery process, suggesting it's far closer to the surface than most coaches realize. The issue isn't that the framework is missing—it's that the coach hasn't yet learned to see it.

The real breakthrough happens when coaches shift from operating purely on instinct to recognizing the repeatable patterns they've already developed. Once named and documented, that hidden framework becomes a competitive advantage, a teaching tool, and a foundation for scaling a coaching business from one-on-one work to group programs, courses, or even training other coaches.

Key takeaways

  • Insider blindness keeps coaches from seeing their own framework because it feels too natural and effortless to recognize.
  • A framework that is unwritten, unnamed, and unrecognized remains invisible to both the coach and their potential clients.
  • When coaches say "I just know what to do," they are already describing their framework in action—their zone of genius.
  • A coach's actual framework emerges within the first 20 minutes when they begin to examine their own process systematically.
