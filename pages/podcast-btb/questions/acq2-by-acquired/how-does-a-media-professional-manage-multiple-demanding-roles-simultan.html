How does a media professional manage multiple demanding roles simultaneously across television, newsletters, and books?

September 18, 2026 ACQ2 by Acquired · Andrew Sorkin

← See the ACQ2 by Acquired fiche

Andrew Ross Sorkin's answer is ruthless time discipline: he wakes at 4:30 a.m., spends roughly one hour before Squawk Box starts (6 a.m.) refining the DealBook newsletter, hosts the live television show from 6 to 9 a.m. Eastern, then manages books, summits, and television work throughout the day—with each project informing the others rather than diluting focus.

The pre-dawn ritual: one hour to lock in DealBook

Sorkin's day begins before most people wake. Between 4:30 a.m. and the start of Squawk Box at 6 a.m., he operates in what amounts to a contained edit window: checking email and messages, then making final tweaks and refinements to the DealBook newsletter before it goes out to over one million subscribers. This is not exploratory work—it is execution of a structure already built by his team.

The newsletter itself has evolved into a distributed operation. Michael Dillamer, who has been with DealBook for approximately 20 years, anchors much of the day-to-day editorial work. Bernard Warner works overnight from Italy, which means the newsletter has real-time global coverage without Sorkin managing every detail personally. This structural delegation allows the 4:30 a.m. hour to be refinement, not creation from zero.

Projects that fuel each other rather than compete

Sorkin's insight—one that separates overwork from sustainable multi-project management—is that his television role, newsletter, books, and the annual Dealbook Summit do not operate in silos. He describes having "the best of both worlds": a television platform that drives newsletter audience and credibility; a newsletter that surfaces stories for deeper book-length investigation; and a summit that generates proprietary interviews and insights flowing back into DealBook.

As detailed in this episode, this approach differs fundamentally from juggling four separate, unrelated jobs. Each project extends the reach and authority of the others, creating leverage rather than duplication of effort.

"I think it's important to understand what their motivation is. But ultimately, as long as I can confirm the information typically from two, three, four different sources, or I can get documentation of the issue, I try not to worry too much about what their ultimate incentives are."

Key takeaways

  • Sorkin's routine starts at 4:30 a.m. with a one-hour window to refine the DealBook newsletter before Squawk Box begins at 6 a.m.
  • Squawk Box runs three hours daily (6 to 9 a.m. Eastern), creating a fixed television commitment that anchors his entire schedule.
  • A distributed team—including Michael Dillamer (20+ years) and Bernard Warner working overnight from Italy—handles ongoing DealBook editorial work, freeing Sorkin to focus on refinement rather than creation.
  • His projects reinforce each other: television drives newsletter audience, the newsletter surfaces book topics, and the Dealbook Summit generates proprietary content feeding back into all platforms.
