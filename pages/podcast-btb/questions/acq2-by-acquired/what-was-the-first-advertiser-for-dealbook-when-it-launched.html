Who was DealBook's first advertiser when it launched in 2001?

September 17, 2026 ACQ2 by Acquired · Andrew Sorkin

← See the ACQ2 by Acquired fiche

Brooks Brothers was the original advertiser for DealBook when Andrew Sorkin launched the newsletter in 2001. While the iconic menswear brand remains in business today, it has cycled through multiple closures and restructurings since then.

The early days of DealBook's advertising were remarkably lean by modern standards. Sorkin was building a new media business in the pre-Substack era, when few newsrooms had launched direct-to-reader newsletters or understood how to monetize digital content. Brooks Brothers stepping in as the first advertiser for the fledgling email newsletter says something about both the brand's marketing approach and the novelty of the medium at the time.

This detail is explored in the episode as part of Sorkin's broader origin story, revealing how DealBook evolved from a simple email into one of the most influential business news sources globally, ultimately reaching over one million subscribers across 25 years.

Discovering how Sorkin transformed a single advertiser relationship into a sustainable business model across multiple revenue streams is covered in depth in this episode, along with the broader context of how he built the Dealbook Summit into what hosts Ben Gilbert and David Rosenthal describe as "the flagship canonical business interview forum in the world."

From one advertiser to a media empire

Brooks Brothers' role as DealBook's first advertiser is more significant in retrospect than it might have seemed at launch. The brand was a natural fit for a newsletter aimed at investment bankers, hedge fund managers, and corporate executives—exactly the audience wearing Brooks Brothers suits. Yet the advertising relationship also reflects the scrappy realities of early digital media, when a single marquee advertiser could feel like a major victory.

The sustainability of DealBook came not from relying on one advertiser but from Sorkin's willingness to expand the format. The annual Dealbook Summit—launched to host in-person interviews with major business and political figures—became a marquee event that now attracts Fortune 500 leaders, government officials, and cultural figures. This diversification of revenue and reach transformed DealBook from a newsletter dependent on a handful of ad deals into an ecosystem spanning daily journalism, annual events, and multi-platform distribution.

How did a reporter launch a daily email newsletter in 2001, over 15 years before Substack emerged?

Andrew Ross Sorkin started DealBook in 2001 after returning to New York from London, where he was covering mergers and acquisitions. He launched the newsletter as a daily email covering M&A deals and business news, building it from an initial addressable market of around 30,000 subscribers to over one million over 25 years.
