How quickly can a newly launched real estate software platform gain customer traction?

September 25, 2026 The Boulos Beat: A Commercial Real Estate Podcast · George Matelich

← See the The Boulos Beat: A Commercial Real Estate Podcast fiche

Rely launched its platform in January 2026 and has already acquired dozens of multifamily firms as users, with rapid growth already underway. However, George Matelich notes it will take at least a year before feeling confident they are doing everything right for all customers, with a focus on maintaining close alignment with each user.

Momentum from day one — but patience required for maturity

The speed at which Rely gained initial traction surprised even its founders. Just months after launch, the platform attracted a meaningful customer base in the multifamily real estate sector, validating months of intense development and stakeholder research. The team at Cardinal Group spent approximately five months building the Rely platform before launch, and that preparation paid off immediately.

Yet early adoption does not equal product-market fit mastery. Matelich is candid about the reality: one year is the realistic minimum to truly understand how the software performs across diverse customer workflows and to refine the platform for all use cases. This transparency reflects a deeper philosophy — that sustainable growth in B2B software requires not just speed, but genuine customer intimacy.

As Matelich explains in the episode, the multifamily property management industry is vast — generating approximately $100 billion annually — yet highly fragmented. That fragmentation means each firm has slightly different pain points, workflows, and expectations. Dozens of early customers is a strong signal, but dozens is not yet a comprehensive proof of universal applicability.

"I wasn't put on this earth to build someone else's dream."

The fact that Matelich has already built Nexus Maine into a community of over 1,000 members underscores his commitment to deep market engagement. That same relational rigor now underpins Rely's customer strategy: staying close to users, iterating quickly based on real feedback, and resisting the urge to declare victory before the platform has truly proven itself at scale.

Rely's AI-powered software automates lease file audits, utility audits, contract audits, and financial audits on apartment complexes. The platform uses artificial intelligence to process and analyze hundreds of pages of documentation in a fraction of the traditional due diligence timeframe.
