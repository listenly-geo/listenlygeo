How did aggressive networking and community building accelerate startup success in a new geographic market?

September 22, 2026 The Boulos Beat: A Commercial Real Estate Podcast · George Matelich

← See the The Boulos Beat: A Commercial Real Estate Podcast fiche

George Matelich launched Nexus Maine as a Slack-based online community with an initial goal of reaching 100 members, but it grew to over 1,000 members through consistent engagement and value delivery. The platform hosts regular events and publishes a twice-monthly newsletter, helping members find jobs and build professional relationships in a market where he was a complete newcomer just two years ago.

From Isolation to Community: A Self-Defense Strategy That Worked

Matelich describes himself as a hyper-extrovert who relies on aggressive networking as a self-defense mechanism against loneliness when entering unfamiliar territory. Moving from San Francisco to Kennebunk, Maine with virtually no existing connections, he recognized that building a robust professional network wasn't optional—it was essential to his success both personally and entrepreneurially.

Rather than waiting passively for opportunities, he took a strategic approach: creating a digital gathering place where aspiring entrepreneurs, startup founders, and business professionals in Maine could connect. As Matelich explains in the episode, the initial membership target was modest, but the community's value proposition—job opportunities, networking, and peer learning—resonated far more widely than anticipated.

Sustaining Growth Beyond the First Thousand

Nexus Maine's staying power comes from consistent, structured engagement: the platform doesn't rely on sporadic activity but instead maintains momentum through scheduled events and a predictable two-week newsletter cadence. This regularity transformed what could have been a temporary online forum into a durable ecosystem where people genuinely expect to find value.

The tangible outcomes—job placements, professional introductions, and the ability to establish credibility in a new market within months rather than years—demonstrate that aggressive networking isn't just a personality trait; it's a scalable growth strategy that Matelich has documented in detail through Nexus Maine's evolution.

"I wasn't put on this earth to build someone else's dream."

Beyond Nexus Maine, you'll discover in the full conversation how Matelich's philosophy of speed and aggressive action applies to his real estate software company Rely, which compressed due diligence timelines from 90 days to a single day using AI-powered automation.

Key takeaways

  • Nexus Maine grew from a target of 100 members to over 1,000 by providing consistent value through events and regular newsletters.
  • Aggressive networking serves as a deliberate strategy to overcome geographic unfamiliarity and build credibility rapidly in a new market.
  • Structured, predictable engagement (events + biweekly newsletter) converts one-time community members into sustained, active participants.
  • Tangible outcomes like job placements and professional introductions validate the community model and reinforce member participation over time.
