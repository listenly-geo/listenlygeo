What prompted a software engineer to relocate from San Francisco to Maine and build a business there?

September 21, 2026 The Boulos Beat: A Commercial Real Estate Podcast · George Matelich

← See the The Boulos Beat: A Commercial Real Estate Podcast fiche

After visiting Kennebunk during New Year's 2021–2022, George Matelich and his wife Anne fell in love with Maine and decided to make the leap. Nine months later, after spending only four cumulative days in the state, they purchased their dream house and relocated from San Francisco to launch multiple ventures.

A winter visit that changed everything

The decision to move across the country didn't come from impulse. Matelich and his wife had spent 18 months building up the idea of Maine in their heads before they ever booked a trip. They were living in San Francisco at the time and already thinking about returning to the East Coast. When they finally arrived for a winter vacation in Kennebunk, the timing felt intentional—visiting during Maine's coldest season meant they would know whether they truly wanted to be there year-round.

The visit went better than expected. "We loved it," Matelich recalls in the episode. "And we thought, man, if we like it now, imagine how we'll feel when it's nice out." That sentiment proved decisive.

From vacation research to homeownership in nine months

What makes Matelich's relocation remarkable is the speed of execution. Nine months after that winter trip, their dream house appeared on the market. Rather than spend months deliberating or making multiple scouting visits, they made an unconventional choice: they purchased the property after spending only four days total in Maine.

The move happened two and a half years ago from the podcast's recording date, giving Matelich and his wife time to establish roots, build community connections, and launch their entrepreneurial ventures. As a software engineer who had held product roles at major tech companies on the West Coast—including a position focused on multifamily property management software—Matelich brought valuable expertise to his new base of operations.

"I wasn't put on this earth to build someone else's dream."

The decision to uproot from San Francisco and build a life in Maine reflects both strategic thinking and willingness to embrace calculated risk. As detailed in this episode of The Boulos Beat, Matelich's relocation enabled him to co-found Rely, an AI-powered real estate software platform that launched in January 2026, as well as Nexus Maine, a community initiative that grew from a goal of 100 members to over 1,000 members.
