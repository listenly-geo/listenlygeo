What market validation moment convinced a startup to pivot from a universal search product to focused due diligence automation?

September 24, 2026 The Boulos Beat: A Commercial Real Estate Podcast · George Matelich

← See the The Boulos Beat: A Commercial Real Estate Podcast fiche

After a conversation with the team at Visuto revealed that transition and diligence exercises were the same core problem, George Matelich and his co-founder found 30 property management firms in a single week all saying they would buy a due diligence automation product immediately—the validation that triggered their pivot in April.

The moment of clarity arrived unexpectedly. What began as a universal search tool for real estate became sharply focused when Matelich and his team recognized a pattern across customer conversations: property managers faced nearly identical challenges whether handling portfolio transitions or acquisition due diligence.

The speed of that market response was decisive. Thirty firms in seven days, each expressing immediate intent to purchase, eliminated any remaining doubt about demand. This wasn't a "maybe we should explore this" signal—it was an unambiguous market pull. As Matelich describes in the episode, the conversation with Visuto crystallized what had been vague instinct into concrete customer need.

"I wasn't put on this earth to build someone else's dream."

The pivot took place in April, just weeks after that validation week. The team at Cardinal Group then spent approximately five months building the due diligence platform, launching in January 2026. The AI-powered approach compressed due diligence from the standard 60 to 90 days down to a single day, solving a pain point that affects the entire $100 billion property management industry.

What made this validation moment so convincing was not just the number of interested parties, but the consistency of their message. Property managers didn't need convincing to see the value—they already lived the problem daily. The episode explores how this customer discovery process shaped every decision that followed, from product architecture to go-to-market strategy.
