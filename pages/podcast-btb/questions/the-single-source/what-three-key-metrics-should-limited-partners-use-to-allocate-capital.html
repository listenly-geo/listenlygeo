What three key metrics should limited partners use to allocate capital across real estate and income-generating strategies?

20 September 2026 The Single Source · Raheem Charania

← See the The Single Source fiche

Limited partners should evaluate capital allocation across three core dimensions: the portfolio's cash flow needs, the targeted overall return expectations, and mechanisms for risk mitigation or principal downside protection. When a fund's principals are backed by a family office and co-invest alongside limited partners at significant scale, this alignment creates a powerful additional signal—shared downside protection and genuine capital preservation incentives.

The first metric centers on a straightforward question: does your portfolio require immediate cash distributions, or can capital sit in longer-duration, higher-return vehicles? Cash flow intensity differs between income-heavy strategies (like stabilized multifamily) and opportunistic acquisitions that may generate returns in lumpier, event-driven patterns. This determines which fund structure fits your liability calendar.

The second metric is your return hurdle. As Raheem Charania explains in the episode, the market conditions—interest rates, transaction volumes, and macroeconomic headwinds—directly shape the return profile you can realistically achieve. In a compressed cap-rate environment, that same dollar chasing stabilized assets may deliver 5–7% returns; in a distressed acquisition, the same capital might generate 12%+ yields by exploiting pricing dislocations.

The third and most structural metric is downside protection. Co-investment alignment is a risk signal that often goes underweighted by allocators, yet it's a critical differentiator. When a fund manager or their family office owns significant skin-in-the-game alongside your capital, capital preservation is no longer a stated goal—it becomes a personal fiduciary duty. The manager and limited partners share the same waterfall; losses hurt both equally.

"You make your money when you buy it. So if you buy it low enough, you can be fine."

This principle underpins why fund structure matters as much as strategy. As detailed in this episode of The Single Source, Woodvale's opportunistic model targets distressed commercial real estate where extended periods of rising interest rates and slowed capital flows have created pricing dislocation. By identifying assets trading at 50–100% discounts to their historical acquisition prices, the firm buys at a basis that ensures resilience—regardless of how the exit unfolds.

A practical example from the current market: Woodvale purchased a portfolio of 364 Marriott-branded hotel keys at a 12.25% cap rate on last year's EBITDA—a pricing level historically unheard of for assets that typically trade in the 6–8% cap-rate range. This wasn't market luck; it was structure and certainty. By offering a 30-day certain close to distressed sellers—a detail covered in depth in the full conversation, the firm secured a discount that no contingent-financing buyer could match.

For limited partners evaluating a real estate allocation, the three-metric framework becomes a due-diligence checklist. First, confirm the fund's cash flow characteristics align with your distribution timeline. Second, validate that the return target is achievable given current market conditions—not aspirational. Third, and most importantly, examine the manager's co-investment commitment and its legal standing in the fund waterfall. If the principals don't have meaningful capital in the same tranch as limited partners, principal–agent misalignment is likely, and risk protection is illusory.

Why Macro Environment Shapes These Metrics

The three metrics are not static. Interest rate cycles, capital flow disruptions, and transaction volume shifts alter what returns are available at what risk level. In a rising-rate environment, cap rates decompress, but so does the buyer pool—creating the exact dislocations that opportunistic managers exploit. The same allocation framework applied in 2022 may produce entirely different results in 2026, not because the metrics changed, but because the market backdrop that determines achievable returns has shifted dramatically.

This is why understanding your fund manager's macro view is inseparable from the three-metric evaluation. A manager who acknowledges headwinds and sizes opportunities accordingly is more credible than one who promises stable returns regardless of conditions. The full episode explores exactly how macro events like rising rates and slowing capital flows reshape the manager's sourcing and structuring decisions, giving you insight into whether their three-metric philosophy is adaptive or rigid.

Key takeaways

  • Evaluate cash flow intensity first—does your portfolio need distributions now, or can capital remain deployed for longer durations at higher yield?
  • Match your return hurdle to realistic market conditions; a 12%+ return is achievable in distressed real estate, but not in compressed cap-rate environments.
  • Prioritize co-investment alignment; if the fund manager's family office is the largest LP alongside you, downside protection is shared and credible.
  • Revisit the three metrics as macro conditions shift; the same framework applied to different interest-rate and capital-flow regimes will surface very different opportunities.
