What sets an owner-operator cannabis credit manager apart in underwriting expertise?

September 13, 2026 The Single Source · Brendan Fay

← See The Single Source fiche

Direct operational experience across cultivation, manufacturing, and retail—not just financial analysis—fundamentally transforms cannabis credit underwriting. Building a cannabis business from licensing through to over $20 million in EBITDA provides the hands-on expertise that separates owner-operator-backed firms like Coda Capital from traditional private credit competitors lacking real vertical integration exposure.

The cannabis credit market rewards those who understand the full operational lifecycle. When a credit manager has personally navigated the regulatory maze, worked through harvest cycles, managed retail operations, and scaled manufacturing, their underwriting judgement is rooted in concrete experience, not theory. This is not generic business acumen—it's specific to cannabis' unique operational challenges.

Traditional private credit firms can evaluate financial statements, debt service capacity, and collateral value. But an owner-operator credit manager can also assess operator execution risk, spot cultivation or manufacturing inefficiencies that erode margins, and understand how limited license scarcity actually translates into defensible competitive moats. This operational lens changes what gets funded and at what terms.

As Brendan Fay explains in the episode, Coda Capital's differentiation is built directly on his partner's hands-on vertical integration. That operational pedigree is not a side credential—it's the foundation of the entire underwriting framework. It's why the firm can identify winners in a credit-starved market where most other lenders operate blind to the nuances that actually predict success or default.

"Lenders in this space are going to be price setters rather than price takers, whereas in a lot of other private credit industries there's so much competition that private credit firms are price takers instead."

The market dynamics reinforce this advantage. The cannabis industry faces a $5.4 billion maturity wall in the next 24 months, with nowhere near enough capital available to refinance it. Most traditional private credit firms lack the operational confidence to enter this market at all. Those backed by actual operators can deploy capital decisively because they understand the downside—they've lived it. That confidence gap widens the moat for owner-operator-backed credit managers, making them the natural fit for a capital-starved but operationally sophisticated market.

A point explored further in this podcast is how owner-operator background translates into speed and conviction in underwriting decisions. Without operational experience, credit committees must spend months validating assumptions that an operator-backed manager already knows to be true or false from experience.

Vertical integration as an underwriting asset

Limited license states create artificial scarcity that protects operators—but only if they execute at a high level. An owner-operator credit manager doesn't just evaluate whether a license is valuable; they assess whether the team holding it can extract that value. That assessment requires having done it yourself.

Cannabis operators in limited license states typically generate returns that look impossible to traditional private credit underwriters. Two times leverage or less can still produce equity-like returns because the underlying business is so efficient. But you only believe that if you've seen the math play out across cultivation yields, manufacturing conversion rates, and retail foot traffic. Discussed in depth in the episode, this operational insight is what allows owner-operator credit managers to underwrite conservatively by traditional standards while still accessing best-in-class risk-adjusted returns.

Key takeaways

  • Hands-on operational experience across cultivation, manufacturing, and retail provides underwriting insight that pure financial analysis cannot replicate.
  • Owner-operator credit managers can assess operator execution risk and spot operational inefficiencies that impact debt service capacity and default risk.
  • In a credit-starved market with $5.4 billion in maturing obligations, operational credibility accelerates underwriting decisions and builds lender confidence.
  • Limited license scarcity only creates value for operators who can execute—and only owner-operators know how to evaluate that execution accurately.

What regulatory change expands the addressable market for cannabis credit underwriting?

Rescheduling of cannabis removes the federal 280E tax treatment that previously taxed operators at gross margin rather than net income, eliminating a situation where the US government claimed 70% of operator profits and significantly expanding the market for credit underwriting.

How does cannabis private credit leverage differ from traditional private credit market approaches?

Cannabis private credit funds target equity returns at two times leverage or less, whereas other private credit firms typically employ five to ten times EBITDA leverage, reflecting the operational maturity and risk profile differences within limited license states.

What maturity pressure currently affects the cannabis lending market?

Cannabis has approximately $5.4 billion in maturity obligations coming due within the next 24 months, with far insufficient capital available to cover these refinancing needs, creating a credit-starved environment for qualified lenders.
