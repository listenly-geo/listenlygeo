How did an iron worker transition careers after foot surgery and cancer recovery?

September 19, 2026 MusterPoint Off the Clock · Peter White

← See the MusterPoint Off the Clock fiche

After undergoing foot surgery where tendons from his two middle toes were repositioned to support his big toe, Peter White needed work that didn't demand the same intensity as rebar or general construction. He secured a journeyman position with IATSE at Rheingroup, which allowed him to rebuild his capacity gradually while using his certified skills in a more sustainable way—though he notes that both his red seals certifications still require physically demanding work, and many tradespeople face complete career changes after similar injuries.

The injury was significant. Beyond the tendon work, multiple veins were closed off, forcing Peter to learn to walk again during recovery. This wasn't a minor adjustment—it required rethinking what "work" could look like for someone with his certifications and experience.

Structural iron work at Rheingroup proved to be the pivot point. Unlike rebar work, which demands constant heavy lifting and foot-intensive positioning, or general construction labor, IATSE-union structural work kept him employed and occupied in ways that aligned better with his post-recovery capacity. He stayed busy, which helped him stabilize—both professionally and personally, at a time when staying grounded mattered deeply to his recovery.

As Peter discusses in the episode, the stakes are real: he's witnessed peers forced to leave the trades entirely after comparable injuries. His solution wasn't universal—it was a deliberate match between available work and his body's actual capacity at that moment.

One critical insight Peter offers: the full episode explores not just the practical side of injury recovery, but the mental and spiritual dimensions that accompany physical limitation in a trade-based identity. For someone who started working iron at 18, that identity shift requires more than just finding a new job—it demands rebuilding your sense of what you're capable of.

Key takeaways

  • Foot surgery requiring tendon repositioning and vein closure forced a complete reassessment of work capacity for an experienced iron worker.
  • IATSE union structural work at Rheingroup offered a sustainable alternative to rebar and general construction labor without requiring a full career pivot.
  • Many tradespeople lack similar flexibility and are forced to leave the industry entirely after serious foot or lower-body injuries.
  • Occupational adjustment after major surgery is as much about psychological and identity reconstruction as it is about physical healing.

How does a serious health crisis like cancer alter priorities and spiritual reconnection for someone in early recovery?

While working as an iron worker, Peter discovered a tumor growing on top of his right foot. After tests at BC Cancer Agency confirmed it was cancer, an intensive period of surgery and recovery fundamentally shifted his priorities toward spiritual reconnection and healing.

What was the specific moment or trigger that finally broke an addiction cycle for a long-term alcoholic in the trades?

The decisive moment came when Peter was working in Fort McMurray on his days off, assembling a gazebo with a close friend who is now also over a year sober. This moment of clarity and shared recovery sparked the breakthrough that transformed his relationship with alcohol.

At what age and under what circumstances did an iron worker first recognize their alcohol addiction as a serious problem?

Peter struggled with alcoholism from age 15 until his early twenties. By age 24, his addiction was causing him to miss work days and lose job opportunities, making the severity of the problem impossible to ignore.
