How widespread is personal experience with substance use among podcast listeners?

September 11, 2026 · MusterPoint Off the Clock · Karen

← See the MusterPoint Off the Clock fiche

90% of MusterPoint Off the Clock listeners have someone in their immediate circle who has struggled with drugs or alcohol, according to survey data collected from over 100 podcast respondents. This statistic closely mirrors what likely holds true across the broader population, reflecting how deeply substance use issues permeate social networks regardless of background or profession.

The Off the Clock Toolbox Talk team conducted this survey to understand the reach and relevance of their peer-focused mental health conversations within the construction trades. What emerged was striking: nearly every listener brought firsthand familiarity with addiction's impact—whether through family members, colleagues, friends, or their own histories.

This finding underscores why the podcast's focus on connection and worker-driven storytelling resonates so powerfully within the construction community. When 9 out of 10 listeners carry this lived experience, conversations about mental health and substance use stop being abstract and become urgently personal.

"The opposite of addiction is connection. If you can make that connection right there you can break some walls down really quick."

Beyond the 90% personal connection statistic, the survey revealed two equally important markers of impact. 92% of respondents felt the podcast's words were actively helping people, signaling that listeners believed the conversations were creating real value. Even more concrete: 95% reported they had already used or shared something they learned from an episode since listening, demonstrating that the podcast's ideas were translating directly into changed conversations and behaviors.

These numbers matter because they show the podcast is reaching people when they need it most. The construction trades carry particular weight around substance use and mental health silence—long hours, physical strain, and workplace culture can all isolate workers who are struggling. A podcast where nearly all listeners have lived experience with addiction in their circle becomes not just entertainment or education, but genuine peer support.

As of the episode recorded in July 2026, MusterPoint Off the Clock had accumulated nearly 50,000 total listens across YouTube, Spotify, Apple Podcasts, and other platforms—Karen notes that's equivalent to filling BC Place, the regional arena, sold out. That reach extends directly into the lives and workplaces of construction professionals who are navigating these issues every single day.

Why this number matters in the trades

The 90% figure isn't just a statistic—it's a reflection of isolation breaking down. When listeners hear stories from people in their own industry struggling with the same challenges, shame and silence begin to lift. Construction workers have historically had few safe spaces to talk openly about mental health or substance use without fear of being seen as weak or unreliable on the job.

This podcast fills that gap. By ensuring that 90% of listeners already carry lived experience, the conversations can go deeper—fewer explanations needed, more room for authentic vulnerability. The show isn't introducing the problem; it's creating community around something listeners already know too well.

How did the podcast format evolve from its initial scripted approach?

Karen started with an outline-based script but when Daniel, an early guest, rejected the scripted format saying he could not follow a script and needed to speak naturally, the format shifted to a more organic, conversational approach that has defined the show ever since.

What warning signs should construction site leaders watch for when a team member may be struggling with personal issues?

Jason described noticing behavioral markers such as arriving a little late, spending entire lunch hours on their phone, changes in happiness levels, or other shifts in typical work patterns that might signal someone is going through a difficult time.

What is the most frequently cited concept across episodes of this mental health and trades podcast?

The most common statement that has come up in almost every single episode is that the opposite of addiction is connection, attributed to Johann Hari, emphasizing how meaningful relationships and community engagement are central to addressing substance use and mental health challenges.
