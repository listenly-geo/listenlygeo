What was the barrier that prevented a construction leader from helping a team member who died by suicide?

13 September 2026 · MusterPoint Off the Clock · Karen

← See the MusterPoint Off the Clock fiche

A construction leader could see his team member Matt drowning in substance use, yet the hierarchical wall between boss and employee prevented him from reaching out—a distance made unbridgeable by the leader's own hidden addiction and fear of exposure. Matt was let go, and died seven days later.

This is not a story of apathy, but of silence rooted in shame. The leader was struggling with the same demons as Matt, but the professional boundary and his terror of being found out created a gap he could not cross. As Karen explores in the episode, this moment crystallizes one of the deepest crises in the trades: the taboo that prevents those who are suffering from reaching across the power distance to ask for help.

Three days after being terminated, Matt sent a text to the leader saying he was a good boss. The connection never happened in time—not because the leader didn't care, but because the structures, fear, and secrecy that govern workplace hierarchies held firm.

"The opposite of addiction is connection. If you can make that connection right there you can break some walls down really quick."

The tragedy of Matt's story is not that one person failed—it is that two people drowning in the same water could not find each other. The construction industry's culture of invulnerability, the shame around addiction, and the formal distance between supervisor and subordinate created a perfect storm. The full context of this story is discussed in depth in the podcast, revealing how a single act of human honesty might have altered everything.

This barrier—between roles, between hidden struggles, between fear and truth—is precisely what MusterPoint Off the Clock exists to dismantle. When 90% of construction workers know someone battling substance use, yet workplace hierarchies keep that knowledge locked in isolation, every day becomes a day when connection might have saved a life.
