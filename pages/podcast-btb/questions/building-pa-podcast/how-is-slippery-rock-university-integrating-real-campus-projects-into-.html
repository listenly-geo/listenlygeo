How is Slippery Rock University integrating real campus projects into the construction management curriculum?

September 17, 2026 Building PA Podcast · Amanda McIsaac

← See the Building PA Podcast fiche

Slippery Rock University partners with its facilities team on actual university construction projects, giving students real blueprints, site walks, and hands-on experience watching amendments and bids unfold—freshmen propose work at the Storm Harbor equestrian facility while junior-seniors tackle projects like Morrow Fieldhouse. This embedded approach transforms the classroom into a live construction laboratory.

From Proposal to Bid: Students Walk Real Construction Timelines

The program's core strategy is embedding students directly into university capital projects that are actually being executed. Students receive authentic construction documents—not textbook approximations—and participate in site walks where they observe the physical space and ask real questions.

As Amanda McIsaac explains in the episode, the curriculum is structured so that students at different levels engage with projects suited to their stage. Freshmen compile proposals for the equestrian facility work, learning how initial planning documents are assembled and justified. Junior and senior students handle more complex assignments on facilities like Morrow Fieldhouse, where they see the full arc from blueprints through bid calculations.

The visibility into how amendments are submitted and pricing is determined gives students a concrete understanding of construction economics they cannot gain from a textbook. They witness how costs shift, how scope gets negotiated, and how professional bids are actually structured in a university environment.

Partnership with the Facilities Team: Industry Mentorship Built In

The program works directly with Juan Perez, head of facilities at Slippery Rock University, who serves as a real-world mentor and project source. This partnership ensures that the projects students handle are not simulations but live work being managed by the university's own capital team.

A detail worth exploring further: the program has also built an advisory board of 12 industry professionals—eight from Western Pennsylvania alone—who provide guidance on curriculum development and offer their own project insights, creating a network that bridges classroom and jobsite in ways few programs achieve.

"Construction is relationships and while we have all of the technology to make our lives easier, when it comes down to it, it is relationship-based."

This hands-on immersion model addresses a fundamental gap in traditional construction education: the jump from theory to practice. Students graduate with portfolio pieces they can point to, having already seen how professional construction actually moves forward at a university scale.

How does integrating OSHA 40-hour certification into the junior year benefit construction management students?

Students earn their 40-hour OSHA certification during their junior year, allowing them to go directly onto job sites with boots on the ground on day one.

According to feedback from the first board meeting in March, communication was identified almost unanimously as a major gap among new graduates.
