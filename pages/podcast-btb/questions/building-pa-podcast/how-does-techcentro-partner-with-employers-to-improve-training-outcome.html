How does TechCentro partner with employers to improve training outcomes?

25 September 2026 Building PA Podcast · Robert Torres

← See the Building PA Podcast fiche

TechCentro invites employers to actively shape training through mock interviews, soft skills presentations, and lunch-and-learn sessions. Partner organizations like UPMC and Penn State Health emphasize that technical certification is assumed; employers prioritize soft skills—teamwork, communication, critical thinking—as the real differentiator in hiring.

The partnership model recognizes a fundamental gap in traditional training: employers don't just want technically competent workers, they need people who can collaborate, solve problems under pressure, and communicate across teams. As Robert Torres explains in the episode, this insight came directly from healthcare employers who already work with TechCentro in allied health programs.

Building feedback loops through direct employer engagement

TechCentro's employer partnership strategy goes beyond one-off recruitment events. The center invites organizations to become embedded partners who help shape curriculum and provide real-time placement feedback. Mock interviews conducted by actual hiring managers give students authentic practice in the exact format they'll face on the job. These aren't generic exercises—they reflect real industry standards and expectations from employers who have a stake in the outcome.

Soft skills presentations run by UPMC and Penn State Health professionals bring direct workplace context into the classroom. Students learn not just what teamwork looks like, but what it looks like in a healthcare setting, where communication gaps can have serious consequences. This employer perspective is invaluable as detailed in this podcast, and it translates directly into better job readiness.

For the construction trades, Torres is preparing to model the exact same approach. He plans to build a network of construction and facilities companies—similar to how the partnership strategy is working in healthcare—to provide curriculum feedback and create direct pathways to employment. Lunch-and-learn sessions will bring tradespeople into the classroom to discuss soft skills specific to construction: safety awareness, problem-solving under time pressure, and clear communication on job sites.

"I want people to understand how to problem solve, the critical thinking behind problem solving. That to me would be someone attractive to hire."

What makes this partnership structure effective is accountability. Employers aren't just vetting graduates—they're co-creators of the training itself. Discussed extensively in this conversation, this two-way feedback loop means TechCentro can adjust curriculum in real time based on what employers actually need, rather than guessing or relying on outdated frameworks.

Key takeaways

  • Employers conduct mock interviews and give soft skills presentations directly within TechCentro programs, providing authentic workplace practice.
  • Healthcare partners like UPMC and Penn State Health emphasize that employers assume technical skills post-certification and instead prioritize teamwork, communication, and critical thinking.
  • Lunch-and-learn sessions bring industry professionals into the classroom to share workplace context and expectations specific to their field.
  • TechCentro is scaling this employer partnership model from healthcare into construction trades, building feedback networks to shape curriculum and create placement pathways.

What is the application and vetting process for TechCentro students?

Prospective students fill out an interest form on the website, then TechCentro conducts interviews to assess commitment to the program and determine placement in the right curriculum track.

Who is eligible to enroll in TechCentro's skilled trades programs?

The programs are intended for adults who have graduated from high school. High school graduates interested in exploring trades are also welcome, though the focus is on post-secondary learners ready to enter the workforce.

What are the main challenges TechCentro faces in launching its construction trades programs?

The primary challenge has been recruiting qualified instructors willing to teach part-time or full-time. Torres is actively working to address this gap to ensure construction trades classes can launch with experienced professionals leading training.
