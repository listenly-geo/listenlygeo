How does Marcus Vega give back to the construction industry through mentorship?

September 1, 2026 Building PA Podcast · Marcus Vega

← See the Building PA Podcast fiche

Marcus Vega mentors young apprentices and workers directly on job sites, teaching them practical skills like laser use, blueprint reading, and proper tool handling. He holds regular Friday training sessions and helps guide workers toward better career paths, while serving as a positive role model and leader on every project he oversees.

Hands-on training every Friday, built into the work itself

On a two-year project in Hershey, Pennsylvania, Marcus worked with multiple apprentices and structured consistent training into the job. Every Friday, he dedicated time after work hours to teach practical, job-specific skills that would otherwise take years to absorb through experience alone.

His training covers the technical foundation young workers actually need — how to use laser levels on site, read and interpret construction drawings accurately, and handle power tools and equipment safely. As Marcus explains in the Building PA Podcast episode, this isn't abstract classroom theory; it's the exact knowledge they'll apply the moment they step back on the job.

Beyond technical skills — building career pathways

Marcus's mentorship goes deeper than tool training. He actively helps young workers identify and pursue better career paths within the construction trades, connecting them to opportunities that match their strengths and ambitions.

By serving as a visible, positive role model and leader on job sites, Marcus demonstrates daily that professionalism and dedication in construction lead somewhere. His presence matters — apprentices see firsthand how leadership and knowledge translate into real responsibility and respect in the industry.

"I want to be a better person tomorrow than I am today, and each and every day I want to exemplify that at the job."

How did Marcus Vega secure his position at Alexander, Boots Family Companies?

The day after the KCA celebration in 2025, Marcus networked with Jeff Smith from Alexander while attending the event as invited by his former employer, which led directly to his hiring as a General Foreman.

What was Marcus Vega's career progression in the construction industry?

Marcus started with basement waterproofing, then moved to drywall and framing work after joining the carpenters union, building expertise over close to 35 years before stepping into general foreman and construction manager roles.

Key takeaways

  • Marcus mentors apprentices on job sites with structured Friday training sessions covering lasers, blueprints, and tool safety.
  • He actively guides young workers toward better career paths within the construction trades, not just technical skills.
  • His mentorship approach combines hands-on knowledge transfer with visible role modeling of professionalism and leadership.
  • This community service work earned him the 2026 KCA Labor Champion award, the Thomas George Memorial Community Service Award.
