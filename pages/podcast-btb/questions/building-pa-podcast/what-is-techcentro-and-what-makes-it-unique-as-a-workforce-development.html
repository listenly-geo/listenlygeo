What makes TechCentro unique as a workforce development program?

September 19, 2026 Building PA Podcast · Robert Torres

← See the Building PA Podcast fiche

TechCentro is a bilingual workforce development center combining adult basic education, employment services, and occupational training in allied health and skilled trades. It uniquely emphasizes workplace English rather than conversational English, helping trainees develop the communication skills needed to advance to supervisory positions, while partnering with community organizations to address barriers like transportation and childcare.

The program operates with a specific focus on practical skills development. As explained in the episode with Robert Torres, TechCentro in Harrisburg is the sixth location modeled after Lancaster's successful centers, which have been operating for more than 10 years. This proven model ensures trainees receive structured, evidence-based training rather than experimental approaches.

TechCentro's commitment to removing barriers sets it apart from traditional workforce programs. The center partners with community organizations, including United Way of the Capital Region and other local agencies, to address the real obstacles preventing people from participating in training—whether transportation, childcare, or housing instability. These partnerships aren't peripheral; they're central to the program's design.

The bilingual delivery is essential in Harrisburg, where the center has already served people from over 20 different countries since opening just over a year ago. This isn't tokenism—it's recognition that workforce development cannot exclude non-English speakers if the goal is genuine economic mobility.

Workplace English vs. Conversational English

TechCentro focuses on "workplace English"—the specific language, vocabulary, and communication patterns required in professional settings and supervisory roles. This differs from conversational English, which emphasizes everyday social communication. Workplace English training directly prepares trainees to communicate with coworkers, understand instructions, write reports, and eventually lead teams.

Skilled trades and allied health careers form the occupational core. The program offers training in fields with genuine demand and career pathways, not just entry-level jobs. The allied health track connects to employers like UPMC and Penn State Health, while the skilled trades program is preparing to launch its first construction trades classes in facilities maintenance, running 8 to 12 weeks, Monday through Friday—intensive, structured schedules designed for working adults.

"I want people to understand how to problem solve, the critical thinking behind problem solving. That to me would be someone attractive to hire."

This philosophy—emphasizing problem-solving and critical thinking—is central to what differentiates TechCentro from workforce programs that treat training as a series of certifications to check off. The program recognizes that employers value adaptive, thinking workers who can handle workplace complexity, not just people who can perform a single task.

The Harrisburg location addresses a particular regional need: the city's poverty rate is significantly higher than the state average, and many residents lack pathways to stable, well-paying employment. TechCentro's presence in Midtown Harrisburg is a direct intervention in that gap, serving adults who need both education and a bridge to jobs—not just classroom time.

Partnerships and practical outcomes

TechCentro's relationships with employers, education providers like Harrisburg Area Community College and Thaddeus Stevens, and community support organizations create a pipeline rather than isolated training. Average class sizes of about 10 students per session allow for individualized attention, and the program tracks outcomes—measuring not just completion but job placement and advancement.

The assessment phase itself reflects the program's practicality: math and reading evaluations take just 15 to 30 minutes, avoiding unnecessary administrative burden while still ensuring proper placement. This efficiency signals a program designed for busy, working-age adults, not traditional students with unlimited flexibility.

Key takeaways

  • TechCentro combines adult education, employment services, and occupational training across allied health and skilled trades in a bilingual format.
  • Workplace English training, not conversational English, prepares trainees for supervisory advancement and professional communication.
  • Partnerships with community organizations remove real barriers—transportation, childcare, housing—that prevent people from accessing training.
  • The program is built on a proven Lancaster model with over 10 years of demonstrated success, ensuring evidence-based practices.
  • Emphasis on problem-solving and critical thinking reflects what employers actually value beyond technical certifications alone.
