How does Marcus Vega define fair leadership in construction?

September 3, 2026 Building PA Podcast · Marcus Vega

← See the Building PA Podcast fiche

Marcus Vega believes in being fair but firm—much like boxing referee Joe Cortez—using simple gestures of respect to lead without creating distance. He maintains respect by referring to people as Mr. and Miss, saying thank you, greeting people with good morning, and focusing on being a strong role model rather than adopting the distant or rude approach he's seen from some construction managers.

The foundation: respect through consistency

For Vega, leadership in construction hinges on daily consistency in how you treat people. These small gestures—a formal address, a genuine thank you, a morning greeting—are not superficial niceties but deliberate tools that help him extract more from the people he works with. He refuses to let his position change his character, understanding that construction work demands trust and collaboration on job sites where safety and quality depend on team cohesion.

As detailed in this episode of the Building PA Podcast, Vega's approach stands in sharp contrast to management styles he's witnessed over his close to 35 years in the industry, where authority is exercised through distance or intimidation rather than earned respect.

Long-term vision: aspiration and personal growth

Beyond his current role as General Foreman at Alexander, Boots Family Companies, Marcus aspires to become a superintendent, seeing it as the next step in his career progression. However, his ambition is inseparable from his commitment to self-improvement: he wants to be a better person tomorrow than he is today, and he measures his leadership daily by how consistently he exemplifies that value on the job.

This philosophy directly connects to his work mentoring apprentices and young people in the trades, a contribution that earned him the 2026 KCA Labor Champion award. The way he leads is the same way he teaches—through consistent personal example, as he shares in the podcast.

"I want to be a better person tomorrow than I am today, and each and every day I want to exemplify that at the job."

Marcus mentors young people and apprentices on job sites, offering his knowledge after work hours. On a two-year project in Hershey with many apprentices, he leads Friday training sessions.

Key takeaways

  • Effective construction leadership rests on formal respect and consistency—using titles, saying thank you, greeting people daily—rather than authority or distance.
  • Marcus Vega models the behavior he expects, refusing to let a management position change his character or turn him rude or distant like some peers in construction.
  • His next career goal is to become a superintendent, but his definition of progress is tied to becoming a better person and role model every single day.
  • Over close to 35 years in construction, Vega has built his credibility by mentoring young people and apprentices, a practice that continues on every project he manages.
