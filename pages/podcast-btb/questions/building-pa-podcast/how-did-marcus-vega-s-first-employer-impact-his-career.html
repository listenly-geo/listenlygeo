How did Marcus Vega's first employer impact his career?

September 4, 2026 Building PA Podcast · Marcus Vega

← See the Building PA Podcast fiche

When Mr. Strawbridge hired Marcus Vega at Strawbridge Enterprises, he gave him far more than a job—he gave him a complete starter set of professional tools and the belief that he could build a lasting career in construction. That investment included a tool bag, T-square, utility knife, and meat saw, all presented as a gift after Strawbridge recognized Marcus's work ethic and potential.

The impact of that first employer went beyond the physical tools. Strawbridge's words and actions planted a seed that would grow over nearly 35 years. More than three decades later, Marcus Vega still possesses some of those original instruments—the T-square and utility knife remain with him, tangible reminders of a mentor who believed in him when he was just starting out.

That initial relationship defined how Marcus approached his entire career, particularly when it came to the people he would later lead. As discussed in the Building PA Podcast episode, Marcus carries forward the same spirit of investment and mentorship that Strawbridge showed him—identifying talent early, providing resources, and communicating faith in someone's potential.

"I want to be a better person tomorrow than I am today, and each and every day I want to exemplify that at the job."

What are Marcus Vega's leadership principles and career aspirations?

Marcus aspires to become a superintendent and believes in being a fair but firm leader, similar to boxing referee Joe Cortez. He maintains respect by leading with integrity and fairness on the job site.

What is the significance of the Thomas George Memorial Community Service Award that Marcus Vega won?

The award was created close to 10 years ago by the KCA board to recognize labor champions in the construction industry, named after Thomas George, a respected figure in the Pennsylvania construction community.

What does Marcus Vega consider his community service contribution in the construction industry?

Marcus mentors young people and apprentices on job sites, offering his knowledge after work hours. On a two-year project in Hershey with many apprentices, he led Friday training sessions to develop the next generation of construction workers.

Key takeaways

  • A first employer's investment in tools and belief shaped Marcus Vega's entire 35-year construction career.
  • Mr. Strawbridge recognized work ethic and potential early, communicating clearly that Marcus could build a lasting professional path.
  • Marcus still keeps original tools from that initial gift, symbols of mentorship that guide his approach to leading others.
