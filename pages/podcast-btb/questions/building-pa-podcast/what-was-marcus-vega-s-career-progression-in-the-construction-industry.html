How did Marcus Vega build his 35-year construction career from basement waterproofing to General Foreman?

August 30, 2026 Building PA Podcast · Marcus Vega

← See the Building PA Podcast fiche

Marcus Vega started his construction career nearly 35 years ago with basement waterproofing, then progressed to drywall and framing work after joining the carpenters union. He eventually transitioned into construction management, and now serves as General Foreman at Alexander, Boots Family Companies.

From Waterproofing to Framing: The Early Trade Years

Vega's entry into construction came through basement waterproofing work, a common entry point for tradespeople building foundational skills. This hands-on experience in a specialized trade taught him problem-solving and precision work on residential and commercial projects.

His transition to drywall and framing marked a significant shift in his skill set. His first notable drywall job came with Strawbridge Enterprises on the remodel of Sylvan Heights Building, a former girls' home in Harrisburg, Pennsylvania. This project gave him visibility in the local construction community and helped establish his reputation as a reliable tradesperson.

Union Membership and the Step Into Management

Joining the carpenters union was a turning point that formalized his apprenticeship and opened pathways to leadership roles. Through union training and mentorship, Vega developed the technical knowledge and professional standards that prepared him for supervisory work.

Over time, Vega moved from site-level trade work into construction management responsibilities. He was hired at Alexander, Boots Family Companies slightly over a year ago as a General Foreman, a role he secured through networking at a Keystone Contractors Association (KCA) celebration in 2025, where he connected with leadership. As described in the Building PA Podcast episode, this transition reflects not just technical growth, but the relationship-building that drives career advancement in the Pennsylvania construction industry.

"I want to be a better person tomorrow than I am today, and each and every day I want to exemplify that at the job."

Beyond his own ascent, Vega has become known for bringing others up the same pathway. As the podcast reveals, he currently mentors apprentices on a major Hershey project with structured Friday training sessions, embedding his philosophy of daily improvement into the next generation of Pennsylvania construction workers.

Key takeaways

  • Vega's career began with basement waterproofing and evolved through drywall and framing work before he joined the carpenters union.
  • His early reputation was built on a high-visibility remodel project at Sylvan Heights Building in Harrisburg, establishing him as a reliable tradesperson.
  • Union membership formalized his training and created a path from hands-on work into construction management and supervisory roles.
  • Networking within the KCA community led directly to his current role as General Foreman, demonstrating how relationships accelerate career advancement in construction.
