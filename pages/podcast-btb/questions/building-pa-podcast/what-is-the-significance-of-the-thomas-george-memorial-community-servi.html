What is the significance of the Thomas George Memorial Community Service Award that Marcus Vega won?

September 2, 2026 Building PA Podcast · Marcus Vega

← See the Building PA Podcast fiche

The Thomas George Memorial Community Service Award recognizes labor champions in the construction industry. Marcus Vega became the first recipient in the award's 10-year history to be nominated by two different KCA employers—a remarkable distinction that reflects his impact across multiple organizations.

A decade-old honor tied to construction industry leadership

The Keystone Contractors Association (KCA) board created the award approximately 10 years ago to honor individuals who exemplify the values of labor advocacy and community service. The award carries the name of Thomas George, the executive director of the KCA during the seventies and eighties, who was known as a close ally to labor leaders and a champion of fair labor practices.

This award is not given lightly. As discussed in the Building PA Podcast episode, it represents the construction industry's recognition of someone who has genuinely contributed to the wellbeing and development of workers and the broader labor movement.

First dual-nominated recipient in a decade

Marcus Vega stands alone as the only recipient to receive nominations from two separate KCA employers. This unprecedented dual nomination speaks to the breadth of his influence and respect within the industry—both his previous employer and his current employer at Alexander, Boots Family Companies felt compelled to nominate him for the same honor.

Such a distinction underscores that his contributions extend beyond a single job site or company. The willingness of two employers to independently advocate for him demonstrates consistency in his character and work ethic across different professional relationships over his nearly 35 years in construction.

"I want to be a better person tomorrow than I am today, and each and every day I want to exemplify that at the job."

The award ceremony itself was part of the annual construction celebration, where Marcus was honored on the Building PA Podcast live event. His mentorship work on job sites—particularly a two-year project in Hershey where he conducts Friday training sessions with multiple apprentices—illustrates the kind of hands-on labor advocacy that the Thomas George award was designed to celebrate.

Key takeaways

  • The Thomas George Memorial Community Service Award was established by the KCA board approximately 10 years ago to recognize labor champions in construction.
  • Marcus Vega is the first and only recipient to date who was nominated by two different KCA employers, a distinction reflecting his reputation across multiple organizations.
  • The award honors individuals like Thomas George who championed fair labor practices and supported labor leaders throughout their career.
  • Vega's recognition reflects both his direct mentorship of apprentices and his broader influence as a steadying force for labor advocacy in the construction industry.

What does Marcus Vega consider his community service contribution in the construction industry?

Marcus mentors young people and apprentices on job sites, offering his knowledge after work hours. On a two-year project in Hershey with many apprentices, he conducts Friday training sessions to support their development in the trades.

How did Marcus Vega secure his position at Alexander, Boots Family Companies?

The day after the KCA celebration in 2025, Marcus networked with Jeff Smith from Alexander while attending the event as invited by his former employer.

What was Marcus Vega's career progression in the construction industry?

Marcus started with basement waterproofing, then moved to drywall and framing work after joining the carpenters union, building nearly 35 years of industry expertise.
