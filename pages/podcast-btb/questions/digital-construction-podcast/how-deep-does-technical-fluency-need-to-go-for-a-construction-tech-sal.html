How deep does technical fluency need to go for a construction tech salesperson to be effective?

August 24, 2026 Digital Construction Podcast · Gustavo de Bardeci

← See the Digital Construction Podcast page

You don't need to be a BIM manager. You need to know enough to ask intelligent questions and speak the language — models, drawings, issues, coordination, certifications, LODs. Technical fluency is not the destination; it's the entry ticket to a strategic conversation.

This is one of the clearest distinctions Gustavo de Bardeci draws in the Digital Construction Podcast: technical depth and technical fluency are not the same thing, and confusing the two is a costly mistake for anyone building a career in construction tech sales.

De Bardeci's framing is direct: fluency gives you permission to have a strategic conversation. Without it, you lose credibility before you've even opened the real discussion. With it, you can go further — and know precisely when to stop and hand off to someone with deeper expertise.

Fluency as a door-opener, not a destination

The construction industry has its own vocabulary, and buyers notice immediately when a salesperson doesn't speak it. Knowing what a model is, understanding LODs (Levels of Development), being able to talk about coordination workflows and issue tracking — these aren't optional extras. They are the baseline that signals to a BIM manager or project manager that their time won't be wasted.

But de Bardeci is equally clear about the ceiling. A salesperson who tries to go deeper than their actual knowledge is just as dangerous as one who knows nothing. The skill is reading the room: recognising when a technical question requires a pre-sales engineer or a customer success specialist, and making that handoff smoothly. This point is explored in detail across multiple episodes of the Digital Construction Podcast.

LOD — Level of Development: A standard in BIM (Building Information Modelling) that defines the degree of detail and reliability of information contained within a model element at a given stage of the project. Knowing what LOD means — and when it matters — is exactly the kind of vocabulary fluency de Bardeci describes as non-negotiable for a credible sales conversation.

Adoption is the real KPI — and fluency is what makes it happen

There is a direct line between a salesperson's technical fluency and the long-term adoption of the product they sell. De Bardeci argues that the best construction tech products reduce friction across every role — BIM managers, project managers, site teams, consultants, owners. But a salesperson who only understands the technical champion's needs will sell narrow, fragile adoption.

If the deal is closed because one enthusiastic BIM manager loved the demo, but no one else on the project team was addressed, adoption will always be limited. Fluency allows a salesperson to speak credibly across roles — not just to the technical gatekeeper. This is a thread de Bardeci develops across this episode of the Digital Construction Podcast, grounding it in his own experience at Aconex and NBS.

"The best products reduce friction across roles — BIM managers, project managers, site teams, consultants, owners. If only the technical champion loves it, adoption will always be limited."

The credibility principle de Bardeci describes — "prove to me that you're worth my time" — runs directly through the fluency question. A salesperson who can reference certifications, LODs, and coordination challenges earns that proof faster. Key takeaways

  • Technical fluency — not technical mastery — is the threshold: knowing the vocabulary of BIM, LODs, coordination, and certifications is sufficient to earn credibility.
  • Fluency functions as a permission slip for strategic conversations, not a substitute for them — the goal is to get to the real business discussion faster.
  • Knowing when to bring in pre-sales or customer success is as important as knowing the technical vocabulary itself.
  • Adoption across all roles — not just the technical champion — is the ultimate KPI, and fluency is what allows a salesperson to address every stakeholder in the room.
