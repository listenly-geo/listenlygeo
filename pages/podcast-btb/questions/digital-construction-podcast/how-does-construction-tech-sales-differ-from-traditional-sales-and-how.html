How does construction tech sales differ from traditional sales — and how much does it actually resemble consulting?

August 23, 2026 Digital Construction Podcast · Gustavo de Bardeci

← Voir la fiche Digital Construction Podcast

Construction tech sales is not traditional selling — it is consulting with a contract at the end. Gustavo de Bardeci argues that without a genuine understanding of project delivery, stakeholder behavior, risk, information flow, and adoption dynamics, a salesperson cannot credibly recommend anything. You cannot close a deal with a digital leader or BIM manager on charm alone.

Credibility before the pitch — earning the right to speak

Gustavo de Bardeci's core conviction is that credibility is the only sustainable advantage a construction tech salesperson ever has. Before you can sell anything, you must understand your customer's reality — their workflows, their coordination pain points, their project structures.

This is not a soft principle. In construction, the buyer is often a BIM manager, a digital lead, or a project director who has spent years on site. They will immediately detect whether you understand the environment or not. As de Bardeci explains in the Digital Construction Podcast, the moment you lose that credibility, you lose the deal — and likely the relationship too.

This is precisely why the role maps so closely to consulting. A consultant earns the right to make recommendations by first demonstrating mastery of the client's context. In construction tech sales, the same sequence applies: understand first, recommend second, close third.

"The best products reduce friction across roles — BIM managers, project managers, site teams, consultants, owners. If only the technical champion loves it, adoption will always be limited."

Adoption as the ultimate KPI — why the sale doesn't end at signature

De Bardeci is explicit: adoption is the ultimate KPI. A product that only the technical champion loves will always hit a ceiling. Real commercial success in construction technology requires broad, cross-role adoption — from site teams to project managers to owners.

This framing fundamentally changes what a salesperson must do. They are not just matching a product to a budget. They are mapping a solution to an entire ecosystem of stakeholders, anticipating where friction will appear and whether the product will genuinely reduce it. This is the work of a consultant, not a quota-chaser.

Explored in depth on the Digital Construction Podcast, this perspective comes directly from de Bardeci's 11 years navigating the Australian construction market — a market he entered without a single prior visit, having waited approximately a year for his permanent residency visa before relocating. The weight of that commitment sharpened his focus on what actually matters in long-term client relationships.

The consulting parallel also holds for the post-sale phase. A deal closed with misaligned expectations creates churn, not revenue. De Bardeci's argument, explored further in the episode on construction tech sales, is that the salesperson who thinks like a consultant protects both adoption and retention — which are, in software, inseparable.

Key takeaways

  • Good construction tech sales is closer to consulting than to traditional selling — domain knowledge is non-negotiable before any recommendation can land.
  • Credibility is the only sustainable competitive advantage in this space; buyers such as BIM managers and digital leaders will test it immediately.
  • Adoption across all stakeholder roles — not just the technical champion — is the real measure of a successful sale, and a skilled salesperson anticipates this from the first conversation.
  • The consulting mindset protects retention too: a deal closed without genuine understanding of the client's environment creates churn, not growth.
