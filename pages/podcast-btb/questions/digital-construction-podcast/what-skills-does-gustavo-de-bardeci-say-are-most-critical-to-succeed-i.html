What skills does Gustavo de Bardeci say are most critical to succeed in construction tech sales?

August 20, 2026 Digital Construction Podcast · Gustavo de Bardeci

← Voir la fiche Digital Construction Podcast

Gustavo de Bardeci names four core skills: curiosity, resilience, relationship-building, and the ability to navigate multiple stakeholder levels. But he adds a harder requirement — you must actively unlearn the belief that product knowledge alone is enough. What actually drives success is understanding the customer's processes, project context, internal politics, and the history of past implementation failures.

Knowing the product is not the job — knowing the customer's world is

De Bardeci is direct: technical fluency matters, but it is not the differentiator. Too many construction tech salespeople arrive armed with feature lists and demo scripts, and walk away without a deal. The reason, in his view, is that they never understood what they were walking into.

Construction projects are layered environments. BIM managers, project managers, site teams, consultants, and owners all have different priorities — and different reasons to resist new software. A salesperson who can only speak to a single technical champion will always hit an adoption ceiling. Winning adoption across roles requires understanding each stakeholder's world, not just their job title.

This is where the Digital Construction Podcast goes further than most industry conversations — it surfaces what sales training rarely teaches: the political and historical context of each account matters as much as the product itself.

Adoption as the ultimate KPI — and why most sales processes miss it

De Bardeci frames adoption as the single metric that reveals whether a sale was real or just a contract. In software, and especially in construction tech, a signed deal that never gets used is a failure for everyone involved. The product, the vendor, and the customer all lose.

This means resilience and curiosity are not soft skills — they are operational requirements. Resilience keeps a salesperson engaged through long enterprise cycles and complex multi-stakeholder accounts. Curiosity is what pushes them to ask the harder questions: what failed before this, why, and who carries the scar tissue from that failure.

"The best products reduce friction across roles — BIM managers, project managers, site teams, consultants, owners. If only the technical champion loves it, adoption will always be limited."

Key takeaways

  • The four core skills de Bardeci identifies are curiosity, resilience, relationship-building, and multi-stakeholder navigation — not product expertise alone.
  • Unlearning is as important as learning: the assumption that knowing the product well is enough is the most common trap in construction tech sales.
  • Adoption — not the signed contract — is the real KPI, and it requires understanding the customer's project context, politics, and history of past failures.
  • A product embraced only by the technical champion will always have limited uptake; real adoption spans BIM managers, site teams, consultants, and owners alike.
