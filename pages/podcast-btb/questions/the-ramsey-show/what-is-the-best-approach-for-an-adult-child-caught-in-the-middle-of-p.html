How should adult children handle being caught in the middle of parents' divorce and financial conflict?

September 22, 2026 The Ramsey Show

← See The Ramsey Show fiche

Remove yourself from the middle of the conflict by refusing to take sides or mediate, express your love and support to both parents independently, and shift your focus entirely to your own emotional processing rather than trying to manage their decisions. Your parents are responsible adults capable of making their own choices, even if you disagree with them.

The instinct to fix your parents' problems often comes from a place of love and concern, but it places an impossible burden on you. When you position yourself as the mediator or the voice of reason, you unconsciously take ownership of their divorce, their financial disputes, and their life choices—responsibilities that were never yours to carry.

The core shift required is accepting that their separation is their journey to navigate, not a problem you can solve. This doesn't mean you're cold or uncaring; it means you're being clear about the boundary between their adult responsibilities and your role as a child, regardless of your age.

Removing yourself from the middle

Practically speaking, this means avoiding conversations where one parent asks you to relay messages, take sides, or validate their grievances against the other. When these moments arise—and they will—you can kindly but firmly say: "I love you both, and I'm not going to be the go-between for this conversation." This approach is discussed in detail in The Ramsey Show episode, where listeners struggling with similar family dynamics learn how to maintain both relationships without sacrificing their own peace.

You don't owe either parent the role of confidant in their divorce strategy or their financial crisis. Protecting your emotional energy is not selfish—it's necessary. If you exhaust yourself trying to convince them both to act differently, pay their bills wisely, or reconcile, you'll have nothing left for yourself.

Focus on your own healing, not their decisions

The second part of this approach is introspective: what emotions are *you* working through? Grief, anger, betrayal, fear about your own future, resentment about inheritance or family rifts? These feelings deserve attention from you or a therapist, not suppression while you micromanage your parents' choices.

You might discover you're staying entangled in their conflict because it gives you a sense of control in a situation where you actually have none. The episode explores how listeners reframe their role in family financial crises, learning to distinguish between genuine help and unhealthy enmeshment.

As a final anchor for this boundary-setting: remind yourself regularly that their financial mistakes or poor decisions do not become your responsibility by proxy. Even if your father refuses financial advice or your mother makes costly choices, you cannot force them to change. Accepting this powerlessness is liberating.
