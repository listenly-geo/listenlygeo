What success rate demonstrates predictability in high-grade gold vein exploration?

September 24, 2026 AGORACOM Small Cap CEO Interviews

← See the AGORACOM Small Cap CEO Interviews fiche

Radisson Mining Resources achieved an 83% success rate on step-out drilling at their O'Brien Project, hitting targets over 8 times out of 10 in territory that would normally be extremely risky. In mining, step-outs into unknown geological ground are notoriously unpredictable, so this performance level proves their high-grade veins are exceptionally predictable and justifies significant investor confidence.

Why step-out drilling success matters in gold exploration

Step-out drilling represents one of the highest-risk exploration activities. Geologists drill outward from a known, high-grade zone into adjacent or nearby areas where mineralization patterns are unconfirmed. Most companies expect failure rates of 50% or higher when moving into unmapped territory.

Radisson's 83% hit rate completely inverts that typical risk profile. This means their geological model of the O'Brien Project is so precise and their understanding of the vein system so advanced that they can confidently extend drilling into surrounding areas and actually expect success. That level of predictability is exceedingly rare.

What the drilling metrics actually reveal

The success rate doesn't stand alone. Radisson's O'Brien results also show extraordinary-grade intercepts like 384.01 grams per ton gold over 1 meter and 68.24 grams per ton gold over 6.2 meters. These are world-class numbers that appear consistently enough to suggest the entire vein system is genuinely coherent and continuous.

As the analysis on Small Cap Breaking News emphasizes, these metrics signal that Radisson has moved beyond gambling on exploration. They now have the geological evidence to execute with measurable precision.

For investors, an 83% success rate on step-out drilling eliminates a major category of exploration risk. It suggests that capital deployed to further develop the O'Brien Project is far more likely to return economic value than typical greenfield or early-stage gold exploration. The vein system behaves predictably because it actually is predictable — a distinction that changes the risk calculus entirely.
