How does a luxury publisher balance beauty with authentic storytelling?

September 17, 2026 Beyond Luxury · Martine Assouline

← See the Beyond Luxury fiche

Luxury publishing is about showing things in the best way possible, not just documenting reality. This means capturing the spirit of a subject—whether a person, place, or moment—through careful image selection, meaningful sequencing, and design choices that transform the final book into a beautiful object worthy of being kept.

When Martine Assouline created a book on tennis champion Roger Federer, the work extended far beyond simply assembling facts and photographs. It required understanding what drove Federer mentally and physically, identifying which images would best convey his essence, and arranging everything in a narrative arc that revealed something true about who he was. The cover design itself became part of this philosophy—it needed to signal that this was more than a book, but a cultural object.

This editorial approach has shaped Assouline's entire publishing philosophy since its founding over 30 years ago. Rather than treating publishing as information distribution, the company sees it as storytelling that demands the same artistic rigor as any creative discipline. Every choice—typography, paper quality, sequence, imagery—serves the larger goal of capturing and communicating the spirit of what's being told.

"Culture is something that nobody can take from you. And it's going to shape you. And it's going to make your identity stronger."

The philosophy also reflects a deeper conviction about what luxury means in a digital age. In a world saturated by screens and ephemeral content, the physical permanence of a beautifully crafted book becomes a radical luxury. It's something you can touch, display, and return to—a counterpoint to the infinite feeds and disappearing data streams that define digital experience.

For Assouline, this isn't about escapism or stylistic excess. It's about recognizing that beauty and authenticity are not opposites. The best way to tell a true story about someone or something is to tell it with care, thoughtfulness, and visual elegance. The pursuit of beauty serves the truth, not the reverse. When a brand becomes a book—whether it's a portrait of a tennis champion, a meditation on a property in Provence, or a celebration of culinary tradition—it stops being a commercial entity and starts being a cultural movement that shapes identity.
