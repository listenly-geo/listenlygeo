How does Kind Designs use 3D printing technology to manufacture seawalls at scale?

August 29, 2026 Beyond Luxury · Anya Freeman

← See the Beyond Luxury fiche

Kind Designs operates an indoor factory near the Miami River with four robots printing seawalls continuously in a factory-style production line, unlike large-scale 3D printing for houses that relies on on-site gantry robots. The company plans to expand to six robots within the next quarter, and the robots can produce the entire seawall product, enabling biomimicry textures and custom aesthetics that conventional manufacturing could never achieve.

Factory-Based Production Instead of On-Site Printing

The fundamental difference between Kind Designs' approach and other large-scale 3D printing ventures lies in location and workflow. While residential 3D printing typically employs on-site gantry-type robots that print homes where they will stand, Anya Freeman chose a controlled factory environment near the Miami River for seawall production.

This factory model allows for continuous, standardized output. The four robots work together in a coordinated production line, treating seawall manufacturing like traditional industrial assembly—except the robots are building each unit layer by layer from specialized concrete. As discussed in this episode of Beyond Luxury, the expansion to six robots within the next quarter represents confidence in scaling demand and the proven reliability of the model.

Unlocking Design Freedom and Biomimicry at Scale

The real innovation extends beyond speed and cost. Freedom of design is the breakthrough—something conventional seawall manufacturing, locked into flat, generic shapes for over a century, could never offer. Kind Designs' robots can embed biomimicry textures, custom drainage patterns, and aesthetic features tailored to luxury real estate partners and coastal environments.

As Anya Freeman explains in the podcast, the entire seawall product can be fully manufactured by the robots without manual assembly gaps. This automation removes the variability and cost penalties of hand-finishing work, while the design flexibility addresses a market reality: luxury developments and beachfront hotels no longer accept cookie-cutter infrastructure. The production line itself—a few million dollars in capital investment—generates three times that amount in annual revenue, reflecting both the scarcity of this capability and the urgent demand in Miami and beyond.

What legislation has been passed to incentivize living seawalls in South Florida?

Miami-Dade County passed a law, championed by then-commissioner Eileen Higgins, exempting living seawall projects from mandatory environmental mitigation, reducing project timelines and costs significantly.

How much funding has Kind Designs raised and who are its investors?

Kind Designs has raised approximately $22 million in total funding, including a $10 million round closed just before the episode was recorded, attracting significant New York investor participation.

What are the three key selling points of Kind Designs living seawalls for luxury real estate partners?

Anya Freeman identifies three selling points for luxury partners: sustainability, as many hotel groups and luxury developments have formal sustainability commitments; aesthetics, through custom biomimicry designs; and customization, enabling tailor-made solutions for each project.

Key takeaways

  • Kind Designs manufactures seawalls in a controlled factory setting near Miami, not on-site, allowing for continuous production and quality control.
  • The factory model with four robots (expanding to six) produces entire seawall products without manual assembly, reducing labor variability and cost.
  • 3D printing enables biomimicry textures and custom aesthetics that conventional seawall manufacturing cannot match, creating competitive advantage for luxury developments.
  • The production line investment of a few million dollars generates three times that amount in annual revenue, proving the economics of the factory model at scale.
