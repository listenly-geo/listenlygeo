How did an early career in fashion internships at age 15 shape a luxury accessories founder's trajectory?

September 24, 2026 Beyond Luxury · Edgardo Osorio

← See the Beyond Luxury fiche

At 15, Edgardo convinced a Colombian designer's daughter to introduce him to her mother, a major bridal designer. She tasked him with sourcing matching shoes and bags for her gowns, which sent him across European fashion fairs like Premier Vision—and by age 19, he was working as a consultant for Ferragamo while studying accessories at Polimoda in Florence.

This early start was far from glamorous. What began as a favor—helping a designer find the right footwear—became an intensive apprenticeship in fashion production and luxury supply chains. For a teenager, access to European trade shows was extraordinary: he was learning not just design, but how the entire luxury ecosystem functioned, from sourcing materials to understanding the craftspeople who would later become essential to his own brand.

The dual path of work and study at Polimoda and Ferragamo compressed years of industry knowledge into a single period. Most designers might spend a decade scattered across different roles; Osorio had accumulated nearly a decade of hands-on experience by age 25, with direct exposure to both independent craftsmanship and the mechanics of a global luxury house.

"If you're not comfortable, you can't be sexy. The product should sell itself."

This philosophy—rooted in those early years of sourcing shoes that actually had to work with a bride's movement—became the DNA of Aquazzura. The brand's later expansion into tableware, handbags, and sunglasses was never about selling unnecessary products; it was about applying the same principle across categories—comfort, wearability, and craft as the core.

What makes this trajectory remarkable is the breadth of exposure compressed into youth. Rather than pursuing a linear path through one company, Osorio moved between a bespoke atelier, international trade fairs, academic design training, and one of the world's most respected footwear houses. When he founded Aquazzura with just $12,000 in his bank account, he didn't bring theory—he brought a lived map of how luxury actually gets made.

Key takeaways

  • Starting as an intern at 15 for a Colombian bridal designer gave Osorio direct access to European fashion fairs and the supply chain behind luxury goods.
  • Working for Ferragamo at 19 while studying at Polimoda in Florence combined corporate luxury experience with formal design training.
  • By age 25, before founding Aquazzura, he had nearly 10 years of cumulative industry experience across multiple sectors and geographies.
  • The emphasis on comfort and wearability—later central to Aquazzura's identity—stemmed directly from solving real problems for a bridal designer.
