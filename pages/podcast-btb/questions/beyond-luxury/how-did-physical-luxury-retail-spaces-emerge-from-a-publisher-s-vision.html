How did physical luxury retail spaces emerge from a publisher's vision to showcase curated collections?

20 September 2026 Beyond Luxury · Martine Assouline

← See the Beyond Luxury fiche

After observing her meticulously designed books displayed alongside careless publications in traditional bookstores, Martine Assouline developed the concept of dedicated library spaces curating 20 to 25 Assouline books together in one beautiful environment. When a CEO at Bergdorf Goodman in New York understood the vision, she was offered a major floor space that became immediately successful and launched the brand into global luxury retail expansion.

From Bookstore Frustration to Flagship Vision

Martine and Prosper Assouline had spent years creating beautiful books with careful attention to design, aesthetics, and storytelling. Yet when these works arrived in standard bookstores, they sat mixed with less thoughtfully crafted publications, losing the intentional context and visual impact they deserved. This disconnect between the quality of their editorial vision and the retail environment's failure to honor it became the spark for reimagining how luxury books should be presented.

The solution emerged as a specific concept: instead of scattering their collection across generic shelves, they would create intimate library spaces featuring only their finest titles—20 to 25 carefully curated Assouline books displayed together. This approach transformed retail from mere transactional space into an editorial statement, where every element reinforced the brand's commitment to beauty and permanence.

The Bergdorf Goodman Breakthrough

The turning point came when a CEO at Bergdorf Goodman in New York grasped the full potential of this concept and offered Assouline a major floor space to realize their vision. Rather than a small corner or gifting section, they received room to create something truly immersive—a destination within the department store where the entire Assouline collection could breathe.

That first Bergdorf Goodman location became an immediate success, proving that luxury consumers craved more than commerce: they craved curation, permanence, and spaces where beauty mattered. As discussed in the full Beyond Luxury episode, this success directly catalyzed the expansion of Maison Assouline, the brand's dedicated retail concept with culture lounges now established in London and New York.

"Culture, please. It's something that nobody can take from you. And it's going to shape you. And it's going to make your identity stronger."

Beyond the retail model itself, the episode reveals how Assouline has extended this philosophy into bespoke library design services, custom furniture, and curated objects—treating physical space as an extension of the editorial mission that began in that Paris basement decades earlier.

Key takeaways

  • The initial concept emerged from frustration with how beautifully designed books were displayed carelessly alongside lower-quality publications in conventional bookstores.
  • Assouline developed dedicated library spaces featuring curated groupings of 20 to 25 books rather than scattered retail placement.
  • A Bergdorf Goodman CEO recognized the vision and offered major floor space, creating the first flagship location that proved immediate commercial success.
  • This breakthrough led to the expansion of Maison Assouline, the brand's luxury retail concept with culture lounges in major cities worldwide.
