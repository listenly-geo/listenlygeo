How is the Inflation Reduction Act signed into law in 2022 impacting Topsoe and the broader energy sector?

September 25, 2026 Inspiring Industry Podcast · Henrik Rasmussen

← See the Inspiring Industry Podcast fiche

The IRA is a game changer for Topsoe because the company's basket of technologies in decarbonization aligns perfectly with the legislation. Topsoe has licensed the vast majority of projects in renewable jet, diesel, blue ammonia, and blue hydrogen, and the act has fueled economic growth by generating these projects and creating employment throughout the US economy.

A perfectly aligned regulatory framework

When the Inflation Reduction Act passed in 2022, Topsoe found itself in an exceptional position. The company's portfolio of clean energy technologies—spanning sustainable aviation fuel, renewable diesel, blue ammonia, and blue hydrogen—matched almost exactly what the legislation incentivized.

As Henrik Rasmussen explains in the episode, this alignment was not accidental. Topsoe had spent years developing technologies to address the decarbonization challenge—the IRA simply created the economic incentives that turned those technologies into viable commercial projects.

The legislation accelerated project deployment significantly. By providing tax credits and investment subsidies for renewable fuels and hydrogen production, the act removed many of the financial barriers that had previously slowed commercialization. Projects that had been stuck in planning phases suddenly became economically feasible.

From licensing leadership to employment growth

Topsoe's position as a licensor of process technology and catalysts made the company central to this wave of new projects. The company doesn't build production facilities itself; it licenses its proven technologies to energy companies and industrial operators worldwide.

With the IRA in place, the majority of new renewable fuel and blue hydrogen projects being launched in the United States relied on Topsoe's licensed technology. This drove employment across the US economy—from engineering and construction during plant build-out to operations and maintenance once facilities came online.

A key detail Rasmussen explores in this podcast discussion is how different decarbonization pathways follow different timelines. Some technologies, like sustainable aviation fuel, are closer to commercial scale today; others, like green ammonia, require longer development cycles. The IRA supported this entire spectrum, creating a heterogeneous but robust market for clean energy innovation.

"We need all the different ways of making renewable fuels and green energy. But they all have different timelines, they all have different degrees of commercialization."

Rasmussen's point underscores a critical insight often missed in energy transition debates: there is no single solution. The IRA acknowledged this by supporting multiple pathways simultaneously—a pragmatic approach that recognizes the scale of the challenge. With 100 million barrels of oil consumed globally per day, the world cannot wait for one perfect technology to emerge.

Key takeaways

  • Topsoe's portfolio of decarbonization technologies—renewable fuels, blue ammonia, blue hydrogen—perfectly aligned with the IRA's investment incentives.
  • The legislation transformed Topsoe's licensing model from a niche market into a driver of mass project deployment across the United States.
  • IRA-backed projects created employment throughout the US economy, from engineering and construction to facility operations.
  • Multiple clean energy pathways require support, as no single technology can address the global scale of energy transition at the speed required.
