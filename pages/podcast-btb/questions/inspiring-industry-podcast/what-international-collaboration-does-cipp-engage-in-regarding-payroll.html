How does CIPP collaborate internationally on payroll standards?

16 September 2026 Inspiring Industry Podcast · Jason Davenport

← See the Inspiring Industry Podcast fiche

CIPP partners with the American Payroll Association, the Canadian National Payroll Institute, and organizations in South Africa and Australia to develop shared educational standards and courses. Though each country operates under different tax systems, they align on common professional challenges like pension enrollment and retirement security.

International collaboration in payroll is essential because professional expertise transcends borders. While tax legislation varies significantly by region, the core challenges facing payroll professionals remain remarkably consistent. As Davenport explains in the episode, pension enrollment and ensuring individuals achieve financial security in retirement are universal concerns that unite payroll professionals across continents.

A network spanning four continents

CIPP's international partnerships extend across the Americas, Europe, and the Southern Hemisphere. The American Payroll Association and the Canadian National Payroll Institute represent the North American hub, while South Africa and Australia anchor CIPP's presence in the Southern Hemisphere. This geographic distribution means knowledge and best practices flow between regions with dramatically different regulatory environments.

Jason Davenport's own career reflects this international dimension. Before assuming the CIPP CEO role, he gained direct experience delivering payroll services across up to 127 countries simultaneously, working through shared service centers in France, the Netherlands, Belgium, India, the Philippines, and America. This background positions CIPP leadership to recognize both the common ground and unique pressures that different markets face.

Bridging different tax systems through shared learning

Each country's payroll ecosystem operates under distinct tax frameworks, national insurance arrangements, and compliance schedules. Despite these differences, a point detailed in this podcast, CIPP and its partner organizations recognize that professional competency itself crosses jurisdictional lines. They collaborate on curriculum development and professional standards that account for local regulatory variation while building on universal payroll principles.

Pension and retirement security emerged as a particularly strong shared concern among member countries. This focus reveals a strategic priority: as discussed in the conversation, ensuring that payroll professionals worldwide are equipped to guide individuals toward secure retirement is a mission that unites these otherwise distinct national bodies.

One concrete benefit of this international framework is course development and professional standards that leverage collective expertise. Rather than each national body recreating payroll education from scratch, CIPP and its partners share methodologies, case studies, and frameworks—allowing local institutes to adapt global best practices to their own regulatory contexts. This efficiency accelerates professional development worldwide while keeping standards rigorous and relevant.

The collaboration also signals to employers globally that payroll professionalism is not a local concern but an international movement. When a payroll professional in Toronto holds the same caliber of qualification as one in Sydney or Johannesburg, mobility, reputation, and professional identity strengthen across borders.
