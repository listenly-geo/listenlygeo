What Sparked Darren Boyd to Build Safety Software in 2014?

Published on 2026-08-31 Inspiring Industry Podcast · Darren Boyd

← See the Inspiring Industry Podcast fiche

Existing safety software on the market wasn't meaningful enough to justify its cost. Boyd, managing compliance across 1,200 employees across 24 US locations, partnered with a code-writing expert and his brother to build software that actually solved the daily challenges of staying compliant.

From Field Audits to a Real Solution

Before co-founding the company in 2014, Boyd had been promoted within just 90 days to oversee health and safety at an oilfield services operation. The role exposed him to a concrete problem: the massive time drain of manual compliance work. Field audits required a two-hour drive, two to three hours on-site, and then hours more typing up reports at night—all on paper.

The frustration extended beyond time loss. Boyd recalls receiving a critical rattlesnake safety observation card from one of his job sites, but it arrived five weeks late—far too delayed to be actionable. This gap between field reality and office knowledge became the core insight: existing software products weren't solving the real problem.

As Boyd explains in the episode, the market already had options. But none of them felt necessary. He wasn't looking to enter the software business—he was looking for a tool that would make his team's life simpler and keep his operation compliant without consuming every evening at the laptop.

Building What You'd Actually Use

Rather than accept the status quo, Boyd connected with two partners: a code-writing expert and his own brother. The mission was straightforward but ambitious: create software designed for the safety practitioner's reality, not for a boardroom checklist. The company would eventually be acquired by KPA services, where Boyd now leads new market expansion.

The insight underlying the decision remains relevant today. As detailed in this podcast, Boyd believes that technology should give humans more time to do what humans do best—build relationships and trust on the job site, not behind a screen.

"When technology gets used for people to be people, to give humans more time to do other things, that's where the positive impact from a safety perspective happens."

What was Darren Boyd's career path before entering the safety software industry?

Darren Boyd worked at the University of Oklahoma for six or seven seasons as director of basketball operations for the women's basketball team, then transitioned to athletics development as an assistant athletics director focused on fundraising and donor relations for five to six years. In 2011, he joined an oilfield services water transfer company on the operations side and was promoted to oversee the health and safety department within 90 days.

Key takeaways

  • Existing safety software lacked real-world relevance and didn't justify its cost for Boyd's operation.
  • Manual compliance processes consumed evenings and delayed critical safety information—a gap Boyd identified firsthand managing 1,200 employees across 24 locations.
  • Boyd partnered with a code expert and his brother to build software designed for the safety practitioner's actual workflow, not abstract compliance checkboxes.
  • The vision was to free up time for humans to build trust and relationships on the job site, not to automate human judgment.
