What does Darren Boyd actually do as SVP of new markets at KPA services?

2 September 2026 Inspiring Industry Podcast · Darren Boyd

← See the Inspiring Industry Podcast fiche

Boyd drives growth and market expansion for the KPA software product his company co-founded, overseeing everything from sales and customer relationships in the oil and gas sector to product development, feature roadmap, and board-level strategy. He manages the day-to-day operations of the acquired business, including revenue operations and competitive positioning against other safety platforms.

From founder to leader of an acquired platform

Boyd's role combines entrepreneurial leadership with enterprise scale. After co-founding the safety software company in 2014 with two partners, the acquisition by KPA services positioned him to expand its reach across approximately 70 oil and gas operators while competing with other vendors in a field where trust and relationships drive adoption. This is why networking in the energy sector remains a core part of his daily work.

The technical side of the role is equally demanding. Boyd oversees product development and feature prioritization, deciding which new capabilities best serve the market while meeting the demands of KPA's diverse customer base. As Boyd explains in the episode, this includes balancing innovation with the reality of field operations, where safety compliance cannot be compromised by technology for technology's sake.

Strategy, operations, and the human side of safety technology

Beyond product, Boyd manages revenue operations, accounting, and board-level strategy — the organizational infrastructure that keeps the business aligned with KPA's larger ecosystem of eight or nine different software products. This operational complexity means he bridges the gap between what the field needs and what enterprise clients demand.

A deeper insight into how Boyd approaches the relationship between technology and human safety can be found in his full conversation on the podcast, where he discusses why pre-job meetings and team trust cannot be replaced by artificial intelligence, even in a digital-first age.

"When technology gets used for people to be people, to give humans more time to do other things, that's where the positive impact from a safety perspective happens."

His perspective on technology as an enabler of human connection rather than a replacement is central to how he shapes the product roadmap and sales strategy.

What is KPA services' business model and primary offerings?

KPA services started in the consulting world with boots-on-ground presence at facilities, primarily in the automotive space including car dealerships and tire shops, before expanding to serve approximately 70 different oil and gas operators with eight or nine different software products.

Why did Darren Boyd and his partners decide to create a safety software product in 2014?

While managing health and safety across 1,200 employees in 24 locations, Boyd found that existing safety software products were not meaningful enough to address the real compliance challenges in the field, prompting him to co-found a company that delivered practical solutions for fieldwork safety.

What was Darren Boyd's career path before entering the safety software industry?

Darren Boyd worked at the University of Oklahoma for six or seven seasons as director of basketball operations for the women's basketball team, then spent five to six years in athletics development focused on fundraising and donor relations, before transitioning in 2011 to an oilfield services water transfer company where he quickly moved into health and safety leadership.

Key takeaways

  • Boyd's SVP role spans growth strategy, sales, product development, and board-level operations for KPA's acquired safety software platform.
  • He manages customer relationships across approximately 70 oil and gas operators while competing with other safety software vendors.
  • His approach prioritizes using technology to enhance human connection and field relationships rather than replacing them with automation.
  • His unique background—from university sports management to field-level safety compliance—shapes his strategic view of technology adoption in energy industries.
