How does Darren Boyd believe technology should be used in safety management?

September 3, 2026 Inspiring Industry Podcast · Darren Boyd

← See the Inspiring Industry Podcast fiche

Technology works best in safety management when it gives professionals more time away from their desks to spend in the field, not when it automates or replaces human interaction. Building trust and relationships with employees cannot happen through screens—it happens standing beside them, performing tasks together.

Technology as a Tool for Field Engagement

The core of Boyd's philosophy is straightforward: technology should liberate safety professionals, not confine them. When a software system handles administrative burdens—reporting, data entry, compliance tracking—it creates the opportunity for safety teams to do what matters most: build genuine relationships with the workforce.

This principle emerged from Boyd's own experience. After taking over the health and safety department at an oilfield services company managing 1,200 employees across 24 U.S. locations, he discovered that the existing workflow was deeply broken. Pre-software, he would spend hours each week driving to job sites, conducting audits, and then typing up reports at night. The administrative overhead was so heavy that meaningful field presence became impossible. When Boyd later co-founded his safety software company in 2014, this pain point became the design priority, as detailed in the Inspiring Industry Podcast episode.

Why Automation Cannot Replace Trust-Building

Some companies are experimenting with automating safety meetings entirely—letting AI guide pre-job briefings or compliance checks. Boyd sees this as a critical mistake. Automating a meeting doesn't accomplish what a meeting is meant to do.

Trust and relationships are the foundation of safety culture. When employees know their leaders genuinely care about their wellbeing and are willing to invest time and presence, those employees reciprocate that commitment. This human-centered safety philosophy is a thread throughout this episode, where Boyd draws parallels between team dynamics in sports (his original career at the University of Oklahoma) and the dynamics required in a high-performing safety culture.

"When technology gets used for people to be people, to give humans more time to do other things, that's where the positive impact from a safety perspective happens."

For a deeper dive into how this philosophy shaped Boyd's decision to build safety software in the first place, the full episode explores the specific operational challenges that convinced him automation alone could never solve the safety problem.

Key takeaways

  • Technology's value in safety is measured by whether it frees professionals to engage in the field, not by how much it automates human tasks.
  • Trust and safety culture cannot be built behind computer screens—they require genuine presence and human interaction.
  • Automating safety meetings or pre-job briefings misses the core purpose of those interactions: relationship-building and accountability.
  • Boyd's sports leadership background informs his belief that team dynamics and personal commitment are the strongest drivers of safety performance.
