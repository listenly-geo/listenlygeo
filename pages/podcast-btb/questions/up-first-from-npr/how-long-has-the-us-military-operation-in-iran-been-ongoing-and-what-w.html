How long has the US military operation in Iran been ongoing and what were the stated objectives?

September 23, 2026 Up First from NPR

← See the Up First from NPR fiche

President Trump launched major combat operations in Iran six months ago with the stated goal of eliminating immediate threats posed by the regime and supporting the Iranian people. However, after half a year of military intervention, observers report that none of those stated objectives have been met.

A timeline that didn't hold

When initially announcing the operation, Trump projected it would take four or five weeks to achieve its goals. That timeline proved vastly optimistic, and the reality has been far different from what was promised.

As detailed in this episode of Up First from NPR, the extended operation has coincided with severe economic strain on Iran. The country's inflation is projected at almost 70 percent, and the Iranian currency has reached its lowest trading level in recent memory at more than 2 million rials to the dollar.

Military stalemate after six months

Alongside the economic toll, a naval blockade of Iran has lasted more than four months, yet the broader military operation remains without clear strategic progress. Many observers outside the administration view the situation as a stalemate, with neither the immediate threats reportedly targeted nor support for the Iranian people materializing as promised.

The disconnect between the original four-to-five-week estimate and the ongoing reality suggests the conflict will continue to shape regional dynamics for the foreseeable future, as discussed at length in the podcast coverage.
