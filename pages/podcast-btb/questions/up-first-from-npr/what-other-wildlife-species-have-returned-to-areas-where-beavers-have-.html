What other wildlife species have returned to areas where beavers have been reintroduced in Britain?

August 16, 2026 Up First from NPR · Lauren Frayer

← Voir la fiche Up First from NPR

At the Ealing Beaver Project in West London, the return of beavers triggered a cascade of biodiversity recovery. Within weeks, freshwater shrimp arrived downstream of the dam, invertebrate diversity and abundance boomed, eight new bird species were recorded, and bat species increased from four to six. Rare brown hairstreak butterflies also began laying eggs on blackthorn branches that beavers had nibbled.

A cascade that began within weeks — not years

The speed of recovery is what makes the Ealing findings so striking. Beavers don't just dam water — they restructure entire ecosystems. By creating standing water and felling trees selectively, they generate a mosaic of habitats that supports species at every level of the food chain.

Freshwater shrimp were the first arrivals, drawn to the cleaner, slower-moving water downstream of the new dam. Invertebrate populations surged in both diversity and sheer numbers — a foundational shift that then pulled birds, bats, and specialist insects into the restored habitat. As Lauren Frayer reports in Up First from NPR, this was not a gradual, decade-long process: it happened fast, visibly, and measurably.

Brown hairstreak butterflies: the detail that tells the whole story

Among all the species data, the brown hairstreak butterfly stands out. It is a rare and highly specific insect: it lays eggs almost exclusively on young blackthorn shoots. Beavers, by gnawing blackthorn branches, produce exactly the kind of fresh regrowth that these butterflies require. No engineer planned for this outcome — it emerged from the beavers simply doing what beavers do.

This kind of unplanned, multi-species benefit is precisely what Frayer's reporting for this episode of Up First documents across four separate beaver sites in Britain. The picture that emerges is consistent: where beavers go, biodiversity follows.

"The beavers can do it, probably a fraction of the cost, probably better than we can, certainly more sustainably for the environment."

The data from Ealing — eight bird species, two additional bat species, rare butterflies, and a wave of invertebrate life — represents a level of ecological return that conventional rewilding projects rarely achieve this quickly or cheaply. As Frayer documents throughout the episode, beavers act as what ecologists call a keystone species: their presence reorganises the entire habitat around them.

How cost-effective are beavers compared to traditional flood engineering solutions in Britain?

Municipal authorities had budgeted hundreds of thousands — if not over a million pounds — for conventional engineering works such as large diggers and earthworks, before beavers provided a natural solution at a fraction of that cost.

How did beavers stop the flooding at a London Underground tube station?

Wildlife conservationist Elliot Newton and veterinarian Sean McCormick relocated beavers into a 20-acre park adjacent to the tube station — a former golf course — which was transformed into a water-retaining wetland within weeks of the beavers' arrival.

Who were Millie and Bjornar and what role did they play in Britain's beaver reintroduction?

Millie and Bjornar were two Norwegian beavers relocated by wildlife officials in 2009 to Napdale Forest, a temperate rainforest in western Scotland, as part of Britain's first official beaver reintroduction programme.

Key takeaways

  • Eight new bird species and two additional bat species were recorded at the Ealing Beaver Project after beavers were introduced — bat count rose from four to six species.
  • Freshwater shrimp appeared downstream of the beaver dam within weeks, and invertebrate populations surged in both diversity and abundance.
  • Rare brown hairstreak butterflies began laying eggs on blackthorn regrowth produced by beaver gnawing — a benefit no engineer could have designed deliberately.
  • The biodiversity recovery at Ealing was rapid and measurable, demonstrating that beavers function as a keystone species whose presence reorganises entire ecosystems.
