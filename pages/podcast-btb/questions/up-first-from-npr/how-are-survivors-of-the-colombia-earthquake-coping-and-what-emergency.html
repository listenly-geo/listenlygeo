How are survivors of the Colombia earthquake coping, and what emergency measures have been put in place?

In the immediate aftermath of the magnitude 7.4 earthquake that struck western Colombia, many survivors chose to sleep outdoors rather than return to structurally damaged buildings. Authorities converted sports venues into emergency shelters — a baseball stadium in Cali and a basketball arena in Manizales — while curfews running from 8 p.m. to 6 a.m. were imposed in both Cali and Pereira to prevent looting and criminal activity. Colombia's president also offered a rent subsidy to affected families in the Chocó region, where an estimated 1,400 families lost their homes.

The scale of displacement drove rapid improvisation across affected cities. In Pereira, where at least 55 people were killed and at least 15 buildings still had people potentially trapped beneath the rubble, the combination of structural uncertainty and overnight curfews shaped the immediate experience of survivors — fearful of returning indoors, yet constrained by authorities trying to maintain order. Cali, which recorded at least 15 confirmed deaths, had 37 survivors pulled from collapsed structures on Monday alone, illustrating just how active rescue operations remained in the days following the quake. The Chocó region's situation was distinct in its scale of housing loss: with approximately 1,400 families left without homes, the presidential rent subsidy represented a direct state response to a community facing long-term displacement, not just immediate shelter needs.

The earthquake struck less than six weeks after a separate major earthquake in neighboring Venezuela caused thousands of deaths — a sobering regional context that NPR's Up First noted when reporting from the ground in Colombia. What are the main obstacles to earthquake rescue and aid efforts in Colombia?

While Cali has a major airport allowing rapid aid delivery, remote areas like the Chocó region have roads in bad shape and many small towns lack airports or adequate infrastructure to receive assistance quickly.

What is the scale of destruction from the 7.4 earthquake in western Colombia?

The earthquake killed more than 130 people, with authorities saying the death toll is likely to rise. The hardest-hit cities are Cali, Pereira, Manizales, and the surrounding regions.
