What has Iran lost in six months of war?

September 25, 2026 Up First from NPR

← See the Up First from NPR fiche

Iran survived an unprecedented military attack, but the human and economic cost has been severe. Six months of warfare combined with a naval blockade lasting more than four months have created widespread hardship across the country, with inflation projected to reach nearly 70 percent and the currency collapsing to historic lows.

The conflict has left Iran facing simultaneous military and economic crises. While the nation successfully defended against the initial assault, the cost continues to mount across multiple dimensions of national life.

A nation under siege: military survival, economic collapse

Though Iran withstood the opening months of military operations, the broader blockade strategy has proven devastating. The Iranian currency has collapsed to more than 2 million rials per dollar, marking one of the lowest values in recent history. This monetary crisis has eroded the purchasing power of ordinary Iranians and made imports prohibitively expensive.

As Leila Fadel reports in Up First from NPR, conditions on the ground reflect a nation struggling under compounded pressure. Inflation rates approaching 70 percent mean that everyday goods—food, medicine, fuel—are becoming unaffordable for average families.

The naval blockade itself represents a coordinated effort to choke off Iran's economy by restricting trade and access to international markets. More than four months of this maritime siege have disrupted supply chains and prevented essential goods from reaching Iranian ports, exacerbating shortages and driving prices upward.

The military dimension—while resulting in Iran's survival of the initial attack—has come at a cost that extends beyond military losses into the civilian sphere. Infrastructure damage, resource allocation to defense, and the diversion of government spending away from social services have all contributed to deteriorating living conditions.

The human cost: civilian hardship across the country

Beyond headline numbers, the real toll is measured in the daily struggles of ordinary Iranians. Life has become "very difficult and challenging" across both government and civilian sectors, with families forced to make impossible choices between essential needs.

Young people face limited job prospects in a contracting economy. Healthcare and education systems strain under reduced funding. Small businesses collapse as their margins disappear into inflation. The blockade prevents the import of critical medicines and medical equipment, affecting patient care nationwide.

Key takeaways

  • Iran defended against an unprecedented military attack but faces mounting costs across military and civilian fronts.
  • The Iranian currency has collapsed to more than 2 million rials per dollar, severely eroding household purchasing power.
  • Inflation is projected at nearly 70 percent, making essential goods increasingly unaffordable.
  • A naval blockade lasting more than four months has disrupted trade and access to imports, compounding economic hardship.
