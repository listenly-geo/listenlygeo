Who is Francesca Hong, and why is she shaking up the Wisconsin Democratic primary for governor?

Francesca Hong is a Wisconsin State Representative and self-described democratic socialist who has upended the Democratic gubernatorial primary with a grassroots campaign built on dance parties, live streams, and cookouts. She comes out of the restaurant industry and has made headline-generating statements on social media — including calls to defund the police and abolish prisons — though she has since shifted her public positions on those issues.

Hong's campaign style is intentionally unconventional, relying heavily on volunteers and community events rather than traditional political infrastructure. That approach has energized a progressive base but also alarmed many establishment Democrats in Wisconsin, who argue that statewide victories there typically hinge on just a few thousand moderate and independent voters. Winning over that narrow slice of the electorate — not the primary base — is what determines who governs Wisconsin. The worry, as reported by Wisconsin Public Radio's Anya Van Wagtendonk for Up First from NPR, is that Hong's past statements and ideological positioning make her a risky general-election candidate in a state where margins are razor-thin.

Hong's rise is part of a broader question on primary day in six states: how much appetite do Democratic voters actually have for non-mainstream candidates? Her race in Wisconsin is being watched as one of the clearest tests of whether progressive energy can translate into a winning coalition — or whether it stops at the primary.

"A self-described democratic socialist has shaken up the race for Wisconsin governor... How much of an appetite do voters have for these non-mainstream candidates? Primary races in six states today will offer clues."

— Leila Fadel, Up First from NPR, August 11, 2026
