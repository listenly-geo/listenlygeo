How Much Home Equity Exists in the United States Right Now?

September 21, 2026 The Diary Of A CEO with Steven Bartlett · David Bach

← See the The Diary Of A CEO with Steven Bartlett fiche

There is now $34 trillion in home equity in America, a figure that has surged 90% since before COVID, according to a Wall Street Journal article. This represents the accumulated wealth stored in residential property across the nation.

This staggering number reflects the scale of residential wealth creation in the United States. As David Bach explains in the episode, home equity stands as one of the two primary sources of wealth creation in the United States, alongside the stock market.

The Magnitude of Residential Wealth Since COVID

The 90% increase in home equity since before the pandemic underscores how dramatically housing values have climbed in the past few years. This growth has been uneven across markets—certain U.S. cities have seen home prices double or even triple, while others have experienced more modest appreciation, yet the overall $34 trillion figure captures this nationwide surge in residential property value.

Understanding this $34 trillion context matters because it demonstrates why homeownership remains central to American wealth building. The sheer volume of equity locked in homes across the nation shows that property appreciation, not stock gains alone, continues to be the dominant vehicle for building personal net worth, a point emphasized throughout this podcast conversation.

"It is an absolute freaking myth that people take this extra money that they could have used to buy a house and they're going to put it in the stock market. They don't do that."

What is the wealth gap between homeowners and renters in America?

Homeowners in America are worth 40 times more than renters. The average homeowner in America today is worth over $400,000, while the average renter is worth significantly less.
