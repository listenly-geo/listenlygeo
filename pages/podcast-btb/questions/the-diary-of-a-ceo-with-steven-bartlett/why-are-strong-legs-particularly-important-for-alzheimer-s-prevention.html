Why are strong legs particularly important for Alzheimer's prevention?

A study on identical twins — sharing the exact same genetic profile — tracked over a 10-year period with cognitive tests and MRIs found a striking result: the twin with the greatest strength and most leg power had a bigger brain, larger gray matter volume, better-preserved cognitive functions, and consistently outperformed the other on cognitive tests. Because twins eliminate genetic variables entirely, the difference in brain health was attributable directly to physical conditioning, making leg strength one of the clearest causal signals researchers have found in Alzheimer's prevention.

What is gray matter?

Your brain consists of both gray and white matter. Gray matter contains the cell bodies and lives on the outer portions of the brain. White matter, found deeper within the brain, is where myelinated neurons reside. Preserving gray matter volume is directly linked to maintaining cognitive function as we age — and the twin study measured exactly this.

This finding doesn't stand alone. The SMART trial reinforced the same conclusion from a different angle: people with mild cognitive impairment who completed resistance training two to three times per week not only preserved their cognitive functions — they enhanced their processing speed, improved their fluid intelligence, and slowed the loss of gray matter. What is the ApoE4 gene and how much does it increase the risk of Alzheimer's disease?

The ApoE4 gene (Apolipoprotein E gene) is one of the strongest genetic risk factors for Alzheimer's disease. One copy raises risk by two to three times for males and six-fold for females; two copies raise it ten times for males and fifteen-fold for females — though carrying the gene is not a foregone conclusion of developing the disease.

What is the most compelling study proving that resistance training protects the brain from Alzheimer's disease?

The SMART trial took a group of people with mild cognitive impairment and gave them resistance training two to three times per week. They not only preserved their cognitive functions but also enhanced their processing speed, improved their fluid intelligence, and experienced a measurable slowing of gray matter loss.
