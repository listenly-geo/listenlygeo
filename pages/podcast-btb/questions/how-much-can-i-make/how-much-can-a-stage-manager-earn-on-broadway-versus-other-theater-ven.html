How much can a stage manager earn on Broadway versus other theater venues?

September 11, 2026 How Much Can I Make? — Real Jobs. Real Stories. Career Insights · Billy

← See the How Much Can I Make? — Real Jobs. Real Stories. Career Insights fiche

On Broadway, the union minimum contract for a production stage manager is $4,000 per week. If you land a hit show like Book of Mormon, Wicked, or Hamilton, you can earn $200,000 a year plus pension contributions, health insurance, and retirement benefits. Regional theaters, off-Broadway venues, and smaller productions pay roughly a quarter of Broadway rates, while freelance gigs can be as low as $1,000 for four weeks of work.

The Broadway premium and beyond

The gap between Broadway and other theater sectors is substantial. A stage manager on Broadway benefits from union protections that lock in baseline pay and benefits, whereas regional and off-Broadway stage managers have far fewer guarantees. Hit productions on Broadway can double or triple baseline earnings depending on the show's commercial success and box office performance.

When you're managing a complex production—whether it's the intricate set changes in Hello Dolly or the technical demands of a Broadway musical—the union structure ensures compensation reflects the scale of responsibility. As Billy details in the episode, even what might look like glamorous work requires serious logistical coordination behind the scenes.

Where the money drops off

Regional and off-Broadway venues typically pay one-quarter of Broadway salaries, which can mean $1,000 to $1,500 per week instead of $4,000. Freelance stage management—common for smaller productions, touring shows, or acting class readings—often pays flat project fees rather than weekly rates, sometimes yielding only $1,000 for four weeks of rehearsals and performances combined.

Billy's career trajectory, documented in this podcast conversation, illustrates how stage managers who break into Broadway can accumulate substantial pensions over 30 years—a retirement safety net that rarely exists outside the union system. Starting in 1988 at $292 a week, the profession's financial evolution reflects both inflation and the union's collective bargaining power.

The distinction between union and non-union work shapes not just weekly paychecks but long-term financial stability. For anyone considering stage management as a career, the full episode reveals additional insights about navigating auditions for production jobs, managing complex technical rehearsals, and the reality of working six days a week, eight shows per week, over extended runs—all factors that justify the significant salary premium Broadway commands.

Key takeaways

  • Broadway union contracts guarantee a minimum of $4,000 per week for production stage managers.
  • Hit Broadway shows can push annual earnings to $200,000+ with full benefits including pension and health insurance.
  • Regional and off-Broadway stage managers typically earn about 25% of Broadway wages.
  • Freelance theater work can pay as little as $1,000 for an entire four-week engagement.
