How does owning your own architecture firm change earning potential compared to working for others?

September 3, 2026 How Much Can I Make? — Real Jobs. Real Stories. Career Insights · Rob Dupont

← See the How Much Can I Make? — Real Jobs. Real Stories. Career Insights fiche

Owning your own architecture firm creates a dramatic jump in earning potential — partners at established firms can make six figures, while starting salaries for new architects hover around $25 per hour. The gap reflects not just seniority, but the fundamental shift from earning an hourly wage to building equity and capturing the full value of your work through client relationships and ongoing business growth.

From Employment to Ownership: The Economic Turning Point

When you work for another firm, your earnings are capped by salary structure and market rates. Entry-level architects in the Boston area today earn approximately $25 per hour—a wage that barely breaks $50,000 annually for full-time work. In contrast, Rob Dupont explains in the podcast, his partner in Boston who specializes in building envelope and holds an ownership stake at their firm is earning six figures.

Rob's own transition illustrates this shift. He started his career as an apprentice under architect Robert Young in Kingston, building foundational skills while earning an employee's wage. But around 1991–1992, during an economic slowdown, he was let go from his position. Rather than seeking another firm job, he made a deliberate choice: he began leveraging his network to generate his own projects and build his own business. That decision transformed his economic trajectory entirely.

Networking as the Bridge to Six-Figure Potential

The leap from $25/hour to six figures doesn't happen overnight, and it doesn't happen in isolation. Over his 40+ years practicing architecture, Rob built deep relationships with builders, engineers, contractors, and past clients. Those connections became the engine of his business. As discussed at length in this podcast, the architect who owns the firm doesn't just earn a salary—they earn referral fees, repeat business, and the compounding value of a reputation built over decades.

This is why ownership changes the math so radically. A partner who was "prepared to work and not make a lot of money at the beginning" gradually builds a client base that becomes a self-sustaining revenue stream. Each project generates not just immediate income but future relationships that feed the firm's pipeline.

"If you're prepared to work and not make a lot of money at the beginning and you love what you do, then be an architect. If you don't love it, don't do it."

What Rob emphasizes is that ownership requires a different mindset than employment. You must accept lean early years and genuine passion for the work itself. Many architects never make that jump precisely because they can't stomach the uncertainty. But for those who do—and who have the drive to maintain and expand a network—the six-figure potential becomes real.

What are typical starting salaries for architects today?

Firms in the Boston area currently pay architecture students still in school around $20 per hour, and recent graduates start at approximately $25 per hour—a significant gap from the six-figure earnings potential of firm partners.

What role does networking play in an architect's career success?

The key to getting work and building a sustainable architecture business is networking. Rob was fortunate to have mentors early in his career who recognized his potential, and over 40 years he built deep relationships with builders, engineers, and contractors—connections that fuel consistent referrals and business growth.

How much time do architects spend on design versus regulatory and administrative tasks?

The vast bulk of time is spent on drawing and construction document drafting in the computer and construction document phase, because regulatory and code compliance work dominates the architectural practice workflow.

Key takeaways

  • Firm ownership can deliver six-figure earnings compared to $25/hour starting wages for employees.
  • The transition from employee to owner requires accepting lean early years and reinvesting in business development.
  • Long-term client relationships and professional networks are the primary drivers of sustainable income growth in independent practice.
  • Passion for architecture is essential—the financial rewards only materialize for those committed to the craft over the long term.
