What certification and training is required to become a doula?

September 19, 2026 How Much Can I Make? — Real Jobs. Real Stories. Career Insights · Mary Riley

← See the How Much Can I Make? — Real Jobs. Real Stories. Career Insights fiche

Doula certification typically involves one or two weekend workshops combined with book learning and practical testing, though the process has become more involved in recent years. Certification must be renewed annually, and there are no rigid medical prerequisites—the focus centers on building hands-on skills through actual birth experience rather than formal medical education.

The path to becoming a doula is remarkably accessible. Unlike many healthcare professions, there is no mandatory medical background required to start the certification process. This approach reflects the core of doula work: learning directly from births themselves rather than through classroom theory alone.

As Mary Riley explains in the episode, the training framework centers on practical competency. You attend concentrated weekend workshops, complete assigned reading, and then demonstrate your knowledge through testing before moving forward.

Annual renewal and continuous skill development

The certification process does not end once you complete initial training. Annual renewal is mandatory to maintain your credential as a practicing doula. This ongoing requirement ensures that doulas stay current and continue refining their approach to birth support.

Beyond the formal certification structure, the real education happens on the job. Mary Riley notes in her conversation with Mirav Ozeri that you develop skills by responding to the unique needs of each birth—no two are identical. The hands-on experience with actual births becomes your greatest teacher, far more influential than any single textbook or workshop alone.

"I read there is a doula for death, doula for birth. Which one are you?" … "Mostly births, although I spent many years doing death, but it was before it was even known as a death doula. It was just something that I did, just giving support to whatever was needed."

The flexibility of doula certification opens interesting career pathways. Many doulas, like Mary Riley, have expanded their practice into adjacent areas such as end-of-life support, showing that the foundational training translates across different moments of profound human transition.

Key takeaways

  • Weekend workshops and book learning form the core of doula certification, with testing required before completion.
  • No formal medical education is a prerequisite—the focus is on hands-on skill development during actual births.
  • Annual renewal of your credential is mandatory to maintain your status as a practicing doula.
  • Real expertise develops through repeated birth experience, which often exceeds the value of initial classroom training.
