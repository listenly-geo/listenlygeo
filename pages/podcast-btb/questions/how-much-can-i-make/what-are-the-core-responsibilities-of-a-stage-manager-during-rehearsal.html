What does a stage manager actually do during rehearsals and live shows?

September 10, 2026 How Much Can I Make? — Real Jobs. Real Stories. Career Insights · Billy

← See the How Much Can I Make? — Real Jobs. Real Stories. Career Insights fiche

A stage manager keeps rehearsals on schedule and ensures actors and directors have everything they need—floor plans, props, costumes—while during performances, they call all cues and watch the show like a hawk, ready to stop the show if technical issues or injuries occur. The role spans logistical coordination during preparation to real-time decision-making once the curtain rises.

Keeping rehearsals organized and running on time

During rehearsals, the stage manager acts as a central coordinator for everyone involved in the production. They schedule costume fittings, wig measurements, and other technical needs that temporarily pull actors away from the rehearsal room—tasks that could disrupt the flow if left unmanaged.

The stage manager also ensures that directors and performers have immediate access to all the materials they need to work effectively: accurate floor plans showing stage dimensions and set layouts, physical props that actors must interact with, and costume pieces for blocking purposes. Without these resources readily available, rehearsals stall.

Taking control in the technical rehearsal phase

Once the production moves into technical rehearsals, the stage manager's role expands significantly. They now work closely with lighting and sound designers to coordinate complex cues and set changes that must happen in precise sequence and timing. For large Broadway productions, this choreography can be intricate—as when Billy stage-managed Hello, Dolly!, a show with mammoth, complex sets requiring careful coordination to move smoothly between scenes.

This is where the stage manager becomes essential to translating the director's vision into technical reality, ensuring every lighting shift, every sound effect, and every scene change happens exactly when it should.

Running the show and making critical decisions

Once performances begin, the stage manager's authority is absolute. They call every single cue during the show—lighting, sound, scenery moves, actor entrances. They watch the performance with complete focus, tracking every technical element and every performer movement.

This vigilance has real stakes. If a technical malfunction occurs or if an actor is injured, the stage manager has the power—and the responsibility—to stop the show. As detailed in this episode of How Much Can I Make?, the stage manager's decision-making can directly impact the safety and continuity of the performance.

"You have to love theater. You have to not be afraid to get your hands dirty. You have to love the process of putting things together."

Beyond the technical demands, Billy shares in the full conversation how stage management became a more fulfilling path than performing itself—discovering that his real passion lay in the infrastructure that makes theater possible, not just the spotlight.

Key takeaways

  • Stage managers schedule and coordinate logistical needs like costume fittings and wig measurements during rehearsals to prevent disruption.
  • They provide directors and actors with essential materials—floor plans, props, costumes—to enable effective rehearsal work.
  • During technical rehearsals, stage managers coordinate lighting, sound, and scene changes with precision for complex Broadway productions.
  • In performance, the stage manager calls every cue and holds authority to stop the show if safety or technical issues arise.
