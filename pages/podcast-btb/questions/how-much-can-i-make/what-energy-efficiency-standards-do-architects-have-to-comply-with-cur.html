What energy efficiency standards must architects follow today?

September 7, 2026 · How Much Can I Make? — Real Jobs. Real Stories. Career Insights · Rob Dupont

← See the How Much Can I Make? — Real Jobs. Real Stories. Career Insights fiche

Most states across the continental United States require compliance with energy conservation codes, which set mandatory efficiency standards for heating, cooling, and insulation in buildings. These standards are becoming increasingly restrictive, and local building departments verify that architectural plans meet them before construction begins.

Energy conservation codes are not optional—they are a legal requirement in the building process. As Rob Dupont explains in the episode, architects must design buildings that comply with these standards from the earliest design stages. The codes dictate how efficiently a building must heat and cool itself, what insulation values are required, and how the building envelope must perform to minimize energy loss.

How enforcement works in practice

The responsibility for ensuring compliance falls to local building departments, which review all architectural plans before construction can begin. These departments have the authority to approve or reject designs based on energy code compliance. Once a project receives approval, the building department also monitors the construction phase to ensure that architects' plans are implemented correctly and that the standards are met in the finished building.

This enforcement mechanism means that architects cannot simply design buildings according to aesthetic or functional preferences alone—energy efficiency is a non-negotiable constraint, discussed in detail in this podcast episode. The codes apply uniformly across most states, creating a baseline level of environmental responsibility built into every new structure.

Tightening regulations and what's ahead

One critical trend is that these standards are becoming more restrictive over time, not less. As climate concerns grow and technology improves, building codes are updated to require even higher efficiency levels. This means architects must stay current with the latest code requirements and design practices to remain compliant. The building department's role in this evolution is to enforce the most recent standards and ensure no building falls below them.

For architects, this creates an ongoing professional obligation to understand and master energy codes as they change. Rob Dupont, who has been practicing architecture for over 40 years, has experienced this evolution firsthand and learned to integrate these requirements into his design process from the start—a detail explored further in the full conversation.

What are the main constraints that affect the final design of a building project?

Budget, codes, planning boards, and the client's needs all affect the building design. Architects are obligated to follow building codes and local zoning regulations while balancing these competing demands.

What is biophilic design, and is it a current trend in architecture?

Biophilic design draws inspiration from natural forms such as plants, topography, and skeletal structures. This approach is increasingly recognized as a meaningful trend in modern architecture.

Is artificial intelligence a threat to architects' jobs?

Architects are not currently threatened by AI. While some clients bring AI renderings to show their vision, the human architect's role in translating needs into functional design remains irreplaceable.
