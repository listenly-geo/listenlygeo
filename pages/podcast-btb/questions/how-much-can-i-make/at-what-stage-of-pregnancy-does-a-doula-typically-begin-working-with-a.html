When does a doula typically begin working with a client during pregnancy?

September 21, 2026 How Much Can I Make? — Real Jobs. Real Stories. Career Insights · Mary Riley

← See the How Much Can I Make? — Real Jobs. Real Stories. Career Insights fiche

A doula typically begins working after an initial meeting where the mother, partner, and any family members who will be present get to know each other. Once everyone feels comfortable, the relationship officially starts. Most clients see their doula two or three more times during pregnancy, with ongoing contact via phone calls and texts during appointments, ultrasounds, or whenever questions arise.

The timing of engagement varies based on client needs and availability. Many expect to book their doula several months in advance—far earlier than many anticipate. This early booking ensures the relationship has time to develop properly before labor begins.

The initial meeting is crucial because it sets the tone for the entire birth experience. Both the doula and the family assess whether they're the right fit for each other. As Mary Riley explains in the episode, comfort and trust must be established before the doula begins official support.

"They have to walk away from this birth looking at each other and hopefully say, look at what we did together, because we have to parent and raise this child for the rest of our lives."

During pregnancy itself, the relationship deepens through periodic in-person meetings—typically two to three visits—combined with responsive communication. This cadence allows the doula to understand the family's preferences, birth plans, and any concerns without overwhelming them with too much contact.

The exact number of prenatal visits can vary depending on the doula's availability and the client's needs. Some families choose more frequent check-ins, while others prefer minimal contact until labor approaches. What matters most is that the foundation of trust is in place before the birth, as discussed in this podcast conversation.

One practical consideration: if you're thinking about hiring a doula, expect to book several months ahead. Riley's calendar, for example, fills up rapidly—she often has clients booked into July while still in January. This early commitment reflects the growing demand for doula services and the importance of securing support before your due date arrives.

How much can a beginning doula earn, and what is the typical fee range by experience level?

Beginning doulas might charge a few hundred dollars to cover costs like childcare and gas, or even do births for free to gain education. Rural and suburban doulas typically earn $1,500 to a couple thousand dollars per birth, while New York City doulas charge $3,000 to $4,000 per birth.

What certification and training is required to become a doula?

Doula certification typically involves one or two weekend workshops, book learning, and tests with practice. The process today is somewhat more involved than it was decades ago, though the core remains accessible to those committed to supporting births.

What does a doula do during pregnancy and childbirth?

A doula provides a combination of physical and emotional support, helping people advocate for themselves within hospital situations or home births. The role is flexible and tailored to each client's unique needs and birth plan.
