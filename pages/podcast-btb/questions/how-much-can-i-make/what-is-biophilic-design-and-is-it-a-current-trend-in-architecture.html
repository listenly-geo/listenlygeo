What is biophilic design, and is it a current trend in architecture?

September 5, 2026 How Much Can I Make? — Real Jobs. Real Stories. Career Insights · Rob Dupont

← See the How Much Can I Make? — Real Jobs. Real Stories. Career Insights fiche

Biophilic design is an architectural approach where buildings draw inspiration from natural forms such as plants, topography, and skeletal structures. This is why you're seeing increasingly amorphous—curved, organic-shaped—buildings emerge worldwide, though these structures can be extraordinarily expensive to construct.

The trend reflects a fundamental shift in how architects think about form and function. Rather than imposing rigid, geometric shapes onto the built environment, biophilic design embraces the flowing, irregular patterns found in nature. As Rob Dupont explains in the episode, this approach is becoming more visible across the globe, from residential projects to major commercial developments.

Why these buildings cost so much to build

The price tag on biophilic buildings stems from their complexity. Curved walls, non-standard structural elements, and organic geometries require specialized engineering and custom fabrication that traditional rectilinear construction does not demand. Every angle, every surface must be precisely calculated and often custom-built, which compounds labor costs and material expenses significantly.

Despite the high construction costs, the aesthetic and design appeal remains strong. The podcast discusses how these visually striking structures have become a defining design trend, even as architects and developers grapple with budget constraints and feasibility challenges.

Is artificial intelligence a threat to architects' jobs?

Rob does not employ anything in AI yet and it is not threatening him at this point. Some clients have brought him AI renderings of what they want, which he uses as a reference tool rather than a replacement for his expertise.

How does owning your own architecture firm change earning potential compared to working for others?

Having your own firm makes a significant difference in earning potential. Rob's partner in Boston who specializes in building envelope design has substantially higher earning potential as a firm partner than architects working as employees.

What are typical starting salaries for architects today?

According to Rob's colleagues in the Boston area, firms typically pay students still in school around $20 per hour, while recent architecture graduates start at approximately $25 per hour in entry-level positions.
