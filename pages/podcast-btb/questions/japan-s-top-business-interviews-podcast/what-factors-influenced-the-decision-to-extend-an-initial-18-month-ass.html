Why did extending the assignment from 18 months to four years prove essential for transformation?

September 24, 2026 Japan's Top Business Interviews Podcast · Laura Bonamici

← See the Japan's Top Business Interviews Podcast fiche

At the six-month and 12-month checkpoints, it became clear that 18 months would not provide enough time to measure the real impact of transformation efforts. Aligning the extension with Fujitsu's three-year midterm plan cycle—ending in March 2025—allowed the company to track concrete results through a complete business planning horizon. By extending to four years, the leadership team could publish the full scope of transformation success: Fujitsu achieved its most successful fiscal year, with results documented at the end of April.

The checkpoint discipline that changed the timeline

Regular structured reviews at six months and 12 months created the decision framework necessary to extend the initial mandate. These checkpoints were not administrative formalities but strategic moments where the real scope of the work became visible. The gap between the original 18-month plan and what the data showed was achievable forced a candid reassessment, as detailed in this podcast episode.

Without these checkpoints, the initial timeline might have been kept, and the transformation results would have remained incomplete or unvalidated. The discipline of measuring progress at predetermined intervals revealed both the opportunity and the necessity of staying longer.

Alignment with the corporate midterm cycle

Fujitsu's corporate strategy operates on a three-year midterm plan cycle, ending in March 2025. Extending the assignment to align with this natural corporate rhythm meant transformation results could be captured within a unified strategic context. This was not arbitrary timing; it reflected how the company measured itself and communicated success to stakeholders.

The Uvance digital services portfolio, launched during this period with ambitious targets, also depended on a full planning cycle to demonstrate real impact. A point discussed at length in the episode, this alignment meant the transformation initiative could be measured not in isolation, but as part of the company's broader strategic delivery.

"Leadership is increasingly an act of translation. By that, I don't mean language translation, but context translation—the ability to move between worlds, between perspectives, between competing realities."

To understand the specific methodologies and cultural insights Bonamici deployed during her four years in Japan, the full interview explores her leadership philosophy and the concrete steps she took to bridge Japanese and Western business approaches.

Key takeaways

  • Six-month and 12-month checkpoints revealed that the transformation mandate required more than 18 months to deliver measurable impact.
  • Extending to four years aligned the initiative with Fujitsu's three-year midterm plan cycle, creating a unified strategic measurement framework.
  • The alignment allowed real business results—Fujitsu's most successful fiscal year—to be documented and published, validating the transformation effort.
  • Regular checkpoints and corporate planning cycles, rather than arbitrary timelines, should drive extension decisions in long-term transformation work.
