What contamination issues were encountered on the Coot Island redevelopment project?

22 September 2026 Creatures of the Industry · Ray Smith

← See the Creatures of the Industry podcast fiche

The Coot Island redevelopment site contained PFAS chemical residue from a historical fire that had leached into the subsoil, contamination that persists to this day. The groundworks uncovered buried materials alongside this chemical contamination, and the severity of the issue was significant enough to later impact the nearby Westgate project.

When Ray Smith arrived at Coot Island in January 2004 for his first construction job—transitioning from the music industry—the site was already in redevelopment. The environmental challenges hidden beneath the surface only became apparent once excavation work began.

PFAS persistence and cross-project impact

The contamination discovered at Coot Island was not isolated to that single site. The PFAS chemicals that seeped into the subsoil created a broader environmental concern that extended beyond the immediate redevelopment boundaries. As work progressed on subsequent projects in the area, this legacy contamination resurfaced as a complicating factor.

This is a point detailed in the episode, where Ray Smith reflects on the tangible obstacles that environmental remediation posed to construction crews. The discovery of chemical residue mixed with buried materials required workers to navigate both physical and regulatory challenges on site.

The Westgate project, which came later, faced its own complications stemming directly from the contamination legacy. This domino effect underscores how environmental issues at one site can ripple outward, affecting adjacent developments and extending cleanup costs across years.

For crews working at the site, understanding the full scope of Coot Island's environmental burden became essential context for managing worker safety and project timelines. PFAS contamination requires careful handling—it is not a substance that simply disappears once a site is cleared.
