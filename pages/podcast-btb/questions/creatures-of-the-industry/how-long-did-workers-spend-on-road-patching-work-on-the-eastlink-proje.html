How many kilometres of Eastlink road did Ray Smith patch over several years?

25 September 2026 Creatures of the Industry · Ray Smith

← See the Creatures of the Industry fiche

Ray Smith and his mate Ed patched approximately 30 kilometres of Eastlink over a period of a few years. They worked as subcontractors, hired by larger companies like Hollands and Fiesa, and were given a ute with their tools to work their way systematically through the patching sections.

Working through Eastlink with a ute and basic tools

Road patching on a major infrastructure project like Eastlink required neither complex machinery nor office oversight. Ray Smith and his mate were allocated a ute and their equipment, then sent out to cover specific sections of the highway. The work was repetitive but essential—filling, resurfacing, and maintaining asphalt across the sprawling route.

As Ray Smith recounts in the episode, this type of contract work was common on major projects. Workers were not directly employed by the main contractor; instead, they were subbed out to companies like Hollands or Fiesa, which specialized in labour-intensive tasks. This arrangement allowed larger construction firms to manage workload flexibly without maintaining permanent crews for every task.

The scale of the work—30 kilometres patched by just two workers over several years—speaks to the sheer length of the Eastlink corridor and the incremental nature of road maintenance. Rather than a single focused campaign, patching was an ongoing responsibility, with teams rotating through sections as needed.

"I loved the camaraderie and the mateship and just the fun these blokes were having on site. The jokes were endless."

The work culture on these road patching crews was distinct from larger building sites. The small teams and repetitive pace created a close-knit atmosphere, even if the labour itself was unglamorous. For Ray Smith, this marked a significant chapter in his career—part of Victoria's broader infrastructure expansion during the 2000s, which reshaped major transport corridors across the state.
