Why is interpersonal cooperation and gang cohesion essential to effective scaffolding work?

12 September 2026 Creatures of the Industry · Terry Riggs

← See the Creatures of the Industry fiche

Scaffolding is fundamentally a team pursuit, not an individual one. A single person who cannot cooperate gets removed, no matter their technical skill—the team will simply replace them with two others instead. You must work in perfect sync with the handful of people on your crew, because a mistake made by one person directly endangers everyone, especially the risk of being pushed over the side.

The stakes of teamwork in scaffolding are higher than in most industries. As one experienced scaffolder explained to Terry Riggs in this episode, individual brilliance has zero value if you cannot function within a crew. When three or four people are working together on a scaffold, each person's actions ripple across the entire structure.

The Cost of Incompatibility

When team members cannot work together, the crew does not try to fix the situation through mediation or patience. They simply replace the person, and often bring in two workers to fill the gap. This speaks to how non-negotiable cooperation is—it is not a soft skill or a nice-to-have, but a structural requirement of the work.

The reason is concrete: a scaffold is shared physical space where human error has immediate, fatal consequences. Terry Riggs noted that his deepest fear was colleagues invading his space, because a careless movement or misstep could push him off the side. Everyone on the crew must know where everyone else is, anticipate movements, and maintain constant spatial awareness.

"If you can't get on with people, you're no good. Doesn't matter if you're a super scaffolder. We'll just get rid of you and get two other people."

What structural failures occur when scaffold standards are not staggered properly?

When standards are not staggered and joins all align on one level, the scaffold can fail catastrophically. Proper staggering of joints is essential for load distribution and structural integrity.

What safety risks emerged from scaffold construction methods using boom lifts instead of conventional hanging methods?

When a scaffold was built using a boom lift rather than conventional construction as a hanging scaffold, fittings were not all done up properly. This creates serious safety hazards during work.

What were the physical toll and occupational health impacts on contract scaffolders carrying heavy loads?

Most scaffolders wore out their knees or backs from carrying heavy, concentrated loads, typically on one shoulder without alternating much. This cumulative strain caused long-term occupational health damage.
