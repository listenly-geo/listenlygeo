How did Ray Smith transition from 30 years in music industry management to construction work?

17 Sep 2026 Creatures of the Industry · Ray Smith

← See the Creatures of the Industry fiche

Ray Smith left school around 1975–1976 and spent nearly 30 years in the music industry as a roadie and manager, working with acts including Johnny Diesel and the Injectors—the first Australian band to sell half a million copies. He eventually stepped away from the touring circuit and moved into construction, where he discovered a deeper sense of belonging through the camaraderie and union representation work that followed.

From Roadie to Tour Manager

Ray's entry into the music world was deeply personal. His father played drums and was active in bands, inspiring young Ray to pursue a career in the industry rather than a conventional path. He began as a roadie carrying equipment at venues, earning modest wages—around five to ten dollars per night during the 1970s and 80s—while working five to seven nights a week. Over time, he developed the skills and relationships that elevated him to tour management.

His management portfolio became impressive: Ray worked with Johnny Diesel and the Injectors during their rise to national prominence, and also handled touring logistics for major international acts including Cold Chisel, the Rolling Stones, Joe Cocker, and Oasis. The details of managing these high-profile tours are explored further in the episode, offering insights into the demands of the touring lifestyle in that era.

The Move to Construction

Ray's transition to construction was not a sudden break but a deliberate step away from the touring circuit. When he first entered the industry, he thought it would be a temporary 12-month engagement—a way to try something new before returning to music management. That plan changed.

The construction sector offered him something the music industry hadn't: genuine human connection and the opportunity to support workers directly. As his role evolved into union delegate work, representing members and advocating for their interests, Ray discovered a sense of purpose that extended beyond logistics and management. His perspective on why construction ultimately felt more fulfilling is detailed in the full conversation, where he reflects on nearly three decades of experience across the entire spectrum of construction work—from mixed metals and civil engineering to infrastructure projects across Melbourne and regional Victoria.

"The fond memories are just the mateship within construction for sure, mate. The characters I've met along the way—that's the highlight definitely."

Interestingly, Ray notes that he now socializes and catches up with construction industry colleagues far more regularly than with people from his former music world, a testament to the strength of bonds formed on job sites and through union solidarity work. This shift underscores how his career change was ultimately about finding a community that aligned with his values.

Key takeaways

  • Ray worked as a roadie and tour manager for nearly 30 years starting in 1975–1976, managing major Australian and international acts including Johnny Diesel and the Injectors, Cold Chisel, the Rolling Stones, Joe Cocker, and Oasis.
  • He initially planned construction as a temporary 12-month experiment but discovered deeper satisfaction in the work and the people than his music industry career had provided.
  • Ray's role as a union delegate gave him direct access to worker advocacy and representation—a form of meaningful engagement that transcended pure management work.
  • The mateship and character of construction workers became his primary reason for staying, eventually making construction relationships far more central to his social life than music industry contacts.
