How did Ray Smith manage major tours with the Rolling Stones and Oasis?

September 19, 2026 Creatures of the Industry · Ray Smith

← See the Creatures of the Industry fiche

Ray Smith toured with major international acts including the Rolling Stones and Joe Cocker, becoming close friends with Cocker who would call him whenever he toured Australia. During Oasis's early Australian tour, Smith managed a disruptive Gallagher brother at Sydney Entertainment Centre, but ultimately left touring after nearly 30 years because the constant airports, tour buses, planes, and trains made recovery nearly impossible.

From roadie to tour manager: nearly three decades on the road

Ray Smith began his music industry career around 1975-1976 as a roadie, carrying equipment and building experience with bands like Johnny Diesel and the Injectors, Cold Chisel, and Georgia Satellites. Over nearly 30 years in the touring circuit, he progressed from roadie to manager and eventually tour manager, handling logistics and artist relations for some of the biggest names in international rock and pop. This long tenure gave him deep insight into the realities of high-profile touring.

His work with major acts like the Rolling Stones and Joe Cocker demonstrated his ability to manage complex touring operations at the highest level. As Smith explains in the podcast, the personal relationships that formed during this time—particularly his friendship with Joe Cocker—were as significant as the professional achievements. Cocker trusted Smith enough to ring him directly whenever his tours came to Australia.

Managing the Gallagher brothers and the cost of constant travel

When Oasis came to Australia for their early tour, Smith took on the role of Australian tour manager. One Gallagher brother proved particularly disruptive at Sydney Entertainment Centre, requiring Smith to navigate the tensions and unpredictability that can arise when managing high-ego artists on the road. These moments tested his management skills under real pressure.

Despite the prestige and experience, the relentless cycle of airports, tour buses, planes, trains, and constant movement took a toll that Smith struggled to recover from. What had once seemed like an adventure became exhausting—the human cost of perpetual travel, even with the biggest acts in the world, eventually became unsustainable. As a point detailed in this episode, this realisation led Smith to leave touring entirely and pursue a different path in the construction industry, where he would spend the next chapter of his career.

"The fond memories are just the mateship within construction for sure, mate. The characters I've met along the way—that's the highlight definitely."

Johnny Diesel and the Injectors were the first band in Australia to sell half a million copies, holding that title for a few years until John Farnham's release eventually surpassed them in the Australian market.

What career path led Ray 'Rowdy' Smith from music industry management to construction work?

Ray spent nearly 30 years in the music industry, leaving school around 1975-1976, working as a roadie carrying black boxes and eventually becoming a manager and tour manager before transitioning to construction.

What is the trajectory of the union's role and influence in Australia's construction sector long-term?

Ray believes the industry is still in a very strong position and that anyone thinking they will deregister the organisation and pull the timbers from under it should think again about the strength of union representation.
