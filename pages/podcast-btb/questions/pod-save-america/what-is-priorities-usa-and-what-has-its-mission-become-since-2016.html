What is Priorities USA and what has its mission become since 2016?

August 24, 2026 Pod Save America · Danielle Butterfield

← Voir la fiche Pod Save America

Priorities USA was originally a presidential super PAC that spent tens of millions of dollars electing Barack Obama, Hillary Clinton, and Joe Biden. After 2016, it pivoted to diagnose and close the Democratic Party's digital investment gap — building an in-house team dedicated to innovation, data, and research to prepare more effectively for each subsequent presidential election.

From presidential super PAC to digital laboratory for the Democratic Party

Before the 2016 reckoning, Priorities USA functioned like most major super PACs: raise money, run ads, elect a president, repeat. It played that role successfully across three presidential cycles, backing Obama, Clinton, and Biden.

The 2016 election changed the organization's self-understanding. Rather than continue as a purely electoral spending vehicle, Priorities USA set out to answer a harder question: why was the Democratic Party systematically under-investing in digital advertising — and what would it take to fix that? This shift in mission is detailed in Pod Save America's conversation with Danielle Butterfield.

Super PAC: A type of independent expenditure-only political action committee in the United States that can raise unlimited funds from corporations, unions, and individuals to spend on elections — but cannot donate directly to or coordinate with candidates or parties.

Building in-house rather than outsourcing: why the distinction matters

The structural decision Butterfield negotiated when she joined in 2017 was deliberate: no outside consultants driven by profit margins. An in-house team focused purely on innovation, not on billing hours or defending existing vendor relationships.

That internal model freed Priorities USA to invest in unglamorous but essential infrastructure — research, data, and testing — across off-year election cycles, not just during presidential campaigns. As Butterfield explains in this episode of Pod Save America, the goal was to build organizational muscle that outside consultants had no incentive to develop.

The stakes became clearer in hindsight. In 2024, Democrats outspent Republicans two to one on digital advertising — and still lost. Spending more was never the problem. Spending smarter, on the right platforms with the right formats, was. That diagnosis sits at the core of what Priorities USA now does, a point explored further in the full episode.

"Nine out of the ten top YouTube ads in 2024 were just 30-second ads that ran on TV first. We are quite literally practically taking 30-second ads that we run on TV and then putting them on YouTube and expecting people to watch them — and that is quite literally lighting money on fire."

Key takeaways

  • Priorities USA spent tens of millions electing Obama, Clinton, and Biden before 2016 forced a fundamental rethink of its purpose.
  • The post-2016 pivot was structural: an in-house team built for innovation, not for profit — replacing the outside-consultant model entirely.
  • Democrats outspending Republicans two to one on digital in 2024 while still losing proved that raw spending volume was never the real lever.
  • By 2026, the organization was already running a $30 million campaign to define 2028 Republicans, operating well beyond its original presidential-cycle mandate.
