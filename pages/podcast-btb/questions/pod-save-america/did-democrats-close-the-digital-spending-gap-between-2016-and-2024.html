Did Democrats actually close the digital spending gap with Republicans by 2024?

September 16, 2026 Pod Save America · Danielle Butterfield

← See the Pod Save America fiche

Yes—Democrats outspent Republicans two to one on digital advertising in 2024, closing a gap that had plagued the party since 2016. But the real problem is not money: it's strategy. Democrats are still running digital programs that mirror television tactics, which means their spending advantage buys far less effectiveness than the raw numbers suggest.

The diagnosis is straightforward but damning. As Danielle Butterfield explains in the episode, the party solved the headline problem—the budget gap—without solving the deeper structural problem: how Democrats think about digital as a medium.

Nine out of the ten top YouTube ads in 2024 were 30-second spots that had originally run on television. Democrats were taking television creative, uploading it to YouTube, and expecting engagement that mirrors the television experience—passive viewing in a living room. On YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, and emerging platforms like ChatGPT and Gemini, that assumption collapses. The format, pacing, and cognitive context are entirely different.

This strategic misalignment matters most where it counts. Priorities USA's post-2024 research found that men watching more than three hours of YouTube per day showed among the highest vote swings toward Trump of any demographic segment studied. Yet the ads reaching them were designed for a medium those men had stopped consuming in significant numbers.

"Nine out of the ten top YouTube ads in 2024 were just 30-second ads that ran on TV first. We are quite literally practically taking 30-second ads that we run on TV and then putting them on YouTube and expecting people to watch them — and that is quite literally lighting money on fire."

The deeper insight is that closing a spending gap is diagnostic theater if the underlying playbook remains rooted in broadcast logic. Butterfield's team is already running a $30 million 2026 campaign to define Republican 2028 candidates, informed by research across TikTok, YouTube, Instagram, and AI chatbots—platforms where strategy cannot simply be a TV ad transplant. That structural rethinking, not more budget allocation, is what Democratic digital strategy needed to learn between 2016 and 2024.

Key takeaways

  • Democrats closed the raw spending gap by 2024, outspending Republicans two to one on digital advertising—but budgets do not solve structural strategy problems.
  • Nine of the ten top YouTube ads in 2024 were 30-second television spots simply repurposed for digital platforms, a mismatch that wasted resources.
  • Men consuming three or more hours of YouTube daily showed the highest Trump vote swing in Priorities USA's research, yet reached them with television-formatted creative.
  • Solving the digital problem requires rethinking platform-native strategy, not increasing spend on television-derived content.
