How accurate were the Iowa electronic markets at predicting elections compared to national polls?

September 25, 2026 Freakonomics Radio · Tarek Mansour

← See the Freakonomics Radio fiche

One research study comparing Iowa electronic markets to a large group of national polls found that the Iowa markets beat the polls 74% of the time in predicting election outcomes. The Iowa markets operated on real money with stakes as high as $500 per trader, creating genuine skin in the game for a small but committed community of traders.

Why Small Stakes and Real Money Made the Difference

The Iowa electronic markets originally called the Iowa political stock market proved remarkably effective precisely because they combined real financial incentives with a tightly managed trading environment. Participants weren't speculating with play money or abstract tokens—they risked their own dollars, which fundamentally changes behavior and commitment.

The market operated on a modest scale with just a few hundred active traders, each capped at $500 per contract. This constraint wasn't a limitation; it was a feature. The real-money mechanism ensured that traders had genuine accountability, unlike national polls which measure sentiment at a single moment without financial consequences.

The Structural Advantage Over Traditional Polling

National polls aggregate responses from individual respondents at a specific point in time. These respondents have no financial stake in their answer and often change their stated preferences based on question framing or timing. Prediction markets replace opinion with incentivized prediction, shifting the focus from what people say they think to what they're willing to bet on.

The Iowa markets beat polls in 74% of head-to-head comparisons because traders continuously update their positions based on new information. When a candidate makes a gaffe, drops in early primary results, or shifts campaign strategy, traders immediately adjust their bets to reflect the new reality. This dynamic adaptation is something a traditional poll, conducted on a fixed schedule, simply cannot replicate.

The results show that when forecasting tools are designed around real consequences and continuous updating, they outperform methods based on static sentiment measurement.

Prediction Market: A marketplace where participants trade contracts whose value depends on the outcome of a future event. Unlike a poll (which asks people what they think will happen), a prediction market lets people bet real money on what they believe will happen. The market price of a contract reflects the collective belief of all traders about that outcome's probability.

To understand how dominant the Iowa markets became at forecasting, consider that this wasn't a massive platform with millions of users. It was a niche community of just a few hundred traders working with limited capital, yet it still outpredicted the combined wisdom of dozens of national polling organizations. The 74% win rate suggests that structural design—real money, continuous trading, and transparent pricing—matters far more than sample size.

What problem in distributed information did economist Friedrich Hayek identify that prediction markets can solve?

During World War II, economist Frederick Hayek identified the knowledge problem: decisions are often centralized with authority figures like governments, but distributed knowledge across markets can aggregate information more efficiently than centralized decision-making.

How did Kalshi win regulatory approval for election prediction markets?

Kalshi sued the CFTC after they initially denied permission for election contracts. A federal appeals court ruled in Kalshi's favor in fall 2024, clearing the legal path for election prediction markets to operate legitimately.

Why did Kalshi choose a regulatory-first approach instead of launching quickly like competitors?

Kalshi abided by a core principle of being regulatory first, spending four years obtaining full regulatory approval before launching a single market in 2021, building legitimacy and compliance into their foundation.
