How does medication-induced weight gain affect body image and what strategies can help reframe it positively?

Published on 2026-09-21 Cliterally Obsessed · Sarah Denny

← See the Cliterally Obsessed fiche

Medication-induced weight gain doesn't have to damage your body image if you reframe it as a conscious trade-off for health. When Sarah Denny gained 25 pounds in a few months while managing severe sciatica pain with medication, she chose to view the weight gain as an opportunity for intentional muscle-building rather than letting it erode her confidence—because the medication gave her life back.

For nearly two years, Denny had been unable to walk properly or sleep through the night due to sciatica pain. The medication that relieved her symptoms came with a side effect: weight gain, from 165 to 190 pounds in just a few months. Rather than resenting this physical change, she made a deliberate mental shift.

Her strategy was straightforward but powerful: she decided to "make it a bulk"—shifting from viewing the weight gain as an unwanted consequence to treating it as the foundation for intentional strength-building. By redefining the situation on her own terms, Denny regained agency over her body and used the momentum to focus on what she could control: building muscle mass and increasing her strength as a power lifter.

"If I'm going to gain weight, I'm going to just make it a bulk and really just try to put on as much muscle as I possibly can."

This reframing allowed Denny to see the bigger picture: the medication had restored her quality of life in ways that mattered far more than body weight. She could walk, sleep through the night, and spend time actively with her daughter again. From this perspective, the weight gain became a worthwhile and temporary trade-off, not a failure of body image or self-care.

As Denny explains in the Cliterally Obsessed episode, the key is shifting from shame to agency—taking ownership of the narrative around your body rather than allowing a side effect to dictate your self-worth. This mindset is especially powerful for anyone navigating medication changes, chronic pain management, or other life circumstances where body composition shifts are temporary or necessary.

Reframing the Trade-Off: Health Over Appearance

Denny's experience reveals a core truth about body image: it's less about the number on the scale and more about what your body allows you to do. When medication enables you to function—to move, sleep, be present with loved ones—the visual cost becomes secondary.

This is not about ignoring body changes or pretending they don't matter. Instead, it's about consciously weighing the gains (pain relief, restored mobility, better sleep) against the losses (temporary weight gain) and deciding that the trade was worth making. You can acknowledge the change and still choose to reframe its meaning.

Key takeaways

  • Medication-induced weight gain becomes more manageable when you prioritize the health benefits it delivers—such as pain relief, better sleep, and restored mobility.
  • Reframing a physical change as a conscious trade-off or even an opportunity (like intentional muscle-building) removes the shame and restores your sense of control.
  • Body image is linked to function and capability, not just appearance; when medication lets you do things you couldn't before, that shift in perspective can outweigh cosmetic concerns.
  • Temporary weight changes tied to health management are often worth the trade-off when measured against long-term quality of life and ability to be present with loved ones.
