How did a career transition from paralegal to personal trainer originate?

September 25, 2026 Cliterally Obsessed · Sarah Denny

← See the Cliterally Obsessed fiche

A single conversation at the hairdresser sparked Sarah Denny's complete career pivot. While venting about her unhappiness as a paralegal, her hairdresser suggested she pursue her passion for fitness—prompting her to contact her former personal trainer about certifications and ultimately leave condo law for full-time coaching.

An unexpected nudge in an ordinary moment

Sarah Denny was deeply unhappy in her paralegal role. The work—centered on condo law—felt repetitive, uninspiring, and emotionally draining. She wasn't simply looking for a new job; she was searching for a way out of a career that no longer aligned with her values or interests.

The turning point came not in a formal career counseling session or after months of deliberation, but in a casual conversation at the salon. As Sarah shares in the Cliterally Obsessed episode, her hairdresser sensed her distress and offered a simple observation: why not turn her passion for fitness into a career? That moment of reflection, offered by someone outside her immediate professional circle, became the catalyst she needed.

What followed was action. Sarah reached out to her former personal trainer to explore certifications and explore the practical steps required to transition into coaching. That inquiry became a commitment: she made the decision to leave her paralegal position and pursue personal training full-time.

From corporate unhappiness to a decade of coaching

Today, Sarah has built a 10-year career as a personal trainer and power lifter. Her journey from condo law to fitness coaching demonstrates how sometimes the permission—or simply the perspective—we need to change direction comes from the most unexpected sources. She began her coaching career working at Good Life Fitness for five years, then transitioned to independent online coaching, where she continues to train clients and share her passion for strength and transformation.

What makes her story particularly relevant is that this wasn't a gradual drift into fitness; it was a deliberate break from a path that no longer served her. The hairdresser's question essentially gave her permission to pursue what she already knew she loved—and that permission was enough to change her entire life trajectory. What early workplace experience motivated a shift from weakness to physical strength?

While working at Blockbuster Video, Sarah Denny could not pick up a crate of ice cream from the freezer—a task she had to perform nightly. Her managers and colleagues made her feel inadequate, which became a powerful motivator to build strength through fitness.

How did a childhood diagnosis of endometriosis and rheumatoid arthritis influence entry into personal training?

Sarah Denny was diagnosed with endometriosis at age 15 and rheumatoid arthritis at age 21, experiences that caused significant body pain and initially made her avoid exercise. After a doctor's recommendation at age 24 to start working out, she discovered fitness as both healing and transformative, eventually leading to her career in personal training.

What role does exercise play in recovery from serious physical injury and chronic pain conditions?

Sarah Denny suffered a massive injury involving sciatica-type pain that lasted almost two years and prevented her from walking, sleeping, cooking, or performing daily activities. Through structured exercise and coaching, she recovered her mobility and built resilience, demonstrating that targeted physical training can be transformative for managing chronic pain.
