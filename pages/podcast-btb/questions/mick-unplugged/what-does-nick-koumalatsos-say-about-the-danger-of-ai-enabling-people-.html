Is AI making it dangerously easy for people to fake expertise and position themselves as coaches?

Nick Koumalatsos fully agrees with host Mick Hunt's concern: AI has made it possible for someone to do something once — or even just research it — and immediately present themselves as an expert or coach. Koumalatsos argues this is a serious problem, because his own coaching credibility rests entirely on having personally lived through every situation he coaches others on. If you haven't done it, AI-generated content cannot substitute for that lived foundation.

The standard Koumalatsos applies is one he calls "receipts" — verifiable, real-world proof that you have actually built, exited, or operated the thing you claim to teach. In his view, if those receipts don't exist, the advice has no genuine evidentiary basis, regardless of how polished or authoritative it sounds. AI, in this framing, makes the surface presentation easier while leaving the underlying credibility gap completely intact.

This isn't an abstract concern for Koumalatsos. His own path from Marine Corps special operations operator to entrepreneur involved co-founding businesses across completely different industries — from the grooming brand Johnny Slicks, to Core Medical, to his leadership coaching platform Agogi — each built from direct operational involvement, not from curated content. About Nick Koumalatsos

NK

Nick Koumalatsos

Co-founder, entrepreneur, and leadership coach

Agogi

Koumalatsos joined the Marine Corps at 18 and graduated recruit training at 19, going on to serve in Force Reconnaissance — one of the military's most demanding special operations roles. He rose to team operations chief before leaving the service, accumulating the kind of high-stakes, real-world decision-making experience that underpins everything he now teaches.

After the military, he channeled that operational discipline into building multiple businesses from scratch: Johnny Slicks, a grooming brand; Core Medical, a medical company; and Agogi, his leadership coaching and development platform. He has built and scaled 8-figure brands, and his coaching roster includes entrepreneurs who have achieved exits at the scale of competing directly against companies like Nike in the fitness wear market.

What makes Koumalatsos's voice on AI-enabled fake expertise particularly credible is the breadth of his own receipts. His coaching does not draw on theory, certifications, or research — it draws on a documented track record of building real companies, managing real P&Ls, and navigating real personal adversity, including divorce and a childhood without a consistent father figure. These lived experiences are, in his own framing, the only legitimate basis for the coaching he delivers through Agogi.
