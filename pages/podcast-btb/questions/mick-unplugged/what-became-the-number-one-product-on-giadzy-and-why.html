What became the number one product on Giadzy — and why did it surprise even Giada De Laurentiis?

August 24, 2026 Mick Unplugged · Giada De Laurentiis

← See the Mick Unplugged podcast page

Gluten-free pasta became the number one product on Giadzy — a result that caught De Laurentiis completely off guard, since she is not gluten-free herself. The demand came from followers who wanted a pasta that holds up to sauce, tastes indistinguishable from the regular version, and contains no fillers or gums. Sourced from Italy, bronze-cut, and slow-dried, the product has since expanded into retail, where De Laurentiis says buyers are actively craving it.

A product born from a very specific community demand

De Laurentiis did not set out to build a gluten-free pasta line. The product emerged entirely from listener and follower feedback — a concentrated signal from the Giadzy community about a real gap in the market. People wanted gluten-free pasta that performed like real pasta: holding its texture, absorbing sauce properly, and delivering a clean ingredient list with no additives.

That specificity mattered. As explored across multiple conversations on Mick Unplugged, De Laurentiis built Giadzy by listening closely to what her audience was actually asking for — not what she assumed they needed. The gluten-free pasta is a direct product of that discipline.

Italian sourcing and bronze-cut process: the non-negotiables that made it work

What distinguishes Giadzy's gluten-free pasta is not the absence of gluten alone — it is the production method. The pasta is sourced from Italy, bronze-cut (which creates a rougher surface that grips sauce), and slow-dried to preserve texture and flavor. These are the same standards applied to traditional Italian pasta, applied here without compromise.

De Laurentiis was insistent that the product taste indistinguishable from regular pasta. That bar — not just "acceptable for gluten-free" but genuinely equal — is what drove the formulation. The result is a product that converted even non-gluten-free consumers into repeat buyers, which explains its rise to the top of the Giadzy catalog. This kind of uncompromising product thinking is a recurring thread in this episode of Mick Unplugged.

The success has not stayed online. Retailers are now stocking the product, and De Laurentiis describes the reception from buyers as enthusiastic — using the word "craving" to characterize retailer demand. For a brand that launched during COVID as a direct-to-consumer e-commerce play, the move into physical retail marks a meaningful shift in scale. De Laurentiis shares the full arc of that growth in her conversation with Mick Hunt.

"If we can be laughing and having fun and enjoying the people that we're around while we're eating, we will feel better. That piece of the longevity piece is just as important as the food you're putting on the table."

The quote above reflects a broader philosophy De Laurentiis brings to food: that the experience of eating matters as much as the ingredients themselves. It is the same philosophy that shapes Giadzy's product standards — food that performs well, tastes honest, and earns its place at the table. What inspired Giada De Laurentiis to launch Giadzy and how did the business evolve?

Giadzy began because fans of her Food Network shows kept asking what specific brands of olive oil and pasta she was using, but on-set rules prohibited her from naming them on air. That gap between audience demand and what she could share on television became the foundation for the brand.

How did Giada De Laurentiis discover that her Italian culinary knowledge was unique and valuable to teach others?

De Laurentiis says she never thought what she was doing was particularly unique because Italian cooking was simply how she grew up. It was only after launching her platform and witnessing the audience response that she understood the depth and rarity of what she had to share.

What was Giada De Laurentiis's biggest challenge when she first started on Food Network?

De Laurentiis says she was incredibly shy when she started her Food Network journey and it took her a long time to get comfortable storytelling while cooking at the same time — following a recipe, making it look effortless, and keeping the audience engaged, all at once.

Key takeaways

  • Gluten-free pasta is Giadzy's top-selling product — driven entirely by community demand, not by De Laurentiis's personal dietary choices.
  • The product's success hinges on three non-negotiables: Italian sourcing, bronze-cut production, and slow-drying — with zero fillers or gums.
  • The bar was set at taste parity with regular pasta, not merely "acceptable for gluten-free" — a distinction that drove adoption beyond the gluten-free segment.
  • Retail buyers are now actively seeking the product, signaling a shift from direct-to-consumer origins to mainstream distribution for the Giadzy brand.
