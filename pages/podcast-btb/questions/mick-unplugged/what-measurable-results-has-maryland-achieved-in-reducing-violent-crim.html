How has Maryland cut homicide rates and reformed criminal justice under Governor Moore?

September 3, 2026 Mick Unplugged · Wes Moore

← See the Mick Unplugged fiche

Maryland has achieved a nearly 60 percent reduction in the homicide rate since Governor Moore took office. The state also issued the largest mass pardon in United States history, pardoning over 175,000 misdemeanor cannabis convictions. These results came through collaborative work with mayors, local law enforcement, community violence interrupters, and partners across jurisdictions rather than through any single solution.

A collaborative approach to public safety

These measurable outcomes didn't emerge from top-down mandates or isolated policy initiatives. Instead, Governor Moore worked across jurisdictions and agencies to align efforts on reducing violence and reforming the criminal justice system. As discussed in the episode, the strategy involved partners at every level—from local mayors to community violence interrupters—recognizing that no single entity can solve these challenges alone.

The homicide reduction reflects both immediate public safety gains and long-term systemic change. Maryland's approach centered on understanding that criminal justice reform and violent crime reduction are interconnected goals, not competing priorities. The mass pardon, in particular, represents a significant shift in how the state treats non-violent cannabis convictions that had historically disrupted communities and created barriers to employment and housing.

"Service saved me. Service will save us because service saved me."

Moore's personal history informs his approach to criminal justice reform. Having experienced the system firsthand and benefited from mentors who saw potential in him, he brings both empathy and conviction to these initiatives. This perspective shaped the collaborative model that produced Maryland's measurable results—recognizing that rehabilitation, opportunity, and community investment are essential components of real public safety.
