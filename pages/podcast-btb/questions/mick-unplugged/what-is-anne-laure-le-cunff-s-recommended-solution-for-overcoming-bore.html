What does Anne-Laure Le Cunff recommend to overcome 'bored out'?

Anne-Laure Le Cunff's solution to 'bored out' is deliberately simple: a 15-minute daily injection of novelty. As she shared on Mitch Matthews' podcast, this small, intentional dose of something new — learning a skill, spending time with someone creative, picking up a hobby, or dipping into an entrepreneurial side project — is enough to reignite a high achiever's sense of aliveness without dismantling their existing career or life.

What is "bored out"? 'Bored out' describes a state of chronic understimulation that produces symptoms — exhaustion, disengagement, low motivation — nearly identical to burnout. The critical difference: burnout comes from too much, while 'bored out' comes from too little meaningful stimulation. Le Cunff identified this pattern specifically in high achievers who are professionally stable but privately unfulfilled.

The elegance of Le Cunff's framework is that it requires no dramatic life overhaul. The prescription isn't to quit a job, pivot a career, or pursue a passion full-time. It's a micro-intervention: fifteen minutes of genuine novelty, repeated consistently. That novelty could look like many things — a new subject to study, a conversation with someone who energizes you, a creative hobby that has nothing to do with your professional identity, or the early steps of an entrepreneurial adventure kept deliberately small and low-stakes. The goal is stimulation, not disruption.

Mitch Matthews, who has spent over 25 years coaching high-capacity professionals, has seen this pattern play out in his own life and in his clients'. The pull toward something new on the side — before it becomes anything serious — is often the earliest signal that 'bored out' has set in. His own career pivot from pharmaceutical sales into coaching began exactly that way: a small exploration alongside an existing job, not a leap into the void.
