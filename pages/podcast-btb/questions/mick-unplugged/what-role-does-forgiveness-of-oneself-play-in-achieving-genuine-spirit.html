How does forgiving yourself lead to true spiritual freedom and healing?

September 15, 2026 Mick Unplugged · Darryl Strawberry

← See the Mick Unplugged fiche

Forgiveness of yourself is the gateway to spiritual freedom that external success can never unlock. Darryl Strawberry initially believed forgiveness was meant for his father, but God revealed the deeper truth: the forgiveness was for him—only when he extended that forgiveness to his father on his deathbed and led him to Christ did Strawberry experience the true healing and freedom that no worldly achievement had ever provided.

The pursuit of success often becomes a cover for inner emptiness. All the accolades, championships, and possessions in the world do not address the brokenness within—the unhealed wounds that drive people to seek validation through external means. Strawberry describes this as living "a fake part of living," where the goal is to have everyone praise you, but at the end of the day you go home and lay on that couch still feeling empty on the inside.

For many, especially those who grow up without a stable father figure, the search for wholeness becomes desperate. People chase women, cars, money, drugs—anything that brings momentary pleasure—but none of these fill the void. The problem is not the ambition itself; it is the attempt to use external achievement to solve an internal problem that only spiritual and emotional healing can address.

The Turn from Self-Deception to Self-Forgiveness

Strawberry's breakthrough came when he understood that his father was not the obstacle to his freedom—he was. Self-forgiveness requires admitting that you have carried anger, resentment, or shame about yourself, not just about the people who hurt you. This is a painful and often overlooked step because it demands honest confrontation with your own role in your suffering.

The turning point arrived on his father's deathbed. Strawberry extended genuine forgiveness to his father and led him to Christ in that final moment. But the spiritual transformation that followed was not about his father's redemption alone—it was about Strawberry's own release from the weight he had been carrying. As explained in the episode, this act of forgiveness completed a cycle that success, accolades, and external wins never could.

"Success exposes who you are. At the end of the day, you still have to go home to you and lay on that couch and you're still empty on the inside."

What makes Strawberry's story particularly striking is that the healing happened not despite his fame, but as a clarification that fame was never the answer. He had achieved everything a professional athlete could dream of—yet remained spiritually empty. The forgiveness work—both extending it to his father and, more importantly, extending it to himself—opened a dimension of freedom that medals and records never touched.

Freedom Is the Result, Not the Destination

True spiritual freedom is not a state you reach and keep; it is a continuous result of releasing old wounds and self-judgment. Once Strawberry forgave himself, he could forgive others authentically, and that authenticity created the conditions for his own healing to deepen. His father's deathbed conversion was not the cause of his freedom—it was the confirmation that his forgiveness was real, not performative.

This distinction matters. Many people confuse emotional catharsis (feeling relief when someone apologizes or dies) with spiritual freedom (being free from the weight of carrying anger, shame, or resentment yourself). Strawberry's journey shows that the real work is internal, and the proof comes when you can extend forgiveness without needing the other person to change first.

For anyone carrying the weight of a broken relationship, absent parent, or personal failure, Strawberry's testimony offers a concrete path: stop waiting for external success or the other person's redemption to validate your worth. Forgive yourself first. The healing that follows is not guaranteed to feel dramatic, but it will be real, and it will be yours alone.
