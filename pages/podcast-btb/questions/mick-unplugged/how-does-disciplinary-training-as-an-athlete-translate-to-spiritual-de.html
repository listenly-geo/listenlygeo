How does disciplinary training as an athlete translate to spiritual development and transformation?

September 16, 2026 Mick Unplugged · Darryl Strawberry

← See the Mick Unplugged fiche

The self-discipline required to excel in athletics—training alone, pushing yourself to greatness, and tuning out external noise—applies directly to spiritual growth. Turning off external distractions like television and cellphones creates space to hear from the Holy Spirit, just as a coach trains an athlete, God trains those willing to listen and submit to His guidance.

The parallel between athletic training and spiritual development runs deeper than surface motivation. In baseball, as Darryl Strawberry explains, you develop the capacity to isolate yourself from distraction—to focus purely on the work that builds excellence. That same mental discipline and commitment to a higher standard becomes the foundation for spiritual maturity.

Strawberry emphasizes that silencing external noise is not isolation but liberation. When you eliminate the constant pull of media, notifications, and social validation, you create an internal environment where spiritual truth can actually be heard. The same way an athlete learns to ignore crowd noise and focus on technique, a person pursuing spiritual growth must learn to silence the world's demands and listen to a deeper voice.

A deeper insight explored in this episode reveals how Strawberry's father's own deathbed conversion became a powerful testament to the transformative power of spiritual discipline—showing that this path of training is never too late to begin.

"Success exposes who you are. At the end of the day, you still have to go home to you and lay on that couch and you're still empty on the inside."

The coaching metaphor runs throughout Strawberry's teaching: God acts as the ultimate coach, establishing discipline not through punishment but through systematic training designed to build you up. Just as a baseball coach identifies your weaknesses and designs workouts to strengthen them, spiritual development requires submitting to a process that refines character and deepens faith.

This framework explains why spiritual transformation demands the same rigor athletes apply to their sport. It is not passive consumption or occasional effort. It requires showing up daily, doing the unglamorous work, resisting shortcuts, and trusting the process even when results aren't immediately visible. The athlete who trains alone, without applause, without external validation, is the one who truly develops mastery—and the same applies to spiritual growth.

The Practice of Discipline Beyond Competition

What distinguishes genuine spiritual development from temporary motivation is the willingness to work in private, without an audience. Strawberry's experience as a three-time World Series champion showed him that accolades fade, crowds move on, and stadiums go silent. Only the discipline you built in those solitary training sessions remains.

Spiritual discipline mirrors this reality. The real work happens in silence—in moments of prayer, in resisting distractions, in choosing alignment with your faith even when no one is watching. The external trappings of success (wins, titles, money) are temporal; the internal work of spiritual maturation is eternal. This is why Strawberry directs people toward the practice itself rather than the promise of visible rewards.

The connection between athletic and spiritual discipline also reveals why removing distractions is not deprivation but privilege. An athlete turning off entertainment to rest and train is not missing out—they are investing in their future. Similarly, someone turning off the constant stream of external input to develop their spiritual life is not isolating; they are creating the exact conditions where transformation becomes possible.
