What role does moral leadership play in solving America's biggest challenges?

September 6, 2026 Mick Unplugged · Wes Moore

← See the Mick Unplugged fiche

Throughout American history, only two things have lifted the country out of difficult moments: God's grace and moral leadership. When people know their history, discover what drives them, and act on that purpose, sustainable progress becomes possible—but only when everyone is brought along on that journey, not just some.

Governor Wes Moore roots this conviction in the relationship between personal purpose and collective movement. The challenge, as he frames it, is not simply having leaders with moral clarity, but ensuring that moral vision translates into action that benefits the entire nation. This distinction matters: a leader who fails to bring people along cannot create lasting change, no matter how strong their convictions.

For Moore, moral leadership begins with self-knowledge. As he explains in the episode, people must first understand where they come from and what actually moves them—what he calls "what makes your heart beat faster." Without that foundation, leadership becomes hollow performance rather than authentic conviction.

"Service saved me. Service will save us because service saved me."

Moore's path from foster care to the governor's mansion illustrates the principle he articulates: moral leadership is not an abstract ideal but a lived practice grounded in the conviction that service—whether in the military, business, or government—creates the conditions for transformation. When leaders have genuinely experienced hardship and overcome it through deliberate action, their moral authority rings credible.

The practical implication is striking: progress at scale requires that leaders do more than set policy; they must actively bring the public along in understanding why the journey matters. This is why Moore emphasizes inclusion—leaving no one behind is not merely compassionate, it is strategically essential to sustainable change.

What guardrails and guidelines should govern data center development to balance economic growth with environmental and community concerns?

Governor Moore has implemented three key guidelines for data centers in Maryland: they must bring their own energy and pay for any infrastructure necessary to support their operations.

What comprehensive energy strategy can states adopt to address rising energy costs and grid reliability?

Governor Moore advocates for an "all the above approach" to energy, including solar, wind, battery storage, and nuclear power. He emphasizes that states need diversified energy portfolios to ensure both reliability and affordability.

What measurable results has Maryland achieved in reducing violent crime and criminal justice reform?

Maryland has achieved a nearly 60 percent reduction in the homicide rate since Governor Moore took office. Additionally, he issued the largest mass pardon in United States history, pardoning 175,000 misdemeanor cannabis convictions.
