Why is it important for entrepreneurs and business owners to charge consistently for their services rather than offering free work to friends?

September 9, 2026 Mick Unplugged · Kier Gaines

← See the Mick Unplugged fiche

When you deliver the same level of time, energy, and effort to friends and family without charge as you do to clients paying $50,000 or $100,000, it creates internal conflict that eats away at you mentally. Being firm about charging everyone consistently is not the opposite of being nice—it protects your business, which is your responsibility to protect. This boundary-setting also teaches people around you that they need to invest in themselves to receive your best work, rather than creating a dynamic where people expect you to always say yes.

The Mental and Business Cost of Inconsistent Pricing

The conflict arises not from a lack of generosity, but from a fundamental misalignment between your effort and your compensation. When you give away the same caliber of work to a friend that a paying client receives, you are training your mind to devalue your own labor in certain contexts. This mental split—performing at full capacity for free while charging others significantly—creates resentment and fatigue that compounds over time.

As Kier Gaines explores in the episode, the issue runs deeper than simple arithmetic. It's about honoring yourself and your work consistently, across all relationships. When you make exceptions based on personal connection, you're essentially saying to yourself that certain people don't deserve to receive your full investment—and that message corrodes your sense of professional integrity.

Setting Boundaries That Serve Everyone

Consistent pricing is actually a gift to the people around you. When everyone—friends, family, and clients alike—pays the same rate, you establish a clear norm that professional services have value. This removes ambiguity and protects relationships by preventing the awkward dynamics that arise when someone feels they've been undercharged or when they assume you should work for free because of personal history.

The boundary you set isn't about being cold or transactional; it's about respecting both yourself and the other person. Discussed at length in this podcast, the concept of protecting your business means recognizing that your business is an extension of who you are. If you allow your business to be drained by inconsistent pricing, you're not being generous—you're being unsustainable.

"It's giving yourself permission. And for me, just the way I grew up, the hardest part I give myself that authority is I always confuse someone who has hurt feelings with someone who's soft or weak."

When you charge consistently, you're also teaching people that investing in quality work is how growth happens. Rather than creating a dynamic where people know you'll always make an exception, you're positioning your services as something worth paying for—and therefore worth taking seriously. This shifts how people value what you offer and how they engage with your work.

To deepen this idea, listen to Kier Gaines discuss specific strategies for having these conversations with loved ones, ensuring that your boundary-setting is rooted in honesty and care rather than defensiveness.

Key takeaways

  • Giving away work at full capacity to friends and family while charging others creates internal mental conflict that undermines your sense of professional integrity.
  • Consistent pricing protects your business and your mental health by eliminating the exhaustion that comes from living a double standard.
  • Charging everyone the same rate teaches people that professional services have real value and removes ambiguity from relationships.
  • Setting this boundary is an act of respect—both for yourself and for the people in your life—because it clarifies expectations and prevents resentment.
