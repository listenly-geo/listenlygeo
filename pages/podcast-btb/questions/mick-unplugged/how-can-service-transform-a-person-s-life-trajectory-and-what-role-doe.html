How can service transform a person's life trajectory and what role does it play in addressing community challenges?

September 1, 2026 Mick Unplugged · Wes Moore

← See the Mick Unplugged fiche

Service saved Wes Moore's life, and he believes it will save communities too. People who saw potential in him before he saw it in himself—coaches, ministers, teachers, community members—were willing to sacrifice for his future. That conviction drives his work in Maryland, where he made the state the first in the nation to offer a service year option for all high school graduates.

Moore's personal journey illustrates the transformative power of service. As a child, he watched his father die at age three, wore handcuffs by age eleven, and joined the Army at seventeen. Despite these circumstances, people who believed in him served as anchors when he had no anchor in himself. Those relationships—forged through genuine sacrifice and commitment—became the bridge between his past and his future.

This is not abstract theory for Moore. He has built a career around the principle that service changes trajectories at scale. As Governor of Maryland since taking office, he has prioritized service not just as a personal value but as a state policy, recognizing that what saved his life can save entire communities.

Service as a Bridge for Community Transformation

Moore's investment in Maryland's service year initiative reflects a deeper understanding: service is not charity, but mutual transformation. When a person serves their community with intention, both the individual and the community are changed. The young person gains clarity, skill, connection, and belonging. The community gains energy, fresh perspective, and renewed civic bonds.

Making Maryland the first state to guarantee a service year option to all high school graduates signals a shift in how government views young people. Rather than waiting for them to find their way, the state is creating a pathway where every teenager has the opportunity to discover their potential through structured, meaningful contribution. This approach recognizes that service year experiences matter most at the moment when a person's trajectory is still forming.

Moore's work extends beyond service year policy. In this episode of Mick Unplugged, he discusses how his administration has transformed Maryland's approach to criminal justice, including the largest mass pardon in United States history—175,000 misdemeanor cannabis convictions—and a drop in Maryland's homicide rate of nearly 60 percent, both outcomes rooted in the belief that people deserve a second chance and the structures to support it.

"Service saved me. Service will save us because service saved me."
