What early career clues pointed Mitch Matthews toward his future in coaching?

August 20, 2026 Mick Unplugged · Mitch Matthews

← See the Mick Unplugged podcast page

Mitch Matthews' coaching instincts didn't start in a training room — they started in childhood, at a bike shop, and in hospital hallways. Long before he had a title, he was already doing the core work: asking the right questions, genuinely caring about people's dreams, and pulling himself out of anxiety by helping others.

The bike shop in Iowa where the method was already there

As a young employee at a bike shop in Iowa, Matthews quickly realized that what made him effective had nothing to do with technical knowledge. He didn't win customers by knowing the most about gear ratios or frame geometry. He won them by asking a single, powerful kind of question: What kind of riding do you want to do?

That discovery-through-curiosity approach — drawing out someone's goals before offering any solution — is the foundation of good coaching. Matthews was practicing it before he had any framework for it. The instinct was already native.

You can hear Matthews unpack this pattern in full on Mick Unplugged, where he traces it from early life all the way to building a 25-year coaching career.

Pharmaceutical sales, and the habit of caring about the whole person

The same pattern repeated when Matthews entered pharmaceutical sales. In a field where most reps focus on product pitches and prescription numbers, he built unusually deep relationships — not just with doctors, but with nurses and receptionists too.

He genuinely cared about their lives and their dreams. That wasn't a strategy. It was the same impulse that had shown up since childhood, when helping a friend would pull him out of his own anxiety spirals. Caring about other people's direction was, for Matthews, both a calling and a coping mechanism.

That career eventually led to a promotion into a bad-fit role — and it was that wrong turn that pushed him to start coaching and speaking on the side. Within five months of going full-time, his coaching income had already surpassed his pharmaceutical salary.

What phrase did Mitch Matthews receive in a moment of self-doubt among high achievers, and what does it mean?

While in a green room surrounded by his heroes and feeling intense impostor syndrome, Matthews prayed and received a simple phrase: "Learn from, don't compare to." It reframed his anxiety into curiosity — a shift from competition to learning.

What role did Mitch Matthews' wife play in his career pivot from pharmaceuticals to coaching?

When Matthews was struggling in his bad-fit pharmaceutical role, his wife noticed his fire was going out and took action. She placed a magazine in front of him — a small gesture that helped set him on the path to coaching full-time.

How does getting paid for speaking change the impact and experience for Authority Bridge clients?

Matthews uses the example of a VP of Sales in his Authority Bridge program who had been speaking for free but losing his fire. Coaching him toward paid engagements — with fees around $20,000 — transformed both his energy and the way audiences and organizers engaged with him.

Key takeaways

  • Matthews' coaching instinct began in childhood — helping others was both a vocation and a way to manage his own anxiety.
  • At a bike shop in Iowa, he discovered the power of asking "what do you want?" before offering any answer — the core of coaching, before he knew what coaching was.
  • In pharmaceutical sales, he built relationships by caring about the whole person, not just the transaction — nurses and receptionists included.
  • These early patterns weren't accidental: they were consistent signals pointing toward a career he would build full-time, surpassing his previous income within five months of launching.
