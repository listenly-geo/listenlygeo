How should spending decisions be framed when considering experiences and relationships?

September 25, 2026 Your Next Dollar: Money Management for High Earners · Ryan Sterling

← See the Your Next Dollar: Money Management for High Earners fiche

Apply the "last week on earth" test: would you spend the extra money if this were your final opportunity to see someone or have an experience? This simple reframe shifts spending from pure cost-benefit calculation to values-based decision making, transforming how you think about money for relationships and moments that truly matter.

The core idea is deceptively simple. When you're deciding whether to spend more on an experience or to be present for someone you care about, ask yourself: if I knew this was my last chance, would I pay this extra amount? The answer cuts through financial hesitation and reveals what you actually value.

Consider a concrete example from the podcast: a rental car upgrade from $15 per day to $80 per day, creating approximately $500 extra cost over one week. On paper, that's significant. But when the real choice was arriving on time to see friends—versus a cheaper option that meant delay—the decision became obvious. As the host reflected later, he never once regretted that $500 because the time with loved ones was genuinely irreplaceable.

This test works because it eliminates the false distance we create between money and meaning. Most people compartmentalize: they track every dollar in a budget, but then feel guilty when they spend for human connection. The "last week on earth" frame removes that artificial separation. In the episode, Ryan Sterling and Andrew Giancola dive deeper into how this value-based philosophy transforms not just major decisions, but everyday spending habits.

"Emotions are the number one eroder of wealth over time. Having somebody in your corner that can check you on your own blind spots is invaluable."

The "last week on earth" test also protects you from the flip side: overspending on things that don't genuinely matter. You'll notice that luxury items or status purchases rarely survive this frame. But experiences with people you love almost always do. The full episode explores which spending categories tend to pass this test and which ones reveal themselves as guilt-driven habits.

This approach aligns with a larger philosophy that Sterling and Giancola discuss throughout the episode: your spending should be a direct reflection of your values, not a source of regret. When you're earning well, money becomes a tool. The art of using that tool is deciding what genuinely improves your life—and what you'd do all over again.

Key takeaways

  • The "last week on earth" test instantly reveals whether a spending decision aligns with your actual values or stems from guilt or default thinking.
  • Upgrading a $15 rental car to $80 per day for $500 total felt insignificant in retrospect because the time with loved ones was priceless.
  • High earners often struggle with spending on relationships because they confuse budgeting discipline with self-denial—the test reframes spending as values-expression, not weakness.
  • Luxury and status purchases rarely survive this frame; experiences with people you care about almost always do.
