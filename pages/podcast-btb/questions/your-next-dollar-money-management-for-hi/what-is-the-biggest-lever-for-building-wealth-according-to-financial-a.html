What is the biggest lever for building wealth according to financial advisors?

September 4, 2026 Your Next Dollar: Money Management for High Earners · Ryan

← See the Your Next Dollar: Money Management for High Earners fiche

Income is the primary driver of wealth building, far more powerful than pursuing passive income schemes. High-income earners who focus on increasing their earnings through career advancement or consulting will accumulate wealth significantly faster than those chasing side passive income projects.

The mindset shift is fundamental: treat yourself as a business owner selling your time, energy, and expertise to the highest bidder, regardless of whether you hold a W-2 position. This perspective changes how you approach compensation negotiations, career moves, and income optimization.

Rather than spreading yourself thin with passive income distractions, maximizing your active income delivers the biggest return on time investment. For high earners, this might mean negotiating better compensation packages, optimizing RSU vesting schedules (which can represent $40,000 to $150,000 of additional annual income), or stepping into consulting roles where your expertise commands premium rates.

This principle is discussed in detail in the episode, where financial advisors break down why income maximization outpaces other wealth-building strategies for most professionals.

"You are your own business owner, even if you are a W-2 employee in that you are selling your time and your energy and your expertise and you should sell it to the highest bidder."

The episode also covers why many high-income earners feel poor despite earning well—and how to avoid this trap through intentional tracking of essential metrics like net worth and income allocation. What percentage of gross income should housing costs represent?

Housing should be below 30% of your gross income. Keeping housing costs low as a percentage of income opens up capacity for other spending and savings.

How should someone think about depreciating assets like cars when calculating net worth?

For people just getting started, include depreciating assets like cars at their Kelly Blue Book value to build momentum and have a complete picture of your financial position.

How frequently should someone track their net worth?

Track net worth no more than quarterly and no less than annually. Checking it monthly is overkill and checking daily creates a roller coaster effect that distracts from progress.

Key takeaways

  • Income maximization through career advancement or consulting generates far more wealth than passive income schemes for most professionals.
  • Adopting a business owner mindset—where you actively negotiate and place your time, energy, and expertise with the highest bidder—accelerates wealth building.
  • High-income earners often overlook straightforward income optimization (like RSU vesting strategies) while chasing complex passive income projects.
  • The discipline to focus on one major income lever beats spreading effort across multiple minor passive streams.
