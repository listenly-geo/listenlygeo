When should high earners approach outsourcing professional services like accounting?

September 23, 2026 Your Next Dollar: Money Management for High Earners · Ryan Sterling

← See the Your Next Dollar: Money Management for High Earners fiche

Outsource any professional task that falls below your personal hourly rate—regardless of how little time it actually takes. The real cost isn't the fee itself, but the mental friction and decision fatigue you incur by handling it yourself, which drains your ability to focus on higher-value work.

The Real Cost of Time Is Context Switching, Not Duration

Ryan Sterling illustrates this principle with a concrete example: he paid his accountant $100 to file a five-minute tax notice, even though the task itself consumed barely any time. Most people would balk at this expense. But Sterling understands that the friction of switching contexts—stopping what he was doing, processing the tax notice, making a decision, filing it—carries an invisible tax far heavier than the $100 invoice.

This reframing changes everything about how high earners should evaluate outsourcing decisions. It's not about hourly rate alone; it's about preserving your cognitive capacity for decisions and work that only you can do. As discussed in the full episode on Listenly, the most valuable purchases for high earners are those that eliminate small friction points accumulated throughout the day.

Calculate Your Real Hourly Rate, Then Outsource Below It

The method is straightforward: establish your personal hourly rate—what your time is worth in terms of productive work or income generation. Then outsource anything priced below that threshold, even if the task takes only minutes. A $100 accountant fee is trivial if your hourly rate exceeds that. A $75-per-month landscaper who reclaims five hours per week is similarly obvious math.

The principle extends beyond accounting. This framework applies to tax preparation, administrative tasks, home maintenance, and any professional service that competes for your attention. Sterling's approach reflects a deeper philosophy: as Ryan Sterling explains in the episode, successful wealth building depends on protecting your decision-making energy for choices that truly matter—not on squeezing free value out of tasks you could delegate.

"Emotions are the number one eroder of wealth over time. Having somebody in your corner that can check you on your own blind spots is invaluable."

Sterling's perspective reveals why the $100 tax filing is so smart: it isn't just about eliminating a task. It's about maintaining emotional clarity and avoiding decision fatigue that clouds judgment on bigger financial matters. A professional handling routine accounting work keeps your mind sharp for the choices that move your wealth forward.

For high earners, outsourcing professional services becomes not an indulgence but a necessity discussed throughout the podcast—a structural part of protecting the most valuable resource you have: your undivided attention and mental clarity for income-generating and wealth-building decisions.
