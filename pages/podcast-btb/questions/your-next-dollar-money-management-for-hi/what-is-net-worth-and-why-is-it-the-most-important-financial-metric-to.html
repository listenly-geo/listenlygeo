What is net worth and why is it the most important financial metric to track?

29 August 2026 Your Next Dollar: Money Management for High Earners · Ryan

← See the Your Next Dollar: Money Management for High Earners fiche

Net worth is your assets minus your liabilities—it's the single most important financial snapshot you can track. Many people overlook a critical distinction: home equity cannot be easily accessed for retirement, so net worth should be tracked in a way that shows investments separately from home value to avoid an inflated sense of wealth.

Home equity skews your real financial picture

Most people track net worth by simply adding up everything they own, including their home. The problem is that when your home appreciates, your net worth rises on paper—but this creates a false sense of financial progress. You cannot pull home equity when you need it for retirement, yet people often treat their rising home value as if it were liquid wealth available to spend.

The solution is deliberately separating your home equity from your investment assets when you track net worth. This forces you to see the truth: are your actual liquid investments growing faster than your home value is rising? As discussed at length in this podcast, the goal is to reach a point where your investments—the wealth you can actually retire on—outweigh your home equity.

Net Worth: The total value of all your assets (home, investments, savings, vehicles, etc.) minus all your liabilities (mortgage, loans, credit card debt). It represents your financial position at a single point in time.

How to set up net worth tracking that actually works

Net worth is calculated by listing all your assets and subtracting all your liabilities. The key is structure: you need a clear system that separates home equity from everything else, so you can see at a glance which portion of your net worth is locked in real estate and which is truly available for retirement or emergencies.

You can use automatic online tools, spreadsheets, or dedicated financial software—the format matters less than the discipline of tracking it. As Ryan explains in the episode, putting net worth front and center is the first and most critical step for anyone serious about building wealth. For high-income earners especially, this baseline snapshot becomes essential when managing complex assets like 401k retirement accounts, Roth 401k contributions, and RSU vesting schedules.

Tracking net worth at minimum once a year—and at most quarterly—gives you a reliable picture of whether your financial strategy is working. More frequent tracking can lead to noise and unnecessary anxiety over short-term market fluctuations. What matters is consistency and the discipline to separate home equity from investable assets, as highlighted in Your Next Dollar: Money Management for High Earners.

Key takeaways

  • Net worth is your total assets minus total liabilities, and it's the single most important financial metric to track.
  • Home equity should be tracked separately from investments because real estate cannot be easily accessed for retirement spending.
  • The real goal is to reach a point where your liquid investments outweigh your home value, giving you true financial flexibility.
  • Set up net worth tracking at least annually, using a clear system that separates home equity from all other assets and liabilities.
