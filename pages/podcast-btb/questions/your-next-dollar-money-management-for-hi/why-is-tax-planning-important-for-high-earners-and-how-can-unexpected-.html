Why is tax planning important for high earners and how can unexpected tax bills be avoided?

Published on September 6, 2026 Your Next Dollar: Money Management for High Earners · Ryan

← See the Your Next Dollar: Money Management for High Earners fiche

High earners often experience a jarring shift: they transition from receiving tax refunds to owing substantial sums, but this should never be an unexpected expense. Tax liability can be projected and managed systematically throughout the year through strategic withholding adjustments and planning, preventing bills that can run into six figures.

The shift from refunds to owing — and why it catches high earners off guard

Most high earners don't realize when their financial position changes, they move from a refund situation to owing money. This transition often feels like a surprise because income patterns shift — through RSU vesting, bonuses, or side income — but withholding doesn't automatically adjust. The gap between what's withheld and what's actually owed creates the problem, not the tax liability itself.

For couples earning over a million dollars combined annually, the numbers become stark: a single year of improper withholding can result in a $100,000 tax bill. This isn't inevitable. As Ryan explains in the episode, the responsibility falls on the earner to understand their own tax situation and take corrective action.

Projecting tax liability and building reserves

Tax planning for high earners starts with projection. Calculate your expected tax liability based on actual income, not assumptions. This means adding up W-2 wages, RSU vesting schedules, bonus expectations, and any other income sources — then determining what federal and state taxes will be owed.

Once you know the number, the solution is straightforward: build that amount throughout the year. Some high earners adjust their withholding on W-2 forms; others set funds aside quarterly. The key is matching your cash outflows to your tax obligation, a point detailed in this podcast, rather than hoping for a refund or scrambling in April.

Tools for high-income tax management

Beyond withholding adjustments, high earners have additional levers. Mega backdoor Roth conversions can shelter significant income from taxation if structured correctly. These accounts allow high earners to save additional pre-tax dollars, reducing taxable income directly.

401(k) contributions, including Roth 401(k) options, also reduce or defer tax burden depending on structure. For those with RSU compensation, understanding when vesting occurs and how that income flows through your tax return becomes essential. Ryan works with clients to layer these strategies together, creating a comprehensive tax shield rather than relying on a single tactic.

The broader principle Ryan emphasizes is this: you are your own business owner, even if you are a W-2 employee, and managing your tax liability is part of running that business efficiently. High earners who treat tax planning as an operational cost — not an afterthought — avoid the shock of unexpected bills entirely.

"You are your own business owner, even if you are a W-2 employee in that you are selling your time and your energy and your expertise and you should sell it to the highest bidder."

Key takeaways

  • High earners shift from receiving refunds to owing taxes as income rises, but this transition can be managed proactively through accurate projections.
  • Couples earning over $1 million annually can face $100,000+ tax bills if withholding is not adjusted to match actual tax liability.
  • Tax planning requires calculating expected liability upfront and building reserves throughout the year, not scrambling at tax time.
  • Strategies like mega backdoor Roth conversions, 401(k) contributions, and withholding adjustments reduce taxable income and prevent surprises.

See also

How should RSUs be treated in income calculations for high earners?

RSUs are real income and should be treated like a cash bonus. They are W-2 income and you are taxed on them when they vest. For high earners, RSU vesting can represent substantial additional annual income that must be factored into tax withholding strategies.

What is the biggest lever for building wealth according to financial advisors?

Income is probably the biggest lever in terms of building wealth. High-income earners should think of themselves as business owners selling their time, energy, and expertise to the highest bidder.

What percentage of gross income should housing costs represent?

Housing should be below 30% of your gross income. Keeping housing costs low as a percentage of income opens up capacity for other spending and savings.
