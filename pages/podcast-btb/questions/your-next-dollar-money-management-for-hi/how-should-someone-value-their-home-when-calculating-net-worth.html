How should someone value their home when calculating net worth?

August 30, 2026 Your Next Dollar: Money Management for High Earners · Ryan

← See the Your Next Dollar: Money Management for High Earners fiche

For homes purchased in the last one and a half to two years, use the purchase price as a conservative estimate. For homes owned three to ten years or longer, reference Zillow estimates as a ballpark figure. However, the true value of your home is what someone is actually willing to pay for it—something you only discover when you actually sell.

The timing trap in home valuation

Most people overestimate their home's worth because they lack a clear method for valuing it. The approach changes depending on how long you have owned the property, as a point detailed in Your Next Dollar.

Recent purchases offer clarity: the price you paid is your most reliable anchor, especially within the first two years. This avoids the temptation to inflate value based on market sentiment or neighborhood changes you hope will happen.

Why home equity can distort your net worth

Many people inflate their net worth by counting inflated home equity, but as Ryan explains in the episode, this creates a misleading financial snapshot. Home equity is not liquid—you cannot withdraw it when you retire unless you sell the home or take out a loan.

The real goal, discussed at length in this podcast, is to reach a point where your investments outweigh your home equity. This shift shows genuine wealth building, not just real estate appreciation that is locked inside your walls.

For older homes—those owned three to ten years or beyond—Zillow estimates serve as a reasonable ballpark. But treat these as guideposts, not gospel. As referenced in the financial education discussed on Your Next Dollar, the only true market value is what an actual buyer will offer you in a real transaction.

What is net worth and why is it the most important financial metric to track?

Net worth is your assets minus your liabilities. It is the single most important financial snapshot because it gives you a clear picture of where you stand financially.

Key takeaways

  • Use your purchase price for homes bought within the last one to two years as the most conservative and reliable estimate.
  • For homes owned longer, Zillow estimates provide a useful ballpark, but they are not definitive valuations.
  • The only true home value is what a willing buyer will actually pay—anything else is speculation.
  • Avoid inflating net worth with home equity; focus instead on building investment wealth that exceeds your home value.
