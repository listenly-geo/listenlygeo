Why is industry-specific financial knowledge so important when accounting for MSPs?

22 August 2026 The Athena Rebels Podcast · Heather McDonnell-Alford

← See the full The Athena Rebels Podcast page

Industry-specific knowledge matters because it changes what you look for — and what you catch. An accountant who understands the MSP model knows that a Pax8 invoice must be split across multiple cost lines, not lumped into a single code. That precision makes it possible to match licence revenue directly against licence costs, a check a generalist accountant simply would not know to run.

Knowing the model means knowing where the numbers should land

In the MSP world, vendor invoices are not straightforward. A single Pax8 invoice, for example, can bundle multiple product lines — licences, services, support — each of which needs to be coded separately in the accounts. Heather McDonnell-Alford makes clear that this is not a minor bookkeeping detail. It is the difference between accounts that tell you something and accounts that obscure everything.

The specific check McDonnell-Alford describes is direct: does licence revenue cover licence costs? That question can only be asked if the data on both sides of the ledger has been structured correctly from the start. This is exactly the kind of operational knowledge her team at Counting Creators brings to MSP clients — a depth a generalist firm would not arrive with.

What is a Pax8 invoice in MSP accounting?
Pax8 is a cloud commerce marketplace widely used by MSPs to procure software licences and services. Because a single Pax8 invoice typically covers multiple product categories, MSP accountants need to split and code each line individually — rather than recording the total as one lump-sum expense — so that costs can be matched against their corresponding revenue streams.

A generalist accountant would not know to ask the right questions

McDonnell-Alford's point is not that generalist accountants are incompetent — it is that MSP financials require a specific mental model of how the business generates revenue. Without that model, there is no frame of reference for what correct coding even looks like. As she explains in The Athena Rebels Podcast, knowing the MSP model means knowing exactly what to look for.

McDonnell-Alford built Counting Creators precisely around this gap. Within five months of starting the business, she had already niched entirely into the MSP space — having recognised that this community had a genuine, unmet need for accountants who spoke their language. The firm covers all outsourced finance functions, from basic mailbox management through to compliance work, with MSP-specific knowledge threaded through every layer.

That specialism has also shaped how McDonnell-Alford talks about finance publicly — a topic she explored further in this episode of the Athena Rebels Podcast, where she discussed building both a business and a personal brand within the MSP community through LinkedIn and major industry stages including the MSP Show 2025 and Pax8 Amsterdam.

"When you speak about something personally online, share the scar, not the wound. If you're still in the trauma, it's the other side."

The broader lesson for any MSP considering their finance function: the question is not just whether your accountant is qualified — it is whether they understand the specific revenue and cost architecture of a managed service business. As covered in depth on The Athena Rebels Podcast, that distinction has real consequences for how clearly owners can see their own numbers.
