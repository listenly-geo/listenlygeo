Why should every employee in an organization understand their role in the sales process?

September 10, 2026 The Athena Rebels Podcast · Lamara Thorne

← See the The Athena Rebels Podcast fiche

Every person in your organization influences the customer journey at some point. A negative interaction anywhere—whether from finance, support, or operations—can damage how customers view your entire business. Sales is not a single pitch; it's the collective responsibility of building and maintaining relationships across every touchpoint.

When customers engage with your organization, they don't segment their experience by department. A poor interaction in billing, onboarding, or customer support registers as a reflection of your company as a whole. One bad moment can undo months of relationship-building work, which is why every employee must recognize their role in the larger sales narrative.

This understanding shifts the entire culture of a business. It moves away from the outdated image of sales as transactional—the stereotype of pushy salespeople or "cheesy car salesmen trying to push you something that you don't want." As Lamara Thorne explains in the episode, modern B2B sales is fundamentally about problem-solving: having a product or solution that genuinely addresses a customer's challenge. But that solution means nothing if a customer's experience elsewhere in the organization contradicts it.

Every department is part of the sales journey

Sales is not a single transaction—it's a continuous process involving multiple interactions at different stages. A receptionist greeting a prospect sets the tone. Finance clarifying pricing demonstrates professionalism and reliability. Implementation teams ensure the customer actually receives value. Each interaction either strengthens or weakens the relationship.

This is why training employees beyond the sales team is critical. When someone from operations understands they're helping close or retain a deal, they approach their work with a different mindset. They become invested in the outcome, not just in completing their task. That shift in perspective directly impacts customer perception and loyalty.

"It's about how do you become a chameleon without losing your authenticity."

Beyond the standard talking points, the full conversation covers how sales maturity develops over multiple customer touches—insight that applies whether you're working directly with buyers or supporting them from behind the scenes.

Key takeaways

  • Customer relationships are built across the entire organization, not just during sales conversations.
  • A single negative interaction from any department can damage the customer's entire perception of your business.
  • Modern sales is about solving real problems, which requires every employee to understand and support that mission.
  • Employees who recognize their role in the sales journey approach their work with greater intention and impact.
