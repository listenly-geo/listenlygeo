The ADHD inner critic and its impact on business decisions

Having ADHD comes with a particular challenge: an intensely critical inner voice paired with extremely high self-expectations. Heather McDonnell-Alford acknowledges that this combination can make her extraordinarily hard on herself when outcomes don't match her vision. She recognizes that imposter syndrome—a feeling of never being good enough—is something she has experienced long before becoming a business owner, but running a company amplifies it.

The pattern is relentless: no matter what she accomplishes, there's always something she believes she could have done better. As discussed in The Athena Rebels Podcast, this internal perfectionism can trap a business owner in a cycle of self-doubt and second-guessing, which directly undermines decision-making and forward momentum.

Peer accountability as an antidote

Rather than sitting alone with these thoughts, Heather has built a deliberate coping mechanism: she reaches out to trusted business peers who know her work and her character. These are industry colleagues who understand both the MSP space and the pressures of business ownership—people who can deliver a reality check without sugarcoating it.

What makes this strategy powerful is its honesty. These trusted friends sometimes tell her bluntly that her self-criticism is simply unreasonable, that the standard she's holding herself to is unrealistic, or that she's overlooking genuine progress. In this episode of The Athena Rebels Podcast, Heather explains that this external perspective breaks the isolation of the internal narrative and provides grounding—a way to test reality against the distorted voice of self-doubt.

What does Heather McDonnell-Alford mean by 'share the scar, not the wound' when posting personal content online?

Heather's principle is that you should only share personal or difficult experiences publicly once you have processed them fully and reached the other side of the experience, rather than sharing while you are still in the trauma or pain.

What is Heather McDonnell-Alford's advice for MSPs or professionals who want to start building a presence on LinkedIn?

Heather advises that you should never outsource your personal LinkedIn content because your brand is fundamentally you, and generic or AI-looking posts will not resonate with your audience or build authentic connection.

How did Heather McDonnell-Alford build her professional brand and speaking career in the MSP industry?

Heather attributes all of her speaking opportunities directly to her LinkedIn presence, stating that without it no one would know who she is. She spent approximately 15 hours per week on LinkedIn in the early stages of building her personal brand.

Key takeaways

  • ADHD paired with perfectionism creates a harsh inner critic that can lead to unreasonable self-criticism and business paralysis.
  • Trusted peer accountability is more effective than isolation—industry friends can provide honest reality checks that counter distorted self-doubt.
  • Building a support network of business peers who understand your industry context and character is essential for combating imposter syndrome.
  • Stepping away from a situation before taking action gives space for rational perspective to replace emotional self-criticism, as outlined in The Athena Rebels Podcast.
