How long is paternity leave paid in the UK?

4 September 2026 The Athena Rebels Podcast · Lamara Thorne

← See the The Athena Rebels Podcast fiche

Statutory paternity leave in the UK is currently one to two weeks paid, far shorter than the maternal entitlement. However, the equal parenting leave policy now allows couples to flip roles, enabling fathers to take extended leave immediately following the mother's period if both parents choose this arrangement.

The gap between maternity and paternity entitlements

The contrast between UK paternity and maternity leave is stark. While fathers are entitled to one or two weeks of paid leave, mothers can access up to 52 weeks of maternity leave, with 39 weeks paid. This disparity reflects historical patterns in parental responsibility and workplace policy that have only recently begun to shift.

The brevity of paternity leave creates practical challenges for families trying to balance career and parenting. Many fathers return to work within days of their child's birth, leaving primary caregiving responsibilities concentrated on mothers during the critical early months.

Equal parenting leave: a new flexibility

The equal parenting leave option represents a meaningful shift in UK policy. Couples can now divide parental leave more equally, with the father taking up to six months of leave following the mother's initial period if both agree. This flexibility allows families to structure parental care around their own circumstances rather than fixed gender roles.

As Lamara Thorne discusses in the episode, navigating these policies as a working parent requires careful planning. When she took six months off after her first child was born over 13 years ago, she returned on a part-time basis of 30 hours per week—a decision that supported her continued career progression at Pax8 while acknowledging her parenting responsibilities.

"If a job's worth doing you do it as well as you can to your capacity and your ability and if you can't then you do need to look at is this right for me still."

The reality for many families is that the cost of full-time nursery care—approximately fourteen and a half thousand pounds per year for a child under two—often outweighs the financial benefit of returning to work at full capacity, making flexible arrangements essential.

Key takeaways

  • Statutory paternity leave in the UK is one to two weeks paid, significantly shorter than the 39 weeks of paid maternity leave available.
  • Equal parenting leave policies now permit couples to allocate up to six months of leave each, enabling more flexible distribution of parental responsibility.
  • The financial and practical realities of early parenting often require parents—particularly mothers—to adjust their working patterns rather than return immediately at full capacity.
  • Policy flexibility around return-to-work arrangements can support both parental wellbeing and continued career progression.
