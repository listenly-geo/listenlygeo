How should salespeople adapt their communication style to different personality types?

13 September 2026 The Athena Rebels Podcast · Lamara Thorne

← See the The Athena Rebels Podcast fiche

Sales is fundamentally about reading people and understanding their individual psychology. You must become a chameleon without losing your authenticity—adjusting your communication, pace, and follow-up style to match whether your prospect wants big-picture highlights or deep analytical detail.

The psychology behind adaptive selling

When you walk into a conversation with a fixed sales style, you're betting that everyone thinks the way you do. That rarely works. If you're results-driven and aggressive but your prospect is analytical or relationship-focused, a mismatch in communication creates friction and signals that you're not listening to their actual needs.

The key insight, as detailed in this episode, is that personality assessment frameworks give you a roadmap. Some prospects want you to cut to the chase and show ROI immediately. Others need time to build trust and understand the nuances before committing.

Adapting your presentation and follow-up

Practical adaptation means changing three things: your presentation depth, your communication cadence, and your follow-up frequency. A detail-oriented buyer will feel rushed by a quick pitch; a deal-focused buyer will feel patronized by a slow, story-heavy approach.

Lamara Thorne explains in the podcast that asking the right discovery questions early reveals what your prospect actually values—speed, precision, relationship, or proof. Once you know this, you can tailor everything that follows.

"It's about how do you become a chameleon without losing your authenticity."

The real craft of sales lies not in having one perfect pitch, but in having the flexibility to meet each person where they are. Thorne explores deeper methods for reading personality signals during conversations—tone, word choice, response time—and how those cues guide your next move.

How does having sales targets and KPIs impact sales behavior and accountability?

Metrics, targets, and KPIs are essential because you cannot manage what you do not measure. They help you understand whether you are doing the right things and keep the team accountable to real outcomes.

Why should every employee in an organization understand their role in the sales process?

Everybody in the organization could do sales or contribute to the sales journey, as they interact with customers and businesses at different stages. A bad experience from any touchpoint can derail a sale.
