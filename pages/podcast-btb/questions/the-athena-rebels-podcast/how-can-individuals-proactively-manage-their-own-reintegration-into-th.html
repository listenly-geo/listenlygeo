How can individuals proactively manage their own reintegration into the workplace after extended leave?

7 September 2026 The Athena Rebels Podcast · Lamara Thorne

← See The Athena Rebels Podcast fiche

Take proactive control by scheduling structured meetings with your manager to understand business changes and reset clear goals, dedicating time to reconnect with colleagues across your team, and actively investigating how organizational structures have evolved during your absence. This self-directed approach works even when formal onboarding isn't provided.

Three steps to own your return

The first move is intentional conversation with your direct manager. Rather than waiting for a formal reintegration plan, book time to discuss what's shifted—new projects, team restructures, process changes, business priorities. This meeting isn't just a catch-up; it's your chance to anchor yourself to the current reality and jointly define what success looks like in your role over the next quarter. Lamara Thorne outlines this strategy in The Athena Rebels Podcast, drawing from her own experience navigating return to work.

Reconnecting with colleagues matters just as much as the manager conversation. Dedicate specific time to one-on-one conversations with team members and adjacent departments. Ask what they've shipped, what problems they've solved, what's stressed them. This rebuilds trust, restores your informal knowledge network, and positions you as genuinely interested in others' work—not just your own job. These touchpoints also often surface informal changes that formal updates might miss.

Finally, become a detective about organizational evolution. Look at your company org chart, review recent communications, attend all-hands meetings, and ask your peers open questions about team and business structure shifts. Understanding the landscape prevents you from operating on outdated mental models and helps you locate the people and resources you'll need. This active learning is entirely in your control, regardless of whether your employer offers structured onboarding.

"If a job's worth doing you do it as well as you can to your capacity and your ability and if you can't then you do need to look at is this right for me still."

For context on the wider landscape, this episode explores the neuroscience of returning to work after maternity leave and reveals that over 80 percent of UK mothers report the transition as very difficult—underscoring why self-directed reintegration is so valuable. When you take the initiative, you're not passively waiting for organizational support; you're actively shaping your own re-entry.

Key takeaways

  • Schedule a dedicated meeting with your manager to understand business changes, new priorities, and reset role expectations and goals.
  • Spend focused time reconnecting one-on-one with team members and colleagues across departments to rebuild relationships and your informal knowledge network.
  • Actively learn how organizational and team structures have evolved, including role changes and new processes, so you operate from a current mental model.
  • Taking proactive control of your reintegration is powerful even when your employer doesn't offer formal onboarding—you own the pace and direction of your re-entry.
