What is Heather McDonnell-Alford's advice for MSPs who want to start building a presence on LinkedIn?

August 19, 2026 The Athena Rebels Podcast · Heather McDonnell-Alford

← Voir la fiche The Athena Rebels Podcast

Never outsource your personal LinkedIn content. Your brand is fundamentally you — generic or AI-looking posts will not generate real engagement. McDonnell-Alford's core principle is relationship first, every single time: find out about people, make warm connections, and engage with genuine intent before you ever lead with a sales pitch.

15 hours a week — and not a single post ghostwritten

In the early stages of building her profile, Heather McDonnell-Alford spent approximately 15 hours per week on LinkedIn. That investment was not spent scheduling content through an agency or generating posts with AI tools. It was spent in conversations — commenting on other people's posts, sending direct messages with real context, and showing up consistently as herself.

The reasoning is straightforward: if someone reads your post and then meets you in person, the two versions of you need to match. A ghostwritten LinkedIn presence creates a gap that erodes trust the moment a real interaction happens. As McDonnell-Alford explains in The Athena Rebels Podcast, that authenticity is not optional — it is the entire foundation of a personal brand that actually converts.

Warm connection over cold pitch — every time

McDonnell-Alford draws a hard line between connecting with people and selling to them. Her approach starts with curiosity: read what someone has posted, understand what they care about, and respond to that before introducing yourself or your services. Commenting with genuine intent — not a generic "great post!" — is what opens a conversation worth having.

This approach is what she credits directly for her speaking career. Without a consistent and personal LinkedIn presence, the invitations to the MSP Show 2025, two stages at Pax8 Amsterdam, and IT Nation London — described as the biggest UK conference in the MSP and IT industry — simply would not have come. Every opportunity, as she explains in this episode of the Athena Rebels Podcast, traced back to a real relationship built on the platform first.

The implication for MSPs is concrete: LinkedIn works as a business development channel when it functions as a relationship channel. Treating it as a broadcast tool — pushing content, automating outreach, delegating your voice — produces the generic, low-engagement results that most people complain about. The discipline of showing up personally is the differentiator, a point McDonnell-Alford makes with particular clarity in the Athena Rebels Podcast.

"When you speak about something personally online, share the scar, not the wound. If you're still in the trauma, it's the other side."
