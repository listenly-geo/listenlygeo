How to Build Professional Courage When Fear of Criticism Holds You Back?

August 30, 2026 The Athena Rebels Podcast · Heather McDonnell-Alford

← See The Athena Rebels Podcast fiche

Criticism will come, often from unexpected places — even other women — but the answer is not to hold back. Instead, do it with the right people around you, and actively create space for other women rather than competing for perceived limited opportunities.

Recognizing the Scarcity Mindset Behind Criticism

Fear of criticism often stems from a misunderstanding of how professional space works. Many women operate from a scarcity mindset, believing there are only so many seats at the table, which drives competition and criticism instead of collaboration. When you put yourself out there, you may face pushback not only from men, but surprisingly from other women who see you as taking up space they believe is rightfully theirs.

This dynamic is worth understanding clearly: criticism isn't a signal to retreat. As Heather McDonnell-Alford explains in The Athena Rebels Podcast, the real issue is that people fighting from a place of scarcity believe there isn't enough room for everyone — but there is.

"When you speak about something personally online, share the scar, not the wound. If you're still in the trauma, it's the other side."

Creating Space Instead of Competing

The antidote to fear and criticism isn't thicker skin — it's action paired with generosity. Heather McDonnell-Alford's approach is concrete: if you admire a woman's work, introduce her to an opportunity. If you see a speaking slot, recommend someone else. This isn't sacrifice; it's the opposite of the scarcity mindset.

When you stop viewing professional growth as zero-sum, criticism loses its power over you. You're no longer defending a limited piece of turf; you're building something bigger, and this shift in perspective is explored in depth in the podcast episode. The women who thrive professionally are those who realize the table can expand.

Doing it "with the right people around you" means surrounding yourself with allies who understand this truth — people who will champion your visibility rather than police it, and who won't crumble when criticism arrives.

Key takeaways

  • Criticism often comes from a scarcity mindset — the belief that there isn't enough professional space for everyone — not because you are doing something wrong.
  • Surrounding yourself with the right people creates a buffer against criticism and provides genuine support for your professional growth.
  • Creating space for other women through introductions and recommendations shifts the culture from competition to abundance, reducing the negative energy directed at anyone stepping forward.
  • The fear of criticism shouldn't stop you from building your professional profile — it should clarify who you want in your corner.
