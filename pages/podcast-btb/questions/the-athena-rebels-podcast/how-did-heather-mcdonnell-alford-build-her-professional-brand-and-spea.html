How did Heather McDonnell-Alford build her professional brand and speaking career in the MSP industry?

23 August 2026 The Athena Rebels Podcast · Heather McDonnell-Alford

← Voir la fiche The Athena Rebels Podcast

Heather McDonnell-Alford attributes every single speaking opportunity she has ever received directly to her LinkedIn presence — without it, she says, no one would know who she is. In the early stages of building her brand, she invested around 15 hours a week on the platform, focusing entirely on relationship-building rather than selling. That sustained presence first earned her a media invitation to Pax8 Berlin, which then opened doors to speaking slots at the MSP Show 2025, Pax8 Amsterdam, and IT Nation London.

15 hours a week on LinkedIn — and not a single sales pitch

McDonnell-Alford was deliberate about how she used LinkedIn. The goal was never to push Counting Creators services directly. It was to show up consistently, engage with the MSP community, and let visibility do the selling over time.

That approach is exactly what The Athena Rebels Podcast explores in this episode: how a finance founder with no existing profile in the MSP world became a recognised voice at its biggest conferences, purely through intentional online presence.

The first concrete result was an invitation to attend Pax8 Berlin — not as a speaker, but as media. It was that initial foot in the door, built entirely on the strength of her LinkedIn profile, that set everything else in motion.

From Pax8 Berlin media pass to IT Nation London main stage — one season

What followed was a remarkably compressed speaking trajectory. Her season opened at the MSP Show 2025 and closed with two stages at Pax8 Amsterdam — all within a single conference season. IT Nation London, widely considered the biggest UK conference in the MSP and IT industry, came as part of the same run.

None of these opportunities came from cold outreach or a PR campaign. As McDonnell-Alford explains in this episode of The Athena Rebels Podcast, they came because people already knew who she was — because she had made herself visible and consistent on LinkedIn long before anyone invited her to a stage.

"When you speak about something personally online, share the scar, not the wound. If you're still in the trauma, it's the other side."

Her advice on sharing personal experiences online — "the scar, not the wound" — reflects a broader philosophy that runs through her approach to LinkedIn. Authenticity, timed correctly, builds trust. Oversharing from inside a crisis does not.

This is the kind of practical, experience-first insight that makes The Athena Rebels Podcast worth listening to for anyone building a personal brand in a specialist B2B industry.

Heather niched Counting Creators into the MSP space within five months of starting the business. She was introduced to MSPs through her first client contact and quickly recognised a genuine opportunity to help an underserved community.
