What is Heather McDonnell-Alford's philosophy on cash flow management for MSPs?

31 August 2026 The Athena Rebels Podcast · Heather McDonnell-Alford

← See The Athena Rebels Podcast fiche

Cash flow is the most misunderstood financial concept among MSPs, who often confuse money sitting in the account with true financial health. Heather McDonnell-Alford advocates for a strict pot system — maintaining separate savings accounts for corporation tax, personal tax, cyber investment, salaries, and other known liabilities — ensuring funds are always reserved and available when obligations arrive.

The Visibility Problem: Money in the Account Is Not Profit

Most MSPs believe they are financially healthy simply because there is money in the account at the end of the month. This surface-level view misses a critical reality: they never examine the bottom of the P&L. A healthy bank balance means nothing if liabilities are looming — tax bills, compliance costs, planned technology investments, or salary obligations that have not yet been accounted for.

This gap between cash and actual profit is where MSPs stumble. As Heather McDonnell-Alford explains in The Athena Rebels Podcast, the confusion stems from a lack of structured financial visibility. Without a system to ring-fence money, business owners spend from a pool that should be reserved, then face shocks when bills arrive.

The Pot System: Compartmentalizing Financial Obligation

McDonnell-Alford's solution is deceptively simple: separate savings pots for each financial responsibility. Rather than holding all cash in a single operating account, the pot system creates ringfenced reserves for:

  • Corporation tax — set aside immediately from profit
  • Personal tax — personal liability that must be funded separately
  • Cyber investment — strategic spending on security infrastructure
  • Salaries and payroll — committed recurring costs
  • Other known liabilities — compliance, insurance, contingencies

Each pot grows month-by-month as revenue comes in. When a liability matures — a tax payment due, a cyber security refresh planned — the money is already there. There are no surprises, no scrambling, and no cash flow crises born from hidden obligations.

This approach transforms cash flow from a mysterious number into a predictable machine. Discussed at length in this podcast episode, the pot system forces business owners to know their obligations and plan accordingly — the fundamental difference between financial health and financial delusion.

P&L (Profit & Loss statement): A financial document showing revenue, costs, and profit over a period. MSPs often overlook it, focusing only on bank balance instead of understanding whether their business is actually profitable after all expenses.

"When you speak about something personally online, share the scar, not the wound. If you're still in the trauma, it's the other side."

Beyond cash flow mechanics, McDonnell-Alford's broader philosophy emphasizes transparency and authenticity in how business owners present their financial realities. The principle of sharing processed experience rather than raw crisis reflects her approach to helping MSPs — moving beyond surface panic to structured, documented solutions. Her work through Counting Creators, as detailed in The Athena Rebels Podcast, demonstrates how finance becomes both a technical discipline and a communication challenge in building sustainable MSP operations.

Key takeaways

  • Cash in the bank is not profit — MSPs must examine the full P&L to understand true financial health.
  • The pot system separates money into ringfenced accounts for each known liability (tax, cyber investment, salaries, compliance).
  • Compartmentalized savings eliminate cash flow surprises and remove the need to scramble when obligations come due.
  • Financial transparency requires both technical structure and honest communication about business realities.
