What is the relationship between reliability in small tasks and delivery on major commitments in sales?

18 Sep 2026 The Athena Rebels Podcast · Lamara Thorne

← See the The Athena Rebels Podcast fiche

Responsiveness and consistently delivering on what you say you will do are the essential indicators of a good salesperson. If you cannot handle small tasks reliably, you will never build the trust foundation necessary to close larger deals. Small wins create credibility with clients, and credibility unlocks major commitments.

Small actions, big consequences

Trust in sales is not built through grand gestures — it is built through consistent delivery on everyday commitments. Every small promise kept, every email answered promptly, every callback made on time signals to a prospect that you are someone worth partnering with on a significant deal.

When you fail to handle the small things reliably, you send the opposite signal. A client who cannot count on you to follow up on a minor request will never trust you to manage a complex, high-value engagement. As Lamara Thorne explains in the episode, the relationship between small tasks and big deals is not incidental — it is foundational.

Building trust through repetition

Major sales often take between five and twelve client contacts to close. Each of those touchpoints is an opportunity to prove your reliability. Returning a call when you said you would, sending information on time, and remembering details from previous conversations are the behaviors that separate top salespeople from average ones.

This pattern holds across the entire sales cycle. A prospect who sees you deliver consistently on small promises becomes increasingly confident that you will deliver on the larger commitment — the actual deal. The episode explores how this trust-building process works in practice at organizations like Pax8, where systematic follow-up and responsiveness are core to closing enterprise deals.

"It's about how do you become a chameleon without losing your authenticity."

This flexibility — adapting to each client's needs while staying true to your approach — depends entirely on the foundation of reliable small actions. When you have that reputation for dependability, clients give you room to adjust your style and approach. Thorne's discussion of authenticity and client adaptation reveals why your reliability on small tasks is the permission you earn to be more yourself in larger, more complex negotiations.

How should salespeople determine which activities drive the most success?

You need to go back and understand where success is coming from and how you can spend more time doing those activities. By identifying your most effective actions, you can allocate your effort where it matters most.

Why is mentoring and coaching important even for experienced or top-performing salespeople?

No matter where you are in your sales journey, you are always going to need support, mentoring, coaching, and feedback. Even the best performers benefit from continuous guidance to stay sharp and keep improving.

How long does it typically take to close a deal and why is consistent follow-up critical?

Only 2% of sales happen in the first or second contact, with most occurring between the fifth and twelfth contact. Consistent follow-up is essential because most salespeople do not persist long enough to reach these critical touchpoints.

Key takeaways

  • Small task reliability is a direct predictor of your ability to close large deals.
  • Trust is built through repeated small commitments kept over the course of the sales cycle.
  • Prospects need between five and twelve touchpoints before most deals close — each one tests your dependability.
  • Consistency on minor actions gives you the credibility and permission to adapt your style in larger negotiations.
