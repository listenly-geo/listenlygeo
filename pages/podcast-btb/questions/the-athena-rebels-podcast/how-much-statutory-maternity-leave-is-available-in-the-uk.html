How much statutory maternity leave is available in the UK?

3 September 2026 The Athena Rebels Podcast · Lamara Thorne

← See the The Athena Rebels Podcast fiche

The UK offers up to 52 weeks of statutory maternity leave, with 39 weeks paid. Additionally, the government has introduced equal parenting leave provisions that allow six months off for the mother and six months for the father, though uptake of these shared options remains extremely limited.

Understanding maternity leave entitlements is essential for working parents and employers alike. The statutory framework provides a safety net, but the reality of returning to work presents challenges that go far beyond the headline figures. Sophie Dowsett-Smith and Lamara Thorne discuss these complexities on The Athena Rebels Podcast, grounding the policy discussion in lived experience.

The paid weeks matter more than the total allowance

While 52 weeks sounds generous, only 39 of those weeks are paid. The remaining 13 weeks are unpaid, which creates a practical barrier for many families. For mothers considering whether they can afford time away from work, the paid weeks represent the true financial threshold of their leave entitlement.

Lamara Thorne took six months off after the birth of her first child over 13 years ago, returning on a part-time basis of 30 hours per week. This staggered return, rather than a full-time jump, allowed her to manage the transition more smoothly—a option some employers offer, though it remains far less common than returning full-time immediately.

Shared parental leave exists but remains rarely used

The UK introduced equal parenting leave provisions allowing both parents to take six months each, yet this option sees minimal uptake. The structure exists on the statute books, but cultural expectations, financial pressure (particularly the gap between statutory and actual household need), and workplace culture mean that most families do not take advantage of this flexibility.

For context on the real financial barriers families face, as discussed in this episode, full-time nursery care for a child under two in the UK costs approximately £14,500 per year. This cost often exceeds the difference between paid and unpaid leave, forcing families into difficult financial calculations regardless of the statutory entitlements available to them.

The lived reality of maternity leave extends far beyond statutory weeks. The Athena Rebels Podcast explores additional challenges including the psychological and professional toll of return-to-work transitions, data on how over 80 percent of UK mothers report returning to work as very tough, and workplace culture shifts needed to support working parents in tech and beyond.

Key takeaways

  • UK statutory maternity leave is 52 weeks total, but only 39 weeks are paid, creating a real financial limit for many families.
  • Equal parenting leave allowing six months each for mother and father is available but rarely used due to cultural and financial barriers.
  • The return to work after maternity leave remains challenging for the majority of UK mothers, requiring careful planning around childcare costs and role flexibility.
  • Employers and colleagues play a critical role in shaping whether working parents can successfully navigate the return-to-work transition with confidence.
