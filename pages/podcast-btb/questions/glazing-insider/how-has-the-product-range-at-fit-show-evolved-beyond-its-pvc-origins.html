How has the product range at Fit Show evolved beyond its PVC origins?

13 September 2026 Glazing Insider · Nikki West

← See the Glazing Insider fiche

Fit Show has transformed from a predominantly PVC-focused event into a comprehensive home improvement showcase spanning aluminium, glass, and timber products. The expansion now covers windows and doors, roof lanterns, garage doors, decking, fencing, guttering, and roofing across 300 brands exhibiting in 2025.

From single-material focus to a complete ecosystem

What started as a PVC-driven event has evolved into a full-spectrum platform serving the entire UK home improvement sector. The 2025 edition demonstrates this shift most clearly through the inaugural presence of 50 members from the Timber Joinery Network, a milestone that signals Fit Show's commitment to sustainability and material diversity in construction.

This expansion addresses a critical gap in the market. As Nikki West explains in the episode, builders and property developers now actively seek events where they can evaluate multiple material choices under one roof—from traditional timber joinery to modern composite systems—all within three days at the NEC (National Exhibition Centre).

The breadth of product categories has become a competitive advantage. 50% of exhibitors at Fit Show 2025 are brand new to the event, bringing fresh innovations in roofing, guttering, and other categories that wouldn't have appeared at earlier editions. This influx reflects growing demand from installers, fabricators, and builders who need to source diverse materials for contemporary construction projects.

"If you're selling in the home improvement sector, you have to be here. And if you're buying, if you've got to source products or you want to buy anything, you've got to be at Fit Show."

For fabricators and installers looking to grow their businesses, this expansion means access to materials they might otherwise source separately from different suppliers. The shift from PVC-only to multi-material representation reflects how the home improvement industry itself has matured—customers demand choice, and suppliers must respond.
