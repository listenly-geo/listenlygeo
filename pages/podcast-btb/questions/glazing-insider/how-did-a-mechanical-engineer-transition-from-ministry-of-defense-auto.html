How did a mechanical engineer transition from Ministry of Defense automotive work into the fenestration industry?

3 September 2026 Glazing Insider · Adam Jones

← See the Glazing Insider fiche

Adam Jones moved into the window industry in 2002 by joining J Tools Limited as a mechanical electrical design engineer, despite coming from a Ministry of Defense automotive background. He spent his first six months actively trying to leave the industry, but once he engaged more deeply with the work and the people, he found it meaningful and chose to stay, eventually growing into roles that supported the business owner directly.

The initial clash: Defence contracts versus toolmaking

Stepping into fenestration from military automotive engineering felt like entering a completely foreign world. Jones had no prior knowledge of the window industry, and the shift in context, culture, and daily work felt misaligned with his career path at the time. The first six months were marked by deliberate attempts to exit, a common phase when a skilled professional encounters an unfamiliar sector with no obvious connection to their past expertise.

What changed was neither a dramatic event nor a sudden epiphany. Rather, as Jones engaged more deeply with the technical challenges and the team at J Tools Limited, the work became less abstract. The people he worked with proved engaging, and the relevance of his daily contributions to something concrete and tangible shifted his perspective entirely.

The turning point: Finding meaning in the ordinary

Jones discovered that the relevance of the day-to-day work mattered more than the industry label itself. Once he committed to the role, his responsibilities grew. He became increasingly hands-on in supporting Mick Joyner, the business owner, taking on operational and strategic duties that gave him real ownership over outcomes. This gradual deepening of responsibility—combined with the trust and respect he built—transformed his initial reluctance into genuine commitment.

This trajectory is detailed more extensively in the full episode on Listenly, where Jones reflects on how personal circumstances eventually led Joyner to offer him and Sean, the external management accountant, the opportunity to acquire J Tools itself.
