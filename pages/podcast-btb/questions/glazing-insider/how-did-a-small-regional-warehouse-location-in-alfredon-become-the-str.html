How a Chance Warehouse Location in Alfredon Built a National Distribution Hub

12 September 2026 Glazing Insider · Greg Kelly

← See the Glazing Insider fiche

The choice of Alfredon as a warehouse location in 1992 was purely opportunistic—Kelly's father needed space for an employee who lived nearby. But the location proved unexpectedly well-positioned geographically for serving the broader UK market, with good motorway links for distribution and proximity to a manufacturing base that supplied profile companies already in Kelly's customer network.

When Kelly's father decided to expand AMK Hardware from Northern Ireland to the UK mainland, the move was driven not by market analysis but by supplier pressure. Distributors who worked with the business in Northern Ireland encouraged him to replicate the model on the mainland. The actual site selection was equally unplanned: an employee needed housing near Alfredon, and because that person required warehouse access nearby, the location became the default choice.

What could have been a logistical compromise turned into a strategic advantage. The area offered good motorway connectivity for nationwide distribution—critical for moving stock efficiently across the UK. More importantly, Alfredon sat close to a manufacturing cluster that supplied profile companies like Sincere and Shearframe, which were already customers through the legacy hardware side of the business. This existing relationship network meant the warehouse wasn't starting from zero; it inherited a customer base and supplier ecosystem ready to support expansion.

In the episode, Kelly reflects on how accidental location choices can unlock unexpected value when geography aligns with logistics and market structure. The Alfredon site became the foundation not just for hardware distribution but eventually for the company's later pivot into composite door manufacturing, serving as the hub from which Kelly managed expansion into full door set production starting in 2017–2018.

"We're not the cheapest, we're not the most expensive, but we offer good value for money and a nicely made product for the price that we charge."

The real insight buried in Kelly's story is that location decisions in family businesses often succeed not because of perfect planning but because they inherit existing relationship networks. Alfredon's advantage wasn't that it was theoretically optimal—it was that it connected directly to suppliers and customers already in the business ecosystem. That inherited network compounded over time and made the location stick, even after the original pragmatic reason (one employee's housing need) became irrelevant.

What role does remote family business structure play in day-to-day management across geographically separated sites?

The business operates with 58 staff across three sites—two in the UK and one in Lurgan, Northern Ireland—requiring careful coordination between remote teams managing both hardware distribution and composite door production.

How does supplier loyalty influence a composite door manufacturer's product choices and relationships?

Kelly uses Winkhouse Locks exclusively on all products because of a strong relationship built through their hardware business agency, showing how legacy relationships shape manufacturing decisions.

What defines the quality approach of a boutique composite door manufacturer competing against larger volume producers?

Kelly emphasizes they do not chase volume and never source cheap components just to win orders, positioning themselves in the middle market with good value and nicely made products.
