Why do in-person trade shows remain strategically important despite easy access to digital product information?

September 14, 2026 Glazing Insider · Nikki West

← See the Glazing Insider fiche

There is nothing quite like being in person, touching and feeling products, and talking to people directly. Face-to-face meetings and the ability to look people in the eyes are critical signals of business credibility, especially in tougher economic climates, and many visitors come specifically to put names to faces they communicate with every day.

The shift from purely digital interactions to physical presence reflects a deeper business need. When economic pressures mount, companies want reassurance that their partners and suppliers are genuinely stable and committed. As Nikki West, Event Director of The Fit Show, explains, personal connections at trade shows prove to the market that a business is fit for purpose.

Trade shows like the NEC in Birmingham consolidate what would otherwise require months of individual supplier visits. You can see all the products under one roof across three days, which saves time and money while giving fabricators and installers the opportunity to grow their businesses through direct product comparison and live conversations with multiple exhibitors simultaneously.

The human element that digital cannot replicate

No digital catalogue or online product database can replicate the experience of handling materials, assessing quality in real light, and reading a supplier's body language and commitment in a conversation. Attendees arrive with specific sourcing needs, but they also come to validate that the people behind the brands are trustworthy and present in the market.

This is particularly true when Fit Show 2025 featured 300 brands and 50% of exhibitors were new to the event, introducing fresh products and new companies to installers, builders, and fitters who needed to evaluate them in person before committing to business relationships.

"If you're selling in the home improvement sector, you have to be here. And if you're buying, if you've got to source products or you want to buy anything, you've got to be at Fit Show."

To understand just how critical in-person presence has become, listen to the full episode to discover how seven systems houses participated in 2025, up from just two in 2023, signalling that even large, established players recognise the strategic necessity of physical exhibition.

Key takeaways

  • Touching, feeling, and physically examining products cannot be replaced by digital information or images alone.
  • Direct eye contact and personal conversation with suppliers and manufacturers build trust and prove business stability, especially during economic uncertainty.
  • Trade shows allow multiple business relationships to be formed and validated in a concentrated time and space, saving cost and travel compared to individual site visits.
  • Major exhibitors and new market entrants alike recognise that physical presence at key trade shows is essential to credibility and business growth in the home improvement sector.
