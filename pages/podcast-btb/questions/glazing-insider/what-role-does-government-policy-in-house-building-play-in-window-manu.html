How does government policy in house building play a role in window manufacturing demand?

2 September 2026 Glazing Insider · Greg Johnson

← See the Glazing Insider fiche

Government housing investment creates direct, measurable demand for windows and doors. The UK government earmarked £39 billion for social and affordable housing in its recent spending review, plus additional funding for retrofit work—and whether homes are built through modern methods construction or traditional methods, every single house requires windows and doors, making house building growth critical to the entire fenestration sector.

With 165,000 children currently stuck in temporary accommodation due to insufficient housing stock, the pressure on government to build is acute. As Greg Johnson explains in the Glazing Insider episode, this housing crisis isn't just a social issue—it's a direct economic driver for manufacturers like his own.

Why housing policy translates into immediate manufacturing orders

The link between government housing targets and window demand is straightforward and inelastic. Every new dwelling requires fenestration products, whether that property is a starter home, social housing, or retrofit renovation. When government commits to building or improving housing stock, manufacturers in the glazing and doors sector see measurable orders downstream.

This connection is discussed in depth across the Glazing Insider podcast, where the relationship between construction policy and manufacturing capacity becomes clear. For Greg Johnson's business, serving the Liverpool City region—the largest window manufacturer there—government housing investment isn't abstract policy. It directly shapes hiring, production planning, and investment in equipment and facilities.

If you want to hear exactly how Greg Johnson sees the interplay between social housing targets, retrofit schemes, and his company's production roadmap, listen to the full episode on Listenly—there's also detail on how modern methods construction affects fenestration specification differently than traditional build methods.

Key takeaways

  • Government spending of £39 billion on social and affordable housing creates sustained demand for windows and doors across the fenestration industry.
  • 165,000 children in temporary accommodation underscores the scale of housing shortage, which in turn justifies continued investment and manufacturing growth.
  • Whether built through modern methods or traditional construction, every new house requires fenestration products, making housing policy a direct economic lever for manufacturers.
  • Manufacturers in the region must align production and workforce planning to government housing cycles and retrofit programmes to capture opportunity and meet delivery timelines.
