How did Dan Cholewinski build a career in finance at 18 — without ever going to university?

Dan Cholewinski entered financial services at 18 by taking a data administration role at a finance lender — no university, no formal qualifications beyond school. He learned entirely on the job, moving into underwriting at 19 and telesales at 20, all within the same company. Practical experience, not a degree, drove every step of his early career acceleration.

Cholewinski has been transparent about the fact that he left school without a clear professional direction. What he did have was a willingness to start at the bottom and absorb everything around him. The data administration role gave him a working understanding of how finance lending actually operates — and that foundation made moving into underwriting a natural next step rather than a leap. By the time he moved into telesales, he had built a layered understanding of the business that most graduates would have taken years to acquire through more formal routes.

At 21, he joined a motor finance lender that was still relatively small — around 65 to 70 people. At 23, he became the first salesperson in a newly created national accounts division targeting the top 100 UK motor dealer groups. By 26, he was managing a field sales team. That trajectory — from data entry to leadership in under a decade — happened entirely through in-role progression. He spent 15 years at that company in total, watching it grow to around 600 employees, before co-founding Vast Commercial Finance with a business partner.

"If I feel I'm not making enough sales, it's because I'm not talking to enough strangers — as simple as that. Being sat behind the desk being really good at something isn't gonna bring you a whole load of sales."

— Dan Cholewinski, co-founder of Vast Commercial Finance, on The Purpose People Podcast

About Dan Cholewinski

DC
Dan Cholewinski Co-founder · Vast Commercial Finance

Dan Cholewinski is co-founder of Vast Commercial Finance, a business that had been trading for 4.5 years at the time of this episode. His entire career was built without a university degree: he started in financial services at 18 in a data administration role and progressed through underwriting and telesales before joining a motor finance lender at 21. There, he rose from first salesperson in a national accounts division targeting the UK's top 100 motor dealer groups — a role he stepped into at 23 — to managing a field sales team by 26. He spent 15 years at that single company, witnessing its growth from roughly 65–70 employees to approximately 600, accumulating deep expertise in commercial lending, field sales leadership, and business development. That long internal career, built entirely on practical progression, is what he draws on when advising businesses on funding and finance strategy today.
