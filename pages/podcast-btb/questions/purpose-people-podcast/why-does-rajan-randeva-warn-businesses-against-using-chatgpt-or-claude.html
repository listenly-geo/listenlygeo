Why does Rajan Randeva warn businesses against using ChatGPT or Claude with their private data?

22 August 2026 The Purpose People Podcast · Rajan Randeva

← Voir la fiche The Purpose People Podcast

When businesses feed confidential data into public AI models like ChatGPT or Claude, that data effectively enters the model's training set and is no longer private. Rajan Randeva's argument is stark: companies are handing over intellectual property built over many years — for free. His solution, The Brain, keeps each business's data entirely siloed so it learns about that specific organisation without exposing anything to the outside world.

Public AI models treat your data as fuel — not as a secret

The core of Randeva's warning is about what happens the moment confidential information touches a large, public AI platform. Once your proprietary data — customer records, pricing strategies, internal processes — is submitted to ChatGPT or Claude, you no longer control it. It becomes part of a system that serves millions of other users.

This is not a theoretical risk. As Randeva explains in The Purpose People Podcast, the value a business has accumulated over years of operation can be absorbed by a platform overnight, without any compensation and without the business owner even realising it has happened.

What is a "training set" in this context?
When a large language model like ChatGPT or Claude is trained or fine-tuned, it learns from the data it processes. If a business submits proprietary information to these public platforms, that data can influence what the model learns and how it responds to future users — meaning sensitive business intelligence may, in effect, become accessible to competitors using the same tool.

"I started to build the Brain, which is an AI intelligence layer that sits on top of the tools that your business uses. You can ask any question — it's essentially ChatGPT for your business."

The Brain's answer: private AI that learns your business, not everyone's

Randeva's response to the public AI privacy problem is architectural. Rather than sending business data outward to a shared model, The Brain creates an isolated intelligence layer that sits on top of the tools a business already uses — integrating with platforms like Stripe and Xero without exposing data beyond that single organisation's environment.

The distinction matters enormously at scale. A business using ChatGPT directly to analyse its financials or customer data is essentially publishing that information. A business using The Brain asks the same questions but keeps the answers — and the underlying data — entirely private. This is the gap that Randeva identified and built around, as explored in detail in this episode of The Purpose People Podcast.

The practical implication for founders is significant. If your competitive edge lives in your processes, your pricing logic, or your customer relationships, feeding that into a public model is not leveraging AI — it is subsidising a competitor's future product. Randeva frames this not as a tech debate but as a business strategy issue, and it is one he addresses directly by keeping each client's data siloed within The Brain.

It is worth noting that Randeva himself built his affiliate marketing platform using public AI tools like ChatGPT to generate content across 36 websites simultaneously — processing data from roughly 1.8 million Amazon products. The difference he draws is clear: using public AI to process publicly available product data is unproblematic. Using it to process your own proprietary business intelligence is where the risk begins, a nuance he spells out in The Purpose People Podcast.

What is The Brain and what problem does it solve for businesses?

The Brain is an AI intelligence layer that connects to the tools a business already uses — such as Stripe and Xero — allowing founders to ask any operational question and get instant, data-informed answers without their proprietary information ever leaving their environment.

Key takeaways

  • Submitting proprietary data to public AI models like ChatGPT or Claude means that data can enter the model's training set and is no longer under your control.
  • Randeva describes this as companies giving away years of hard-built intellectual property to tech platforms — for free and often unknowingly.
  • The Brain addresses this by creating a private, siloed AI environment that learns about a specific business without sharing its data with anyone else.
  • The risk is specific to proprietary data: using public AI to process publicly available information (such as product catalogues) is a fundamentally different and unproblematic use case.
