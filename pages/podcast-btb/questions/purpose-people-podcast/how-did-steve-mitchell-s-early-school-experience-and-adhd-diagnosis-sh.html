How did Steve Mitchell's early school experience and ADHD diagnosis shape his later success?

August 29, 2026 The Purpose People Podcast · Steve Mitchell

← See the The Purpose People Podcast fiche

Steve Mitchell was labeled a daydreamer and naughty kid in school—what would now be recognized as undiagnosed ADHD—and told by his teacher Mrs. Hamza that he would never succeed in the army. Instead of accepting that verdict, he used her dismissal as fuel to prove her wrong, going on to serve six years successfully as an infantryman across multiple postings.

School was difficult for Mitchell, not because of academic ability but because of how his mind worked. His teacher reports from that era were blunt: unable to concentrate, daydreaming in class, poor timekeeping, no ability to focus on detail. Back then, these traits were simply blamed on bad behavior rather than recognized as a neurological condition.

When he was about to leave school, Mitchell decided he wanted to join the army—partly because of his time in the army cadets and partly because, coming from a council estate, the realistic paths forward were menial jobs, construction work, a trade, or military service. But when he told his teacher Mrs. Hamza about his plan, she laughed out loud, telling him his terrible timekeeping and lack of attention to detail meant he would not last two minutes.

"You are the most un-timekeeping person. Your timekeeping is absolutely terrible, your attention to detail is absolutely terrible, you can't concentrate—you won't last two minutes in the army."

That moment of ridicule became a catalyst. Mitchell explains in the episode that her dismissal made him think: "I'm going to do that and prove you wrong." He enlisted in the British Army and, despite—or perhaps because of—his history of poor attention and timekeeping, went on to thrive. He served as an infantryman for six years, deployed to Colchester, Northern Ireland (three separate tours), Cyprus, the Middle East, Canada, America, and Egypt.

The rejection Mitchell faced was not a barrier but a proving ground. His ADHD, which school had pathologized as mere misbehavior, did not disappear in the army—but in that structured environment with clear objectives, it transformed from a weakness into a strength. The very traits that made him look like a failure in the classroom became assets in a mission-driven setting.

This experience of turning doubt into determination did not end with the army. As discussed in this podcast, Mitchell went on to build a multifaceted career as a musician, entertainer, chef, and eventually founder of Release the Best You, a personal development coaching business. He has now been sober for 10 years and is focused on helping others reimagine their potential—the very lesson his teacher's laughter unwittingly taught him.

Key takeaways

  • School labeled Mitchell as a daydreamer with poor concentration and timekeeping—symptoms now understood as ADHD—but no one recognized the neurological root.
  • A teacher's dismissive laugh about his army ambition became a turning point: instead of internalizing the rejection, he used it as motivation to prove her wrong.
  • Structure and purpose transformed what school called a weakness into an asset; Mitchell thrived as an infantryman and later built a successful personal development coaching practice.
  • His journey shows how a single moment of belief in oneself, sparked by the desire to defy doubt, can reshape an entire life trajectory.
