How did a Facebook community become the heart of customer loyalty for a running brand?

12 September 2026 The Purpose People Podcast · Nicky Chrascina

← See the The Purpose People Podcast fiche

Nicky Chrascina built Flancy Activewear's customer loyalty by launching the Flanders Active Community on Facebook eight years ago at customer request. The community has remained remarkably positive, requiring only one member to be moderated in its entire history, while organizing park run meetups and child running weekends across the country that sell out within 24 hours.

A community born from customer demand

The Flanders Active Community emerged organically—it wasn't a top-down marketing initiative, but rather a direct response to what customers were asking for. Nicky and her designer Jan recognized that Flancy's core audience of women aged 35-55 shared a common value: supporting other women. This demographic naturally gravitated toward connection and shared purpose beyond just purchasing activewear.

As Nicky explains in the episode, the minimal moderation required—only one member in eight years—reflects the self-policing culture within the group. The women-supporting-women ethos created a foundation so strong that the community naturally enforced its own positive culture without friction.

From digital connection to real-world events

What distinguishes the Flanders Active Community is its ability to translate digital engagement into physical meetups. The group organizes Park Run gatherings across the United Kingdom, creating opportunities for members to run together in their local areas and build tangible relationships beyond the Facebook group.

The child running weekends represent another layer of community-building that extends beyond the core customer base. These events sell out almost within 24 hours, signaling both the appetite for structured running experiences and the trust members place in events organized under the Flancy umbrella. This transforms casual community members into brand advocates who introduce their families to the Flancy ecosystem.

A concrete example of how these community efforts drive loyalty is documented in this podcast episode, where Nicky discusses how community participation directly fuels repeat purchases and word-of-mouth promotion among participants.

How did Nicky transition from a 25-year career in pharmaceutical sales to launching a retail activewear business?

While working as a pharmaceutical representative specializing in oncology, Nicky became increasingly stressed by high pressure and constant weekend and evening commitments. Her passion for running and frustration with the lack of colorful activewear on the market ultimately prompted her to launch Flancy Activewear.

What was the unexpected market demand that proved initial skepticism about skorts wrong?

When Nicky proposed launching with skorts, a sportswear designer told her there was a good reason people did not wear them because people would not buy them. However, skorts have become Flancy's best-selling product, accounting for more than 50% of total sales.

What are the key differences between Flancy Activewear and major competitors like Nike?

Flancy's primary differentiation is color and design diversity. While Nike primarily focuses on black and very plain colors, Flancy features an in-house design team that produces approximately 85 different bespoke designs in their current collection.
