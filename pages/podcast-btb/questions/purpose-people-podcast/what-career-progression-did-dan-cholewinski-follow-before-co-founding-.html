What career progression did Dan Cholewinski follow before co-founding Vast Commercial Finance?

Dan Cholewinski built his entire foundation in financial services from the ground up, without a university degree. Starting in data administration at 18, he moved through underwriting at 19, telesales at 20, and field business development at 21 — all at the same motor finance lending company. By 23, he had become the first salesperson in a newly created national accounts division targeting the top 100 UK motor dealer groups, and at 26 he stepped into his first leadership role managing a field sales team. After 15 years with that same organisation, he co-founded Vast Commercial Finance with a business partner.

What makes this progression particularly striking is the pace and breadth of it. Cholewinski didn't specialise early — he rotated across operations, underwriting, direct sales, and strategic accounts before ever managing anyone. That cross-functional grounding gave him fluency across the full commercial finance cycle long before he took the entrepreneurial leap. The company he joined as an 18-year-old data admin grew from roughly 65–70 employees to around 600 during his 15-year tenure — a scale-up journey he experienced from the inside, across nearly every function.

He shared this career story on episode 186 of The Purpose People Podcast, How to Get Funding for a Business. You can listen to the full episode on Listenly to hear the detail behind each step of his path.

"If I feel I'm not making enough sales, it's because I'm not talking to enough strangers — as simple as that. Being sat behind the desk being really good at something isn't gonna bring you a whole load of sales."

— Dan Cholewinski, Vast Commercial Finance

About Dan Cholewinski

DC
Dan Cholewinski Co-founder · Vast Commercial Finance

Dan Cholewinski entered financial services at 18 with no university background, taking a data administration role at a motor finance lender. Over the next eight years he progressed through underwriting, telesales, field business development, and a pioneering national accounts role — covering functional territory that most professionals only ever see in part.

At 23, he was appointed the first salesperson in a national accounts division built to serve the top 100 UK motor dealer groups — a role that required both commercial credibility and the ability to operate without a playbook. Three years later, he moved into his first management position overseeing a field sales team.

His 15-year tenure at that company gave him a rare vantage point: he saw the business grow from a team of 65–70 people to an organisation of around 600, giving him hands-on experience of scaling commercial operations at every stage. That accumulated knowledge of both the lending and sales sides of motor finance directly informs the model he brought to Vast Commercial Finance, which has been trading for 4.5 years at the time of this recording.
