What is FlexVision and how does it differ from Google Lens?

21 August 2026 The Purpose People Podcast · Rajan Randeva

← Voir la fiche The Purpose People Podcast

FlexVision is a visual affiliate tool built by Rajan Randeva: you take a photo of anything, and the platform instantly returns monetized affiliate links for every product or service visible in that image. Google Lens does something visually similar — but generates links that keep users inside Google's own ecosystem, with zero affiliate monetization. FlexVision's entire point is to turn every photo into revenue, immediately.

A photo becomes a storefront — detecting products, locations, and services at once

FlexVision sits inside Randeva's broader affiliate platform, the same infrastructure that already indexes 1.8 million products pulled from Amazon Associates. When a user snaps a photo, FlexVision doesn't just identify objects — it also detects location context to surface relevant service-based offerings. Photograph a home interior, and FlexVision can suggest home insurance products alongside the physical items in frame.

This dual layer — product recognition plus location-aware services — is what separates FlexVision from a standard visual search tool. Randeva describes this combination as something no one else is currently doing at scale, a claim he makes in The Purpose People Podcast. Partners like Booking.com and Expedia illustrate the kind of service-side affiliate relationships the platform is built to activate.

Google Lens keeps users in Google's world — FlexVision routes them to a purchase

The functional gap between the two tools is commercial intent. Google Lens is designed to answer a visual query and surface more Google content — search results, Shopping tabs, knowledge panels. Monetization for the end user pointing a phone at something is simply not part of the design.

FlexVision is built on the opposite premise: every image is a conversion opportunity. The moment the photo is processed, affiliate links fire. Brands like Dyson or Louis Vuitton, already indexed across the platform's affiliate catalogue, become clickable and commissionable directly from a single image. As Randeva explains across multiple discussions featured in this episode, the business logic is straightforward — visual search already exists, so the question was why it wasn't already generating affiliate revenue at scale.

Visual affiliate marketing — an approach where image recognition technology identifies products or services within a photo and automatically generates trackable affiliate links, allowing the platform operator to earn a commission on any resulting purchase. FlexVision applies this to both physical products (via Amazon Associates and brand partners) and location-relevant services (such as insurance or travel bookings).

"I started to build the Brain, which is an AI intelligence layer that sits on top of the tools that your business uses. You can ask any question — it's essentially ChatGPT for your business."

The scale Randeva has already built on the affiliate side — 1.8 million indexed products, peak sales of around 30 treadmills per day during December New Year campaigns — gives FlexVision a ready-made catalogue to draw from the moment a photo is processed. The visual layer is, in his framing, the next interface on top of infrastructure that already works. The full context of how this connects to his wider AI ambitions is detailed in this conversation.
