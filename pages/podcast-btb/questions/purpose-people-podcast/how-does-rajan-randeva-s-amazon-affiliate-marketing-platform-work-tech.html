How does Rajan Randeva's Amazon affiliate marketing platform work technically?

20 August 2026 The Purpose People Podcast · Rajan Randeva

← Voir la fiche The Purpose People Podcast

Rajan Randeva signed up to Amazon Associates, obtained an API key, and pulled data from approximately 1.8 million products. ChatGPT then used that data to generate blog content, published across 36 active websites he had purchased specifically for their domain authority and backlink history — allowing them to rank highly on Google and sometimes outrank Amazon itself. At peak, in December, the network was moving around 30 treadmills per day.

From a single API key to a 36-site network outranking Amazon on Google

The technical foundation is straightforward: Amazon Associates provides both the affiliate tracking and the product API. By connecting to that API, Randeva ingested roughly 1.8 million product listings — a dataset large enough for ChatGPT to generate targeted, keyword-rich blog posts at a scale no human editorial team could match.

The distribution strategy is what gave the model its edge. Rather than launching new domains from scratch, Randeva acquired existing websites — paying up to $500 per site — chosen specifically for their established domain authority and backlink profiles. That head start meant freshly published AI content could index quickly on Google, bypassing the typical months-long sandbox period for new sites.

£300,000 in three months — and 30 treadmills a day in December

The timing was deliberate. December's New Year fitness surge translated directly into high-intent search traffic for equipment like treadmills. With 36 active websites feeding that demand simultaneously, the network was converting around 30 treadmill sales per day at its peak.

Over the first three months of operation, the platform generated £300,000 in total revenue — roughly £100,000 per month. That figure came entirely through affiliate commissions: no inventory, no fulfilment, no customer service.
