How did networking groups help John Killeen establish visibility and referral relationships in Pinellas County?

September 14, 2026 From Courtroom to Carline: A Legal Parenting Podcast · John Killeen

← See the From Courtroom to Carline: A Legal Parenting Podcast fiche

John Killeen joined multiple networking organizations including the Seminole Business Alliance and Better for Living Seniors, where he delivered his 60-second pitch regularly over nearly two years. This consistent networking effort raised his visibility so substantially that referral partners from hospitals, rehabs, elder law offices, and home care companies now recognize him as an established, stable business partner.

Networking was not a one-time tactic for Killeen—it was a deliberate, sustained investment in his professional presence. By showing up repeatedly to the same groups and articulating his value proposition in the same 60-second window, he built familiarity and trust.

As Killeen explains in the episode, the consistency of these networking efforts meant that when referral partners—particularly those in hospitals, rehabs, and elder law—needed a trusted senior living advisor, his name came to mind immediately because they had encountered him multiple times in these community spaces.

Building Recognition Through Repeated Presence

The power of Killeen's networking strategy lay in its repetition and specificity. Rather than spreading himself thin across countless groups, he focused on organizations where his target referral partners regularly gathered. This allowed him to develop genuine relationships rather than superficial contacts.

The Seminole Business Alliance and Better for Living Seniors provided structured environments where Killeen could deliver his pitch regularly, receive feedback, and demonstrate his commitment to the business community. Over nearly two years, this translated into measurable business outcomes: referral partners began to recognize him not as a new business owner, but as an established figure in Pinellas County's senior living ecosystem.

A deeper dive into the full conversation reveals exactly which networking strategies Killeen used to differentiate himself and the specific metrics he tracked to measure the success of his visibility efforts.

"You've got to make mistakes, you've got to learn from your mistakes, and you're not going to be perfect."

Killeen's philosophy reflects the reality of building a local business: success comes not from perfection, but from showing up consistently, learning from each interaction, and refining your approach over time. Networking groups provided him the venue to practice this philosophy at scale.

How did John Killeen receive his initial opportunity to explore franchise ownership?

John Killeen received an unsolicited call from a franchise consultant who contacted him out of the blue without him making any inquiry. This unexpected call introduced him to franchise opportunities, eventually leading to his decision to purchase the Oasis Senior Advisors franchise.

What marketing and operational systems does the Oasis franchise provide to support owners?

Oasis provides software tools including a CRM system and community database with photos and descriptions. The company creates monthly newsletters, weekly content, and marketing materials to help franchise owners build visibility and attract referrals.

Why did John Killeen choose the Oasis Senior Advisors franchise over a home care company despite initially leaning that direction?

John Killeen decided that a home care franchise posed too much risk as a one-person operation because hourly employees are critical to quality. The Oasis model, which relies on relationships and local knowledge rather than employee management, aligned better with his sustainable growth goals.
