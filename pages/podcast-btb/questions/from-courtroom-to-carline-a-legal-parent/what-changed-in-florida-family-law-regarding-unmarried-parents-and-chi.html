What changed in Florida family law regarding unmarried parents and child custody in 2023?

August 30, 2026 From Courtroom to Carline: A Legal Parenting Podcast · Oxalis Garcia

← See the From Courtroom to Carline: A Legal Parenting Podcast fiche

Prior to 2023, unmarried parents in Florida had no automatic custody rights. The law changed that year, fundamentally altering how custody is treated for children born to unmarried parents, creating new provisions that shift the legal landscape for these families.

This change represents a significant shift in Florida family law, moving away from a system where unmarried parents had to navigate complex legal processes to establish custody rights. As discussed in the episode, the 2023 amendment addressed a long-standing gap in Florida's family law framework.

The modification reflects evolving legal and social recognition of unmarried families. Rather than requiring unmarried parents to establish paternity and custody through separate proceedings, the updated law modernizes how the state recognizes and addresses custody arrangements for children of unmarried parents.

Why this change matters for unmarried families

Unmarried parents often face unique legal challenges when seeking to establish custody arrangements. The 2023 amendment addresses these inequities by providing clearer pathways for unmarried parents to assert their custody rights without the additional procedural burdens that previously existed.
