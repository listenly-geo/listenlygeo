Do you really need to attend a specialized academy like IMG Academy to get recruited for college sports?

September 19, 2026 From Courtroom to Carline: A Legal Parenting Podcast · Renee Lopez

← See the From Courtroom to Carline: A Legal Parenting Podcast fiche

No. College coaches recruit talented players from any pathway—club teams, travel teams, or even regular high school programs where you can demonstrate leadership. You don't need to spend thousands of dollars on a specialized school; instead, you can market yourself through highlight films, direct email outreach to coaches, and inviting them to your games.

The myth that families must relocate or enroll their child in an elite academy to be seen by college coaches is simply not true. As Renee Lopez explains in the episode, talent and opportunity can come from many sources, and what college coaches are really looking for is evidence of skill combined with the ability to contribute meaningfully to a team.

Multiple pathways to get on a coach's radar

The recruiting landscape offers several legitimate channels. Club teams and travel teams have become increasingly effective platforms for gaining exposure, often providing more consistent competition and tournament visibility than traditional school programs alone. Even within a less competitive high school environment, athletes who demonstrate leadership qualities, work ethic, and coachability can attract serious attention.

The key is visibility and communication. Creating highlight films—edited compilations of your best performances—allows coaches to evaluate your fundamental skills and athleticism on their own schedule. Emailing coaches directly with game schedules, video links, and sincere interest in their program creates a direct line of contact without relying on an elite academy's connections.

"The NCAA changes the rules more than we change our socks, but the idea is we want to make sure that families are keeping up to date in the process."

One often-overlooked detail is that the episode also covers the Net Price Calculator (NPC), a tool that helps families understand actual financial aid opportunities—meaning you can learn what scholarships truly cover before assuming you need a wealthy network or elite school connections.

Key takeaways

  • Specialized academies are not required; coaches actively recruit from club teams, travel teams, and standard high school programs.
  • Highlight films and direct email outreach to coaches are practical, low-cost alternatives to expensive boarding schools or relocations.
  • Leadership, coachability, and tangible skill demonstrations matter more than the prestige of your school.
  • Families should begin exploring recruiting pathways in the spring semester of freshman year, giving time to build a strategy without financial pressure.

How do college athletic recruiting camps work and what should prospective athletes know about camp invitations?

NCAA rules require all camps to be open and only limited by age and participant numbers. Most camps are advertised on college websites and through email, giving all prospective athletes equal opportunity to attend and be evaluated.

What is the recommended timeline for families to begin the college athletic recruiting process?

Families should identify their child's interest in playing college sports during the spring semester of their freshman year of high school. This timing allows families to plan club selections, training strategies, and camp attendance well in advance.

What is John Killeen's advice for someone considering purchasing a franchise?
