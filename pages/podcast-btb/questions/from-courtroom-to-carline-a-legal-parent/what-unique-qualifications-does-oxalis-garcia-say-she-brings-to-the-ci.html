What unique qualifications does Oxalis Garcia bring to the circuit court bench?

September 3, 2026 From Courtroom to Carline: A Legal Parenting Podcast · Oxalis Garcia

← See the From Courtroom to Carline: A Legal Parenting Podcast fiche

Garcia argues she brings a multicultural and multi-jurisdictional perspective to the bench that she believes no other judge currently has in the Sixth Judicial Circuit. She emphasizes that understanding cultural nuances is critical for a judge, since many factors beyond the written law influence the outcome of a case.

Twenty years of practice across three jurisdictions

Garcia's unique qualifications stem from her career trajectory across multiple states and legal systems. Since obtaining her first bar license in 2006, she has been licensed to practice law in Puerto Rico, Florida, and Maryland—three distinct jurisdictions with different legal frameworks and cultural contexts. She has been practicing in Florida specifically since 2012, building deep expertise in the state's court system while maintaining connections to her multicultural background.

This multi-jurisdictional experience, as Garcia explains in the episode, is not merely a matter of holding multiple licenses. It reflects lived experience navigating different legal cultures, moving between Puerto Rico and the mainland United States during her childhood, and practicing law in geographically and culturally distinct regions. This breadth of exposure sets her apart from judges who have practiced in a single jurisdiction their entire career.

Specialized expertise in complex family law matters

Beyond geography, Garcia brings deep specialization in family law cases involving complex jurisdictional and international dimensions. She is known for handling complicated jurisdictional matters and international Hague child abduction cases—a niche area requiring both cultural sensitivity and sophisticated legal knowledge. This specialization means she has dealt with cases that inherently demand understanding of how different legal systems and cultural values intersect.

The Hague Treaty work in particular has exposed her to families from diverse backgrounds navigating cross-border disputes. As Garcia discusses in this podcast, many factors beyond the written law—including cultural expectations around family, parenting, and authority—shape how these cases unfold. A judge who has practiced extensively in this arena understands that strict legal interpretation alone cannot deliver justice.

"I bring a multicultural and multi-jurisdictional perspective to the bench, which is something that I don't believe any other judge has at this moment in time in the Sixth Judicial Circuit."

Garcia's decision to run for the circuit court bench reflects her conviction that judicial experience should include not only legal training but also deep cultural competency. She argues that a judge serving a diverse community like Pinellas County needs to understand the real lives and backgrounds of the families who appear before them. This perspective, rooted in her own multicultural identity and professional experience across multiple legal systems, is what she believes she uniquely brings to the Sixth Judicial Circuit. You can hear more about her judicial philosophy and her complete campaign vision in the full episode.
