How does Oxalis Garcia address questions about political affiliation during her judicial campaign?

Garcia's answer is straightforward: the circuit judge position is a nonpartisan role, which legally prohibits her from affiliating with any political party. She invokes the figure of Lady Justice — blindfolded and impartial — as the clearest symbol of what the role demands. Even so, she acknowledges that some voters still press her to reveal her political leanings.

A role that is defined by its political blindness

When voters ask Garcia which party she belongs to, her first move is to reframe the question entirely. The circuit judge seat she is seeking — Pinellas County Circuit Judge Group 44, within the Sixth Judicial Circuit — is, by design, a nonpartisan position. That is not a campaign talking point; it is a legal constraint.

This means Garcia cannot formally affiliate with any political party, attend partisan events as a candidate, or solicit donations based on party loyalty. The position requires her to step outside the partisan framework entirely, something she addresses head-on whenever the question comes up on the trail, as she explains in episode #55 of From Courtroom to Carline.

Lady Justice as the answer she gives voters who keep pushing

Garcia does not stop at citing the legal rule. She reaches for the most recognizable image in the legal world: Lady Justice, blindfolded, holding her scales. The blindfold is not decorative — it represents the principle that justice must be administered without regard to who is standing before the bench.

That image, Garcia argues, is the honest answer to any question about her political leanings. What matters to her is whether the facts and the law support a party's position, not which party that person voted for. This framing is something she returns to throughout her campaign, and it is discussed in depth in From Courtroom to Carline.

Still, she is candid about the reality on the ground: some voters are not satisfied with the institutional explanation. They push past the nonpartisan label and want to know, practically, where Garcia stands. She acknowledges this tension openly rather than dismissing it — a point worth noting for anyone following judicial campaigns in Florida, and one that the podcast explores with real frankness.

Nonpartisan judicial election: A judicial election in which candidates are not permitted to formally affiliate with or campaign on behalf of a political party. The circuit judge positions within Florida's Sixth Judicial Circuit, including Pinellas County Circuit Judge Group 44, are nonpartisan by law. Candidates may not solicit partisan endorsements or organize their campaigns along party lines.

What has Oxalis Garcia learned while campaigning for circuit judge in Pinellas County?

One of the most striking things Garcia discovered is that many people are unaware they can vote for judges, and that most judicial elections are decided in the primary rather than the general election.

Why is Oxalis Garcia running for Pinellas County Circuit Judge Group 44?

Garcia says she is running because she has reached a stage in her career, after 20 years of practice, where it is time to start a new chapter. She states that she wants to give back to the community and bring her multicultural, multi-jurisdictional perspective to the bench.

How did Oxalis Garcia become involved in international Hague child abduction cases?

Garcia became involved by chance when a client's daughter was taken to Trinidad and Tobago by the father. While trying to get a child pickup order in that situation, she discovered the International Hague Treaty framework and went on to build deep expertise in this area.
