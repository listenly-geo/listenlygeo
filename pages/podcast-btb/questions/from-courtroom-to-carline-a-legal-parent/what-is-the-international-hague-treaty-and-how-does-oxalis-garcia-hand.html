What is the International Hague Treaty and how does Oxalis Garcia handle those cases?

The International Hague Treaty deals specifically with international child abductions — situations where a parent takes a child out of one country and retains them in another. Attorney Oxalis Garcia helps the left-behind parent navigate the legal process to get their child returned, working across borders and frequently with Spanish-speaking clients.

International Hague Treaty (child abduction): A multilateral treaty that establishes a legal mechanism for the prompt return of children who have been internationally abducted or wrongfully retained by one parent in a country other than their country of habitual residence. It applies both ways — a child taken from the United States to another country, or brought into the United States from abroad.

A niche she entered by chance — and never left

Garcia did not set out to specialize in international Hague cases. As she explains in From Courtroom to Carline: A Legal Parenting Podcast, the work came to her by chance through a single client — and from there, it became one of the defining pillars of her practice.

Her profile makes her unusually well-suited for this area of law. Garcia is licensed in three jurisdictions — Puerto Rico, Florida, and Maryland — and has approximately 20 years of total legal experience. She handles cases in both directions: parents trying to retrieve a child brought into the United States, and parents trying to recover a child taken abroad.

The cross-border nature of these cases requires navigating multiple legal systems simultaneously, a challenge Garcia addresses through her multi-jurisdictional background. Her work often involves Spanish-speaking clients, an area where her Puerto Rican roots and bilingual fluency give her a direct practical advantage — a point she develops further in this episode.

From family law specialist to candidate for the bench — with the same caseload in view

Garcia is primarily known for family law, with a particular focus on complicated jurisdictional matters. International Hague cases sit at the intersection of both: they require family law expertise and the ability to reason across multiple legal frameworks at once.

This dual expertise informs her candidacy for Pinellas County Circuit Judge Group 44 in the Sixth Judicial Circuit. As she notes in the episode, she brings a multicultural and multi-jurisdictional perspective that she believes no other judge currently offers in that circuit.

"I bring a multicultural and multi-jurisdictional perspective to the bench, which is something that I don't believe any other judge has at this moment in time in the Sixth Judicial Circuit."

Who is Oxalis Garcia and what is her legal background?
