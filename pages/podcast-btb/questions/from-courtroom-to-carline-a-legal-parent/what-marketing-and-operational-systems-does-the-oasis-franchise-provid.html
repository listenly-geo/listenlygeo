How does Oasis equip franchise owners with marketing and operational tools?

September 12, 2026 From Courtroom to Carline: A Legal Parenting Podcast · John Killeen

← See the From Courtroom to Carline: A Legal Parenting Podcast fiche

Oasis provides franchise owners with a complete software suite including a CRM system and community database containing photos and descriptions of senior living facilities. The company handles content creation by supplying monthly newsletters, weekly social media posts adaptable across Facebook, LinkedIn, and Instagram, plus all marketing materials—so owners simply enter client information into the system and let automation distribute content.

The operational foundation relies on technology that removes repetitive marketing tasks from daily operations. Rather than franchisees creating content from scratch each week, Oasis pre-builds social media posts and newsletters that owners can customize for their specific communities and market positioning. This approach lets owners focus on the core work: meeting with families, touring facilities, and guiding seniors through placement decisions.

As John Killeen explains in the episode, the CRM stores all details about available communities, allowing owners to match seniors and families with the right facilities based on health care needs, location, and budget constraints. The database becomes the central nervous system—it holds the institutional knowledge that would otherwise require manual research for every single client conversation.

The monthly newsletter and weekly social cadence serve two purposes: they keep owners visible to potential clients while requiring minimal original effort. The support systems are designed to scale, which is critical when Oasis has 120 franchise owners across the country, each managing their own market but sharing the same operational backbone.

What makes the Oasis system particularly scalable is that franchise owners benefit from a shared marketing framework across all communities in their territory. The software ensures consistency while allowing local customization, so each franchise maintains its own brand voice and community relationships without recreating the operational wheel. This is especially valuable for franchisees newer to the senior living space, who gain immediate access to proven templates and processes.

Key takeaways

  • Oasis provides a CRM system and community database as the core operational infrastructure, eliminating manual lookups and file management.
  • Monthly newsletters and weekly social media content come pre-created and adaptable, reducing franchisees' marketing workload to data entry and customization.
  • Owners enter client information once into the system and let automation handle content distribution across multiple channels.
  • The shared framework allows all 120 franchise owners nationwide to scale their placement operations without building marketing and operational systems from scratch.

Why did John Killeen choose the Oasis Senior Advisors franchise over a home care company despite initially leaning that direction?

John Killeen decided that a home care franchise posed too much risk as a one-person operation because hourly employees are critical to quality, and one person cannot manage those operations effectively. Oasis, by contrast, is a placement service model that allows a single owner to scale without direct employee dependencies.

What specific factors should families evaluate when touring senior care communities?

John Killeen advises families to observe staff interactions with residents and among themselves, sample the meals since seniors eat three meals daily there, check facility cleanliness and maintenance, and assess whether the community feels warm and welcoming rather than sterile or institutional.

What flexibility exists in assisted living lease terms that families should know about?

Many assisted living communities have month-to-month lease options despite lengthy paperwork appearing to require a year-long commitment. Residents can often leave with 30 days' notice even though initial documentation may suggest longer terms, so families should ask about this flexibility explicitly.
