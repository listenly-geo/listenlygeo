When should families start the college athletic recruiting process?

September 17, 2026 From Courtroom to Carline: A Legal Parenting Podcast · Renee Lopez

← See the From Courtroom to Carline: A Legal Parenting Podcast fiche

Families should identify their child's interest in college athletics during the spring semester of freshman year in high school. This timing strikes a balance: students have settled into high school after the demanding first semester, accumulated some grades to share with coaches, and can begin exploring schools without early pressure.

Starting in spring of freshman year gives student-athletes a realistic window to begin the recruiting conversation. By this point, they're no longer adjusting to high school and have academic records coaches can evaluate.

The timing also protects families from premature pressure. Waiting until spring allows students to focus on the transition to high school itself during those critical first months, rather than simultaneously juggling new academics and recruiting expectations. This approach, as Renee Lopez explains in the podcast, reflects advice from 65 college coaches and athletic directors across 19 different sports.

The spring semester timing also means families can begin conversations with coaches about school of interest without the intensity of an earlier start. This creates a more sustainable recruiting timeline that respects both the student's academic adjustment and their athletic development during freshman year.

"The NCAA changes the rules more than we change our socks, but the idea is we want to make sure that families are keeping up to date in the process."

Beyond just knowing when to start, families benefit from understanding the deeper strategy behind recruiting timelines. The full episode covers specific recruiting strategies that build on this foundational timeline—including what coaches actually look for in early conversations and how families can position themselves effectively without over-investing too early.

Key takeaways

  • Begin recruiting conversations in spring of freshman year, after students have adjusted to high school and accumulated grades.
  • This timing avoids overwhelming students who are already managing the transition to high school during fall semester.
  • Coaches need at least some high school grades to evaluate student-athletes, making spring semester a realistic starting point.
  • Starting during freshman year spring allows families to explore schools without excessive early pressure, spreading the recruiting process over a sustainable timeline.
