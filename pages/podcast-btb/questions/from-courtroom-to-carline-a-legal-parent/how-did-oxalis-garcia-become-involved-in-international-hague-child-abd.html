Garcia became involved entirely by chance. A client came to her after the child's father took their daughter to Trinidad and Tobago. When Garcia's request for a child pickup order was denied by the Hillsborough County court, she sought guidance at an INS meeting — and was introduced to attorney Michael Berry, who specialized in Hague cases and was actively looking for a Spanish-speaking attorney to work with.

What is the International Hague Treaty?
As Oxalis Garcia explains in From Courtroom to Carline, the International Hague Treaty deals specifically with international child abductions. It applies when a parent takes a child out of a country and retains them elsewhere — whether that means bringing a child into the United States or removing one from it. The treaty provides a legal framework for the left-behind parent to seek the child's return.

A denied court order that changed the direction of her career

When Garcia first tried to help her client, she did what any family law attorney would do: she went to court. She filed for a child pickup order in Hillsborough County. The court denied the request.

That denial forced her to look elsewhere for answers. At an INS meeting, she was connected with Michael Berry, an attorney who had built a practice around Hague convention cases. Berry was specifically searching for a Spanish-speaking attorney — a fit that made the introduction immediately meaningful. The two eventually merged their firms, as Garcia recounts in the episode, forming a partnership built directly from that first accidental encounter.

From one client's crisis to a niche carved out over 20 years

Garcia has been practicing law since 2006 — and in Florida specifically since 2012. Her multilingual and multi-jurisdictional profile (licensed in Puerto Rico, Florida, and Maryland) made her a natural fit for cases that cross borders and legal systems. That one client's situation opened a door that has defined a significant part of her practice ever since.

Her background — born in Puerto Rico, raised partly in Ohio, trained across three bar jurisdictions — is precisely what makes her effective in cases that require navigating different legal cultures. This is a point she develops further in this episode of From Courtroom to Carline, where she also discusses her broader family law work and her candidacy for Pinellas County Circuit Judge Group 44.

"I bring a multicultural and multi-jurisdictional perspective to the bench, which is something that I don't believe any other judge has at this moment in time in the Sixth Judicial Circuit."
