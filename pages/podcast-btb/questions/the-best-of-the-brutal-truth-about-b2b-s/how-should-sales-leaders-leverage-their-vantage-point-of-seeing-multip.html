How should sales leaders leverage their vantage point of seeing multiple deals across their entire team to improve overall performance?

September 21, 2026 The Best of the Brutal Truth about B2B Sales & Selling · Guillaume

← See the The Best of the Brutal Truth about B2B Sales & Selling fiche

A sales leader with 10 reps sees 10 times the deal volume an individual rep encounters, giving them access to patterns others won't recognize for months or years. By aggregating and sharing these experiences across the team—distinguishing which companies fit your model from which are time sinks—leaders enable smarter time allocation and help reps avoid wasting effort on deals unlikely to close.

This vantage point is where great sales leadership separates from the rest. While many leaders lean on data to avoid difficult conversations, the real leverage comes from direct human exchange. When you consolidate insights from 10 simultaneous deal flows, patterns emerge that stay invisible to any single contributor working in isolation.

The practical impact is straightforward. Most sales professionals waste approximately 50 percent of their time on deals that either never close or show low likelihood of closing. As Guillaume explains in the episode, this pattern repeats because individual reps lack the comparative lens that comes from seeing how similar deals play out across an entire team portfolio.

Sharing Good Deals and Time Sinks Across the Team

The mechanism is simple but powerful: identify which companies and deal profiles align with what your business actually wins, and flag which ones—despite surface similarity—consistently become resource drains. This distinction prevents reps from chasing look-alike opportunities that feel promising but historically convert poorly.

When a team aggregates these experiences, pattern recognition accelerates dramatically. A rep might spend three months pursuing a prospect that shows every sign of success but ultimately fails. A leader who has watched this scenario unfold across the team already knows the warning signs. By sharing that institutional knowledge, you compress months of individual learning into immediate actionable guidance.

This isn't about selling as a team in the traditional sense—it's about pooling the raw intelligence each rep generates and redistributing it as collective wisdom. The best reps don't just work harder; they work smarter because they benefit from the accumulated experience of nine peers, multiplied by the deal cycles those peers have already navigated.

Key takeaways

  • A sales leader with 10 reps observes 10 times the deal flow of any single contributor, surfacing patterns individual reps miss for months or years.
  • Most sales professionals waste approximately 50 percent of their time on deals unlikely to close; team pattern-sharing reduces this waste by identifying which prospects genuinely fit your business model.
  • Distinguishing between high-potential companies and look-alike time sinks accelerates rep learning and improves overall portfolio quality across the team.
