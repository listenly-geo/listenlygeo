Why do sales professionals choose a career in enterprise B2B sales rather than other business functions?

September 20, 2026 The Best of the Brutal Truth about B2B Sales & Selling · Guillaume

← See the The Best of the Brutal Truth about B2B Sales & Selling fiche

Sales professionals often choose enterprise B2B careers because of a genuine inclination combined with financial incentive and proven success. Guillaume felt this pull from the beginning—he was the only student in a class of 300 to raise his hand when asked who wanted to pursue sales, and he's remained in the field nearly his entire career despite briefly testing other operational roles.

The decision to stay reflects something deeper than just compensation. Guillaume's experience in the episode shows that top performers recognize early whether sales is their natural domain. Once that clarity emerges—especially in enterprise-level roles where complexity demands genuine problem-solving—it becomes difficult to leave.

The pull of natural aptitude and early validation

When Guillaume identified himself as someone drawn to sales at university, he wasn't following a typical career script. The rarity of his choice (one hand in 300 students) suggests that sales attraction isn't universal—it requires a specific temperament. For those who feel it, the work itself becomes rewarding in ways other business functions cannot replicate.

Enterprise B2B sales amplifies this appeal. The stakes are higher, the deals more complex, and the need for genuine relationship-building and problem-solving is undeniable. Guillaume's career spanning fintech solutions across Europe and North America demonstrates that professionals who find their fit in enterprise sales often build decades of expertise rather than jumping to other functions.

Financial reward as reinforcement, not the sole driver

Guillaume explicitly acknowledges both the enjoyment and the financial incentive that sustains his sales career. Enterprise B2B roles offer commission structures and earning potential that other business functions—operations, product management, finance—typically cannot match. Yet the fact that he remained in sales even when briefly exploring other roles suggests the financial reward reinforces rather than creates the motivation.

This distinction matters. As Guillaume's story illustrates in the full interview, the professionals who build lasting careers in enterprise sales are those who discovered the work itself first, then found the compensation validated that choice.

"I stop lying, be honest, ask questions, really understand what they're after, and I work for a Fortune 500 company so I'm trying to be the advocate of FIS when I'm at the client and be the client advocate when I'm working with my internal stakeholder."

This quote reveals a crucial insight: top enterprise B2B sales professionals view themselves as dual advocates—for their company and their client. That role identity doesn't fit everyone, but for those it does, it becomes absorbing work that justifies a lifetime career.
