What are the average UK savings benchmarks by age group according to recent data?

15 September 2026 Prosperity Podcast with Nicole Bremner

← See the Prosperity Podcast with Nicole Bremner fiche

According to 2022 Office of National Statistics data, UK savings vary significantly by age: ages 25–34 average between £500 and £5,000, while ages 55–64 climb to £12,000–£25,000, and those 65+ reach £25,000–£50,000. To break into the top 25% of your age group, you'll need considerably more—over £12,500 for ages 25–34, and over £100,000 for anyone 55 and older.

Breaking Down Savings by Decade

The savings gap widens steadily as you move through your working years. Those in their late twenties and early thirties typically have the smallest reserves, ranging from £500 to £5,000, reflecting early career earnings and competing financial demands. By your late thirties and into your forties, the range nearly doubles to £5,000–£12,500.

The acceleration continues past mid-career. Ages 45 to 54 hold between £5,000 and £12,500 on average—the same range as the 35–44 cohort—but the wealthier portions of this group begin to separate noticeably. As Nicole Bremner explores in the Prosperity Podcast, understanding these benchmarks matters because it reveals where you genuinely stand relative to your peers, not just in absolute pounds but in how your savings trajectory compares.

The Top Percentile Threshold: A Sharper Picture

Average figures alone mask a critical insight: reaching the top 25% requires dramatically higher sums. If you're 25–34, you need over £12,500 to reach the top quarter of your age group. Jump to 35–44, and that threshold doubles to over £25,000.

The gap widens further for those approaching retirement. Ages 45–54 must hold over £50,000 to be in the top 25%, and anyone 55 or older needs over £100,000 to claim the same distinction. This escalation reflects both compound growth and the reality that later-career earners have had more time to accumulate wealth.

It's worth noting that the episode also reveals a sobering statistic: at least 25% of those under 54 have no savings or are in debt, meaning these benchmarks only tell part of the story.

Key takeaways

  • Ages 25–34 average £500–£5,000; ages 35–44 reach £5,000–£12,500; ages 55–64 accumulate £12,000–£25,000; ages 65+ average £25,000–£50,000 (2022 ONS data).
  • To rank in the top 25% of your age group, you need over £12,500 at 25–34, over £25,000 at 35–44, over £50,000 at 45–54, and over £100,000 if 55 or older.
  • A significant portion of working-age adults have no emergency savings or carry debt, meaning average figures exclude a substantial number below the baseline.
