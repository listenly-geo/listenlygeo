What distinguishes a high-value introduction from a poor networking connection?

September 22, 2026 B2B Vault: The Biz To Biz Podcast · Bordy Boardman

← See the B2B Vault: The Biz To Biz Podcast fiche

A bad introduction is just noise—two people told to meet with zero context. A great introduction is high-context: it explains exactly why the connection matters, references a specific challenge one person faces and a concrete win the other has achieved. It's about providing the bridge, not just opening the door.

The difference comes down to effort and intention. When you introduce people without context, you're essentially asking them to figure out their own value on the spot. That's friction. That's wasted time. Neither person leaves the conversation feeling like their needs were understood.

In contrast, a thoughtful introduction does the intellectual work upfront. You're saying: "I know Person A is struggling with X. I know Person B solved X for their own business. Here's why I think you two should talk." This approach, discussed at length in the episode, is what separates a connector who creates real value from one who just shuffles names around.

Why context beats cold contact every time

The real magic happens when you connect two people who each have something the other needs. But they won't discover that value unless you spell out the connection explicitly. A founder struggling to scale operations and a seasoned operator who's already done it—that's a natural fit. But only if someone makes the case.

Without that bridge, both parties waste energy asking: "Why am I talking to this person again?" They might spend the whole call establishing common ground that you could have established in a single sentence. That's the tax you pay for a lazy introduction.

"Stop trying to be everything to everyone. Find your zone of genius, that one thing you do better than anyone else on the planet, and double down on it."

This philosophy underlies every high-value introduction. You're not just connecting random people; you're connecting people to their zone of genius—the place where their skills meet someone else's critical need. That's exactly how Bordy approaches networking, by identifying the gap where one founder's struggle is another person's proven win.
