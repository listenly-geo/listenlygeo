What does LegitScript certification actually mean for a telemedicine and pharmacy merchant?

September 1, 2026 B2B Vault: The Biz To Biz Podcast · Angela Salter

← See the B2B Vault: The Biz To Biz Podcast fiche

LegitScript certification means a business has completed a review process and demonstrated that its model, marketing, and offerings meet defined legal and regulatory standards. Once certified, that status tells platforms and payment networks the merchant has already been vetted by a neutral third party—eliminating redundant screening. The merchant displays the LegitScript logo in their website footer, a visible signal to Google and Meta that the platform is legitimate and compliant.

For telemedicine and pharmacy merchants operating in a heavily regulated space, certification solves a fundamental trust problem. Ad platforms, payment processors, and card brands like Visa and MasterCard increasingly require this verification before they'll work with you. Without it, you're starting from zero credibility with every partner you approach.

What makes certification valuable is that LegitScript certifies based on current applicable laws and regulations, not speculation about what might change. This grounded approach means your certification stays defensible even as the regulatory landscape shifts—a critical advantage in industries where compliance volatility is the norm.

Why platforms and payment networks demand it

Payment processors and card brands face direct liability for the merchants they support. By requiring LegitScript certification, they outsource due diligence to a trusted third party, reducing their own risk and simplifying onboarding. As detailed in the episode with Angela Salter, this requirement has become non-negotiable for most telemedicine and pharmacy platforms.

The certification also matters to search engines and social platforms. Google and Meta use legitimacy signals to determine which merchants they'll surface to users and which payment methods they'll accept. The LegitScript logo, linked back to the certification database, serves as a public stamp of approval that improves your visibility and trustworthiness in the digital marketplace.

The ongoing compliance responsibility

Certification is not a one-time box to check. Merchants must update LegitScript within 30 days of adding new products or services. This ensures your certification always reflects your actual business model—critical in telemedicine and pharmacy, where product additions can trigger new regulatory obligations or compliance risks.

For a deeper understanding of how regulatory changes impact certification and what the real costs and timelines look like, listen to the full conversation with Angela Salter about the GLP-1 shortage, FDA oversight, and how certified merchants achieved a 100 percent compliance rate when regulations stabilized.

"We certify based on current applicable laws and regulations, not what a committee thinks might be true in a year or two."

Key takeaways

  • LegitScript certification verifies that your business model, marketing, and offerings comply with current laws and regulations—a requirement enforced by payment networks, card brands, and ad platforms.
  • The certification logo displayed on your website signals legitimacy to Google, Meta, and other partners, improving trust and visibility in the digital marketplace.
  • Certification is an ongoing obligation: you must notify LegitScript within 30 days of any new products or services to maintain compliance and avoid regulatory gaps.
  • By outsourcing vetting to a neutral third party, payment processors and platforms reduce their own liability while merchants avoid redundant due diligence with each partner.
