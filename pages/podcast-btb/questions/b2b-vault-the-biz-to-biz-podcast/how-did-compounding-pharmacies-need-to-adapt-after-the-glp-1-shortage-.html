How did compounding pharmacies need to adapt after the GLP-1 shortage ended?

September 4, 2026 B2B Vault: The Biz To Biz Podcast · Angela Salter

← See the B2B Vault: The Biz To Biz Podcast fiche

When the FDA announced the end of its GLP-1 shortage declaration, it set a specific legal deadline for compounding pharmacies to cease all GLP-1 operations. LegitScript verified that 100 percent of merchants affected reached full compliance by the mandated timeframe, successfully exiting this temporary business line across the board.

The FDA Deadline and Merchant Coordination

The FDA's closure of the shortage window created an immediate compliance requirement rather than a gradual transition. Merchants received a formal deadline and had no discretion to continue compounding GLP-1 medications beyond that date. This regulatory action reflected the temporary nature of the shortage exemption that had allowed compounding pharmacies to operate in this space in the first place.

LegitScript's role was to ensure that merchants who had been selling GLP-1 products under the compounding window understood the requirement and executed their exit strategy on schedule. As discussed in the episode, the certification body reached out directly to affected merchants to confirm their compliance plans before the deadline arrived.

How Full Compliance Was Achieved

Proactive outreach and verification were key to ensuring no merchant inadvertently remained in violation after the FDA deadline. LegitScript contacted merchants across its network to document their intention to cease GLP-1 compounding operations and tracked their adherence to the announced timeline.

The fact that LegitScript achieved 100 percent compliance among merchants in its certification portfolio underscores the importance of clear regulatory communication and merchant education in a rapidly shifting market. Unlike a voluntary business decision, this exit was legally mandated, and the high compliance rate reflected both the clarity of the FDA's directive and merchants' understanding that non-compliance would jeopardize their broader business operations and payment processing relationships.

"We certify based on current applicable laws and regulations, not what a committee thinks might be true in a year or two."

This principle proved essential when the GLP-1 shortage window closed. Merchants had been operating under a temporary FDA exemption, and when that exemption ended, the regulatory ground shifted immediately. LegitScript's focus on actual law—not anticipated changes—meant merchants knew exactly when and how they needed to adjust their operations.

Key takeaways

  • The FDA's closure of the GLP-1 shortage window established a firm deadline for compounding pharmacies to exit this business line entirely.
  • LegitScript verified merchant compliance plans in advance and achieved 100 percent adherence to the FDA's mandated timeframe.
  • Regulatory clarity and direct merchant outreach ensured that temporary business operations ceased on schedule without violations.
