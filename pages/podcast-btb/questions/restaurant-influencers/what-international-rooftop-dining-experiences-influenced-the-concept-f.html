Which international rooftop restaurants shaped Dublin's Dion concept?

25 September 2026 Restaurant Influencers · Alan Clancy

← See the Restaurant Influencers fiche

Alan Clancy drew inspiration from a curated selection of world-class rooftop venues — Sushi Samba in Dubai at 78 floors up, Delilah in Vegas, Sexy Fish and Carbone in Miami, and Komodo — to shape Dion's concept as a complete night-out destination combining fine dining, entertainment, and hospitality in Dublin's city center. These international benchmarks demonstrated what was missing from Dublin's premium market at that time.

From Dubai to Dublin: Benchmarking the World's Best

Alan Clancy spent considerable time researching how successful rooftop restaurants merged multiple experiences into a single venue. The venues he studied were not just restaurants—they were lifestyle destinations that offered dining, live entertainment, and a complete social experience. Sushi Samba's location 78 floors above Dubai provided an aspirational blueprint: height combined with crafted cuisine and design excellence. This wasn't simply about height; it was about creating an environment where guests felt transported.

As Alan explains in the podcast, the common thread across these international benchmarks was a shift in how consumers approached their night out. Delilah in Vegas, along with Sexy Fish and Carbone in Miami, all represented a one-stop entertainment model that Dublin had not yet seen at premium price points. Each venue integrated food, cocktails, design, and social energy into a seamless experience—something Clancy identified as an untapped opportunity in the Irish capital.

The research phase was deliberate and thorough. Clancy's deep dive into these Miami and Las Vegas concepts shaped not only the menu and design direction for Dion but also the operational philosophy. He recognized that customers no longer wanted to compartmentalize their evening—moving between a restaurant, then a bar, then a club. They wanted everything in one beautifully executed space.

When the opportunity to develop the historic Central Bank of Ireland building arose, Clancy had already internalized the lessons from these global benchmarks. The podcast discusses how this international research directly influenced every decision—from the layout of the dining floors to the bar positioning, from the cocktail program to the entertainment strategy. The rooftop location in Dublin's city center would finally deliver what Sushi Samba, Delilah, Sexy Fish, Carbone, and Komodo had each perfected: a destination that justified the visit in its own right.

What was the timeline from initial agreement to opening for a rooftop restaurant development in Dublin?

Alan agreed with the development team in early 2021, with negotiations and legal processes taking approximately six to eight months. Designs were completed by mid-2023 before tendering began for the final build phase.

How did the Central Bank of Ireland building become available for restaurant development?

The Central Bank of Ireland relocated from the building in 2014 to new premises on the north side of the docks. The entire development was subsequently sold and made available for hospitality transformation.

Key takeaways

  • Sushi Samba Dubai, Delilah Vegas, and Miami venues like Sexy Fish and Carbone demonstrated the one-stop night-out model that Dublin lacked at premium price points.
  • International rooftop benchmarks showed that successful venues integrated dining, entertainment, and social experience into a single, seamless destination rather than separate activities.
  • Alan Clancy's research revealed a consumer shift toward complete evening experiences that didn't require moving between multiple venues.
  • These global insights directly shaped Dion's layout, bar positioning, cocktail program, and entertainment strategy for Dublin's city center location.
