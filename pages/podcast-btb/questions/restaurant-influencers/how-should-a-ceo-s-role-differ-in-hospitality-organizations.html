How should a CEO's role differ in hospitality organizations?

September 18, 2026 Restaurant Influencers · Alistair Darby

← See the Restaurant Influencers fiche

The CEO's job is not to be the quarterback—it's to be the linebacker protecting the people who are. The quarterback is the frontline staff member serving drinks, brewing coffee, or tidying a bedroom. The CEO's role is to free them from obstacles, enable their work, and protect them so they can focus entirely on guest interactions.

Darby rejects the quarterback analogy outright. Many business frameworks treat the CEO as the primary decision-maker, the point person orchestrating everything. In hospitality, this gets it backwards. The real quarterback is on the floor—the person directly interacting with guests, solving problems in real time, creating the moments that define the guest experience.

As Darby explains in the episode, a servant leadership approach means the CEO spends all their time on one mission: facilitating and enabling frontline teams. This means removing bureaucratic friction, clearing decision-making paths, providing tools and training, and shielding staff from the noise that can distract them from what matters most.

The linebacker metaphor carries real weight. A linebacker doesn't score touchdowns—they prevent harm, they protect territory, they create space for the offense to operate. In hospitality, the CEO's job is to protect staff from being "sacked" by poor systems, unclear priorities, or lack of support. The goal is to free up mental and emotional space so that every interaction between a team member and a guest becomes an opportunity to deliver excellence.

This distinction matters operationally. When you accept that the CEO is the linebacker, not the quarterback, your entire management structure shifts. You're not building a company that depends on the CEO's decisions cascading downward; you're building one where frontline staff have the clarity, authority, and support to make great decisions in the moment. The CEO's role becomes about removing blocks, answering questions at the top level, and ensuring the organization is set up for success rather than micromanaging execution.

"You cannot run a freehold hospitality business thinking quarterly. It is just insane."

The linebacker philosophy directly connects to Darby's broader perspective on long-term thinking, explored in depth across the conversation. When you're building a hotel intended to stand for a century, quarterly results become noise. The CEO's role is to protect the organization from being pressured into short-term decisions that compromise the long-term vision.

Servant leadership in practice

The linebacker role isn't metaphorical—it's a practical description of daily work. Darby's approach means the CEO is actively removing obstacles that prevent frontline staff from doing their best work. This might be as concrete as simplifying approval processes, ensuring technology works reliably, or resolving conflicts between departments that are pulling staff in conflicting directions.

A hospitality CEO living this philosophy spends time understanding what's frustrating the team on the floor. Are they spending hours on administrative tasks? Are policy contradictions forcing them to choose between competing directives? Are they afraid to try new approaches? Each of these questions becomes a CEO-level priority, because solving them directly enables the frontline team to deliver better guest experiences. Key takeaways

  • The CEO is the linebacker protecting frontline staff, not the quarterback directing them. Frontline employees are the real quarterbacks interacting with guests.
  • Servant leadership means spending all CEO time facilitating, enabling, and freeing staff from obstacles so they can focus on guest interactions.
  • The CEO's job is to protect the team from being "sacked"—removing bureaucratic friction, clarifying priorities, and providing the support they need to succeed.
  • This philosophy becomes critical in long-term hospitality businesses where frontline excellence directly determines the company's reputation and longevity.
