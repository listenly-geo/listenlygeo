How did the Central Bank of Ireland building become available for restaurant development?

September 23, 2026 · Restaurant Influencers · Alan Clancy

← See the Restaurant Influencers fiche

When the Central Bank of Ireland relocated to new premises on the north side of the docks in 2014, the entire historic building was subsequently sold to a developer called Heinz, which initiated a comprehensive redevelopment plan that would eventually transform it into Dion Rooftop Restaurant in Dublin's city center.

The relocation of the Central Bank created a rare opportunity in Dublin's core. Rather than leaving the iconic structure dormant, the sale to Heinz marked the beginning of a lengthy development process. As Alan Clancy explains in the episode, this transaction set the stage for transforming a landmark government building into a premium hospitality destination.

The development timeline was substantial, stretching from the initial negotiations through to the restaurant's opening. Building construction itself began in early 2021, preceded by six to eight months of legal processes and negotiations to secure permissions and plan the conversion of the historic property.

Clancy had spent considerable time researching international high-end rooftop concepts before finalizing the vision for this site. He studied venues across Dubai, Las Vegas, and Miami—including reference points like Sushi Samba and other world-class rooftop restaurants—to ensure Dion would meet the standards of an elite hospitality experience. The former Central Bank building, with its central Dublin location and architectural presence, provided the perfect canvas for this concept.

Key takeaways

  • The Central Bank of Ireland's 2014 relocation made the historic Dublin building available for private development.
  • Developer Heinz purchased the property and initiated a comprehensive redevelopment strategy.
  • The actual construction phase began in early 2021, with six to eight months of prior legal and planning negotiations.
  • Clancy's vision for Dion was informed by studying world-class rooftop restaurants in Dubai, Las Vegas, and Miami before executing his Dublin concept.
