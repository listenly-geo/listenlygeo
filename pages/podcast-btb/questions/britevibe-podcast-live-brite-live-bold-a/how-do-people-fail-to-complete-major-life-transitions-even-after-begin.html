How do people fail to complete major life transitions even after beginning them?

September 23, 2026 BriteVibe Podcast: Live Brite, Live Bold, and Share BriteVibes · Carley Weissert

← See the BriteVibe Podcast fiche

People who fail to complete major life transitions are those who retreat to their former position before finishing the process—returning to the same job, the same way of thinking or being. Those who push through to the end, by contrast, are able to build genuinely fulfilling lives, whether through raising great children, building strong families, or pursuing other meaningful goals.

The key difference lies in commitment. When someone begins a life transition but then pulls back to what feels familiar and safe, they abandon the transformative work mid-journey. This retreat typically happens because the uncertainty of continuing feels overwhelming—the discomfort of staying in limbo is greater than the discomfort of returning to what they know, even if that old life felt empty.

Those who persist through the entire transition experience something fundamentally different. As Carley Weissert explains in her episode on the BriteVibe Podcast, people who see light at the end of the tunnel and push through are able to emerge with clarity about who they are and what actually matters to them.

The distinction is stark: those who retreat end up living lives of apathy and dissatisfaction, stuck between their old identity and the possibility of a new one. They remain in the space of "is this really the life I'm supposed to be living?" without ever answering the question. Their external circumstances may look unchanged, but internally, they are living with unresolved tension.

"The most impactful thing that you can do as a human is just live in alignment."

The completion of a transition requires staying present with the discomfort long enough to move through it. The episode explores how slowing down and doing inner work are not luxuries during major life shifts—they are necessities. Without this internal work, people cannot build the foundation needed to sustain the new identity they are stepping into.

What separates those who complete their transitions from those who don't is the willingness to sit with the unknown for as long as it takes. What is the typical timeline for completing a major life transformation or 'lifequake'?

Most people experience two to three major life interruptions during an average lifespan, with a typical completion timeline of four to five years for full recovery and transformation.

What role does slowing down and inner work play in navigating major life transitions?

The transition process requires slowing down, getting quiet, being with oneself, and tuning out external expectations—a form of inner work essential to genuine transformation and discovering your authentic self.

How does identity attachment prevent personal growth and evolution?

When you build an identity around checking external boxes—wife, mother, homeowner—rather than authentic self-discovery, you become trapped by a persona designed to meet others' expectations instead of your own truth.
