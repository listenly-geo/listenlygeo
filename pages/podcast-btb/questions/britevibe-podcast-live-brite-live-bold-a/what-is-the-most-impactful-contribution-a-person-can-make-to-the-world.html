What is the most impactful contribution a person can make to the world?

September 24, 2026 BriteVibe Podcast: Live Brite, Live Bold, and Share BriteVibes · Carley Weissert

← See the BriteVibe Podcast: Live Brite, Live Bold, and Share BriteVibes fiche

The most impactful contribution you can make is to live in alignment with your authentic self. When you honor your true values and passions—whether that means raising a remarkable child, pursuing a career that fulfills you, or any other aligned path—you positively influence everyone around you. You give others permission to make the shifts they need to make for themselves.

Living authentically creates a ripple effect. As Carley Weissert explores in the episode, when you step into your own power and stop performing for others' expectations, you model a way of being that inspires those around you to do the same.

This insight comes from a lived experience. Weissert underwent a profound personal transformation starting in fall 2019, which intensified during the COVID-19 pandemic. She worked through deep personal development, healing patterns of people-pleasing and perfectionism that had shaped her earlier life. That inner work—the choice to align her external life with her internal truth—became the foundation of her impact.

Alignment as a Gift to Others

The relationship between your own authenticity and your influence on others is direct and undeniable. When you operate from a place of alignment, you're no longer depleting energy trying to meet external standards. Instead, you channel that energy into what genuinely matters to you.

This authenticity is contagious. As discussed in the podcast, the people closest to you—your family, your colleagues, your community—pick up on whether you're living true to yourself or performing a role. They sense the difference, and they're given permission (or not) to pursue their own authentic paths based on what they witness in you.

The irony is that this approach isn't selfish. Choosing alignment is a gift to yourself, but it becomes a gift to everyone in your orbit. Carley's story of transformation shows that the courage to live differently—ending a marriage, shifting careers, healing from old patterns—sends a powerful message: it's possible. It's worth it. You matter enough to honor your own truth.

"The most impactful thing that you can do as a human is just live in alignment. And like when you're in alignment with your life, whatever that is for you, then you are positively impacting those around you. You're giving permission to other people to make shifts that they need to make for themselves."

To understand more about how people navigate these transformative moments and what derails them along the way, listen to the full conversation where Carley and the host dive into the research on lifequakes and the real-world obstacles people face when trying to live more authentically.

Key takeaways

  • Living in alignment with your authentic self has a direct, positive impact on those around you.
  • Authenticity gives others permission to pursue their own truth instead of following external expectations.
  • The choice to live authentically is both a gift to yourself and a powerful influence on your community.
  • Alignment doesn't require a specific life outcome—it's about honoring whatever path is genuinely yours.

How do people fail to complete major life transitions even after beginning them?

People who went through lifequakes but didn't fully recover were those who pulled back to their previous position before the transformation was complete, unable to sustain the discomfort of change.

What is the typical timeline for completing a major life transformation or 'lifequake'?

Author Bruce Fuller's book 'Lifequakes' found that most people experience two to three major life interruptions during an average lifespan, with each transition typically taking four to five years to complete.

What role does slowing down and inner work play in navigating major life transitions?

Both Carley and the host emphasized that the transition process required a lot of slowing down, getting quiet, being with oneself, and tuning out external pressures and expectations.
