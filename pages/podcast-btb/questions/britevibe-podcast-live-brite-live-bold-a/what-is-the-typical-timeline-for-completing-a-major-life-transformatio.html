How long does a major life transformation actually take?

September 22, 2026 BriteVibe Podcast: Live Brite, Live Bold, and Share BriteVibes · Carley Weissert

← See the BriteVibe Podcast: Live Brite, Live Bold, and Share BriteVibes fiche

Most people need four to five years to complete a major life transformation, according to Bruce Fuller's research on "lifequakes." During an average lifetime, people experience two to three of these significant life interruptions—and knowing this timeline is normalizing for anyone in the middle of their own transformation.

What Bruce Fuller's research reveals is that life transformations aren't quick fixes. They're extended processes that require patience and self-compassion. As Carley Weissert discusses in the podcast episode, discovering this framework offered her real relief—especially when her own transformation began before COVID-19 and extended well beyond it.

Understanding your timeline gives permission to breathe

When you're in the depths of personal upheaval, the uncertainty about "how long this will take" creates its own anxiety. The four to five-year benchmark isn't meant as a hard deadline, but rather a realistic window that helps people understand they're not broken or moving too slowly. Carley noted that she was still in recovery phases years after her transformation began, which she found entirely normal once she learned this framework.

The insight that most people go through multiple major life shifts during a lifetime also reframes these moments. You're not experiencing a singular crisis that will define you—you're undergoing one of perhaps two or three significant recalibrations that shape who you become. This perspective shift alone can reduce the shame or urgency people often feel when a transformation doesn't resolve in months.

Explore the deeper layers of how personal development work led to Carley's transformation during the pandemic, which offers concrete insight into what those four to five years might look like in practice.

"The most impactful thing that you can do as a human is just live in alignment."

The real relief in knowing this timeline comes from permission—permission to not rush, permission to still be processing, permission to recognize that transformation is ongoing work rather than a destination you simply arrive at and close the door on. Carley's full conversation in the episode explores how slowing down and inner work become the actual tools that move you through these years with intention.

Key takeaways

  • Major life transformations typically require a four to five-year timeline, not months—normalizing the extended process.
  • Most people experience two to three significant lifequakes during an average lifetime, making transformations a recurring rather than one-time event.
  • Knowing this framework reduces shame and anxiety about "how long this is taking," offering permission to move at a sustainable pace.
  • Transformation is not a destination but an ongoing process of alignment and self-discovery.
