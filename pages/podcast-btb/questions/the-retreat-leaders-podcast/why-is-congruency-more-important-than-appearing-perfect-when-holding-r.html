Why is congruency more important than appearing perfect when holding retreat space?

September 25, 2026 The Retreat Leaders Podcast · Michelle Villalobos

← See The Retreat Leaders Podcast fiche

Retreat leaders often believe they must appear to have everything figured out to earn trust, but participants actually respond to congruency, not perfection. When you pretend to be fine while struggling internally, that disharmony creates disconnect—and people sense it instantly. Showing up authentically, even when you're not okay, builds far deeper trust.

The misconception runs deep: many retreat facilitators assume that vulnerability signals weakness or incompetence. In reality, the opposite is true. As discussed in this episode of The Retreat Leaders Podcast, when there is a gap between your inner state and your outer presentation, that mismatch creates a subtle but unmistakable tension that undermines everything you teach.

What horses teach us about presence and congruency

Perhaps the most striking evidence of this principle comes from equine-assisted experiences. Horses respond exclusively to congruency—they have no capacity for social politeness or the performance dynamics humans use daily. If you show a horse you are struggling, it reads that as truth and responds to it. If you claim to be calm while internally anxious, the animal detects the dissonance and reacts accordingly.

This is precisely why retreat participants—many of whom arrive seeking transformation and healing—are so attuned to authenticity. They unconsciously mirror your congruency or lack thereof. Michelle Villalobos explains in the full episode that when you allow people to see your genuine state—including moments when you're not okay—they trust you more deeply because they recognize you as real, not as a performer on a stage.

"When you are doing work that aligns with who you really are, it naturally elevates your emotional state and energy."

The deeper insight here is that congruency itself becomes an energy. When you stop the exhausting performance of perfection and instead show up as you actually are in that moment—grounded, honest, maybe imperfect—your retreat participants feel that shift. They relax. They trust. They become more open to the transformation you are facilitating. The paradox is that appearing perfect actually distances people, while being congruent brings them closer.

For retreat leaders building long-term impact, congruency is not optional—it is the foundation. Every interaction, every teaching moment, every silence in the room reflects either alignment or pretense. Participants know the difference. And as Michelle Villalobos explores further in the full conversation, your business cannot outperform your own energy and congruency—which makes this not just an interpersonal principle, but a business imperative.

Key takeaways

  • Participants trust congruency over perfection, because disharmony between inner state and outer presentation creates palpable disconnect.
  • Horses demonstrate this principle directly—they respond to your actual state, not your performance, which is why equine-assisted work reveals truth instantly.
  • Showing authentic struggle builds deeper trust than pretending to have everything figured out.
  • Your retreat business cannot outperform your own energy and alignment—congruency is both a leadership principle and a business ceiling.
