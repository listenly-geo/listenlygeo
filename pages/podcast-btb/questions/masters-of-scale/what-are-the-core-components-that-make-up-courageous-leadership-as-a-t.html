What are the core components that make up courageous leadership as a teachable skill?

September 17, 2026 Masters of Scale · Brené Brown

← See the Masters of Scale fiche

Courage is teachable, measurable, and observable—a skill set built on four distinct components that any leader can develop. The framework begins with clarity about your values and the discipline to align your behavior with them, then operationalizing those values across your organization. It requires the ability to stay in integrity and vulnerability, which means understanding that vulnerability is simply uncertainty, risk, and exposure—a constant presence in leadership. The hidden barrier is not fear itself, but armor—the self-protective mechanisms we unconsciously deploy when afraid.

Values clarity and accountability as the foundation

The first pillar of courageous leadership is being crystal clear about your values and assessing whether you're behaving in alignment with them. Many leaders can articulate values, but the real work lies in operationalizing them—turning them into concrete practices and holding both yourself and your organization accountable to them. As Brené Brown explains in this episode, this alignment creates the foundation for everything that follows in courageous leadership.

Vulnerability and armor: The overlooked barrier

Brown's most counterintuitive finding was that the biggest barrier to courageous leadership isn't fear—it's armor. The leaders she studied across her research openly admitted to being afraid every day. But when asked what actually stopped them from acting courageously, they didn't blame fear; they described how they self-protect when afraid, usually without conscious awareness of it.

Vulnerability, defined simply as uncertainty, risk, and exposure, is inescapable in leadership. The skill isn't eliminating vulnerability—it's recognizing when you're in it and choosing not to reach for the protective mechanisms that undermine authentic, courageous action. This insight is explored in depth in Brown's conversation with Reid Hoffman, where she shares how this reframing has transformed how leaders think about their own resilience.

"Courage is teachable, measurable, and observable. It's a skill set that's made up of four things."

The distinction between identifying your values and living them operationally is something Brown emphasizes throughout the podcast discussion—and she explores how organizations can build accountability systems that reinforce courageous behavior at scale.

Key takeaways

  • Courage is a teachable, measurable skill made up of four distinct, learnable components—not an innate trait.
  • Clarity about values must be paired with the discipline and organizational accountability to operationalize them in daily decisions.
  • Vulnerability (uncertainty, risk, and exposure) is constant in leadership; the skill is recognizing it and resisting automatic self-protective armor.
  • Fear is not the barrier to courageous leadership—self-protection mechanisms deployed unconsciously are what actually block authentic action.
