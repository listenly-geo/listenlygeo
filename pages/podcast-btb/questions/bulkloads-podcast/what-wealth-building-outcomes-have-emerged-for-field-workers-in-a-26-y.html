From Field Workers to Millionaires: How a 26-Year ESOP Built Generational Wealth?

September 25, 2026 Bulkloads Podcast · Robert

← See the Bulkloads Podcast fiche

After 26 years operating as an ESOP, ESS Companies has created 98 millionaires within its workforce, with 59% of them working in field positions. This year alone, 168 employees exceeded $500,000 in their accounts, and 15 new millionaires were created—a direct result of employee ownership compounding over more than two decades.

The wealth-building outcomes reveal a fundamental shift in how field workers accumulate assets. Unlike traditional contractor models where profits flow to ownership, ESS Companies structured its ESOP so that every employee owner participates directly in the company's gains. Employees earn approximately 25 percent of their salary annually as an ESOP contribution, which accumulates in personal accounts alongside the company's equity growth.

The company marks this milestone each March during an annual Shareholder Week event. At these gatherings, employees receive detailed account statements revealing how their long-term participation—sometimes spanning two decades or more—has generated multi-year returns that far exceed their original salary base. For field workers accustomed to hourly wages, watching a $500,000 or seven-figure balance appear in their account represents a generational shift in wealth security.

"Every dollar that's made makes its way to the employee owners, because everybody in between has a stake in the ownership of this company."

The concentration of millionaires among field workers—59 percent of the 98 total—contradicts conventional assumptions that blue-collar trades cannot generate substantial long-term wealth. This outcome reflects both the company's revenue growth—approximately 340% over the last five years—and the ESOP structure itself, which ensures field crews benefit proportionally from business success rather than remaining wage-earners disconnected from ownership gains.

Beyond individual wealth, the episode explores how this ownership model has also reduced turnover to 10-20% better than the industry average—a secondary benefit that stabilizes field teams and deepens institutional knowledge across projects.

The 1999 Decision That Changed Everything

Founder Billy Sapp, who established the company in 1972, made the critical choice in 1999 to transition from family ownership to employee ownership. Rather than pursue a traditional sale or keep the company within the family, Sapp and his partners chose to sell the company to its employees through an ESOP structure. This decision meant that future profit growth would flow directly to the workers building the projects, not retained by external investors or concentrated among a small family.

What makes this transition remarkable is the consistency of execution over 26 years. The company did not abandon the ESOP model during downturns or pivot to a different ownership structure when external capital became available. Instead, leadership reinforced the ESOP commitment, and employees benefited from both steady salary increases and compounding equity gains as the business scaled.
