What drove a military-trained consultant to launch a freight brokerage?

18 September 2026 Bulkloads Podcast

← See the Bulkloads Podcast fiche

A mentor in the trucking industry and a recognition of untapped market potential sparked the transition. After 12 years at Deloitte Consulting supporting federal agencies, the opportunity to enter freight brokerage emerged when industry contractions made traditional consulting roles scarce, while the logistics sector remained resilient and growth-ready.

The pivot was not random. A mutual friend who owned a trucking company became a critical mentor, guiding the early business case development and providing real-world insight into an industry unfamiliar to someone coming from management consulting.

The timing proved strategic. Following six years of active service in the U.S. Army—commissioned through Appalachian State University's ROTC program and including a 12+ month deployment to Iraq with the 3rd Infantry Division—the transition to consulting had offered stability. But by the time that chapter closed, the broader business environment had shifted.

As explored in detail in the episode, corporate sector headwinds opened space for an entirely different play: recognizing that trucking and freight brokerage remained somewhat insulated from the wider contractions hitting other industries.

"I think everything happens for a reason in many instances, and sometimes you can't really recognize that in the moment."

What makes this story instructive is the absence of prior trucking experience—yet the founder had spent years helping federal agencies optimize their operations. That skillset translated directly. The mentor relationship eliminated much of the industry knowledge gap, while his consulting background equipped him to build business infrastructure from day one.

The move also reflects a deliberate reading of market resilience. The conversation dives deeper into how he structured the early growth and navigated the transition from white-collar consulting to logistics operations.

Key takeaways

  • A mentor in trucking provided the missing industry knowledge during business case development.
  • Market timing mattered: consulting roles contracted while freight brokerage remained insulated and growing.
  • His 12 years at Deloitte—working with the Defense Health Agency, Veterans Health Administration, and FDA—built operational and problem-solving muscle transferable to logistics entrepreneurship.
  • Military discipline and consulting structure combined to launch an industry outsider into freight brokerage with credible business fundamentals.
