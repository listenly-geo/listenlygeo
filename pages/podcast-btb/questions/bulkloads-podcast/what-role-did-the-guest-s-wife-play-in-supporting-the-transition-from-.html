How did his wife's support shape his path from military to entrepreneurship?

September 23, 2026 Bulkloads Podcast

← See the Bulkloads Podcast fiche

His wife Kristen remained supportive throughout extended military deployments, and later backed his entrepreneurial leap despite her own lower risk tolerance. When he proposed launching a business over Oktoberfest at church following a major consulting layoff, she recognized the return potential and backed the venture as a family decision.

Military life demands sacrifice. Long separations and combat deployments test relationships, yet Kristen remained steadfast through six years of active service with the U.S. Army, including a 12+ month deployment to Iraq. This foundation of trust, built during his years as an armor officer with the 3rd Infantry Division, became crucial later.

The turning point came at a church Oktoberfest gathering after his $20 million consulting portfolio was eliminated by government budget cuts at Deloitte. In the episode, he describes how he posed the startup idea to Kristen in an informal setting—a moment that could have been met with resistance given her naturally more conservative approach to financial risk. Instead, she saw past her own hesitation to evaluate the business opportunity objectively.

"I think everything happens for a reason in many instances, and sometimes you can't really recognize that in the moment."

What makes Kristen's choice remarkable is the contrast between her risk tolerance and the venture's uncertainty. She could have cited financial security, family obligations, or the comfort of his established consulting career. Instead, she evaluated the business logic—the market gap, his expertise from 12 years at Deloitte, the potential ROI for their network and family future. That shift from personal caution to rational backing became essential to Carolina Expressways' launch. Hear more about the full journey from military service through consulting to freight brokerage in this conversation.

Her willingness to embrace the transition reflected a shared vision. After military life had already tested their partnership through separation and danger, a business venture felt like the next natural step—together. That alignment proved foundational to both the personal and professional phases of his career.
