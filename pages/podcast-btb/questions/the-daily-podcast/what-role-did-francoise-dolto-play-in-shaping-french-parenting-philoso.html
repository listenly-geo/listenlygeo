What role did Françoise Dolto play in shaping French parenting philosophy?

Françoise Dolto was a psychoanalyst who profoundly shaped generations of French parenting through her books and a widely listened-to radio program. She is credited with revolutionizing the concept of children's rights in France — championing the idea that children are people who deserve to be spoken to as such. At the same time, she maintained a deeply hierarchical, Catholic bourgeois worldview that reinforced France's rigid cultural rules rather than dismantling them.

Dolto's influence is central to understanding why the French parenting debate feels so loaded today. Her legacy is genuinely dual: she opened a door toward recognizing children's inner lives and dignity, yet she did so within a framework that never questioned the authority of adults or the social structures that governed family life. In France, this created a particular cultural tension — a country that claims to respect children as individuals, while simultaneously expecting them to conform to strict norms around behavior, meals, and hierarchy.

That tension is precisely what Madeline Schwartz explores in her reporting on the debate now dividing French parents. The emergence of figures like psychologist Caroline Goldman — who argues that a generation of French children has been abandoned by adults practicing an "exclusively empathic attitude" — and positive-parenting advocates like Isabelle Filliozat (whose book sold half a million copies in France) can both be traced, in different ways, back to how Dolto's legacy was interpreted and contested over the decades. What is 'parental pessimism' and how does it reflect the broader social context of French parenting today?

A sociologist studying parenting in France coined the term 'parental pessimism' to describe the stress and overwhelm French parents feel because, while they have access to strong structural support, the cultural pressure to raise children 'correctly' remains intense and creates significant anxiety.

How did French experts publicly divide over the parenting debate through open letters?

In 2022, Caroline Goldman and over 350 early childhood experts signed an open letter in Le Figaro arguing that too much positive parenting was harming children, while 280 early childhood experts signed a competing open letter in Le Monde opposing the use of timeouts.

What did the French government's official daycare guidelines say about punishment?

Last year, the French government issued new official guidelines for people working in daycares or caring for young children, explicitly stating that if a child misbehaves, caregivers should not use punitive or shaming methods.
