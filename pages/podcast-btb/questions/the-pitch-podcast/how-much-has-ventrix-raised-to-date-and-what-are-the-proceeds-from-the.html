How much has Ventrix raised to date and what are the proceeds from the current 2 million dollar round?

September 5, 2026 The Pitch · Sonny

← See The Pitch podcast fiche

Ventrix previously raised £400,000 to £500,000 in an angel round at a £5.75 million valuation, with backing from Alexis Ohanian and family offices. The new $2 million Series A round is allocated specifically to complete the industrial demo unit deployment, build out the AI control layer, scale the sales operation, and prepare manufacturing infrastructure for mass production by late 2027.

The angel round represented early validation of the core technology—a modular gas plant capturing CO2 at source for breweries and industrial carbonation operations. That capital enabled the team to build and test their first unit in three and a half months, a pace that conventional industrial gas plants cannot match. Large-scale gas plants typically take two to seven years to construct and cost hundreds of millions to over a billion dollars. Ventrix's compact, stackable approach fundamentally changes that timeline.

The specificity of the current fundraise is revealing. As Sonny outlined in The Pitch, the $2 million is not a general operations fund. Each dollar has a defined purpose tied to concrete milestones. Completing the industrial demo means building and stacking five units together to prove the modular architecture works at semi-commercial scale. The AI control layer is critical—software that optimizes gas capture and recovery in real time, turning individual units into an intelligent network.

Sales scaling is the third component. Ventrix has already signed its first commercial offtake contract with a brewery in Wales paying £10,000 per year. That pilot validates product-market fit, but moving beyond one customer requires a dedicated sales team and customer acquisition infrastructure. The $2 million makes that expansion possible without waiting years for revenues to cover payroll.

The final allocation—preparing mass manufacturing—is perhaps the highest-stakes use of capital. The company is targeting end of 2027 for the transition from handmade prototypes to factory-scale production. At that threshold, Ventrix moves from venture-scale startup to industrial manufacturer. The episode details how the first unit cost approximately £40,000 to build, but scaled manufacturing is projected to bring that down to £20,000 per unit. That 50% cost reduction directly improves customer payback periods and unit economics. Preparing tooling, supply chains, and manufacturing partners now—rather than scrambling when demand arrives—is why manufacturing prep is embedded in this raise.

"We can cut the deployment time from years to days, and reduce CO2 costs by up to 50% for our customers."

To understand the competitive stakes, the full episode context on Listenly includes the UK's CO2 shortage of 2021, when the country lost approximately two billion pounds in economic value due to gas supply disruption alone. The broader carbon capture sector has seen 80 billion dollars invested globally with an 80% failure rate—a cautionary tale of how capital alone does not guarantee success. Ventrix's focused, modular approach attempts to side-step those failures by avoiding the mega-scale infrastructure trap.

From demo to manufacturing readiness

The progression from £400K angel round to $2M Series A mirrors a clear maturation path. The angel money proved the physics and built the first prototype. The Series A money moves Ventrix from proof-of-concept into commercial manufacturing readiness. Stacking five units together in an industrial demo is not a marketing exercise—it's the validation step that tells manufacturers, customers, and future growth investors that modularity works at scale. The AI control layer transforms loose hardware into a coordinated system. Sales and manufacturing prep turn a lab achievement into a repeatable business.

The end-of-2027 mass manufacturing target is also a competitive forcing function. CO2 demand in UK breweries alone runs to hundreds of tons per year, but is supplied almost entirely by centralized plants with 87% of costs tied to transportation. Any player that can eliminate that transportation cost equation—and Ventrix's distributed model does exactly that—captures significant margin and customer stickiness. The $2 million raise is sized to ensure Ventrix reaches that inflection point before competitors recognize the same opportunity.

What intellectual property protections does Ventrix have?

Ventrix's IP focuses on process engineering and how the unit is designed, along with how units communicate with each other. The mechanical design enabling modularity represents a key proprietary advantage.

What is the payback period for Ventrix's customers and how does this compare to competitors?

For the Welsh brewery pilot at 10K per year on a 40K build cost, the payback is four years. Once manufacturing scales to 20K per unit plus a 20K installation fee, the payback period improves significantly.

Who is Ventrix's first commercial customer and what are the contract terms?

Ventrix signed its first commercial offtake contract with a brewery in Wales paying 10K pounds per year. The brewery is their pilot customer demonstrating real-world deployment and recurring revenue potential.

Key takeaways

  • Previous angel round raised £400–500K at a £5.75M valuation with backing from Alexis Ohanian and family offices.
  • The $2M Series A funds five specific priorities: industrial demo stacking, AI control software, sales team scaling, and mass manufacturing preparation.
  • First prototype built in three and a half months; conventional industrial gas plants take two to seven years and cost hundreds of millions to billions.
  • Manufacturing costs are projected to drop from £40K per unit to £20K at scale, directly improving customer payback periods and competitive positioning.
  • Target mass manufacturing launch is end of 2027, positioning Ventrix to capture the CO2 shortage premium before competitive alternatives emerge.
