What structural barriers prevent people from accessing affordable mental health care?

September 16, 2026 Habits and Hustle · Keir Gaines

← See the Habits and Hustle fiche

Therapy has become prohibitively expensive, with qualified therapists in major cities charging $400 to $500 per session while remaining largely outside insurance coverage. Beyond cost, state-specific licensing laws prevent therapists from crossing state lines—even for virtual sessions—creating geographic gaps that leave millions unable to access care they desperately need.

The Price Wall That Blocks Access

Affordability is the first structural barrier. In cities like Los Angeles, the cost of a single therapy session rivals a month's rent for many people. When therapists are not contracted with insurance networks, the entire cost falls on the patient, making ongoing treatment a luxury only the financially secure can sustain.

As Keir Gaines explains in the Habits and Hustle episode, insurance itself has become a deterrent: navigating insurance frameworks is administratively so burdensome that many therapists simply opt out of the system entirely, further restricting the supply of affordable options.

State Licensing: A Hidden Cage

The second barrier is less visible but equally damaging. Therapists are licensed to specific states, meaning virtual sessions cannot legally cross state lines no matter how geographically close two people are. This creates an absurd situation where someone in Virginia cannot receive care from a therapist in Washington D.C.—despite modern technology making the connection trivial.

This regulatory structure, designed to protect consumers, paradoxically amplifies scarcity. Rural and underserved areas see even fewer therapists available, and the inability to work across borders eliminates what should be a natural solution to regional supply shortages.

"Fantasy can be whatever you imagine it but reality can only be what it is—actions are where the rubber meets the road."

The real impact of these barriers is simple: millions of people who actually need therapy are not receiving it, not because help doesn't exist, but because structural systems make it inaccessible.

Addressing affordability and licensing reform would require action at both the insurance and regulatory level—changes that demand more than individual effort. For now, those barriers remain the defining obstacles to mental health care access in the United States, discussed at length in this conversation on Habits and Hustle.

Key takeaways

  • Therapy in major metropolitan areas costs $400–$500 per session, far exceeding what most people can afford without insurance.
  • Insurance reimbursement is so administratively complex that many therapists avoid it entirely, eliminating this as a cost-reduction avenue.
  • State licensing laws prohibit therapists from providing virtual sessions across state lines, artificially restricting geographic access.
  • The combination of cost and regulatory barriers leaves millions in need of mental health care unable to receive it.
