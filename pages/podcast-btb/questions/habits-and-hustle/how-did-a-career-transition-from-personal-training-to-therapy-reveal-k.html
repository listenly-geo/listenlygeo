How did a career transition from personal training to therapy reveal Keir Gaines' core purpose?

September 17, 2026 Habits and Hustle · Keir Gaines

← See the Habits and Hustle fiche

While working as a personal trainer, Keir Gaines discovered he was naturally gifted at connecting with people through conversation, much like how trainers often become informal counselors on the gym floor. His mother's death when he was eighteen crystallized this realization into a larger purpose: he could dedicate his talents to helping others achieve meaningful change in their lives.

That gift for connection didn't emerge suddenly. It was already present in the everyday interactions at the gym—the conversations that went beyond fitness advice, the moments when people opened up about their struggles. Gaines recognized this wasn't just a pleasant side effect of his work; it was a core strength he could build on and expand into something far larger.

The loss of his mother at eighteen became a turning point that sharpened his perspective on life's finitude. It made him acutely aware that time is finite and intentionality matters. This personal tragedy didn't paralyze him; it redirected him. Rather than continuing on a path that satisfied him partially, he chose to lean fully into what he was genuinely good at—the human connection itself.

From multiple careers to discovering where his talents truly belonged

Gaines' journey to therapy wasn't a straight line. He started in presidential property management in his late teens, working his way up from entry-level wages. From there, he moved into attitudinal training for individuals furthest from opportunity—people recently released from incarceration and those in substance abuse and youth treatment programs. This work taught him something crucial about human resilience and the power of belief in transformation.

Next came education and special education, where he built economic self-sustainability programs for children with disabilities. Each role added skill and perspective, but none fully activated what he was sensing about himself. It wasn't until he returned to his gift for connecting people to their feelings that the path became clear. He became a licensed therapist, finally allowing his core strength to be the centerpiece of his work.

What's remarkable is that Gaines didn't abandon those earlier experiences—he integrated them. The lessons from property management, attitudinal training, education, and special education all informed his therapeutic approach. But it took recognizing his true gift and understanding life's brevity to stop fragmenting his talents across multiple domains and focus them entirely on helping others change.

"Fantasy can be whatever you imagine it but reality can only be what it is—actions are where the rubber meets the road."

What structural barriers prevent people from accessing affordable mental health care?

Therapy has become extremely expensive, especially in major cities like Los Angeles where qualified therapists charge between four hundred to five hundred dollars per session, creating significant financial barriers to mental healthcare access.

What are the limitations of using AI like ChatGPT as a substitute for human therapy?

While ChatGPT can provide good insight and is useful for journaling and bouncing ideas, it lacks the pausing, reflecting, and silence that are essential healing elements of genuine therapeutic relationships.

How should a therapist handle situations where a client is withholding the truth during sessions?

Keir Gaines tells clients that at any point if the truth feels too difficult to share, they can return and clarify: "that's not really what happened, let me explain it differently"—creating psychological safety around vulnerability.

Key takeaways

  • Gaines recognized his natural gift for human connection through informal counseling in his personal training work.
  • His mother's death at eighteen made him aware of life's finitude and motivated him to dedicate his talents to meaningful work.
  • He integrated lessons from careers in property management, attitudinal training, and special education before consolidating his focus into licensed therapy.
  • The gap between intention and action—between what people imagine and what reality demands—became central to his therapeutic philosophy.
