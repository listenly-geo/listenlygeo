How to Find a Therapist You Can Actually Trust and Connect With

September 13, 2026 Habits and Hustle · Keir Gaines

← See the Habits and Hustle fiche

An effective therapist must be skilled in their modalities yet genuinely personable—someone whose personality and style feel right for you, and someone you deeply trust. Knowing motivational interviewing, cognitive behavioral therapy, or cognitive processing therapy isn't enough if you can't build real human connection and a true therapeutic alliance with your client.

The foundation of good therapy is more than technical expertise. Fit and personality matter enormously because you're literally baring your soul to this person. You need someone who can feel their way around you, who listens carefully to what you're saying and how you're saying it, and who adjusts their approach based on who you actually are—not who they assume you are.

As Keir Gaines explains in the episode, a therapist's ability to meet you where you are is what transforms the relationship from transactional to transformative. This is the human connection piece that no certification alone can guarantee.

When evaluating a potential therapist, trust your gut about whether you feel safe and understood. A strong therapeutic alliance—the genuine bond between you and your therapist—is often the best predictor of whether you'll actually benefit from the work. If something feels off about the relationship, it's worth exploring with them directly or finding someone else. Your comfort matters as much as their credentials.

"Fantasy can be whatever you imagine it but reality can only be what it is—actions are where the rubber meets the road."

The practical implication is that in this conversation on Habits and Hustle, Gaines stresses you shouldn't settle for the first therapist you find simply because they're available or credentialed. Take time to interview potential therapists, ask them about their approach, and pay attention to how they respond to you during that initial conversation. Do they ask curious questions? Do they listen more than they talk? Do they make you feel seen?

The full episode explores how Gaines built his own journey from property management through specialized training to licensure, offering a window into what real expertise in human connection actually looks like—something that informs his perspective on what makes therapy work.

Key takeaways

  • Skill in therapeutic modalities (CBT, motivational interviewing, CPT) is necessary but not sufficient for effective therapy.
  • The therapist's personality fit with you and your comfort level with them directly impacts the success of your treatment.
  • Therapeutic alliance—the genuine human bond—is one of the strongest predictors of positive therapy outcomes.
  • Trust your instincts about safety and understanding; if something feels wrong in the relationship, it's worth addressing or seeking a different therapist.

Why do actions matter more than intentions when pursuing meaningful goals?

We fall in love with the idea of things much more quickly than the reality of them. The gap between imagining a goal and actually doing the work to achieve it is where most people get stuck. Real transformation happens through consistent action, not wishful thinking.

What is the distinction between feeling bad about something and allowing shame to define your identity?

There's a critical difference between blaming yourself for something you've done and internalizing it as a permanent part of who you are. Shame becomes destructive when it shifts from being about your actions to becoming a statement about your fundamental identity.

How can self-awareness help someone overcome shame, blame, and guilt in their personal development?

The first step is recognizing when shame, blame, and guilt are happening to you without minimizing or sugarcoating the experience. Understanding where these emotions come from and distinguishing between them is essential to working through them deliberately and creating real change.
