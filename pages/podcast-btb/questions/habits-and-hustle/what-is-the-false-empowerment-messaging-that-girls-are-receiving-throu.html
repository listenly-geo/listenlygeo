What is the false empowerment messaging that girls are receiving through social media and how does it perpetuate traditional power imbalances?

September 25, 2026 Habits and Hustle · Shefali Tsabary

← See the Habits and Hustle fiche

Girls are being told they can monetize themselves and achieve independence through platforms like OnlyFans and influencer culture, but this represents a rebranding of toxic patriarchy rather than true empowerment. In reality, they are still selling their bodies for approval and validation, unconsciously playing to the male gaze and seeking male approval—the exact same dynamic that has existed for generations, merely repackaged with a veneer of female autonomy.

The core deception lies in the language of choice. Young women believe that hypersexualization equals empowerment when Dr. Shefali explains in the episode, they are still fundamentally operating within systems designed by and for the male gaze. The platforms themselves—Meta, Facebook, YouTube—are algorithmically engineered to reward visual sexualization and engagement based on appearance and body commodification.

This false messaging is particularly dangerous because it obscures the underlying power imbalance. Girls internalize the narrative that they are "choosing" and "empowering themselves" when they present themselves in sexualized ways, but the price they seek—approval, validation, financial reward—remains controlled by external (predominantly male) gatekeepers and algorithms. The agency appears to be theirs, but the incentive structure and the ultimate approval mechanism have not shifted at all.

What makes this especially insidious is that as discussed in the podcast, girls as young as eight or nine are already beginning to diet and internalize appearance-based self-worth. By the time they encounter social media platforms promising monetization and independence, they have already absorbed decades of messaging that their value is tied to how they look and how appealing they are to others. The digital platforms simply provide new tools for selling an already-commodified version of themselves.

"The conscious parent raises themselves so they don't pass on their crap to their children."

The tragedy is that genuine empowerment—actual sovereignty and independence—requires girls to develop a sense of self that exists independent of external validation and the male gaze. The full conversation explores how mothers and fathers play different roles in helping daughters build this autonomy, but social media algorithms actively work against that development by constantly rewarding and amplifying the exact behaviors that keep girls trapped in the approval-seeking cycle.

The platforms profit from perpetuating this false empowerment because genuine confidence and independence would actually reduce engagement and monetization potential. Dr. Shefali discusses how to build actual resilience and protection, which involves parents and girls understanding this mechanism clearly enough to opt out of the false narrative entirely.

How are boys wired differently from girls in terms of emotional expression and physical development?

Boys are wired through their body and need to move, express themselves physically, seek challenge, friction, competition, and problem-solving. Understanding this neurological difference is essential for recognizing why girls' and boys' vulnerabilities to social media manipulation differ fundamentally.

What is the different role of mothers versus fathers in raising confident daughters?

The father represents the outside world to the daughter and teaches her how to be powerful and sovereign by allowing her to have boundaries and rebel. This paternal role is crucial in counteracting the false empowerment messaging girls receive from social media.

What are the primary actionable steps parents can take today to protect daughters from social media harm?

First, de-stimulate and limit exposure—no phones until age 16 or 17 because girls must find themselves before they lose themselves in digital fragmentation. This foundational boundary prevents the internalization of false empowerment narratives before identity formation is complete.
