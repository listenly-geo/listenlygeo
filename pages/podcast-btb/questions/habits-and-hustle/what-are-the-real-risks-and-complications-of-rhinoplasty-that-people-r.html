What are the real risks and complications of rhinoplasty that people rarely talk about?

August 20, 2026 Habits and Hustle · Shani Suissa

Rhinoplasty can leave you with a massive septum perforation — a large hole in your septum — requiring a second corrective surgery. A smaller nose also means smaller nasal canals, making breathing measurably harder post-operation. And the swelling? It doesn't disappear in weeks: Shani Suissa was still visibly swollen two full years after her procedure, confirmed by her dermatologist during a routine appointment.

A rhinoplasty, a septoplasty, a turbinate reduction — and then a second surgery to fix the first

Suissa underwent three procedures simultaneously: a rhinoplasty (reshaping the external nose), a septoplasty (correcting the septum), and a turbinate reduction (reducing nasal tissue to improve airflow). The combined intervention left her with a septum perforation — a hole through the wall dividing the nasal cavity — serious enough to require a corrective operation.

This is the complication that rarely appears in before-and-after content online. It isn't a minor side effect. A large septum perforation can cause whistling sounds during breathing, crusting, nosebleeds, and persistent discomfort. It demands a dedicated surgical repair.

Breathing harder, swelling longer — the long tail nobody films for Instagram

There is a structural reality to nose jobs that surgeons don't always emphasize: a smaller nose has smaller nasal passages. Suissa found her breathing more labored after surgery, not easier. This runs directly counter to the common assumption that cosmetic nasal surgery is a neutral or beneficial change for breathing function.

The recovery timeline is equally misrepresented. Two years after her operation, a dermatologist spotted residual swelling during a laser facial appointment — not because Suissa mentioned it, but because the practitioner identified it unprompted. That detail matters: it means the signs were still clinically visible, not just subjectively felt. You can hear the full conversation on Listenly, where Suissa describes exactly how this unfolded.

Septum perforation: A hole or opening in the nasal septum — the wall of cartilage and bone dividing the two nostrils. It can result from surgical trauma during a septoplasty or rhinoplasty. Depending on size, it may require a separate corrective procedure and can cause chronic breathing difficulties.

Key takeaways

  • A rhinoplasty combined with a septoplasty and turbinate reduction can result in a septum perforation serious enough to require a second corrective surgery.
  • A smaller nose means smaller nasal canals — breathing can become more labored after surgery, not easier.
  • Swelling after rhinoplasty can remain clinically visible for at least two years, even when the patient feels recovered.
  • These complications are almost never shown in the polished before-and-after content that drives people toward cosmetic procedures.
