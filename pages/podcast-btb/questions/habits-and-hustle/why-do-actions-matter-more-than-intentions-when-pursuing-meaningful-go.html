Why do actions matter more than intentions when pursuing meaningful goals?

September 12, 2026 Habits and Hustle · Keir Gaines

← See the Habits and Hustle fiche

We fall in love with the idea of things far faster than with their reality. You can imagine building a wonderful company or envision feeling important, but actions are where the rubber meets the road—without them, you're just holding a polished idea in your mind, not real evidence or genuine experimentation.

The gap between intention and action is where most people get stuck. You can spend weeks planning the perfect startup, picturing yourself as a successful founder, and feeling the emotional reward of that vision. But as Keir Gaines explains in Habits and Hustle, that fantasy doesn't account for the unglamorous moments—the 2 a.m. hotel room work sessions, the failures you have to push through when you're tired and unmotivated, the relentless perseverance required when excitement wears off.

Intention feels safe. It's the moment before risk, before you have to prove anything. But intention also gives you nothing—no data, no learning, no real transformation. Only action generates evidence of what actually works and what doesn't. You discover obstacles, limitations, and your own resilience only when you start doing the work, not when you're imagining it.

"Fantasy can be whatever you imagine it but reality can only be what it is—actions are where the rubber meets the road."

This distinction becomes critical when you're building anything meaningful—a business, a healthier life, deeper relationships. The actions you take today aren't glamorous. They're often small, repetitive, and disconnect from the grand vision you hold. But that's where the real growth happens, as Gaines emphasizes throughout the episode. Each small action teaches you something your imagination never could.

The practical implication is simple: if your actions don't match your intentions, your intentions don't matter. What matters is what you actually do—how you show up, what you build, how you handle the messy middle when motivation fades. Gaines digs deeper into how self-awareness helps you align these two, and why that alignment is the foundation of real, lasting change.
