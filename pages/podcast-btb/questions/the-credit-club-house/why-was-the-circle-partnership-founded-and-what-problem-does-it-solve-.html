Why did The Circle Partnership exist — and what gap in construction was it built to fill?

The Circle Partnership was founded to address a very specific and largely overlooked problem: mid-level women were leaving the construction and property sectors at a rapid rate. Graduate schemes were often gender-balanced on entry, but women disappeared at a certain seniority level — creating a broken pipeline long before anyone reached the boardroom. Co-founders Vanessa Murray and Ceri Moyers built The Circle Partnership to fix precisely that gap, bringing their own lived experience of the built environment to a problem they had both witnessed first-hand.

The core insight behind the organisation is that the sector had been looking at the wrong end of the problem. Most energy and investment went toward improving graduate intake figures or hitting board-level diversity targets — two ends of the career spectrum that received most of the attention. The mid-level, where women were actually leaving, was being ignored. Murray and Moyers argued that if an organisation had already invested in developing talented women and retained them to a mid-career point, the logical next step was to focus on keeping them — not watching them leave and starting over. The Circle Partnership has been operating on this premise for approximately six to seven years. About Vanessa Murray

VM
Vanessa Murray
Co-founder · The Circle Partnership

Vanessa Murray co-founded The Circle Partnership alongside Ceri Moyers, bringing direct lived experience from the property side of the built environment sector. Rather than observing the mid-level retention crisis from the outside, Murray experienced the dynamics of this world personally — giving her a grounded and credible perspective on why talented women leave before they reach senior roles.

Together, Murray and Moyers built a business with two distinct arms: the Circle Academy, a structured programme that supports mid-level women in advancing their careers and building confidence; and a consultancy practice that works directly with organisations on culture change — helping businesses create environments where diverse talent can genuinely flourish, not simply arrive and leave again. The Circle Partnership has now been operating for approximately six to seven years, and the Circle Academy's reach is notably broad: applicants range in age from 23 to 55, all self-identifying as mid-level, which reflects just how varied individual career trajectories can be at this stage — particularly for women. Murray's authority on this subject comes not from theory but from having built a sustainable organisation around a problem the industry consistently failed to name, let alone solve.
