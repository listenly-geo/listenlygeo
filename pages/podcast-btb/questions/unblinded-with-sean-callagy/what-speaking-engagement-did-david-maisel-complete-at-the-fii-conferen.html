What speaking engagement did David Maisel complete at the FII conference, and what was the topic he addressed?

August 24, 2026 Unblinded with Sean Callagy · David Maisel

← See the Unblinded with Sean Callagy podcast page

David Maisel was invited to speak at the FII (Future Investment Initiative) Miami event, organized by the Saudi government. He was one of five speakers immediately before President Trump closed the conference, and he addressed the question of whether a new worldwide mythology could emerge in today's age — a talk he delivered over approximately 20 minutes. Following that appearance, he was invited to speak at FII Europe in Rome in June.

From Marvel mythology to world mythology — Maisel's pivot on the global stage

The FII (Future Investment Initiative) is a high-profile summit organized by Saudi Arabia that convenes global business, political, and cultural leaders. Being positioned as one of five speakers immediately preceding a presidential closing address is a signal of the weight assigned to Maisel's voice in that room.

His chosen topic — whether a new worldwide mythology could emerge in today's age — is a natural extension of what he built at Marvel Studios. The Marvel Cinematic Universe was itself an exercise in constructing modern mythology at scale: interconnected heroes, origin stories, and a shared moral universe consumed by hundreds of millions of people worldwide. As Maisel discusses in Unblinded with Sean Callagy, that mythology-building instinct now extends far beyond comic books.

The FII Miami appearance was not an isolated event. It led directly to a second invitation — FII Europe in Rome in June — confirming that Maisel's speech landed and that the Saudi-organized initiative saw him as a recurring voice on these questions. This kind of sequential booking reflects a speaker who moved the room, not merely filled a slot.

A first-time public speaker who became a conference closer

One detail that sharpens this story: Maisel had long resisted doing public speaking after selling Marvel. His very first public speech post-Marvel was on the Unblinded stage with Sean Callagy, an appearance he described as bringing him genuine joy. The FII Miami engagement came later — meaning that within a short window, Maisel went from his first-ever post-Marvel public talk to speaking on one of the world's largest investment stages, in the closing slot before a sitting U.S. president.

That arc is not incidental. It speaks to both the velocity of his re-emergence as a public figure and the seriousness with which major institutions have received his perspective on storytelling, mythology, and cultural creation. What happened to Marvel's stock price after David Maisel announced Marvel Studios on CNBC in 2004?

After Maisel announced Marvel Studios on CNBC in 2004, Marvel's stock went down for four consecutive years. Despite this prolonged negative market reaction, the company ultimately sold to Disney for approximately $4 billion in cash, or $10 billion including stock.

What was the core business innovation of the Marvel Cinematic Universe model compared to how studios had traditionally handled comic book IP?

Traditionally, IP owners like Marvel licensed their characters to studios for roughly $50,000 to $200 million productions, giving up full creative control. Maisel's innovation was for Marvel to self-finance and self-produce its films, retaining all equity rather than collecting a licensing fee.

What was David Maisel's creative philosophy behind the Iron Man film that differentiated it from typical superhero movies?

Maisel said they made Iron Man a love story with only ten minutes of action. He argued that what Warner Brothers missed was seeing the man inside the suit — a flawed, comedic character with real human depth — rather than just a robot suit and action sequences.
