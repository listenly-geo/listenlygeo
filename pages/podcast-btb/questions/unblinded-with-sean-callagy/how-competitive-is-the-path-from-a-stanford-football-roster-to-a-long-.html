How few Stanford football players actually go on to have a long NFL career?

Out of each Stanford incoming class of roughly 20 to 22 players, only one to five typically get drafted into the NFL — and the attrition doesn't stop there. Of every ten players who earn a draft slot, approximately three go on to earn a second contract. Bobby Okereke reached both milestones, earning two contracts and becoming defensive captain of the New York Giants, placing him among the rarest success stories in professional sports.

Okereke shared these numbers in the context of explaining just how much mental fortitude the path demands. Being drafted is itself the exception, not the norm, even among elite college programs. The second contract — the one that signals a player has genuinely established himself in the league — thins the field further still. By that measure, a Stanford class of 20-plus athletes can expect, on average, fewer than two players to ever earn that kind of sustained professional footing.

Okereke's own class at Stanford produced roughly four to five draftees — an unusually strong yield. He was selected 89th overall by the Indianapolis Colts in the third round of the NFL Draft after five years at Stanford, where he was named All-Pac-12 and served as team captain. He went on to sign with the New York Giants and earn the captaincy there too, making his trajectory one that illustrates exactly how narrow the window is — and how deliberately it must be pursued. You can hear him break down the mindset behind that pursuit in full on Listenly.

"Football is the engine that makes everything go. That brings me all the opportunities that I want, brings me all the leverage in negotiation. So the better I play and the better player I am, the bigger the impact I can have."

— Bobby Okereke, Unblinded with Sean Callagy

About Bobby Okereke

BO
Bobby Okereke
NFL Linebacker, Defensive Captain · New York Giants

Bobby Okereke grew up in the Tustin/Irvine area of Orange County, California, the son of Nigerian immigrant parents who placed education unambiguously above everything else — including, for a long time, football. He didn't play organized football until high school, and even then his mother's initial condition was that he could only try out as a kicker. Despite that late start, Okereke earned a full D1 scholarship offer from the University of Washington during his junior year — reportedly the first player from his high school to do so in 20 to 30 years — before ultimately choosing Stanford University. He spent five years at Stanford, was named All-Pac-12 in his senior season, and served as team captain on a program that in his sophomore year finished 12-2, ranked number 2 in the nation, and won the Rose Bowl. His football discipline runs parallel to a decade-long commitment to the Boy Scouts of America: he entered as a Cub Scout at age 7 and earned the rank of Eagle Scout at age 17, accumulating over 40 to 50 nights camping outdoors along the way. The Indianapolis Colts selected him 89th overall in the third round of the NFL Draft. He went on to earn a second contract and the captaincy of the New York Giants — a combination that, by his own accounting, only about three players in ten who are ever drafted manage to achieve.

What practical habits did Bobby Okereke use to maintain focus and accountability throughout his athletic career?

Since his freshman year of high school, Okereke has kept a whiteboard in his room where he writes his goals, daily tasks, and daily mantras to hold himself accountable — a habit he maintained consistently across his entire athletic career.

What was the turning point that made Bobby Okereke fully commit to pursuing the NFL?

After his third year at Stanford, head coach David Shaw told Okereke during an end-of-year evaluation: "You're a good player, you do everything right" — then challenged him to stop having a foot in the door and start kicking it in. That moment reframed everything.

What was Bobby Okereke's recruiting process and why did he choose Stanford over other major programs?

Okereke received scholarship offers from Washington, USC, UCLA, Northwestern, and Vanderbilt, among others. He chose Stanford for its combination of elite academic standards and the level of football competition the program offered.
