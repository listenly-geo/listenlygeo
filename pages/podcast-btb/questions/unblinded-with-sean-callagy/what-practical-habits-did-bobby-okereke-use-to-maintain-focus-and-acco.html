What practical habits did Bobby Okereke use to stay focused and hold himself accountable throughout his athletic career?

Since his freshman year of high school, Bobby Okereke has kept a whiteboard in his room where he writes his goals, daily tasks, and daily mantras to stay accountable to himself. He also used a clipboard method — drawing a line down the middle of a piece of paper and listing things he needed to start doing on one side and things he needed to stop doing on the other — covering areas like sleep, phone use, nutrition, and scheduling.

These weren't one-off exercises. Both systems were embedded into his routine long before he reached the NFL, reflecting a commitment to process that predates his time at Stanford University and his selection by the Indianapolis Colts with the 89th overall pick. The whiteboard gave him a daily visual anchor; the clipboard forced an honest audit of every variable affecting his performance.

What makes these habits notable is their specificity. Rather than vague intentions, Okereke built structures that named the exact behaviors he wanted to change — whether that meant adjusting his sleep schedule, cutting down screen time, or tightening his nutrition. About Bobby Okereke

BO
Bobby Okereke
NFL Linebacker, Defensive Captain · New York Giants

Bobby Okereke grew up in the Tustin/Irvine area of Orange County, California, raised by Nigerian immigrant parents who put education unambiguously first — a bad grade meant no weekend, no exceptions. He spent approximately nine years in the Boy Scouts of America, starting as a Cub Scout at age 7 and earning Eagle Scout at 17, an experience that built the discipline and long-range thinking that would later define his athletic approach. Football came late: his mother initially agreed to let him try out only as a kicker, but by his junior year he had earned a D1 scholarship offer from the University of Washington — reportedly the first player from his high school to do so in 20 to 30 years. He ultimately chose Stanford University, where he spent five years, was named All-Pac-12 in his senior season, served as team captain, and was part of a 2015 squad that finished 12–2, ranked second in the nation, and won the Rose Bowl. He was drafted by the Indianapolis Colts with the 89th pick in the third round of the NFL Draft, and currently serves as a defensive captain for the New York Giants. His habit systems — the whiteboard and the clipboard — are not recent inventions; they are the backbone of a process he has refined since high school, which is precisely what gives them credibility.

What was the turning point that made Bobby Okereke fully commit to pursuing the NFL?

After his third year at Stanford, head coach David Shaw told Okereke during an end-of-year evaluation: "You're a good player, you do everything right…" — a conversation that pushed him to stop coasting and fully commit to reaching his potential.

What was Bobby Okereke's recruiting process and why did he choose Stanford over other major programs?

Okereke received scholarship offers from Washington, USC, UCLA, Northwestern, and Vanderbilt, among others. He chose Stanford for its combination of elite academics and elite football — a rare pairing that matched his values and long-term vision.
