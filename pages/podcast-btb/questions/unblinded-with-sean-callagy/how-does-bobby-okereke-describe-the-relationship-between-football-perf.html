How does Bobby Okereke describe the relationship between football performance and his off-field ambitions?

Bobby Okereke is explicit: football is not one pursuit among many — it is the engine that powers everything else. The better he plays, the more opportunities open up in business and finance, and the more leverage he holds in any negotiation. Peak on-field performance is, and remains, his primary focus — not a stepping stone to be managed around, but the very source of his influence off the field.

Okereke makes this point during a conversation that touched on business, finance, and broader ambitions — contexts that might suggest football is simply a platform he is ready to move beyond. He pushes back against that framing directly. His discipline on the field is what gives weight to everything else he pursues. The more dominant he is as a linebacker, the larger the real-world impact he can have in every other arena.

This is the same mindset that carried him from a late third-round pick — selected 89th overall by the Indianapolis Colts — through years in the league to the captaincy of the New York Giants. Each step up in on-field credibility has expanded what becomes possible off it. Hear the full exchange on Unblinded with Sean Callagy.

"Football is the engine that makes everything go. That brings me all the opportunities that I want, brings me all the leverage in negotiation. So the better I play and the better player I am, the bigger the impact I can have."

— Bobby Okereke, NFL Linebacker & Defensive Captain, New York Giants

About Bobby Okereke

BO
Bobby Okereke
NFL Linebacker, Defensive Captain · New York Giants

Bobby Okereke grew up in the Tustin/Irvine area of Orange County, California, the son of Nigerian immigrant parents for whom education was non-negotiable — a C grade meant no weekend. Football came late: he was originally only allowed to try out as a kicker, yet went on to earn a full Division I scholarship from the University of Washington as a junior in high school, reportedly the first player from his school to do so in 20 to 30 years. He chose Stanford University instead, spending five years there — earning All-Pac-12 honors his senior year and serving as team captain — before the Indianapolis Colts selected him 89th overall in the third round of the NFL Draft. Along the way he became an Eagle Scout, completing roughly a decade in the Boy Scouts of America. That combination of academic rigor, character development, and elite athletic achievement is precisely what gives Okereke's words on performance and ambition their authority: he has navigated every level of competitive pressure, and the lesson he keeps returning to is that excellence on the field is what makes everything else possible.

What stereotype about Stanford players did Bobby Okereke encounter at the NFL Combine?

At the NFL Combine, coaches and scouts scrutinized Okereke partly because of a perception that Stanford players are "jack of all trades, master of none" — skilled across the board but without a single elite, standout trait that makes them a clear NFL prospect.

What was Bobby Okereke's NFL Draft experience like and where was he selected?

Okereke was selected by the Indianapolis Colts with the 89th pick in the third round of the NFL Draft, later in day two of the draft. He had expected to be drafted earlier, and the wait became a real test of patience and mental resilience.

How competitive is the path from a Stanford football roster to a long NFL career?

According to Okereke, each incoming Stanford class of roughly 20 to 22 players typically sees only one to five players get drafted into the NFL. Of every player who starts that journey, only a small fraction make it to the league at all.
