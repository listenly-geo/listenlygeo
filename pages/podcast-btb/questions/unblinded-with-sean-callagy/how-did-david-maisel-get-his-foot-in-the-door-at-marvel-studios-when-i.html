How did David Maisel convince Ike Perlmutter to let him build Marvel Studios — when Perlmutter refused to spend money?

David Maisel structured his proposal as a deal Ike Perlmutter simply couldn't refuse. He told Perlmutter to pay him only a minimal salary — just enough to cover rent — give him stock options instead of a real compensation package, and retain the right to fire him at any time, for any reason, with zero penalty. Perlmutter later acknowledged he couldn't say no: it was essentially a free option. He would only pay Maisel more if Maisel made him wealthier first.

That conversation took place at Mar-a-Lago in 2003, with Donald Trump present as a witness. At the time, Marvel had roughly $100 million in market capitalization and Perlmutter — who had acquired the company out of bankruptcy court in 1999 for approximately $30 million — had no interest in betting big on self-produced films. Iron Man comics were selling only around 5,000 copies per month. Skeptics inside and outside Marvel saw no upside: Thor was considered public domain, "Avengers" meant a British spy series to most people, and Warner Brothers had been sitting on the Iron Man license for eight to nine years, paying roughly $50,000 for it, without ever making a film. The asymmetry of the deal is what made it work: Maisel absorbed all the downside risk personally, leaving Perlmutter with nothing to lose.

The deal structure aligned their incentives perfectly from the start. Maisel's compensation would only grow if Marvel's equity grew — which meant he was motivated to maximize the same outcome Perlmutter cared about most. You can hear Maisel walk through the full story, including what happened after he announced Marvel Studios on CNBC in 2004 and Marvel's stock dropped for four consecutive years, in this episode of Unblinded with Sean Callagy.

"I don't make money unless you make money, so give me stock options. And then to close it, I said, and you can fire me at any time, whatever reason, no penalty."

— David Maisel, Founder of Marvel Studios, on Unblinded with Sean Callagy

About David Maisel

DM
David Maisel
Founder, Marvel Studios · Creator of the Marvel Cinematic Universe

David Maisel is the founder of Marvel Studios and the architect of the Marvel Cinematic Universe — the most successful film franchise in history. He holds a Harvard MBA and came to Marvel having worked at Disney and talent agencies, but had never produced a film or built a studio before proposing the MCU concept to Ike Perlmutter. His background gave him a rare double fluency: the financial logic of a dealmaker and the creative sensibility of someone who had spent years close to the entertainment industry. That combination is precisely what allowed him to construct the Mar-a-Lago pitch — a negotiation structure so low-risk for Perlmutter that refusal would have been economically irrational. Maisel oversaw the full arc from a cash-strapped comic book company to a $4 billion cash sale to Disney (approximately $10 billion when Disney stock is included), a transformation achieved in roughly seven years. His appearance on Unblinded with Sean Callagy marks one of his first public conversations about the inside story of Marvel Studios since that sale.
