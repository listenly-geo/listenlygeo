How did Bobby Okereke first get into football despite his parents' reluctance?

Bobby Okereke's mother gave him exactly one condition for trying out for football: he had to play kicker — a position he quietly abandoned the moment he stepped onto the field. For years, his parents remained largely in the dark about how involved he actually was, learning about his games not from him, but from strangers who approached his mother at the grocery store. The turning point came during his junior year of high school, when he received his first scholarship offer — a full ride from the University of Washington — which convinced his parents to fully and enthusiastically embrace his football career.

Okereke grew up in Orange County, California, to Nigerian immigrant parents for whom education was the unambiguous priority. A C grade or below meant no weekend plans — sports were a secondary concern at best. Okereke navigated this environment by keeping a low profile about his playing time through his freshman, sophomore, and junior years. The gap between what his parents knew and what was actually happening on the field was wide enough that his mother would hear about his performances secondhand at the grocery store, genuinely unaware he had even played a game the day before.

The scholarship offer changed the entire conversation. When Okereke told his mother he had received an offer, she asked what that meant. He explained it was a scholarship. She asked how much. When he said it was a full ride, the family's position on football shifted immediately — reportedly making Okereke the first player from his high school to earn a Division I scholarship in 20 to 30 years. That offer from Washington opened the door to a path that eventually led to Stanford University, an All-Pac-12 selection, a team captaincy, and a third-round NFL Draft pick by the Indianapolis Colts. You can hear Okereke walk through this entire story in his own words on Unblinded with Sean Callagy.

"Football is the engine that makes everything go. That brings me all the opportunities that I want, brings me all the leverage in negotiation. So the better I play and the better player I am, the bigger the impact I can have."

— Bobby Okereke, NFL Linebacker & Defensive Captain, New York Giants

About Bobby Okereke

BO
Bobby Okereke NFL Linebacker, Defensive Captain New York Giants

Bobby Okereke was born and raised in the Tustin/Irvine area of Orange County, California, to Nigerian immigrant parents who placed education at the very center of family life. Before football ever entered the picture, he spent nearly a decade in the Boy Scouts of America — starting as a Cub Scout at age 7 and earning the rank of Eagle Scout at 17, logging over 40 to 50 nights camping outdoors along the way. His entry into organized football came in high school, where his mother's single condition — play kicker or don't play — lasted approximately one practice. He went on to become reportedly the first player from his high school to earn a Division I scholarship in 20 to 30 years, receiving a full-ride offer from the University of Washington in his junior year. He chose Stanford University instead, where he spent five years, earned All-Pac-12 honors in his senior season, and served as team captain. Stanford's 2015 season — his sophomore year — ended ranked number 2 in the nation with a 12–2 record and a Rose Bowl title. He was selected 89th overall by the Indianapolis Colts in the third round of the NFL Draft and later became Defensive Captain of the New York Giants. His story is one of quietly outpacing every expectation set for him — including his own parents' — one scholarship offer at a time.
