How did Darren Prince sell his first business for $1 million at age 19?

August 25, 2026 Unblinded with Sean Callagy · Darren Prince

← Voir la fiche Unblinded with Sean Callagy

Darren Prince started collecting sports cards as a child, keeping meticulous price notes in shoeboxes — a habit that revealed a rare aptitude for numbers. After splitting a $10 table at a car show with his childhood friend Stephen Simon, he made over $1,000 in a single Sunday afternoon. He spent five years building that sports memorabilia business before selling it for $1 million in the late 1980s, while still a teenager.

A $5 table, a shoebox, and a father who saw what the school system missed

Prince grew up in Livingston, New Jersey — classified in special education classes, a label that followed him through his early years. His school record didn't reflect what his father saw. In a conversation Prince describes as life-changing, his father told him plainly: "I've noticed something with your brain with numbers that's mesmerizing that none of them have — and we're going to tap into that to become a success."

That conviction translated directly into action. Prince had been collecting sports cards from childhood, tracking prices with the kind of precision most adults don't apply to their finances. The car show moment — half of a $10 table, one Sunday, more than $1,000 made — confirmed that the instinct was real and scalable. You can hear Prince walk through that turning point in detail on Unblinded with Sean Callagy.

Five years of building — then an exit most entrepreneurs never reach in a lifetime

Prince didn't flip the business overnight. He spent five disciplined years growing his sports memorabilia operation before the $1 million sale came through in the late 1980s. That exit then became the foundation for his pivot into sports and entertainment marketing — the world that would eventually bring him inside the orbit of Muhammad Ali, Magic Johnson, Hulk Hogan, Joe Frazier, Pamela Anderson, Chevy Chase, and David Goggins, among others.

The career that followed accumulated over $500 million in deals representing those names. But the first chapter — the one that made everything else possible — started with a shoebox and a father's belief. Prince and childhood friend Stephen Simon later went on to co-found Prince Markets together, the company that would anchor his entertainment marketing work for decades.

What makes Prince's story particularly striking is the gap between institutional assessment and actual ability. The school system placed him in special education. His father identified a gift for numbers that was, in his words, "mesmerizing." The car show proved it. The $1 million exit confirmed it. The full story — including what came after that success — is told on the Unblinded podcast.
