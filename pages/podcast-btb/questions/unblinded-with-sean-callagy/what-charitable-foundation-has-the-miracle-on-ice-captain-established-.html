How much has Mike Eruzione's charitable foundation raised?

September 22, 2026 Unblinded with Sean Callagy · Mike Eruzione

← See the Unblinded with Sean Callagy fiche

Eruzione founded Winthrop Charities, named after his parents, which has raised approximately $350,000 to $400,000 over the years. He obtained $1.6 million from selling nearly all his Olympic memorabilia, which he used to endow a scholarship in his parents' name at Boston University and to support his charitable foundation.

From Olympic Memorabilia to Enduring Philanthropy

The foundation's name carries deep personal meaning—honoring both his mother and father through the institution itself. This naming choice reflects Eruzione's belief that legacy extends beyond individual achievement into generational impact and family values.

The capital that fueled Winthrop Charities came from a decisive choice: selling his Olympic memorabilia. When Eruzione appeared on Who Wants to Be a Millionaire, he also won $150,000, but his largest financial contribution to the foundation came from liquidating nearly all of his historical artifacts and mementos—a total of $1.6 million.

Rather than hold onto the physical symbols of his achievement, Eruzione redirected that capital toward education. The funds endowed a scholarship program at Boston University in his parents' name, ensuring that future generations of students would benefit from his decision to prioritize philanthropic mission over personal memorabilia collection.

"What we did is what we did. What you do is what you do. This is your opportunity to go out there and show the world where the best hockey player in the world has come from."

Key takeaways

  • Winthrop Charities, named after Eruzione's mother and father, has raised between $350,000 and $400,000 over its years of operation.
  • Eruzione sold nearly all his Olympic memorabilia for $1.6 million to fund the foundation and endow scholarships at Boston University.
  • The primary charitable vehicle became a scholarship program established in his parents' name, demonstrating a commitment to education and generational impact.
