Why did Marvel choose Iron Man — and not Spider-Man or X-Men — as the first film it produced itself?

The answer comes down to licensing constraints and an extraordinary piece of luck. Spider-Man had been licensed to Sony and X-Men to Fox — and those deals were siloed, meaning the characters couldn't be combined into a connected universe. That left David Maisel looking at Marvel's remaining characters: Thor, Captain America, and Iron Man. Nobody in Hollywood wanted any of them. But Warner Brothers had been sitting on the Iron Man license for approximately eight to nine years, paying roughly $50,000, without ever producing a film. When Warner Brothers let the rights lapse, Maisel was able to reclaim Iron Man — and it became the cornerstone of the entire Marvel Cinematic Universe.

The skepticism Maisel faced from every direction is hard to overstate. Industry insiders told him Thor was in the public domain and that "Avengers" just reminded people of a British spy series. Marvel's stock fell for four consecutive years after Maisel announced Marvel Studios on CNBC in 2004. At the time Maisel joined, Marvel had only five million dollars in the bank — and he was asking Ike Perlmutter to greenlight a $100 million self-produced film. The idea that Iron Man could anchor a billion-dollar cinematic universe was, at the time, not a conviction shared by anyone outside Maisel's own mind. You can hear him tell the full story on Listenly.

Part of what Warner Brothers missed, as Maisel describes it, was what Iron Man actually was as a character. They saw a man in a robot suit — or just the robot suit. They missed the human story underneath: the character's addiction, his flaws, his humor. Iron Man was never conceived as a conventional action hero. That human dimension, not the suit, is what Maisel and his team built the first film around — making it, in his words, a love story with ten minutes of action. That creative instinct, combined with a licensing accident at Warner Brothers, is the real origin story of the MCU.

— David Maisel, Founder of Marvel Studios, on Unblinded with Sean Callagy

About David Maisel

DM
David Maisel
Founder, Marvel Studios · Creator of the Marvel Cinematic Universe
Marvel Studios

David Maisel is the founder of Marvel Studios and the architect of the Marvel Cinematic Universe — the highest-grossing film franchise in history. He holds a Harvard MBA and arrived at Marvel having worked at Disney and talent agencies, with no prior experience making films or building a studio. That outsider profile, paradoxically, is what allowed him to conceive the MCU as a connected universe rather than a series of isolated licensing deals.

Maisel secured his position at Marvel through an unconventional negotiation with Ike Perlmutter at Mar-a-Lago in 2003. He accepted a minimal salary, took stock options in lieu of cash compensation — ensuring he would only profit if Marvel did — and explicitly gave Perlmutter the right to fire him at any time for any reason with no financial penalty. That structure, designed to remove every objection a notoriously frugal executive could raise, is what got Maisel through the door.

What followed was one of the most consequential bets in entertainment history. Marvel's market cap stood at roughly $100 million when Maisel joined. He raised $525 million in non-recourse debt financing in 2004 to fund Marvel's self-produced films while retaining all equity — a financial structure Hollywood had never seen applied to superhero content at that scale. When Disney acquired Marvel, the transaction valued the company at approximately $10 billion including stock, representing one of the largest value-creation events in media history. Maisel's authority on the Iron Man story is not theoretical: he was the person who identified the licensing gap at Warner Brothers, reclaimed the rights, and made the creative and financial decisions that turned a character selling 5,000 comics a month into the foundation of a global cinematic universe.
