How does a celebrity agent maintain sobriety and mental clarity every morning?

September 15, 2026 Unblinded with Sean Callagy · Darren Prince

← See the Unblinded with Sean Callagy fiche

Darren Prince starts his day with 45 minutes of complete no-contact—no phone, emails, texts, or social media—followed by self-guided meditation, breath work, and 500 jump rope loops. This foundation is anchored by reading from 12-step recovery literature and practices that emphasize service to others, with consistency (not discipline) as the key to maintaining 18+ years of sobriety.

The routine is deliberately minimal on external stimulation. Those first 45 minutes of isolation protect the day before the noise begins—no news, no notifications, no decisions that aren't internal.

After the no-contact window, Prince moves into guided meditation and breath work, often using audio guidance from his life coach. This isn't passive listening; it's active neurological reset. The breath work component functions as a physiological anchor, grounding his nervous system before the demands of representing high-profile clients begin.

The physical component matters equally. 500 jump rope loops per day isn't just cardio—it's a ritual that builds discipline through repetition and creates a measurable, non-negotiable commitment. A grounding mat is also part of the practice, connecting him literally and symbolically to something stable.

The 12-step foundation: consistency over motivation

Reading from 12-step recovery literature is non-negotiable in Prince's routine. The emphasis on God and serving others reframes the day away from ego or commercial success toward a larger purpose. This isn't theology—it's architecture for sustained sobriety.

What Prince stresses repeatedly is that consistency matters more than willpower or temporary motivation. The routine works because it repeats every single day, regardless of how he feels. This is what differentiates a practice that sticks from New Year's resolutions that fade.

When Prince discusses his daily regimen on the Unblinded podcast, he frames it not as punishment or compensation for his past, but as the operating system that allows him to show up for his clients, his foundation, and his life without relapse.

"Life is how successful I can make you and everybody else around me because you're going to do the same."

This quote encapsulates why the morning routine matters to Prince: it's not selfish restoration. It's preparation to be useful. A mind clouded by unmanaged addiction or daily chaos cannot serve others effectively. The routine is an act of service to the people around him.

The specificity of his daily structure—which Prince describes in detail in this episode—reveals something often missing from generic wellness advice: accountability through ritual, not just intention. The jump rope count is measurable. The no-contact window is defined. The recovery literature is named. This eliminates the possibility of drifting.

Why consistency compounds over years

Eighteen years of sobriety is not the result of a single brilliant decision or a short sprint of motivation. It's the accumulated effect of 6,570 mornings where the same routine was executed, often when execution felt pointless. That's the real practice.

Prince's routine also includes what might seem counterintuitive for someone representing celebrities: radical disconnection at the start of the day. While many executives pride themselves on immediate responsiveness, Prince's 45-minute fortress of silence gives his mind—and his sobriety—first claim on his time. Everything else follows.

The full episode explores how Prince's recovery mindset translates into client representation and his broader philosophy of giving, revealing why someone who once faced felony charges now serves as a trusted advisor to legends.

Key takeaways

  • Start with 45 minutes of zero external contact—no phone, email, texts, or social media—to protect the day before chaos begins.
  • Layer in guided meditation, breath work, and a grounding mat to reset the nervous system physiologically.
  • Execute a non-negotiable physical practice (500 jump rope loops daily) to build discipline through measurable repetition.
  • Read from 12-step recovery literature daily to anchor the day in purpose and service, not ego.
  • Prioritize consistency over motivation—the power is in the 6,570+ mornings of execution, not the intention behind any single day.
