How did Darren Prince first get addicted to opioids?

August 30, 2026 Unblinded with Sean Callagy · Darren Prince

← See the Unblinded with Sean Callagy fiche

At age 14, while at sleepaway camp, Darren Prince suffered severe stomach pain and was given a green liquid syrup by the camp nurse—later revealed to be liquid Demerol. Within five minutes, he felt confident, popular, and talkative for the first time in his life. He immediately began faking illness for three consecutive weeks to access the drug again, establishing a pattern of manipulation that would define the early stages of his addiction.

A moment of false escape at summer camp

Darren Prince had grown up in New Jersey feeling deeply inadequate. Placed in special education classrooms, bullied by peers and teachers alike, he carried a crushing sense of worthlessness into adolescence. That weight made the effect of the liquid Demerol almost supernatural when the camp nurse administered it.

The transformation was instant and intoxicating. As Prince recounts in the Unblinded podcast, "I felt like Superman. I felt just as smart, just as popular, just as good looking. I never had this feeling before in my life." For a teenager drowning in self-doubt, that chemical relief was not just pleasant—it was salvation.

Learning to lie from day one

The critical danger was not the single dose. It was what came next: the decision to chase that feeling. Prince learned within days how to manufacture symptoms, how to manipulate adults, and how to prioritize access to a substance over truth. For three consecutive weeks, he faked stomach pain repeatedly, each time receiving his dose of liquid Demerol.

This early lesson in deception became foundational. By the time he reached his early twenties, Prince's addiction had escalated to the point where he was arrested four times in six months, but the seed of manipulation had been planted at age 14 in that camp infirmary. He was not simply becoming dependent on a drug; he was learning that lying and scheming were the pathway to getting what he wanted—even when what he wanted was slowly destroying him.
