What was the turning point that made Bobby Okereke fully commit to pursuing the NFL?

After his third year at Stanford, head coach David Shaw delivered a blunt end-of-year evaluation to Bobby Okereke: "You're a good player, you do everything right, you've made a lot of plays — but right now it looks like you have your foot in the door. If you really want to reach your potential, you gotta kick the door in." That direct challenge, paired with a saying from his college strength coach — "we all suffer one of two pains, the pain of discipline or the pain of regret and disappointment" — pushed Okereke to go all-in and dial his intensity to 100 percent.

What made Shaw's words land so hard was their specificity. Okereke wasn't being told he lacked talent. He was being told he lacked full commitment — that he was holding something back. For a player who had fought his way from a high school where few even earned D1 scholarships to a program that sent roughly 4 to 5 players from his graduating class of 20 to 22 to the NFL, the idea of leaving potential on the table was unacceptable. The strength coach's phrase reframed the choice entirely: discipline now, or regret later. There was no comfortable middle ground.

Okereke describes the process of committing fully as building a kind of internal force field — feeding himself conviction, tuning out external noise, and choosing to go against the grain when necessary. The result: he was named All-Pac-12 his senior year, served as team captain at Stanford, and was selected 89th overall by the Indianapolis Colts in the third round of the NFL Draft. You can hear him describe this mindset shift in detail on Listenly's full episode page for Unblinded with Sean Callagy.

"Football is the engine that makes everything go. That brings me all the opportunities that I want, brings me all the leverage in negotiation. So the better I play and the better player I am, the bigger the impact I can have."

— Bobby Okereke, NFL Linebacker & Defensive Captain, New York Giants

About Bobby Okereke

BO
Bobby Okereke
NFL Linebacker, Defensive Captain · New York Giants

Bobby Okereke grew up in the Tustin/Irvine area of Orange County, California, the son of Nigerian immigrant parents who placed education firmly above all else — a strict household where a C grade meant no weekend plans. He spent approximately nine years in the Boy Scouts of America, earning the rank of Eagle Scout at age 17, a formative experience in discipline and structured achievement that preceded his football career entirely.

Okereke didn't touch football until high school, and only because his mother agreed to let him try out under one condition: he had to play kicker. He quickly moved past that constraint, eventually becoming a standout player and the first from his school to earn a Division I scholarship in reportedly 20 to 30 years — his first offer coming from the University of Washington while he was a junior.

He chose Stanford University, where he spent five years developing as a linebacker under coach David Shaw. His senior year was defined by the commitment shift described in this episode: named All-Pac-12, serving as team captain, and finishing in a class of roughly 20 to 22 players that produced approximately 4 to 5 NFL draftees. He was selected 89th overall in the third round of the NFL Draft by the Indianapolis Colts, later joining the New York Giants where he serves as a defensive captain. His trajectory — from reluctant high school kicker to NFL captain — gives full weight to everything he says about commitment and going all-in.

What was Bobby Okereke's recruiting process and why did he choose Stanford over other major programs?

Okereke received scholarship offers from Washington, USC, UCLA, Northwestern, and Vanderbilt, among others. He chose Stanford for its combination of elite academics and competitive football.

What role did the Boy Scouts of America play in Bobby Okereke's development?

Bobby Okereke spent approximately nine years in the Boy Scouts of America, starting as a Cub Scout at age 7 and earning the rank of Eagle Scout at age 17. The program instilled discipline and a commitment to excellence that shaped his character long before he reached the NFL.

How did Bobby Okereke first get into football despite his parents' reluctance?

Bobby Okereke's mother told him he could try out for football only if he played kicker, a position he quickly abandoned. His parents remained largely unaware of how seriously his career was progressing until he received his first D1 scholarship offer as a junior in high school.
