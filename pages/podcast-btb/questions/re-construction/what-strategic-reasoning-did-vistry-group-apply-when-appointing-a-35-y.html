Why did Vistry Group appoint a 35-year-old as chief executive after a multi-year succession process?

15 April 2026 Re:Construction

← See the Re:Construction podcast fiche

Vistry Group appointed Adam Daniels, who had just turned 35, as CEO after a rigorous multi-year succession process overseen by the nomination committee. The decision signals a deliberate shift toward younger, more adaptable leadership, moving away from the forceful personality of outgoing CEO Greg Fitzgerald.

Adam Daniels brought solid operational experience to the role. For the three years prior to his appointment on 13 April 2026, he had managed Vistry's regional offices across Yorkshire, Midlands, and Western England—a substantial portfolio demonstrating his capacity to handle large-scale regional operations. His journey to the top started earlier: he joined Countryside Partnerships in 2016, and when Vistry acquired Countryside in 2022, he transitioned smoothly into the combined group.

The appointment marked a notable departure from Vistry's previous leadership model. Greg Fitzgerald, the retiring chairman and CEO, represented a different leadership archetype—someone with a stronger force of personality and more established industry standing. As discussed in the 200th episode of Re:Construction, the board's choice of a younger successor suggests a strategic preference for a leader who could prove more malleable and adaptable to evolving market conditions and stakeholder needs.

The board's willingness to compress the succession timeline proved telling. Although Fitzgerald initially announced he would remain CEO for up to a year while the company searched for a replacement, the succession was accelerated and completed within just five weeks of Fitzgerald's initial announcement on 4 March. This speed signals confidence in Daniels and the board's conviction that the succession process had already identified the right person.

This appointment reflects a broader pattern in UK construction: as the industry faces cyclical pressures and shifting regulatory demands, boards increasingly favour internally developed, proven operators over external hires with dominant personalities.
