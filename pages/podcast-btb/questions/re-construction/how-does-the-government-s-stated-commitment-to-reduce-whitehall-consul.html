Why announce Whitehall consultation cuts on the same day as launching new consultations?

21 September 2026 Re:Construction

← See the Re:Construction fiche

Ministers announced a crackdown on Whitehall consultation culture to accelerate decision-making and eliminate unnecessary consultations—then immediately announced a batch of new consultations on the same day. The Ministry of Housing Communities and Local Government currently runs 15 active consultations, contradicting the stated commitment to reduce bureaucratic drag.

The contradiction highlights a persistent gap between government rhetoric and implementation. While the policy statement positioned consultation reduction as a reform to speed up governance, the simultaneous launch of new consultations—including one on late payment legislation—suggests either poor coordination or a gap between stated priorities and actual practice.

As explored in this episode of Re:Construction, these consultations directly affect industry stakeholders. The 15 live consultations under the Ministry of Housing Communities and Local Government cover various regulatory changes that businesses must respond to, whether the government's stated intent is to reduce consultation demand or not.

This contradiction matters for the construction sector specifically. Government consultations on late payment, building regulations, and housing standards demand significant time and resources from industry bodies. When ministers announce a consultation reduction policy while simultaneously expanding the consultation pipeline, it creates uncertainty about which consultations actually merit participation—and suggests that the reform commitment may lack enforcement mechanisms.

The gap between policy announcement and delivery

The timing of the announcement reveals a structural problem in how policy reform is communicated. Launching new consultations on the same day as announcing a consultation reduction sends conflicting signals to stakeholders who are expected to engage with both the old and new processes.

For construction professionals and industry associations, this creates real operational friction. They must commit resources to responding to live consultations—including on late payment, which has been a legislative priority for over six years—while also monitoring whether future consultation requests will actually decrease.
