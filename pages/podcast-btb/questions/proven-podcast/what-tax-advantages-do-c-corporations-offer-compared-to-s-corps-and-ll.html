What tax advantages do C Corporations really offer compared to other entity types?

September 11, 2026 Proven Podcast · Garrett Gunderson

← See the Proven Podcast fiche

C Corporations offer capital-raising flexibility through multiple share classes, the ability to retain earnings without triggering immediate personal taxation, and access to medical reimbursement accounts unavailable in LLCs or S Corps. On lower revenue amounts, C Corps actually pay less total tax than pass-through entities, since income in S Corps and LLCs flows directly to owners at their individual tax rates—often 37% at the top bracket—whereas a C Corporation can retain profits at corporate rates.

The entity type you select determines which tax strategies remain available to you later. Most business owners treat this decision casually, relying solely on their CPA's filing recommendation rather than a coordinated legal and tax strategy. This oversight can cost millions, as Garrett Gunderson explains in the Proven Podcast, because certain advantages are locked in by entity choice from day one.

The capital gains advantage of C Corporations

One of the most significant C Corporation benefits is eligibility under Section 1202 of the Internal Revenue Code. If a C Corporation has been in operation for at least three years, the owner can sell it for up to $7.5 million tax-free. Extend that to five years of operation, and the limit rises to $15 million tax-free per partner.

S Corps and LLCs do not qualify for Section 1202 protection. A pass-through entity owner selling their business faces ordinary capital gains taxation at 20%, versus the preferential long-term capital gains rate—and this exclusion can be the difference between a $15 million exit and a $12 million take-home. This single structural choice, made at incorporation, determines whether this advantage ever exists.

Earnings retention and double taxation myth

C Corporations can retain profits within the entity without immediately distributing them to shareholders for personal taxation. While the company itself pays corporate tax on those earnings, the ability to hold and reinvest capital strategically is unavailable in S Corps and LLCs, where all profits must flow through to owners regardless of whether they are actually distributed.

The "double taxation" concern often cited by advisors overlooks a practical reality: retaining earnings at corporate rates is often cheaper than distributing them and taxing them at individual rates, especially for owners in high tax brackets. On lower revenue amounts, this differential is decisive—a small business in a C Corp structure pays less total tax than the same business as an LLC or S Corp where 37% individual rates apply immediately.

See also

What is the difference between delaying taxes and actually saving taxes?

Delaying tax and saving tax are completely different things. Most CPAs focus on delay strategies, filing taxes after the fact and suggesting postponement. True tax savings involve strategic entity selection and proactive planning that permanently reduces what you owe, not just when you pay it.

What is the strategy for paying family members through a business structure?

You can pay your children just over $15,000 per year as a tax deduction to the business while keeping it tax-free to the child, and you still control that money. This strategy allows you to shift income to lower-bracket family members while maintaining financial oversight of the funds.

How can business owners take advantage of Section 280G for residential rental deductions?

Section 280G allows you to rent your home out for 14 days to your business, write it off as a business expense, and not claim it as personal income. This provides significant deduction flexibility without triggering personal income reporting on those rental days.

Key takeaways

  • C Corporations support multiple share classes, enabling more sophisticated capital-raising strategies than S Corps or LLCs.
  • Section 1202 allows tax-free exits of up to $7.5 million after three years or $15 million per partner after five years—available only to C Corps.
  • C Corporations can retain earnings without immediate personal taxation, while S Corps and LLCs force profits through to owners at individual rates.
  • On lower revenue, C Corps often pay less total tax than pass-through entities because corporate rates are lower than the 37% top individual rate.
  • Entity selection is permanent and determines which tax strategies are available; changing structure later cannot unlock benefits you missed at incorporation.
