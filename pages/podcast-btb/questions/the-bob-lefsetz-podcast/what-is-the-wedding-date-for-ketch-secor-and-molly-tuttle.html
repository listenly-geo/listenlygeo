When is Ketch Secor marrying Molly Tuttle in the Redwood Forest?

September 25, 2026 The Bob Lefsetz Podcast · Ketch Secor

← See The Bob Lefsetz Podcast fiche

Ketch Secor is getting married to Molly Tuttle on August 22nd. The wedding is being held in the Redwood Forest, and they are throwing quite a celebration.

When Bob Lefsetz asks about the date, Secor confirms it's just weeks away. For someone who has spent 28 years building Old Crow Medicine Show into a cornerstone of modern bluegrass and country music, this moment marks a significant personal turning point.

The union with Tuttle, whom Secor first heard on AM650 WSM radio in 2018, represents more than romance. Both are accomplished musicians—Tuttle is an acclaimed singer-songwriter in her own right—and their relationship bridges two generations of commitment to roots music.

"I'm a romantic, Bob. You know, I'm somebody like my love, my arms are barely wide enough to contain them. I love people very much, I love music very much."

Secor's ability to balance multiple demanding roles—touring extensively, chairing a nonprofit school board, and now stepping into marriage—reflects the same intensive energy that he describes during his conversation with Lefsetz about what keeps him going. The Redwood Forest setting for the wedding is notably deliberate, suggesting a celebration rooted in nature and authenticity—values central to both his music and his educational mission.

A second marriage, pursued with full intention

When asked how seriously he is taking this second marriage, Secor's openness about his romantic nature and his capacity for deep commitment becomes clear. The wedding isn't just a date on the calendar; it reflects the personal growth and healing he has experienced since his earlier years in the band. For someone constantly in motion—performing 85 shows in a single year while serving as chairman of his nonprofit school board—this celebration represents an intentional pause and a public declaration of devotion.

The fact that they are "throwing quite a bash," as Secor puts it, underscores that this is not a quiet, private affair but a gathering worthy of his expansive love and gratitude for the people around him. The Redwood Forest venue connects the celebration to something larger than the couple themselves—to land, legacy, and the natural world that has always informed country and bluegrass music.
