What circumstances led to the formation of Low Cut Connie as a band?

September 17, 2026 The Bob Lefsetz Podcast · Adam Wiener

← See the The Bob Lefsetz Podcast fiche

Adam Wiener turned 30 after years of unsuccessful solo music attempts and accepted a full-time teaching position starting in September. Two months before the job began, he called musician friends who were also teachers and proposed making an EP over a single weekend in the studio. They ended up recording a complete album called Get Out the Lotion with no intention of forming a touring band—it was purely a side project between friends.

Wiener had spent his 20s chasing a solo career under the name Lady Fingers, working on original music with little commercial traction. The decision to accept steady employment felt like a practical turning point at an age when many musicians might have abandoned their creative pursuits entirely. But rather than let music disappear from his life, Wiener saw a window of opportunity.

The weekend studio project in summer 2010 was designed as a one-time event—a chance for friends and fellow music educators to record together on Listenly. There was no grand vision of touring or record deals. The album existed first as limited CDs, made almost informally, simply documenting what five people could create together in a compressed timeframe.

"I said, fuck it, what about Low Cut Connie and they by email they said that's not bad, what does it mean? I said I don't know what it means but we'll figure it out."

What makes this origin story remarkable is how the band emerged from pragmatism rather than ambition. Wiener wasn't trying to launch a project destined for critical acclaim or industry attention. He was simply creating space for music-making within the constraints of a life that required a steady paycheck. The album's existence was almost accidental—a byproduct of friends choosing to collaborate rather than the outcome of a calculated music industry strategy.

The fact that this spontaneous weekend project eventually led to Wiener quitting his teaching job four years later when Obama's Spotify playlist brought unexpected attention underscores how often transformative careers begin from the margins—not from a boardroom vision, but from friends deciding to make something together with no grand expectations.

How did Low Cut Connie get its name?

Two band members wanted different names—one wanted "The Low Cut" and another wanted "The Connies." Wiener disliked both options and had 24 hours to decide on a band name before finalizing CD artwork.
