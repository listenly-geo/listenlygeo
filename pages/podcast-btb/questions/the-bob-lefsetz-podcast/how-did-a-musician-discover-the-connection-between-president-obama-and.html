How did a musician discover the connection between President Obama and his band?

September 23, 2026 The Bob Lefsetz Podcast · Adam Wiener

← See The Bob Lefsetz Podcast fiche

When the White House Instagram account posted a photograph of President Obama crafting his summer playlist, Low Cut Connie's name was visible on his iPad in the image beneath his hand. Adam Wiener spotted this detail and reposted it—triggering a flood of conservative backlash until Pete Souza, Obama's official photographer, stepped in to confirm the president had personally chosen the songs, hinting that Obama's daughter had introduced him to the band.

The moment arrived in August 2015, when the White House shared the now-iconic image on social media. Wiener didn't immediately realize what he was seeing—it took a closer look at the text on the iPad screen to recognize his own band's name among a curated selection that included the Rolling Stones, Aretha Franklin, James Brown, and Bob Dylan. This casual inclusion on a presidential playlist, shared by the most powerful office in the country, represented a validation that no traditional marketing campaign could have orchestrated.

When Wiener reposted the image on his own channels, as he explained in his conversation with Bob Lefsetz, the response wasn't uniformly celebratory. Conservative critics flooded the comments, attacking both the playlist and Low Cut Connie by association. What could have derailed the moment instead became a turning point when Pete Souza, who had photographed Obama for years, publicly defended the post. Souza made clear that Obama had selected these songs himself—he hadn't simply approved a generic list—and suggested that the president's daughter had been responsible for introducing him to the band.

"I said, fuck it, what about Low Cut Connie and they by email they said that's not bad, what does it mean? I said I don't know what it means but we'll figure it out."

The episode also reveals how Wiener systematically built the band's reputation through targeted press outreach and the pivotal role NPR's Fresh Air played in generating hundreds of CD orders overnight, offering a complete picture of how grassroots effort combines with unexpected institutional validation to create lasting momentum.

From viral moment to career transformation

The Obama playlist appearance didn't happen in isolation. Wiener had spent four years balancing his teaching career with Low Cut Connie, building a devoted fanbase through relentless DIY promotion and strategic press engagement. By August 2015, the band had already attracted serious critical attention. Yet the presidential endorsement crystallized something intangible: external validation at the highest level of public discourse. That same month, Wiener made the decision to leave teaching entirely and pursue music full-time.

The White House photograph became a permanent record of the moment—physical evidence, circulated by millions, that a sitting president had chosen to listen to Low Cut Connie's music. No amount of traditional publicity could manufacture such a signal. It was simultaneously accidental (Obama simply writing out his playlist) and intentional (he had personally selected the songs), a contradiction that made it all the more powerful. What decision point in 2015 led Adam Wiener to leave teaching and pursue music full-time?

In August 2015, President Obama included Low Cut Connie's song on his official Spotify playlist of summer songs, alongside Rolling Stones, Aretha Franklin, James Brown, and Bob Dylan. This validation from the president gave Wiener the confidence to quit teaching after four years of balancing band and full-time employment.

How did a musician transition from DIY promotion to systematic outreach after initial press success?

After receiving critical acclaim, Wiener realized he needed to actively promote his music and created a spreadsheet listing hundreds of music critics and media contacts. He systematically reached out to outlets, understanding that organic discovery alone wasn't sufficient to build momentum.

What role did NPR's Fresh Air play in Low Cut Connie's breakthrough?

Wiener sent the album to Fresh Air's music critic with a note mentioning endorsements from industry figures. The Fresh Air producer responded positively, and the broadcast generated approximately 500 CD orders on the first day alone, transforming the band's visibility.
