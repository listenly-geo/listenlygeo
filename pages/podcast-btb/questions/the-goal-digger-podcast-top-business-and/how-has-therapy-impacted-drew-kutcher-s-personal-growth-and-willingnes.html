How has therapy impacted Drew Kutcher's personal growth and willingness to address emotional challenges?

September 25, 2026 The Goal Digger Podcast · Drew Kutcher

← See The Goal Digger Podcast fiche

Therapy has fundamentally shifted Drew Kutcher's relationship with emotional work. His therapist Julia has opened doors he had consistently avoided, and leaning into difficult conversations has become pivotal—so much so that he describes the recent individual therapy work as monumental, even acknowledging that fears about unearthing painful things dissolve once you actually begin the work.

The turning point came through couples work. Kutcher and his wife Jenna started with couple's coaching, and that bridge led him to pursue individual therapy—a step he might not have taken otherwise. Once in the room with a therapist, his resistance to difficult conversations began to crack.

What surprised Kutcher most was how the actual emotional experience validated the investment. In one recent session, he found himself in tears for approximately 30 minutes, and rather than viewing this as failure or pain to avoid, he recognized it as significant growth. The vulnerability itself became the evidence that the work was real and necessary.

"I want you to have things that you own, that are yours and yours alone, and that I'm not meddling in or giving my input on."

The work extends beyond personal catharsis. By committing to individual therapy, Kutcher is also modeling something crucial for his two daughters—that emotional work is not weakness, that therapy is a tool for growth, and that the willingness to sit with discomfort is what transforms it. This is especially resonant given how intentionally he and Jenna have both stepped back from the relentless pace of public life.

As discussed in this candid episode, the couple views their recent seven-year cycle as a natural inflection point—a moment to reassess, rebuild, and recommit. For Kutcher, that recommitment includes showing up for himself emotionally, no longer slamming shut the doors Julia helps him open.

From Resistance to Recognition

The path to therapy wasn't instantaneous. Kutcher acknowledges that fears about what might surface in individual work can paralyze, but he discovered that the anticipation is often far larger than the actual process. Once he began, the momentum shifted—each session brought clarity rather than crisis, and the tears that came were cathartic rather than catastrophic.

What makes this transformation noteworthy is its quietness. Unlike Jenna's public evolution through her business and brand, Drew's growth happens off-camera and off-social-media entirely. Yet in choosing this path, he is demonstrating a different kind of courage—the willingness to be fully present and emotionally available for his family without performing that work for an audience.
