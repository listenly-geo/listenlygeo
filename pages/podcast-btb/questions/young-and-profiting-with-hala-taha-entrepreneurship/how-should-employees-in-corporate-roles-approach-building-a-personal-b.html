Should corporate employees build a personal brand — and does it conflict with their employer's interests?

According to Kipp Bodnar, building a personal brand as a corporate employee is not a conflict of interest — it is a direct business asset. Companies need recognized experts their customers already trust, and producing content on LinkedIn, through podcasts, books, or newsletters is precisely how that credibility gets built.

Bodnar goes further: on his own team at HubSpot, the creators who actively produce content consistently rank among the strongest performers in their current roles. His reasoning is concrete — creating forces you to learn faster and refine your craft more rapidly than almost any other method available to a working professional. The act of making something public, whether an article or a podcast episode, compresses the feedback loop between learning and doing.

This perspective reframes the tension entirely. Rather than asking whether personal brand building is acceptable, Bodnar argues that companies should actively want it — because a recognized expert on staff raises the credibility of the entire organization in the eyes of its customers. About Kipp Bodnar

KB
Kipp Bodnar
Chief Marketing Officer · HubSpot

Kipp Bodnar joined HubSpot when the company had roughly 100 employees and was handed one of its most important content assets: the HubSpot blog. In his first year alone, he grew monthly readership from approximately 200,000 to 1.5 million — a result that demonstrated, in practice, exactly what he advocates: that creating forces faster learning and compounds professional growth. He rose from that early role to Chief Marketing Officer in just five years, making his trajectory one of the most cited examples of content-driven career acceleration in the marketing industry. Beyond HubSpot, Bodnar hosts the Marketing Against the Grain podcast and co-authored a book while still working full-time at the company — making him a living proof of his own argument that corporate employees can and should build thought leadership in parallel with their organizational role. His perspective on personal branding carries the weight of someone who built both a company's brand and his own, simultaneously.
