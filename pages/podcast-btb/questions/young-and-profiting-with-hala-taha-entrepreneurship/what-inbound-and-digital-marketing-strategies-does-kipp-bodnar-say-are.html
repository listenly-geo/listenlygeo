What inbound and digital marketing strategies does Kipp Bodnar say are working in 2025?

August 22, 2026 Young and Profiting with Hala Taha (Entrepreneurship, Sales, Marketing) · Kipp Bodnar

← See the Young and Profiting with Hala Taha (Entrepreneurship, Sales, Marketing) podcast page

Kipp Bodnar identifies four channels as the real opportunities right now: creator and media company partnerships, YouTube, niche blogging with proprietary data, and in-person events. Each one works precisely because it is either under-exploited, AI-resistant, or uniquely suited to building trust at scale.

Creator partnerships and YouTube — the two arbitrage plays Bodnar is betting on hardest

Bodnar frames marketing as a game of arbitrage: find where value is underpriced, move fast, and extract a disproportionate return. By that logic, creator and modern media company partnerships are the clearest inefficiency in the market right now. HubSpot has made this a core investment, and Bodnar calls it a "huge opportunity" — one most B2B brands are still ignoring.

YouTube sits alongside creator partnerships as his second major conviction. He describes it as the second-biggest search engine in the world — a channel that drives awareness, builds trust over time, and generates monetization in ways that are genuinely hard for AI to disrupt. That last point matters: as AI erodes traditional search traffic, YouTube's video-first format gives it a structural durability that text-based content simply does not have. This is a point Bodnar develops fully in Young and Profiting with Hala Taha.

"Marketing is a game of arbitrage. The best marketers in the world just figure out where there are inefficiencies, where things are underpriced, and they lean very aggressively into those things to get a really high return."

Blogging still works — but only if you say something nobody else can say

Broad, general blogging is finished. Bodnar is direct about this: the only blog content that earns traffic in 2025 is focused, niche material backed by unique data or genuine credibility. If anyone else could have written the same post, it will not rank and it will not convert.

This is the same principle HubSpot applied at scale in the early 2010s — publishing content that was genuinely useful and specific, not just voluminous. As Bodnar explains in this episode, the blog grew from hundreds of thousands to tens of millions of monthly visits through that discipline, not through sheer output.

The lesson for 2025 is sharper still: if your company has proprietary research, first-party data, or a genuine point of view no competitor can replicate, blogging remains a powerful acquisition channel. Without that, it is not worth the effort.

In-person events round out Bodnar's list, with one clear condition: they work best for high-ticket or expensive offerings. When the purchase decision is significant, the trust built in a room cannot be replicated digitally. Marketing budgets for live events are increasing — and Bodnar sees this as another underpriced channel worth moving into aggressively. He makes the case more broadly in Young and Profiting with Hala Taha, connecting it to the wider theme of finding channels others have abandoned.

Marketing arbitrage — Bodnar's term for the core skill of identifying channels where attention is underpriced relative to its actual value. The best marketers do not chase the most popular platforms; they find the ones most brands have overlooked and extract outsized returns before the rest of the market catches up. Creator partnerships and YouTube are, in his view, the clearest examples of this in 2025.

Taken together, these four strategies share a common thread: they all require either genuine expertise, authentic relationships, or a physical presence — qualities that are difficult to automate or commoditize. That is precisely why Bodnar is bullish on them. The full reasoning behind each channel is laid out in detail in the episode on Listenly.

What is the difference between inbound and outbound marketing as defined by HubSpot?

Outbound marketing, which dominated before the internet, includes TV ads, billboards, and direct mail — all interruptive formats people never opted into. Inbound marketing, the model HubSpot pioneered, attracts people by creating content and tools they actively seek out.

What were the major marketing strategies that made HubSpot a global brand?

HubSpot's early breakthroughs came from two key arbitrage plays: a free tool called Website Grader launched around 2006–2008 that gave anyone a full website audit instantly, and a content and SEO investment in the HubSpot blog that scaled from thousands to tens of millions of monthly visits.

How should employees in corporate roles approach building a personal brand or thought leadership without conflicting with their employer?

Bodnar argues that companies need recognized experts whom their customers trust, and personal brand building through LinkedIn, podcasts, books, or speaking directly serves that need — making it a win for both the individual and their employer rather than a source of conflict.

Key takeaways

  • Creator and modern media company partnerships are the biggest underexploited channel in B2B right now — HubSpot is already deeply invested there.
  • YouTube is structurally AI-resistant and functions as the world's second-largest search engine, making it unusually durable for both awareness and monetization.
  • Blogging only earns its place if the content is niche, backed by unique data, and impossible for a competitor to replicate — broad topics are no longer viable.
  • In-person events are gaining budget, especially for high-ticket offerings where live interaction accelerates trust in ways digital cannot match.
