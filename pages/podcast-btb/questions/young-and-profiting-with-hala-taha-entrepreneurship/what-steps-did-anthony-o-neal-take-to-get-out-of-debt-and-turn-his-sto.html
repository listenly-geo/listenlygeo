How did Anthony O'Neal turn $35,000 in debt into a career in financial coaching?

September 2, 2026 Young and Profiting with Hala Taha (Entrepreneurship, Sales, Marketing) · Anthony O'Neal

← See the Young and Profiting with Hala Taha (Entrepreneurship, Sales, Marketing) fiche

Anthony O'Neal educated himself by reading Rich Dad Poor Dad and Total Money Makeover, then committed to following Dave Ramsey's seven baby steps to eliminate his debt. He began teaching financial literacy as a youth pastor in high schools, covering scholarships, credit cards, and credit reports—work that eventually caught Ramsey Solutions' attention and launched his professional coaching career.

Education and the foundation of change

The turning point came through two foundational books: Robert Kiyosaki's Rich Dad Poor Dad and Dave Ramsey's Total Money Makeover. These weren't casual reads—they represented a complete reorientation of how O'Neal understood money, debt, and personal responsibility.

With $35,000 in debt (approximately $32,000 of it in collections), O'Neal made a deliberate choice to follow Ramsey's structured seven baby steps framework. This wasn't about quick fixes or one-off solutions; it was a systematic approach to rebuilding from the ground up. A point detailed in this podcast episode, the book education gave him both the theory and the psychological permission to believe change was possible.

Teaching as the launch pad

Once he'd begun stabilizing his own finances, O'Neal made a bold move: he started teaching financial literacy to high school students as a youth pastor. Rather than waiting until he was "perfectly recovered," he used his lived experience as credibility.

His classroom wasn't abstract economics—it was practical, street-level finance. He taught students about scholarships, credit cards, and credit reports, the exact tools most young people never learn about until they're drowning in debt themselves. As Anthony O'Neal explains in the episode, this hands-on teaching built one of the largest African-American youth ministries in the world, drawing 3,000 to 4,000 attendees at conferences.

The attention of Ramsey Solutions

O'Neal's consistent, authentic work in high schools didn't go unnoticed. Ramsey Solutions identified him as a rare talent—someone who could speak credibly to young people about money because he'd lived the consequences of poor financial decisions.

He went on to work at Ramsey Solutions for approximately six years as the youth financial literacy speaker, eventually becoming a number one national bestselling author and founding The Table, his own podcast and media brand. What makes this trajectory remarkable is that it all began with self-education and showing up to teach, without needing permission or a credential first.

"I want freedom more than I want to be impressive. Debt robs us from freedom. The moment that you borrow money from somebody, you are now enslaved to them."

How does Anthony O'Neal define financial freedom and what does it look like in his daily life?

Anthony O'Neal defines freedom as being able to make decisions based on his own habits rather than being enslaved to debt. Concretely, this means taking ownership of your financial choices without external constraints.

What is Anthony O'Neal's philosophy on credit cards and how does he use them today?

Anthony O'Neal believes roughly 90% of everyday people are not responsible enough to have a credit card, and recommends being out of debt for a minimum of 24 months before using one responsibly.

What role did media and culture play in Anthony O'Neal's financial mistakes?

Anthony O'Neal says that BET, MTV Cribs, and music videos taught him finance—not his parents or school. These cultural influences showed him that success meant looking wealthy, not building actual wealth.

Key takeaways

  • Self-education through books like Rich Dad Poor Dad and Total Money Makeover was the foundation for O'Neal's mindset shift around money.
  • Following a structured debt-elimination framework (Ramsey's seven baby steps) turned abstract goals into manageable action steps.
  • Teaching high school students about financial literacy gave O'Neal purpose and built credibility that eventually attracted Ramsey Solutions.
  • Success came not from waiting to be "perfect," but from showing up consistently to share hard-won knowledge with others facing similar struggles.
