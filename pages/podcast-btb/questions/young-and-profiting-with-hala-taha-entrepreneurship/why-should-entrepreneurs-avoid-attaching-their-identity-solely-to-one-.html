Why should entrepreneurs avoid attaching their identity solely to one professional mechanism or platform?

September 7, 2026 Young and Profiting with Hala Taha (Entrepreneurship, Sales, Marketing) · Lewis Howes

← See the Young and Profiting with Hala Taha (Entrepreneurship, Sales, Marketing) fiche

If you attach your professional identity to a single mechanism—whether podcasting, authorship, or any platform—the collapse of that mechanism threatens your entire ability to serve your mission. Instead, start with your core mission, then identify multiple channels (podcasts, books, video, AI, live events) to reach your audience and accomplish your goals through diverse routes.

Lewis Howes refuses to label himself as merely a podcaster, author, or any single professional title. This deliberate choice protects both his identity and his impact. When one mechanism disappears or loses relevance, his mission remains intact because it was never dependent on that single pathway.

The reasoning is practical and urgent: your platform or medium can vanish at any moment—algorithm changes, market shifts, technology disruption, or platform collapse can all strip away the tool you've built your career around. If your entire identity and income depend on that one mechanism, so does your ability to serve the people you're trying to help.

Mission First, Mechanisms Second

Howes' approach reverses the typical entrepreneurial priority. Rather than asking "What platform should I build on?" he begins with: "What is my mission?" and "What unique gifts and talents do I have?" Only after clarifying those does he identify the multiple mechanisms available to accomplish that mission.

This framework has shaped his entire career. His mission is to reach 100 million lives weekly in pursuit of personal development and greatness. To get there, he doesn't rely on The School of Greatness podcast alone. He leverages podcasting, yes—over 1,400 episodes and nearly a billion downloads in 10 years—but also books, over 1,000 videos on YouTube, live events, AI tools, and direct communication channels. Each mechanism carries his message, but no single one is his identity.

As explained in this episode of Young and Profiting, the specific messages may differ across platforms, but they all serve the same mission. A podcast episode reaches millions of listeners; a book reaches a different audience over years; video captures visual learners; live events create deep personal connection. The mission stays constant; the delivery adapts.

"What's the unique talents and gifts we have? Figure those out over our journey. Try to help as many people as possible on the pursuit of our dreams and goals."

The episode also discusses how Howes invested 5 years building his YouTube channel without monetization before turning on ads—a decision rooted in mission, not platform mechanics. Listen to the full conversation to hear how he navigated those early years of building multiple channels simultaneously.

Key takeaways

  • Attach your identity to your mission, not to any single platform or professional label.
  • Identify multiple mechanisms (podcasting, video, books, events, AI, email) that can all serve the same core mission.
  • If one mechanism fails or disappears, your mission and identity remain intact because they were never dependent on it.
  • Different channels reach different audiences with slightly different messaging, but all funnel toward the same ultimate goal.
