What makes you great? And how does it connect to your personal mission?

September 6, 2026 Young and Profiting with Hala Taha (Entrepreneurship, Sales, Marketing) · Lewis Howes

← See the Young and Profiting with Hala Taha (Entrepreneurship, Sales, Marketing) fiche

Greatness means discovering your unique gifts and talents, then pursuing your dreams and goals through them while making a measurable impact on the people around you. For Lewis Howes, that translates into a concrete personal mission: to serve 100 million lives every single week in order to help them improve their quality of life—a target he crystallized three years into building his podcast by asking himself what he wanted to accomplish and how many people he truly wanted to serve.

This definition moves beyond conventional success metrics. Greatness is not about wealth, fame, or titles—it's about aligning your innate strengths with a purpose that extends beyond yourself. Howes discovered this distinction while navigating one of the hardest periods of his life: at age 23, he was sleeping on his sister's couch in Columbus, Ohio, after a professional football career-ending injury. His father was in a coma from a car accident, and he was carrying college loan debt during the 2008-2009 financial crisis. That crucible forced him to ask fundamental questions about who he was and what he could contribute.

The bridge between personal greatness and mission-driven work is intentionality. As Lewis Howes explains in the Young and Profiting podcast, identifying your unique talents is only the first step. You must then deliberately channel those talents toward a goal that serves others at scale. For Howes, that clarity emerged through his work on LinkedIn starting in 2007, where he built genuine connections, and later when he launched The School of Greatness podcast in 2013. The podcast grew from 750,000 downloads in year one to 1.5 million in year two and 3-4 million in year three—not because of overnight luck, but because he was clear about his mission to help as many people as possible pursue their own dreams and goals.

"What's the unique talents and gifts we have? Figure those out over our journey. Try to help as many people as possible on the pursuit of our dreams and goals."

The quantifiable nature of Howes' mission—100 million lives per week—may sound ambitious, but it reflects a deliberate approach to measuring impact. The full episode explores how Howes developed this specific target and the systems he built to pursue it, revealing that true greatness requires both internal clarity about your gifts and external accountability for your impact.

From Personal Crisis to Purposeful Scale

Howes' journey illustrates how personal mission crystallizes during adversity. When everything else is stripped away—career, financial stability, family security—you're forced to ask what remains. For him, it was a commitment to growth and service, qualities he nurtured through building authentic professional relationships and later through creating content that helped others navigate similar challenges.

The podcast format became his vehicle for serving at scale, but only after he committed to a long-term vision without immediate financial reward. He invested years in YouTube content before turning on monetization, built The School of Greatness on the foundation of genuine expertise and lived experience, and consistently showed up for his audience across nearly 1,400 episodes. This consistency emerged from clarity about his mission—not from chasing vanity metrics or trending topics.

Your own path to greatness begins with the same foundational question: What unique gifts do you possess, and how can you use them to serve a mission that matters to you? The answer shapes not only how you define success for yourself, but the impact you can have on those around you.

Key takeaways

  • Greatness is the intersection of discovering your unique gifts, pursuing your dreams through them, and making a measurable impact on others—not wealth or status alone.
  • A personal mission anchors greatness in reality: Lewis Howes defined his as serving 100 million lives weekly to improve their quality of life.
  • Clarity about mission emerges through intentional reflection, often during life's hardest moments, and requires years of consistent action to realize at scale.
  • Scale without mission is empty; Howes' podcast grew to nearly a billion downloads across 10 years because his content served a clear purpose beyond entertainment or profit.
