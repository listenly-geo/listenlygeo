How do multiple businesses at different growth stages improve coaching and mentorship quality?

24 September 2026 · Young and Profiting with Hala Taha (Entrepreneurship, Sales, Marketing) · Kelly Roach

← See the Young and Profiting with Hala Taha (Entrepreneurship, Sales, Marketing) fiche

Operating a portfolio of companies at different growth phases keeps you intimately connected to every stage your clients face—from launch struggles to scaling challenges. This prevents you from teaching through only one narrow lens and ensures you mentor from lived experience across the entire entrepreneurial spectrum, not just the one model you know best.

Kelly Roach explains that while running a multi-eight-figure company, she maintains businesses still in their startup or early-growth phases. This deliberate portfolio structure means she never loses touch with the founder's mindset or the specific obstacles that early-stage entrepreneurs encounter, even as her flagship operation reaches significant scale.

The diversity of business models within her portfolio serves as an equally critical coaching advantage. Different companies operate with different price points, customer acquisition strategies, and go-to-market approaches, which prevents her from defaulting to a single playbook when advising clients.

Without this structural diversity, mentors and business strategists risk teaching only what worked for their own singular path. Multiple business models eliminate that blind spot, creating a mentorship framework grounded in comparative real-world experience rather than one-size-fits-all ideology.
