Why did Tiffany Carter only land 2 clients after pitching 50 companies when she launched her digital marketing agency?

When Tiffany Carter launched her digital marketing business approximately 18 years ago, she pitched 50 companies and walked away with just 2 clients. She attributes this failure directly to being visibly "thirsty" — over-talking, over-giving, and radiating excessive eagerness — because she did not genuinely believe she was enough on her own. That behavior signaled desperation to prospects, which caused them to question whether they should look elsewhere.

The root of that desperation, Carter explains, was not a lack of skills — she had already been the number one salesperson and sales trainer in her corporate jobs. The problem was psychological. Growing up in an abusive environment from ages 11 to 21 had conditioned her to have no sense of personal boundaries. That conditioning carried over directly into her pitches: she gave too much, said too much, and tried too hard, all because she hadn't yet developed genuine self-worth independent of external validation.

What "being thirsty" means in a sales context

In Carter's framing, being "thirsty" describes a state of visible desperation during a pitch — over-explaining your value, over-delivering before any contract exists, and making it obvious that you need the client more than you serve them. This psychological posture subconsciously signals to prospects that something may be wrong, prompting them to hesitate or walk away.

The dynamic Carter describes is a direct psychological loop: low self-worth produces desperate behavior, desperate behavior reads as a red flag to buyers, and buyers self-protect by looking at other options. None of the 48 companies that declined were responding to her offer — they were responding to her energy. About Tiffany Carter

TC
Tiffany Carter
Entrepreneur & Business Coach

Tiffany Carter became a millionaire by age 30 — not through a windfall, but through disciplined corporate sales and savings built primarily in the pharmaceutical industry, where she rose to become the number one salesperson and sales trainer at her company. Before that, she worked as a TV newscaster, giving her an unusually broad foundation in communication and persuasion. After repeatedly hitting a glass ceiling in corporate, she left to launch her own digital marketing business approximately 18 years ago — the same venture where she pitched 50 companies and landed only 2 clients.

Within approximately three months of reaching seven figures, Carter lost nearly her entire fortune after being defrauded by a financial advisor who funneled her money into fake gold mines. That loss forced a reckoning with the deeper psychological patterns she had carried since childhood: she was exposed to abuse from ages 11 to 21 and has since been diagnosed with complex PTSD. She has undergone outpatient trauma healing work and now speaks with authority on how unaddressed childhood wounds — people-pleasing, scarcity mindset, absent boundaries, and fractured self-worth — quietly sabotage entrepreneurial performance and financial stability. Her lived experience on both sides of that equation is what makes her voice on sales psychology and mindset genuinely credible.

What is the psychological reason why people lose wealth shortly after acquiring it?

According to Tiffany Carter, people lose wealth because their internal identity and self-worth do not match the level of success they have achieved. When who you believe you are on the inside doesn't align with your financial reality on the outside, the mind finds ways to return to what feels familiar.
