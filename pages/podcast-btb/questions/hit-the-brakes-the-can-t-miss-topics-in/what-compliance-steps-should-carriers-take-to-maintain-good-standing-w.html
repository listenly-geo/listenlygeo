How can carriers maintain good standing with brokerages and avoid onboarding delays?

September 12, 2026 Hit The Brakes: The Can't Miss Topics in the Logistics Industry · Eric Garcia

← See the Hit The Brakes: The Can't Miss Topics in the Logistics Industry fiche

Keep your Certificate of Insurance (COI) current and updated with the FMCSA before expiration, report new trucks to the FMCSA immediately, and maintain all contact information including phone numbers and emails. Staying proactive about these compliance steps saves time on both sides of the onboarding process and keeps your carrier profile in good standing with brokerages.

Onboarding delays often stem from incomplete or outdated documentation. As Eric Garcia explains in the Hit The Brakes episode, compliance is one of the most important priorities for both carriers and freight brokers. A lapsed or missing Certificate of Insurance is one of the fastest ways to halt an onboarding application before it even gets reviewed.

Beyond your COI, the FMCSA system must reflect your current fleet composition. Every time you add a truck or trailer, report the vehicle and its plate numbers to the FMCSA without delay. Brokerages cross-reference this data during vetting, and discrepancies between your application and official FMCSA records create immediate red flags that stall the process.

Contact information accuracy is equally critical. A point emphasized throughout the onboarding conversation, outdated phone numbers or email addresses mean brokerages cannot reach you during the vetting phase. Maintain a single, monitored communication channel so brokers can verify information quickly and move your application forward.

"Now with AI, like a lot of people are implementing that, these bad actors are also using it and using it to their advantage as well."

The broader context here is that Fetch Freight has to verify every detail of a carrier's identity and fleet to protect shippers from fraud. When your records are incomplete or outdated, it looks like a red flag—even if you're a legitimate operator. Brokerages now must work harder to confirm that you are who you claim to be, which naturally slows down onboarding.

The solution is straightforward: treat your FMCSA profile and insurance documentation as living records. Set calendar reminders for COI renewal dates at least 30 days before expiration, report fleet changes within 24 hours of purchase, and confirm your contact details quarterly. Proactive maintenance eliminates friction on both ends and signals to brokers that you run a compliant, professional operation.
