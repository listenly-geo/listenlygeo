What operational steps should a logistics team take to anticipate and stay ahead of weather-related supply chain disruptions?

September 2, 2026 Hit The Brakes: The Can't Miss Topics in the Logistics Industry · Garrison Till

← See the Hit The Brakes: The Can't Miss Topics in the Logistics Industry fiche

Stay ahead of weather disruptions by building a three-part operational system: constantly monitor weather reports and track your active shipments, coordinate backup plans with your carrier network, and communicate those plans to customers before disruptions strike. Once all details are in place, your team brings solutions to customers so they can make informed choices about routing and timing—turning uncertainty into managed risk.

The Three-Step Monitoring System

Your logistics team must treat weather forecasting as an ongoing operational duty, not a reactive response. This means assigning responsibility for real-time weather monitoring across your office and tracking individual shipments against emerging weather patterns simultaneously.

As Garrison Till explains in the episode, the team constantly monitors weather reports and tracks shipments together—creating a shared awareness that makes the rest of the workflow possible. The goal is to spot potential conflicts between weather and your freight movement days in advance, not hours before a road closure.

Coordination with Carriers and Backup Planning

Once weather risks are identified, your team must immediately engage with your carrier partners to develop viable alternative routes and timing scenarios. This is not a suggestion passed to the customer later; it's an internal exercise completed before any customer notification.

Garrison Till emphasizes that proactive communication with carriers—sharing the intelligence your team has gathered—allows you and your partners to develop real solutions, not just contingencies. The backup plan must be specific and tested, not theoretical.

"Proactive communication just gives our customers time to adjust their plans and reinforces that we're actively managing their freight every step of the way."

The real value of this coordination emerges when you present options. A detailed discussion of how Fetch Freight handles specific carrier scenarios during hurricane season reveals that the best outcome is always one where your customer, armed with clear information and concrete alternatives, chooses the solution that works best for their own constraints.

Why is advance planning critical for maintaining freight movement during severe weather and hurricane season?

Planning ahead allows shippers to secure capacity and adjust routes as needed before severe weather disrupts operations. A strong backup plan helps minimize delays, control costs, and keep freight moving safely even when conditions are unpredictable.

Key takeaways

  • Assign dedicated responsibility for real-time weather monitoring and active shipment tracking—treat it as an ongoing operational system, not a reactive response.
  • Develop backup plans with carriers before disruptions occur, so you have concrete alternatives ready to present to customers.
  • Communicate proactively and clearly with both customers and carriers, so everyone can adjust plans with full information and time to act.
  • Position yourself as a solutions provider by offering customers clear routing and timing options, letting them choose what works best for their business.
