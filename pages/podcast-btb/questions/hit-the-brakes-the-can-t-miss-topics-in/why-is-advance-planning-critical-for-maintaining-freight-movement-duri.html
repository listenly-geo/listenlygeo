Why is advance planning critical for maintaining freight movement during severe weather and hurricane season?

September 1, 2026 Hit The Brakes: The Can't Miss Topics in the Logistics Industry · Garrison Till

← See the Hit The Brakes: The Can't Miss Topics in the Logistics Industry fiche

Planning ahead allows shippers to secure carrier capacity and adjust routes before severe weather disrupts operations. A strong backup plan minimizes delays, controls costs, and keeps freight moving safely even when conditions are at their most unpredictable.

Securing Capacity Before the Storm Hits

The critical window for freight planning is before weather impacts operations. Advance communication with carriers locks in available capacity and prevents the scramble that happens once roads flood or ports close. This proactive approach gives your logistics team time to identify alternative routing options and negotiate terms under normal conditions rather than under emergency pressure.

As Garrison Till explains in the episode, teams at Fetch Freight constantly monitor weather reports and track shipments across their network to identify disruptions before they cascade through customer orders. This visibility—built through continuous planning—is what separates reactive scrambling from controlled contingency execution.

The Hidden Work Behind the Scenes

Most of the value happens before customers ever see a delay or route change. Behind-the-scenes coordination with carriers, real-time shipment tracking, and documented backup plans mean solutions arrive as offers, not as apologies.

The reason this matters: shippers who plan ahead can present their customers with multiple viable options and remain in control of the narrative. Those who wait until weather hits are forced to react, typically with higher costs, limited carrier availability, and damaged customer trust. A detailed look at how logistics teams prepare for hurricane season shows that the difference between minimal disruption and major delays often comes down to whether planning happened in July or September.

"Proactive communication just gives our customers time to adjust their plans and reinforces that we're actively managing their freight every step of the way."

Key takeaways

  • Secure capacity and agree on backup routes before severe weather creates bottlenecks and carrier scarcity.
  • Continuous monitoring of weather patterns and shipment tracking allows logistics teams to surface solutions before delays materialize.
  • Customers with advance planning receive multiple routing options and maintain control over their freight, rather than facing forced delays.
  • Proactive communication reinforces trust and demonstrates active freight management even under unpredictable conditions.
