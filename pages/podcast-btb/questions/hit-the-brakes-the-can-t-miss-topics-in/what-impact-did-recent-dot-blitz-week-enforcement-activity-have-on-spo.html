How DOT Blitz Week enforcement reshaped spot market rates and shipper decisions

September 20, 2026 Hit The Brakes: The Can't Miss Topics in the Logistics Industry · Brett Leifert

← See the Hit The Brakes podcast fiche

DOT Blitz Week enforcement triggered what was described as an absolute bloodbath in spot market pricing, with rates doubling and tripling on certain lanes. The impact was severe enough that shippers questioned whether to move freight that week at all, and the rate spike persisted into subsequent weeks.

The enforcement action exposed the fragility of market conditions during a period already characterized by tightening capacity. As Brett Leifert discusses in the episode, the sudden removal of capacity from the market—whether through regulatory enforcement or carrier exits—directly translates to acute pricing volatility.

When regulation meets tight capacity: the Blitz Week effect

DOT Blitz Week represented a concentrated enforcement period that collided with an already capacity-constrained market. The timing amplified the impact on shippers, forcing immediate decisions about whether to ship product during that specific window or defer freight movement entirely.

The scale of the rate shock—doubling and tripling on affected lanes—was substantial enough that it reshaped shipper behavior for weeks afterward. This wasn't a minor price adjustment; it was a market disruption significant enough to change operational planning. Shippers had to recalibrate their understanding of what spot market rates could reach under enforcement pressure.

A deeper context underlies this volatility: tens of thousands of non-domicile CDL drivers exited the market following FMCSA enforcement actions, and roughly 2,000 Mexican drivers lost visa privileges due to cabotage enforcement. These cascading capacity reductions created an environment where even a focused enforcement week could produce outsized rate spikes. Additionally, north of 50 CDL schools closed in the southwest, Texas, and California regions, further constraining the pipeline of new driver capacity entering the market.

"This is truly a supply driven recovery. Demands have some green shoots, but it's really more about the exit of capacity."

The broader context reveals why DOT Blitz Week hit so hard: the market was already experiencing its fastest climb in spot market rates in recorded history, even outpacing the rate shock of early COVID. Spot rates stood 30–40% higher than the prior year when including fuel, and 20–30% higher excluding fuel. Into this environment, the enforcement action created a perfect storm for shipper disruption.
