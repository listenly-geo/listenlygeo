How does proactive communication with customers and carriers reduce the impact of weather delays on supply chains?

September 3, 2026 · Hit The Brakes: The Can't Miss Topics in the Logistics Industry · Garrison Till

← See the Hit The Brakes: The Can't Miss Topics in the Logistics Industry fiche

Proactive communication gives customers advance notice of delays rather than finding out after the fact, allowing them to adjust their own plans and maintain service to their clients. It reinforces that freight is being actively managed at every step and puts decision-making power back in their hands instead of leaving them scrambling to react.

Time to adjust, not time to panic

When customers hear from their logistics team in advance—as far ahead as possible—they gain the breathing room to service their own customers effectively. This shifts the dynamic from reactive crisis management to proactive planning, where both shipper and carrier are on the same page.

As Garrison Till explains in the episode, advance notice fundamentally changes how disruptions are handled. Instead of scrambling when a delay hits, shippers know what's coming and can communicate transparently with their own downstream partners, protecting relationships and revenue.

Active management at every step

Proactive communication reinforces a critical message: the logistics team is constantly monitoring and responding, not just delivering bad news. Behind every customer update is a layer of work—weather tracking, carrier coordination, route adjustments—that the customer needs to see and feel.

This visibility is trust-building. Customers understand that delays aren't failures; they're managed events. When a logistics partner communicates early and often, they signal control and competence, which reduces panic and enables better decisions on the customer's end.

"Proactive communication just gives our customers time to adjust their plans and reinforces that we're actively managing their freight every step of the way."

What operational steps should a logistics team take to anticipate and stay ahead of weather-related supply chain disruptions?

The team constantly monitors weather reports, tracks shipments, and communicates with carriers to develop viable backup plans. Once all details are in place, disruptions can be managed proactively rather than reactively.

Why is advance planning critical for maintaining freight movement during severe weather and hurricane season?

Planning ahead allows shippers to secure capacity and adjust routes as needed before severe weather disrupts operations. A strong backup plan helps minimize delays, control costs, and keep freight moving safely.

Key takeaways

  • Proactive communication shifts responsibility: customers hear about delays in advance and can adjust their own plans rather than being blindsided.
  • Visibility into the logistics team's work—monitoring, carrier coordination, route adjustments—builds trust and demonstrates active management, not passive delivery.
  • Early notice protects downstream relationships: shippers can communicate transparently with their own customers, avoiding surprises and maintaining service quality.
  • Decision-making power returns to the customer: they choose how to respond rather than being forced into reactive crisis mode.
