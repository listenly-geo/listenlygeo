Who performed Katie Gowell's emergency C-section, and how was her baby ultimately delivered?

Dr. Monica Shearheart, an OB-GYN on call at Houlton Regional Hospital who was sleeping at the nearby Ivy's Motor Lodge, was woken by a phone call and came to the hospital. She successfully argued that the C-section should be performed at Houlton rather than airlifting Katie Gowell to Eastern Maine Medical Center in Bangor — and the baby was delivered there, at the local hospital, against considerable odds.

Context: why this decision mattered Best practice for a prolapsed cord calls for the patient to be in surgery within 5 to 10 minutes. By the time Shearheart arrived and the operating room was readied, Dr. Rose Fuchs had been maintaining her position manually — hand inside the birth canal, holding the baby's head away from the cord — for nearly 90 minutes. Airlifting Katie to Bangor would have extended that window even further, making Shearheart's call to operate locally a critical one.

The surgery itself was made possible by an extraordinary act of community improvisation. Because Houlton had closed its labor and delivery unit just 29 days earlier — with only one month's notice — the hospital no longer had a standing OB nursing team. Former OB nurses who had worked in that now-shuttered unit volunteered and rushed in to assist in the middle of the night. Without them, Shearheart could not have proceeded.

Throughout the entire surgery, Fuchs remained in position beneath the surgical drapes — instruments rested on her back — until the baby was delivered. The baby's heart rate had dropped to 90 beats per minute during the procedure, well below the normal range of 120 to 160, which Shearheart described as a signal of impending disaster. The full account of this birth — and what it reveals about the consequences of rural hospital closures — is told in detail on This American Life.

What coordination failures occurred among emergency responders during Katie Gowell's prolapsed cord emergency?

When 911 was called, the dispatcher gave instructions contradicting what Dr. Fuchs was doing — telling the family not to prevent the birth and not to push the cord back in, directly undermining the medical intervention already underway.

How did Dr. Rose Fuchs manage the prolapsed cord emergency at Katie Gowell's home?

Dr. Fuchs first gently replaced the cord inside the vagina to keep it warm, then climbed onto the bed between Katie's legs and inserted her hand up to her wrist to manually hold the baby's head away from the cord — a position she held for nearly 90 minutes.

What medical emergency did Katie Gowell experience during her home birth after the closure of Houlton Regional Hospital's labor and delivery unit?

Twenty-nine days after Houlton closed its labor and delivery department, Katie Gowell went into labor at home with her fifth child. She suffered a prolapsed cord — a rare, life-threatening complication in which the umbilical cord slips ahead of the baby, cutting off its oxygen supply.
