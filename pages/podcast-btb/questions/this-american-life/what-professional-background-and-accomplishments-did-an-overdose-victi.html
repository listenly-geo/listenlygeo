What professional background and accomplishments did she have before addiction took over?

September 21, 2026 This American Life

← See the This American Life fiche

Sasha was a high school valedictorian who studied psychology at Yale and went on to earn two master's degrees in social work and public health, becoming a clinical social worker. She was known for her deep compassion—the kind of person who remembered homeless people by name and stopped to help strangers in genuine need.

The contrast between Sasha's early promise and her later struggle reveals how addiction cuts across all backgrounds. Her credentials and education placed her among the most accomplished professionals in her field, yet none of that protected her. As the episode explores, her sister Nadia would spend years trying to understand how someone with such a strong foundation could become trapped by drugs.

Sasha's compassion for vulnerable populations—a trait that likely drew her to social work in the first place—defined how others remembered her long after her death. Her bleeding heart wasn't simply a personality quirk; it was the core of who she was professionally and personally. Yet addiction operates indiscriminately, and by the time she was 44 years old on July 31, 2015, her life ended from a heroin and fentanyl overdose.

The tragedy is compounded by the gap between her achievements and her fate. Someone who had earned respect as a clinical professional, who had studied the human mind and the roots of social suffering, found herself unable to escape the chemical grip of opioids. As documented in this podcast, her younger sister Nadia would later reconstruct Sasha's final hours through phone records and messages, searching for answers that professional training and education had failed to prevent.

What makes Sasha's story resonate is not her credentials alone, but the fuller picture of her humanity revealed through her sister's investigation—the person who gave everything to help others, yet couldn't help herself.
