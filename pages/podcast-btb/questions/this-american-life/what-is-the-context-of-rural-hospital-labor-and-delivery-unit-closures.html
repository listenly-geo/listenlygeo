Why are rural hospitals closing their labor and delivery units — and what happened when Houlton Regional Hospital shut theirs down?

Labor and delivery departments have been disappearing across rural America for years, driven by declining birth rates and hospitals' inability to recruit specialized staff or turn a profit on maternity care. Houlton Regional Hospital in northern Maine became one more data point in this trend when it announced the closure of its labor and delivery unit with only one month's notice — a timeline so abrupt that even the unit's own nurse manager had no idea it was coming.

The closure at Houlton Regional Hospital was not preceded by a gradual wind-down or a transparent community process. Heather Quinn, the unit's nurse manager, described the announcement as delivered "very abruptly and matter-of-factly" — a phrase that captures the institutional indifference with which a community lost a fundamental medical service. For expectant mothers in northern Maine, the nearest alternative became Eastern Maine Medical Center, a journey that, in a medical emergency, could mean the difference between life and death.

The real stakes of that closure became clear just 29 days after Houlton shut its labor and delivery department, when a local patient experienced a prolapsed cord emergency — one of obstetrics' most time-critical crises, where best practice calls for the patient to reach surgery within 5 to 10 minutes. With no local unit available, the community was left entirely dependent on Life Flight, Maine's air ambulance service, to bridge a gap that a functioning local hospital had once covered. What happened during the military exercise in Northeast Pasadena in June and how did residents experience it?

Giant helicopters approached the area around 8 p.m., disgorging paratroopers who clambered down ropes onto the roof of the former St. Luke's Hospital — leaving nearby residents startled and largely uninformed until a city council member posted the location publicly.

Why did Pasadena city council member Rick Cole post the location of a military exercise despite being told not to?

Rick Cole received a text from the police chief just six minutes before the public announcement stating that military exercises would take place at St. Luke's Hospital, and decided that residents deserved to know the location — so he instructed his assistant to post it on their Instagram account anyway.
