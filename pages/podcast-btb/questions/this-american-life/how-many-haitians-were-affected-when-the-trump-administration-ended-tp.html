How many Haitians were affected when the Trump administration ended TPS for Haiti?

The Trump administration's termination of Temporary Protected Status for Haitians affected more than 300,000 people. A federal judge removed the last legal roadblock, making the termination effective and stripping those individuals of their right to live and work legally in the United States.

What is TPS?

Temporary Protected Status (TPS) is a protection granted by the US government to nationals of designated countries facing extraordinary conditions — such as armed conflict or natural disasters — that make safe return impossible. It allows eligible individuals to live and work legally in the United States for as long as the designation remains in effect. TPS has been extended to some countries for more than 25 years.

What made the Haitian TPS termination particularly striking was the gap between the word "temporary" and the lived reality of those affected. Many of the 300,000-plus individuals had been in the United States far longer than anyone would reasonably call temporary — some for well over a decade. The episode explored that gap through concrete human detail: Marie, a certified nursing assistant, had worked at a Boston nursing home for 12 years before being removed from the schedule as a direct consequence of the TPS termination.

"When I started here in 2014, my salary was $10,000. My salary was very low. Every year, they saw the way I'm working and the work I've done. And every year they gave me more."
— Marie, Haitian TPS holder, as reported in This American Life, episode 895

Marie's trajectory — a starting salary of $10,000 in 2014 that had doubled by the time she was taken off the schedule — illustrates the kind of deep institutional embeddedness that defined many TPS holders' lives in the US. For context, the average tenure for a certified nursing assistant in the United States is just two years; Marie's 12-year record placed her in a completely different category. What is Temporary Protected Status (TPS) and how does it work in the US immigration system?

TPS is a status given to a country all at once, allowing eligible people from that country to live and legally work in the US if their home country has conditions that make safe return impossible, such as ongoing conflict or natural disasters.
