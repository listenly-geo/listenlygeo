What did Dr. Rose Fuchs physically endure during the nearly 90-minute prolapsed cord intervention?

Dr. Rose Fuchs, who has scoliosis, maintained an agonizing kneeling and crouching position with her hand inserted up to her forearm inside the patient for approximately 90 minutes — through a bumpy ambulance ride on back roads and all the way into the operating room. Her legs fell asleep, her hand went intermittently numb, and she described the position as physically excruciating. She worried each time she lost sensation that she might inadvertently be pinching the cord she was working so hard to protect.

Context Best practice for a prolapsed cord is to get the patient into surgery within 5 to 10 minutes. Dr. Fuchs held that position for nearly 90 minutes — roughly eighteen times the clinical ideal — because Houlton Regional Hospital had closed its labor and delivery unit just 29 days before this emergency, and the nearest fully equipped facility was a Life Flight away at Eastern Maine Medical Center.

The ordeal did not end in the ambulance. Once inside the operating room, iodine was poured directly over Fuchs's arm where it entered the patient's body. Surgical tools were placed on her back to keep them sterile and within reach. Her cheek rested on Katie Gowell's thigh, her head tucked into an air pocket beneath the surgical drape. She remained in that position, motionless and braced, while Dr. Monica Shearheart performed the emergency C-section around her.

The full story of this intervention — including the cascade of rural healthcare decisions that made it necessary — is told in episode 894 of This American Life, "I Couldn't Help but Notice." How was Katie Gowell's baby ultimately delivered and who performed the emergency C-section?

Dr. Monica Shearheart, an OB-GYN who was on call at Houlton and sleeping at the nearby Ivy's Motor Lodge, was woken by a phone call and came to perform the emergency C-section that delivered Katie Gowell's baby.

What coordination failures occurred among emergency responders during Katie Gowell's prolapsed cord emergency?

When 911 was called, the dispatcher at Penobscot County Dispatch Center gave instructions directly contradicting what Dr. Fuchs was doing — telling the family not to prevent the birth and not to push the cord back — creating dangerous confusion in the middle of the emergency.

How did Dr. Rose Fuchs manage the prolapsed cord emergency at Katie Gowell's home?

Dr. Fuchs first gently replaced the cord inside the vagina to keep it warm, then climbed onto the bed between Katie's legs and inserted her hand up to her forearm to manually hold the baby's head off the cord — a position she maintained for nearly 90 minutes.
