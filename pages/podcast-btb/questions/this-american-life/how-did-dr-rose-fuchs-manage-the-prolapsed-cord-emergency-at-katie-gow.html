How did Dr. Rose Fuchs manage the prolapsed cord emergency at Katie Gowell's home birth?

When Dr. Rose Fuchs arrived at Katie Gowell's home and found a prolapsed umbilical cord, she first gently replaced the cord inside the vagina to keep it warm, then climbed onto the bed, inserted her hand — and then her entire forearm up to the wrist — inside the patient, and pushed the baby's head back up the birth canal. She held this position continuously for nearly 90 minutes: through the ambulance ride, through the hospital doors, and directly into the operating room at Eastern Maine Medical Center.

What is a prolapsed cord? A prolapsed umbilical cord occurs when the cord slips out ahead of the baby during labor. Every contraction risks compressing it between the baby's head and the birth canal wall, cutting off the oxygen supply. Best practice calls for the patient to be in surgery within 5 to 10 minutes. In Katie Gowell's case, that window was never an option.

The stakes could not have been higher. Gowell's emergency happened just 29 days after Houlton Regional Hospital closed its labor and delivery unit — with only one month's notice — leaving the area without local obstetric care. There was no nearby operating room to rush to. Dr. Fuchs had no choice but to become a human barrier between the baby and catastrophe, monitoring the fetal heartbeat through her fingers and pushing harder whenever she felt the rate drop. The normal fetal heart rate is 120 to 160 beats per minute; during surgery, it dropped to 90 — a level Dr. Shearheart described as a signal of impending disaster.

Penobscot County Dispatch Center coordinated the response, and Life Flight, Maine's air ambulance service, was part of the emergency chain that ultimately brought Gowell to Eastern Maine Medical Center. Throughout the 90-minute ordeal, Dr. Fuchs never once withdrew her hand. What medical emergency did Katie Gowell experience during her home birth after the closure of Houlton Regional Hospital's labor and delivery unit?

Twenty-nine days after Houlton closed its labor and delivery department, Katie Gowell went into labor at home with her fifth child.
