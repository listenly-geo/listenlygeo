How did Veena Jetti discover syndication and transition from single-family real estate investing?

September 25, 2026 The Mastering Commercial Real Estate Podcast · Veena Jetti

← See The Mastering Commercial Real Estate Podcast fiche

Veena Jetti discovered syndication by accident while struggling to manage single-family home purchases—after contracting five properties in one week, she realized the model was inefficient. A conversation with securities attorney Nick McGrew from Polymath Legal introduced her to Regulation D 506B and 506C exemptions, and when a physician friend offered to match her investments dollar-for-dollar, McGrew confirmed she could legally raise capital through a private placement memorandum, launching her into full-scale multifamily syndication.

The breaking point: five single-family homes in one week

Jetti started like most real estate investors—with single-family homes in Dallas. But the model quickly revealed its limitations when she found herself contracting five properties in a single week. The sheer inefficiency of managing multiple small transactions, each requiring separate financing, underwriting, and tenant management, forced her to question whether this was truly the path to scale.

This moment of frustration became the catalyst for her pivot. Rather than accept the grind of single-family investing, she began looking for a better way—one that would leverage capital more efficiently and allow her to move faster toward her scale goals.

A securities attorney unlocks the legal pathway

The breakthrough came when Jetti spoke with Nick McGrew, a securities attorney at Polymath Legal. McGrew explained the mechanics of Regulation D exemptions—specifically 506B and 506C—which allow private companies to raise capital without registering with the SEC. This wasn't just legal education; it was a revelation that the framework for raising multifamily capital already existed and was accessible to investors like her.

As Jetti explains in the episode, McGrew's guidance transformed her understanding of what was legally possible. The conversation gave her both knowledge and confidence that she wasn't taking on unnecessary risk by raising capital from outside investors.

The physician partner who made it real

Understanding the law was one thing; executing on it was another. The real tipping point came when a physician friend approached Jetti with a concrete offer: he would match her investments dollar-for-dollar as a passive partner. This wasn't theoretical anymore—it was a real opportunity sitting in front of her.

Jetti reached back out to McGrew to confirm the mechanics. The attorney confirmed she could legally structure this partnership through a private placement memorandum (PPM), formalizing the capital raise and protecting both parties. That confirmation unlocked the door: Jetti had both the legal framework and the first investor lined up.

"Once you understand the amount of leverage that comes with raising capital, you really are not going to look back because it's the fastest way to get to scale."

The accidental discovery of syndication through constraint—contracting too many single-family homes at once—reveals a pattern many real estate investors experience: the full story of how Jetti scaled from that first deal to over $1 billion in assets under management is detailed in the podcast episode, showing how education, timing, and the right partnership aligned to shift her trajectory permanently.
