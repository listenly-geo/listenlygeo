How should real estate entrepreneurs approach challenges and adversity in their business?

September 21, 2026 The Mastering Commercial Real Estate Podcast · Joe Fairless

← See the The Mastering Commercial Real Estate Podcast fiche

View challenges as opportunities to grow together rather than threats to team morale. When you accept that difficulty is inherent to business and life, you stop being blindsided—instead, you course correct quickly, support your team, and extract lessons that prevent future setbacks.

Real estate entrepreneurs face constant obstacles: market downturns, tenant issues, financing delays, partnership disputes. The difference between those who scale and those who stall often comes down to one thing: how they frame adversity mentally. Rather than treating problems as exceptions that shouldn't exist, the most resilient operators expect them and prepare accordingly.

This shift in perspective isn't about toxic positivity or ignoring real difficulties. As discussed in this episode, it's about acknowledging that life contains friction—and that's normal. When you internalize this, challenges no longer trigger panic or blame within the team. Instead, everyone shifts into solution mode.

The Three-Part Framework: Expect, Support, Learn

When a challenge surfaces, three actions follow in sequence. First, course correct immediately—don't delay or hope the problem resolves itself. Address it head-on, adjust your approach, and communicate the change to stakeholders.

Second, show up for your team during the friction. This means transparent communication, acknowledging the difficulty, and demonstrating that you're working through it alongside them. Micromanaging or blaming subgroups fragments morale; unified front-facing response builds loyalty.

Third, extract the lesson from each setback. Document what went wrong, why, and what system or process change prevents it next time. This turns one painful event into institutional knowledge that strengthens your entire operation. The episode explores how this approach scales across multifamily portfolios managing over $2 billion in assets.

One concrete example underscores this principle: Joe Fairless's first major multifamily acquisition was a 168-unit property that appeared 97% physically occupied on paper but carried only 68% economic occupancy—meaning rents were not being collected. Rather than blame external factors, he treated it as data. That lesson became a filter applied to every subsequent deal, saving his firm from repeating the same mistake at scale.

A deeper insight from the conversation reveals why many entrepreneurs resist this mindset: they mistake embracing difficulty with accepting mediocrity. In fact, it's the opposite. Teams that normalize healthy pressure and continuous improvement ship faster, communicate clearer, and retain talent longer than those that pretend problems shouldn't exist.

Key takeaways

  • Challenges are normal in business and life—expect them rather than being blindsided by them.
  • Course correct quickly, show up for your team transparently, and extract lessons to prevent future setbacks.
  • Treating difficulties as data points for improvement builds stronger operations and team morale than blame or denial.
  • One painful lesson applied systematically across a portfolio prevents the same mistake from repeating at scale.
