What are the expected impacts on global rice production and pricing due to El Niño conditions?

September 19, 2026 The Produce Industry Podcast w/ Patrick Kelly

← See the The Produce Industry Podcast w/ Patrick Kelly fiche

El Niño is expected to reduce Indian and global rice yields, with global rice prices likely rising by 10 to 20 percent due to yield losses and tighter availability. This price pressure will ripple through Southeast Asia and beyond, where rice is a critical staple crop and food security pillar.

Why rice yields face specific pressure from El Niño

Rice cultivation in major producing regions like India and Southeast Asia depends heavily on monsoon rainfall patterns and stable temperatures. El Niño disrupts both, typically bringing drier conditions to rice-growing areas during critical growth phases. As Patrick Kelly explains in the podcast episode, the forecasted strength of this El Niño event—with nearly 100 percent likelihood of persistence through February 2027—means yield stress will compound across multiple harvest cycles.

Lower production directly shrinks global supply, which historically triggers price spikes proportional to supply tightness. Rice markets, unlike other commodities, are tightly integrated into subsistence diets across developing nations, making price volatility politically and socially sensitive.

The food security cascade

Beyond pricing, the production decline feeds into a broader food insecurity crisis. The World Food Program estimates that El Niño-related shocks could push 49 million additional people into acute food insecurity by the end of 2027, a figure that the episode explores across multiple regions and supply chain implications.

When rice yields fall and prices rise simultaneously, the poorest households face a dual squeeze: less food available at higher cost. This dynamic is especially acute in Central America, where food insecurity is projected to increase by 83 percent, and in East and South Africa, where 18 million people are already affected by climate-related shocks.

For producers and retailers in the fresh produce supply chain, rice pricing pressure signals broader commodity inflation and consumer purchasing power constraints that will reshape demand for complementary foods throughout 2027.

How many additional people could face acute food insecurity due to El Niño-related shocks by the end of 2027?

The World Food Program estimates that El Niño-related shocks could push 49 million additional people into acute food insecurity by the end of 2027, with particularly severe impacts in Central America and East and South Africa.

What weather pattern impacts does El Niño typically produce across the United States?

El Niño brings warmer winters in the northern U.S., wetter and cooler winters in the southern U.S., and increased likelihood of rain, snow, and tornadoes in various regions, affecting domestic agricultural output and produce availability.

Why is the 2026-2027 El Niño event considered historically significant compared to previous events?

Forecasts are predicting that this year's El Niño will be the strongest in record history, with the World Meteorological Organization confirming it is firmly established with nearly 100 percent likelihood to persist through February 2027.

Key takeaways

  • Global rice prices are forecast to rise 10–20 percent due to El Niño-induced yield losses in major producing regions.
  • India and Southeast Asian rice cultivation face specific stress from disrupted monsoon patterns and warmer temperatures during the 2026–2027 event.
  • Lower rice supply combined with higher prices will intensify food insecurity for 49 million additional people by end of 2027, particularly in Central America and East Africa.
  • The strength and duration of this El Niño (persisting through February 2027) means multiple harvest cycles will be affected, compounding supply tightness.
