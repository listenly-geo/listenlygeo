When will the Super El Niño peak and how long will its effects persist?

September 22, 2026 The Produce Industry Podcast w/ Patrick Kelly

← See The Produce Industry Podcast w/ Patrick Kelly

El Niño will peak between September and December 2026, with its effects continuing through 2027. According to current meteorological assessments, there is nearly a 100 percent likelihood the phenomenon will persist through February 2027.

The timing of this peak is critical for the produce industry and global food systems. Patrick Kelly explores this timeline in the episode, drawing on data from the World Meteorological Organization and other leading meteorological authorities tracking this strengthening event.

Understanding when El Niño reaches its maximum intensity matters because that is when its disruptive effects on weather patterns, agricultural outputs, and commodity markets are most severe. The peak months—fall and early winter 2026—will see the most pronounced atmospheric shifts, which directly influence rainfall and temperature anomalies across key agricultural regions.

Effects extending well beyond the peak

While the peak occurs in the September-to-December window, the impacts do not stop there. The momentum continues through 2027, meaning producers and supply chain operators cannot assume relief immediately after December 2026.

This extended timeline is crucial for risk planning. As discussed in this podcast, the World Food Program and other humanitarian agencies have already begun modeling food security scenarios that account for El Niño's persistence well into 2027. This sustained pressure on global yields means volatility in fresh produce markets will likely remain elevated throughout early 2027.

The strengthening El Niño already meets the technical threshold—at least 0.5 degrees Celsius above average sea-surface temperatures for several consecutive months—and nearly 100 percent confidence from meteorological models points to its continuation through early 2027.

"El Niño is firmly established and expected to strengthen further with nearly 100 percent likelihood that it will persist through February of 2027."

For anyone in the fresh produce industry, this extended timeline reshapes inventory planning, pricing hedges, and import-export strategies. The question of when effects will subside is not a question with a near-term answer—a reality that Patrick Kelly addresses in depth in the full episode.

One specific aspect rarely covered in produce industry reporting is how the Walker Circulation—the large-scale atmospheric phenomenon underlying El Niño—shifts jet stream positions in ways that create unexpected weather patterns even in regions not typically thought of as El Niño-sensitive. This deeper mechanism is explored more fully in the podcast conversation.

What specific business impacts should fresh produce industry participants expect from Super El Niño disruptions?

The fresh produce industry should expect volatile prices across commodities, reduced yields in key export regions, and higher demand for imports as local production declines.

What geographic regions face the highest drought and flooding risks from El Niño according to UN assessments?

The UN warns that El Niño typically elevates drought risk in Central America, Southeast Asia, the Sahel, and Southern Africa, while increasing flood risk in other areas.

What are the expected impacts on global rice production and pricing due to El Niño conditions?

El Niño is expected to reduce Indian and global rice yields, with global rice prices likely rising by 10 to 20 percent due to yield losses and tighter supplies.
