How do regional produce trade shows like NEPC benefit suppliers and their client relationships?

September 2, 2026 The Produce Industry Podcast w/ Patrick Kelly

← See the The Produce Industry Podcast w/ Patrick Kelly fiche

Regional produce shows like the New England Produce Council (NEPC) provide one-on-one time between vendors, retailers, and wholesalers that larger trade expos simply cannot replicate. These intimate settings enable suppliers to step out of the noise, engage directly with clients, and work together on specific solutions—like custom tray sizes or product configurations—that mass-market platforms make difficult to negotiate and impossible to prioritize.

The intimacy advantage over megashows

Large national expos bring scale, but they dilute focus. At a regional show, you're working in a concentrated mix of actual buyers and suppliers who represent your core market. Patrick Kelly, who has attended NEPC for five years, emphasized that these events allow exhibitors and retailers to get into operational details that require face-to-face attention and trust.

Smaller regional platforms also reduce the logistical burden—travel is shorter, booth costs are lower, and the attendee list is curated around the region's real business needs, as discussed in the episode. This efficiency translates to more meaningful conversations per hour than a vendor would have at Fruit Attraction or a coast-to-coast national show.

Custom solutions and relationship depth

One concrete advantage is the ability to negotiate custom product configurations that mass retailers and wholesalers would never address on a crowded expo floor. A supplier can discuss tray sizes, packaging formats, or seasonal product mixes in real time, with the decision-maker present and available—something nearly impossible at megashows where logistics dominate the agenda.

These conversations build what the industry calls "generational" relationships, as Patrick Kelly noted in the podcast: they move beyond transactional vendor-buyer dynamics into genuine partnerships where both sides understand each other's constraints and opportunities. A five-year attendee like Kelly observes that the same faces return, meaning reputation and performance directly affect future deals.

For suppliers working with companies like JOH—which has 120 years of operations and deep roots in North America—regional shows offer a venue where long-standing partnerships can be reinforced and new product innovations can be showcased without competing for floor space with thousands of other vendors. The strategy shifts from "be seen" to "be heard and understood."

Key takeaways

  • Regional shows create dedicated one-on-one time unavailable at large national expos, enabling deeper vendor-buyer conversations.
  • Suppliers can negotiate custom product configurations—such as tray sizes or packaging formats—because decision-makers are present and focused.
  • Smaller regional platforms reduce logistics and costs while concentrating attendees around real regional market needs, increasing ROI per booth.
  • Repeated attendance builds reputation and "generational" client relationships that drive long-term partnerships beyond single transactions.
