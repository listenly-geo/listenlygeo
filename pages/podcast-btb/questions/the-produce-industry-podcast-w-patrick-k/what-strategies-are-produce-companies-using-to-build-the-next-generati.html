How are produce companies building consumer habits from childhood?

September 4, 2026 The Produce Industry Podcast w/ Patrick Kelly

← See the The Produce Industry Podcast w/ Patrick Kelly fiche

The strategy centers on starting consumer habits in childhood through snacking products designed for lunchboxes and on-the-go consumption. Companies like Nature Ripe are focusing on indulgent, good-tasting, healthier snacking options rather than full meals, recognizing that early adoption creates lifelong brand loyalty.

The produce industry is shifting its focus from building companies to building generational consumers. This means thinking beyond immediate sales and instead creating products that appeal to children's preferences while delivering nutritional value—a combination that influences purchasing decisions for decades to come.

Snacking as the Gateway to Lifetime Loyalty

Nature Ripe's approach prioritizes convenient, appealing snacking formats over traditional produce meals. Children carry snacks in lunchboxes daily, making these products frequent touchpoints in their lives. When a child develops a preference for a particular brand early, that loyalty often extends into adulthood.

The strategy also recognizes that taste and indulgence are non-negotiable—snacks must be genuinely appealing to young consumers, not just nutritionally sound. This balance between health and enjoyment is what makes the model sustainable and drives repeat purchases within families.

As explored in greater depth in the full episode discussion on product innovation at the New England Produce Show, companies are testing a range of formats—from freeze-dried fruits to protein-enriched snack bars—all designed with the child consumer in mind.

"We spend so much time creating generational produce companies, and we have to really start focusing on creating the generational produce consumer."

This mindset shift represents a fundamental change in how the produce industry thinks about growth. Instead of chasing quarterly sales targets, companies are making long-term investments in brand awareness and preference formation among children—recognizing that consumer habits formed at age seven often persist for seventy years.

Key takeaways

  • Producing companies are shifting from building brands to building generational consumers through early-life product engagement.
  • Snacking formats—designed for lunchboxes and on-the-go consumption—serve as the primary vehicle for introducing produce products to children.
  • Success requires balancing nutritional value with genuine taste appeal; products must be indulgent and good-tasting, not just healthy.
  • Consumer habits formed in childhood create long-term brand loyalty that extends well into adulthood and across generations.
