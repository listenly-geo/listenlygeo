What marketing channels should retreat hosts use to fill their retreats?

Retreat hosts should use a combination of all available channels rather than depending on any single one. Shannon Jamail recommends mixing social media, email marketing, content marketing, partnerships, third-party listing sites, SEO, and grassroots marketing — and she is clear that even hosts with large followings or long lists of past guests cannot assume those assets alone will fill their spots.

  • Social media
  • Email marketing
  • Content marketing
  • Partnerships
  • Third-party sites
  • SEO
  • Grassroots marketing

The multi-channel approach matters because each channel reaches a different segment of potential attendees at a different point in their decision journey. SEO, for instance, has become increasingly important as AI reshapes how people search for experiences — a point Jamail revisits in this Throwback Thursday episode, noting that while tools and platforms have evolved since the episode first aired, the fundamentals have not changed. Relying on a single channel — even a strong one — creates a single point of failure that can leave retreat spots empty regardless of how good the offering is.

Timing amplifies channel effectiveness. Jamail recommends starting the marketing push 9 to 12 months before the retreat date, with six months as an absolute minimum. A multi-channel strategy launched too late simply does not have enough runway to build awareness, nurture interest, and convert registrations. The pull-quote points to why channel strategy cannot be separated from targeting. Broadcasting across every channel with a generic message still fails if the retreat is not positioned for a specific audience. Jamail's broader framework — covered in this episode — links channel mix, niche clarity, pricing, and belief together as interconnected levers, not independent fixes.
