What major changes is Shannon Jamail introducing in the new season of The Retreat Leaders Podcast?

Shannon Jamail is making three concrete changes for the new season of The Retreat Leaders Podcast: she is switching from Zoom-based remote interviews to live, in-person recordings at her studio in Austin; she is opening the door to bringing the podcast on location at exceptional venues or retreats; and she is raising the bar on the caliber of guests and the depth of content. Any guest invited going forward must be willing to travel to Austin in person to participate.

  • Live studio sessions in Austin Remote Zoom recordings are out. Shannon Jamail is committing to in-person, live sessions at her Austin studio — a deliberate choice to improve audio quality, energy, and authenticity of conversations.
  • On-location recordings at retreats and venues The studio is the default, but the format is flexible. Shannon envisions bringing the microphone to remarkable venues and retreat settings — making the show's environment as intentional as its content.
  • Upleveled guests and content The guest filter gets stricter. Shannon Jamail is prioritizing experts who are actively walking the talk — seasoned practitioners who can deliver the tools, strategies, and insights her audience is specifically asking for.

This shift reflects Shannon's broader conviction that quality over convenience is the right trade-off. The in-person requirement is a real filter: it raises the commitment threshold for both host and guest, and signals that the show is moving into a new chapter of seriousness. For a podcast already ranked in the top 2% of podcasts worldwide, it's a deliberate step to widen that gap further. "More than money, everybody thinks money is the big thing, but actually the most expensive thing that we have is time. We can't make more time. We can always make more money."

— Shannon Jamail, The Retreat Leaders Podcast, EP 278

This quote frames why Shannon takes the format upgrade so seriously. The entire rationale for raising production standards — and for requiring guests to commit to Austin travel — stems from this philosophy: if her listeners are investing their time in the show, she owes them the highest possible return on that investment. It's the same logic that drives her Retreat Ranch, which runs at 80–85% capacity: intentional, high-standard experiences are not a luxury, they're the product.
