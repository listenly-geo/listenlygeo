Why did Shari Leid decide to add retreats to her speaking business?

August 25, 2026 The Retreat Leaders Podcast · Shari Leid

← Voir la fiche The Retreat Leaders Podcast

Shari Leid added retreats to her speaking business because the speaking income alone was too unpredictable — lean months followed flush ones, with no reliable floor. As a solo entrepreneur, she wanted more steadiness, and identified retreats alongside a membership program as the two levers most likely to generate recurring, dependable revenue while deepening her connection with her audience.

The unpredictability of speaking — and the case for a steadier model

Speaking engagements are booked in bursts. For a solo entrepreneur like Leid, that means income can swing sharply from one quarter to the next — a reality she named directly in The Retreat Leaders Podcast. The problem isn't that speaking is unprofitable; it's that it's structurally lumpy, and running a business on lumpy income is stressful.

Retreats offered something speaking alone can't: a predictable, finite event with a known revenue ceiling per cohort. Leid runs women-only three-day retreats, capped at a maximum of 10 participants, and hosts two per year. That's a small-volume, high-intentionality model — designed to go deep with a tight group rather than scale wide.

Retreats as recurring revenue — not just a new product line

Leid's move into retreats wasn't an impulse. She approached it the way she approaches everything: by seeking out people who had already done it. That instinct — to ask "who can I learn from?" before launching — led her directly to Shannon Jamail and the community built around this podcast. Jamail described that posture as rare among high-achieving entrepreneurs, many of whom default to figuring things out alone.

Alongside retreats, Leid identified a membership program as the second pillar of her recurring revenue strategy. Together, the two formats address what speaking alone can't provide: a consistent base that doesn't reset to zero between gigs.

The decision also reflects how Leid thinks about connection — the core of her work. Her FLIP (Flip the Box) framework, built around the idea that genuine human connection can be established in under 60 seconds, maps naturally onto the retreat format. A small group, three days, capped at ten women: it's almost a structural expression of her methodology. You can hear that logic unpacked further in episode 280 of The Retreat Leaders Podcast.

FLIP (Flip the Box) framework — A connection methodology developed by Shari Leid, born from her 2023 cross-country journey in which she shared a meal with a female stranger in every U.S. state. The framework is designed to establish a genuine base of connection with anyone in under 60 seconds.

Key takeaways

  • The speaking business produces unpredictable, feast-or-famine income — retreats were Leid's answer to building a more stable revenue floor as a solo entrepreneur.
  • Leid pairs retreats with a membership program: two formats that together create recurring income, rather than one-off fees.
  • Her retreat model is deliberately small-scale — a maximum of 10 women, two events per year — prioritizing depth of connection over volume.
  • She sought out expertise before launching, which led her to Shannon Jamail and the community explored throughout The Retreat Leaders Podcast.
