How should retreat leaders use email marketing to support their retreat business?

September 18, 2026 The Retreat Leaders Podcast

← See The Retreat Leaders Podcast fiche

Email is the owned channel you control entirely — it's far more powerful than social media where less than 1% of your audience sees your content. Build trust by sending at least one email every other week, even if you only have 10 people on your list.

Your list is your most defensible asset

Unlike social media algorithms that constantly shift, your email list belongs entirely to you. As discussed in the episode, social platforms like Instagram and Facebook reach a tiny fraction of your followers organically. Email, by contrast, lands directly in your subscribers' inboxes with no middleman.

The trust-building power of email comes from consistent, direct communication. When people opt into your list, they've given you permission to speak to them regularly. This permission-based relationship is far more valuable than any vanity follower count on social media.

The minimum cadence that actually works

The guidance is clear: send at least one email every other week at minimum. Some retreat leaders send daily emails, which works for them, but it's not a requirement for everyone. The key is consistency over intensity.

Even with a list of just 10 people, you should be using email. This isn't about scale — it's about building the habit and the relationship. As Shannon Jamail points out in this podcast, people need to see something 17 to 20 times before it hits their brain as relevant to them. Bi-weekly emails help you reach that frequency threshold without overwhelming your subscribers.

Email within a realistic time commitment

Running a retreat business demands at least 30 minutes every working day dedicated to retreat marketing and operations. Email isn't separate from that — it's one of the core channels where you invest that time. If you cannot commit at least 30 minutes daily to your retreat business, including email marketing, the timing may not be right to host a retreat.

The reason this matters is that visibility requires 6 to 12 months of lead time to compound. Your email list becomes more valuable the longer you nurture it. Small, consistent actions over time outperform sporadic effort.

Key takeaways

  • Email is the only marketing channel you fully own and control — social media algorithms filter out 99% of your reach.
  • Send at least one email every other week, regardless of list size, to build trust and stay visible.
  • Start with your smallest list if you must — the habit and relationship matter more than the numbers.
  • Budget 30 minutes daily minimum for your entire retreat marketing effort, of which email is a core piece.

What credibility elements should retreat leaders include to build trust with potential guests?

Testimonials, video ones, photos, reviews, media, podcast appearances, and your about page need to be super clear and abundant. If you are new in this space, focus on building these signals from day one.

What lead time should retreat leaders allow between booking announcement and event date?

Six to 12 months is preferred because it allows visibility to compound and gives potential guests time to plan and budget for your retreat.

How should retreat leaders structure calls-to-action on landing pages to maximize bookings?

Too many CTAs create friction. One clear, compelling call-to-action performs better than multiple competing options like "book your spot," "book a call," or "join this list."
