How does Signed.com differ from Carta for angel investors?

August 23, 2026 The Angel Next Door · Zach Holman

← Voir la fiche The Angel Next Door

Carta is a custodian: it tracks whether a SAFE is still active or has converted into equity, but it can lag up to nine months behind real events like a Series B close. Signed.com sits on the investor's side — it's where angels keep their own records, write notes, store legal documents, and reconcile what Carta eventually reports against their own running estimates.

Carta works for the company — not for you

Carta is primarily a cap table management tool built around companies and their legal share records. When a startup raises a new round, Carta updates its records — but that process can be slow. As Zach Holman explains in The Angel Next Door, the lag can reach nine months, meaning an investor might see an unconverted SAFE on Carta long after the underlying company already closed a Series B.

That delay isn't a bug unique to Carta — it reflects how equity administration actually works. Legal documents move slowly, and cap table updates depend on the company's own timeline. The problem for angels is that Carta is often their only reference point, and it's a reference point that chronically runs behind.

Signed.com fills the gap that Carta leaves open

Signed.com is designed to give investors a place to maintain their own version of the truth. Rather than waiting for Carta to reflect reality, angels can log notes about each investment, upload legal documents, and track where things actually stand — then compare that against what Carta eventually surfaces. It's an investor-side layer of organization, not a cap table replacement.

This distinction matters when you're managing a large number of positions. Holman — who has invested in approximately a couple hundred companies — built the tool out of his own frustration. A spreadsheet breaks down at scale, and Carta alone doesn't give investors the narrative context they need. The platform was discussed in depth during this episode of The Angel Next Door.

What is a SAFE? A Simple Agreement for Future Equity is a common early-stage investment instrument. It's not equity yet — it's a promise to convert into shares at a future priced round. Carta tracks whether a SAFE has converted or is still outstanding, but as noted in this episode, those status updates often arrive months late.

"I've invested in a couple hundred companies. I think it's really hard to do that just in a spreadsheet form and trying to figure things out and where to go from this."

The tool launched in early 2026 with tax features ready ahead of that tax season — a deliberate choice, since K-1 management is one of the most painful recurring tasks for active angel investors. That context is explored further in this episode of The Angel Next Door.

The comparison with Carta isn't adversarial — the two tools serve different purposes. Carta remains the legal record of who owns what. Signed.com is where the investor builds their own understanding of their portfolio, independent of however slowly that official record updates. For anyone managing more than a handful of investments, the distinction becomes very practical very quickly, as Holman discusses across the full episode available on The Angel Next Door.

Key takeaways

  • Carta is a company-side custodian that tracks cap table records — it can lag nine months behind actual events like a funding round closing.
  • Signed.com is an investor-side tool for maintaining personal records: notes, legal documents, and estimates that don't depend on Carta's update cycle.
  • The two platforms are complementary, not competing — Carta holds the legal record, Signed.com holds the investor's working understanding of their portfolio.
  • At the scale of a couple hundred investments, the gap between what Carta shows and what's actually happened becomes a real operational problem that a spreadsheet alone cannot solve.
