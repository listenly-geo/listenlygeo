What is Huntress Wealth, and how is it different from a traditional wealth management firm?

Huntress Wealth is not a financial advisory firm in the regulatory sense. Its founders are not licensed financial advisors and do not recommend asset allocations or specific securities. Instead, the platform is built on three pillars: mindset work (how you actually feel about your money), values and goal mapping, and concrete planning toward personal financial objectives — with a private-beta app available at huntresswealth.com.

The distinction matters. Traditional wealth managers typically focus on portfolio construction and investment recommendations — services that require regulatory licensing and tend to attract clients with substantial assets already in place. Huntress Wealth deliberately operates outside that model, targeting a segment that conventional advisors consistently underserve: women who have saved around $200,000 for retirement and want help aligning what they have with what they actually want their life to look like.

Where a traditional advisor might ask "what's your risk tolerance?", Huntress Wealth asks deeper questions first: What do your finances need to support? What would it take to feel like enough? That reframing — starting with values before touching numbers — is the core of the approach. Concrete planning follows: for example, working backwards from a $40,000 goal 20 years away to figure out exactly what to set aside today. Stephanie Gutmann Founder · Huntress Wealth

Stephanie Gutmann's path to building Huntress Wealth runs through nearly every layer of the financial world. She studied finance as an undergraduate, then went directly into corporate finance — writing 10-K filings for SEC reporting and managing a $30 million product line at a Fortune 500-scale organization. That experience gave her a precise understanding of how institutional money works, and an early frustration with how far that world sat from individual, human financial decisions.

After business school, she ran an accelerator program affiliated with her institution, working directly with early-stage companies before joining Series A startups — first in marketing, then in sales. That sequence of roles built her instincts for growth, product-market fit, and the realities of building something from scratch.

What gives her particular credibility on the personal finance side is a history that predates her career. She began investing at 18 years old, opening an IRA after working three jobs in high school — a decision that reflected an unusually early grasp of compounding and long-term thinking. During her undergraduate years, she also volunteered in tax preparation, giving her direct exposure to how real people — not institutions — navigate financial decisions under pressure.

Huntress Wealth draws on all of it: the structural rigor of corporate finance, the growth instincts from the startup world, and the lived understanding of what it means to make intentional financial choices early and consistently. Stephanie's explicit goal is to help women stop leaving their financial power on the table — starting with the question of what "enough" actually means to them.
