How did Nourhan Farhat come to hold the combined roles of strategy, people, and ventures at Kareem?

August 24, 2026 Beyond the Deck · Nourhan Farhat

← Voir la fiche Beyond the Deck

Nourhan Farhat joined Kareem as Head of Corporate Strategy and Chief of Staff. Ventures was added because Kareem's smaller business units — including Kareem Box, Shops, Bikes, Dine Out, and tenant partners — were closely tied to central strategy decisions. The People role was introduced as a temporary fix while the position was vacant; that temporary arrangement has now lasted four years.

From one role to three: how each scope was added

The starting point was clear: a corporate strategy mandate, with Chief of Staff responsibilities layered on top. As Farhat explains in Beyond the Deck, the path from there to a triple remit was driven less by a deliberate org design than by operational logic.

Ventures came first, and it came naturally. Kareem's smaller business units — Kareem Box, Shops, Bikes, Dine Out, and its tenant partners — were so intertwined with strategy that keeping them in a separate silo made little sense. The decisions that shaped those units fed directly into central strategic planning, so the two functions were merged under one owner.

People followed through a different path. The Head of People role was temporarily vacant, and Farhat stepped in to cover it. What was framed as a short-term fix has remained unchanged for four years — a detail she shares candidly in the episode.

The conviction behind the People role: culture and strategy cannot be separated

The longevity of that "temporary" arrangement is no accident. Farhat holds a firm view that culture and strategy must be aligned — and that separating accountability for the two is a structural risk. Owning both gives her a direct line between strategic intent and the people systems that either enable or undermine it.

That conviction is one of the central threads of this conversation on Beyond the Deck — and it shapes how Farhat approaches all three of her portfolios simultaneously.

"Culture eats strategy for breakfast, lunch, and dinner. Strategies could be great on paper, but unless you really fix the operating model, the incentives, the entire culture of the company, the values of the company, I think that's the make it or break it."

The through-line of Farhat's career — engineering, a decade in consulting, then a corporate leap into Kareem — is explored in full in Beyond the Deck, hosted by Dorival Bettencourt, Regional Managing Director at Oliver Wyman.
