How does Nourhan Farhat manage work-life balance across her demanding professional and personal roles?

August 29, 2026 Beyond the Deck · Nourhan Farhat

← See the Beyond the Deck fiche

Nourhan Farhat operates with a personal operating model built on explicit boundaries: she is fully present at family dinners, never checks her phone after putting her children to sleep, and keeps weekends reserved for family. She advises ambitious professionals—particularly young women—to write down their operating model, discuss it openly with colleagues and family, and make clear that family comes first as a non-negotiable priority.

The foundation of Farhat's approach is radical honesty about her constraints. Rather than pretending to have limitless availability, she has designed a life structure that acknowledges competing demands and sets firm rules around them. This is not a theoretical framework—it is communicated explicitly to both her team at Kareem and her family, ensuring everyone understands what to expect.

At the dinner table, Farhat is fully present. This means no emails, no Slack messages, no half-listening while mentally tending to work crises. Once she puts her children to sleep, her phone stays down. Weekends belong to family, not to catch-up calls or weekend work sessions. As she explains in the episode, these are not aspirations—they are operating rules she has built into her day.

Writing it down and making it explicit

What distinguishes Farhat's method from the typical work-life balance platitudes is that she does not leave her operating model to chance or good intentions. She writes it down. She discusses it. She repeats it. As detailed in this podcast, the conversation with both family and colleagues is not a one-time event—it is an ongoing communication that keeps the boundary in place.

For young professionals, especially women climbing into leadership, Farhat's advice is direct: do not assume others will read your mind. Do not hope that boundaries will magically protect themselves. Write your operating model. Share it. Defend it. Make it clear which commitments are fixed and which are flexible.

Family as the non-negotiable anchor

The core insight underlying Farhat's system is that family is not a competing priority to be balanced against work—it is the anchor. Work fits around family, not the reverse. This reframing is subtle but powerful, and it has enabled her to maintain both a senior executive role at Kareem and a deeply engaged presence with her children. As discussed in the episode, this clarity prevents the false choice between ambition and family.

Key takeaways

  • Write down your personal operating model and communicate it explicitly to your team and family—do not leave boundaries to assumption or hope.
  • Protect specific moments as non-negotiable: family dinners without phones, no work after bedtime, weekends reserved for family.
  • Reframe family as your anchor, not a competing priority—work is organized around family commitments, not the other way around.
  • Particularly for ambitious women, make it clear that family comes first; this clarity enables both career growth and genuine presence at home.

Farhat joined Kareem as Head of Corporate Strategy and Chief of Staff. Ventures was added because Kareem's smaller business units — including Kareem Box and Kareem Mafia — needed dedicated leadership and strategic oversight, expanding her mandate.

What does Nourhan Farhat believe is the most important trait to look for when hiring?

Farhat says that drive and resilience are her make-or-break criteria when evaluating candidates. She argues that skills can always be trained and learned on the job, but raw determination and grit are harder to develop in people who lack them.
