What are accessorial charges and how dramatically can they impact total shipping costs?

September 8, 2026 The FreightFA Brief Podcast · Oliver Najumi

← See the The FreightFA Brief Podcast fiche

Accessorial charges are surcharges layered onto freight costs for dimensions, weight, handling, and other special conditions. These fees can easily represent 47% of a company's total shipping spend—and they shift quietly, with one retailer facing a sudden $200,000 annual cost jump when weight and dimension thresholds changed overnight.

From $9 to $58.75: How a Single Surcharge Can Spiral

One furniture company's experience illustrates the scale of the problem. An accessorial charge for items over 48 inches or exceeding 40 pounds started at $9 in 2015. By 2024, that same charge had climbed to $58.75—a 582% increase over 11 years. This wasn't driven by inflation, which over the same period rose far more modestly. The charge compounded silently across thousands of shipments, invisible to many finance teams still budgeting off last year's baseline.

What makes accessorials particularly dangerous is that they're not negotiated upfront like base rates. As Oliver Najumi explains in the episode, carriers adjust these thresholds and fees with minimal transparency. When a 48-inch length limit suddenly becomes 42 inches, or when weight bands shift, exposure multiplies across entire SKU portfolios—sometimes overnight.

Why Accessorials Matter More Than the Headline Rate

Most CFOs benchmark shipping against inflation rates or carrier base-rate increases. That's a critical mistake. Accessorial fees often dwarf headline rate changes in their total impact. For the furniture customer mentioned above, nearly half of the $400,000 annual budget came from these surcharges alone, not from the stated freight rate.

The reason accessorials fly under the radar is structural: they're embedded in invoices, scattered across dozens of charge codes (dimensional weight, fuel, residential delivery, handling, etc.), and tracked loosely by most shippers. Carriers know this, and they've learned to quietly nudge thresholds year after year. A point detailed in this podcast episode is that this pattern repeats across USPS, UPS, FedEx, and LTL carriers—each maintaining their own matrix of fees that shift with little advance warning.

Accessorial Charges Defined

Accessorials are supplemental fees added to base freight charges. Common examples include dimensional weight surcharges (for oversized items), fuel surcharges, peak season premiums, residential delivery fees, and hazmat handling charges. They are applied on top of the base shipping rate and can vary significantly by carrier and service level.

Key takeaways

  • Accessorial charges can represent up to 47% of annual shipping spend, dwarfing base rate increases.
  • A single accessorial charge increased 582% in 11 years (from $9 to $58.75), far outpacing inflation.
  • Threshold shifts (dimension, weight, service area) can trigger sudden $100,000+ annual cost jumps with no advance notice.
  • Freight auditing and threshold monitoring are essential to catching these changes before they cascade across your entire shipment volume.

How can companies validate tariff classifications to avoid unnecessary cost penalties?

Companies must ensure their HTS codes are accurate and that all product descriptions and labeling are correct, as incorrect tariff classifications can result in significantly higher costs and penalties.

Why do shipping cost increases consistently outpace inflation rates year over year?

Shipping rates increase at a much higher rate than inflation, averaging 6% per year across the board. Even when inflation is low, shipping costs consistently rise faster, driven by fuel surcharges, peak season premiums, and carrier capacity constraints.
