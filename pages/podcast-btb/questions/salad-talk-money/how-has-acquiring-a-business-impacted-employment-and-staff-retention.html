How has acquiring a business impacted employment and staff retention?

23 September 2026 The Responsible Finance Podcast · Kaz Burness

← See The Responsible Finance Podcast fiche

Since taking over Beacon Barkers in October 2022, the business added two full-time staff members and has retained 100% of its team. This zero-turnover achievement came through listening to staff concerns, offering upskilling opportunities, improving benefits, and creating a genuine collaborative culture where ownership visibly participates in daily operations.

The transformation began immediately after the acquisition. Kaz Burness brought in an additional full-time groomer to expand the kennels' grooming capacity, and recruited Rachel as a second dedicated full-time dog walker to meet growing demand. Rather than viewing these as simple hires, Burness treated them as strategic hires tied to her broader vision for the business.

Retention, however, proved more valuable than mere recruitment. Zero staff turnover over an eight-month period is remarkable in the pet care industry, where burnout and low wages often drive departures. The key to this success was not structural complexity—it was fundamental: Burness listened closely to her team's needs and acted on their feedback.

Building trust through presence and investment

What distinguishes Burness's approach is her hands-on involvement. As discussed in the episode, she doesn't sit in an office—she participates directly in the day-to-day work, including cleaning kennels. This visible commitment signals that leadership is not detached from frontline operations.

Burness also prioritized staff development. She created upskilling opportunities that gave team members a genuine career path rather than just a job. Staff saw that their employer was invested in their growth, which deepens emotional commitment far more than wages alone.

Benefit improvements rounded out her retention strategy. By enhancing what staff received beyond their base salary, Burness addressed a common pain point in the industry. The combination of listening, investment, and visible leadership created an environment where people wanted to stay.

"There are good finance companies and there are not so good finance companies and if you've got a good feeling that this is not the right company for you please change the company and go with somebody else."

Kaz initially approached several finance companies but felt let down by their attitude, their plans, and unhelpful paperwork. The stark contrast with BCRS's supportive approach made the decision straightforward.

Key takeaways

  • The acquisition added two full-time roles (groomer and dog walker) while maintaining 100% staff retention over eight months.
  • Active listening to staff concerns and acting on feedback is foundational to reducing turnover.
  • Offering upskilling opportunities creates genuine career progression and emotional commitment.
  • Visible leadership involvement in daily operations builds trust and demonstrates ownership commitment to the team.
