How can a kennels business create youth training and volunteer opportunities?

22 September 2026 The Responsible Finance Podcast · Kaz Burness

← See The Responsible Finance Podcast fiche

A licensed kennels can host multiple tiers of youth engagement: veterinary nurse trainees attending weekly placements, school work experience placements for younger students, university students volunteering during holidays, and structured apprenticeships leading to full kennel personnel roles. Each pathway requires minimal setup and builds a pipeline of trained staff while delivering real educational value.

Real apprenticeship pathways at Beacon Barkers

Beacon Barkers has already launched several active programmes. A first-year veterinary nurse student attends two days per week as part of her training placement, gaining hands-on experience in a working kennels environment. This mirrors the standard format for veterinary education partnerships — the student earns credit toward her qualification while Beacon Barkers gains skilled support.

In June, the business hosted three year-10 school pupils for a week-long work experience placement. These younger students trained as kennel hands, learning practical animal care, health and safety protocols, and business operations from entry level. As Kaz Burness explains in the podcast episode, these placements introduce teenagers to the sector and sometimes spark longer-term interest in pet care careers.

University partnerships and apprenticeship expansion

Beacon Barkers is also hosting a veterinary student from Liverpool University during university holidays, creating a seasonal volunteer arrangement that fits academic calendars. This type of partnership is mutually beneficial: the university gets feedback on practicum quality, and the business gains capable temporary staff during peak holiday seasons.

Looking ahead, the business plans to formally launch apprenticeships next year, with a clear pathway: training individuals from their first day through to becoming qualified head kennel personnel. This structured progression offers stability to school leavers or career-changers seeking a defined skill development route, while ensuring Beacon Barkers builds its own leadership bench. The apprenticeship model also often qualifies businesses for government incentives in the UK, reducing net training costs.

Beyond formal staff recruitment, these programmes address a sector-wide skills gap. Pet care and animal husbandry roles often struggle to attract qualified candidates — by building a visible training culture, kennels businesses become employers of choice and help develop the next generation of professionals. Hear more detail on how Beacon Barkers integrates youth development into its growth strategy throughout the full conversation.

Key takeaways

  • Veterinary nurse students can attend kennels placements two days per week as part of formal training qualifications.
  • Secondary school work experience weeks (year-10 age group) introduce teenagers to kennel hand roles and animal care fundamentals.
  • University students volunteer during holiday periods, providing flexible seasonal support while building industry networks.
  • Apprenticeships offer a structured multi-year pathway from entry to head kennel personnel, creating long-term staff retention and development.
