How did HALO certification achieve legal recognition in the UK?

September 10, 2026 The Responsible Finance Podcast · Kate Barnard

← See The Responsible Finance Podcast fiche

The HALO certification became legally recognized after undergoing a 14-month formal process with the UK Intellectual Property Office, resulting in a trademarked certification status. This legal recognition serves as intellectual property proof for data-based businesses, which cannot patent software directly, providing critical credibility for investor engagement and fundraising traction.

For evidence-based companies like Enjoy the Air, intellectual property protection extends beyond traditional patents. As Kate Barnard explains in the episode, data-driven businesses face a unique challenge: their core innovation—the analysis and intelligence they generate—cannot be protected through conventional patent mechanisms. HALO certification bridges this gap by providing formal legal recognition of the certification framework itself.

Trademarked certification as investor-facing proof

The 14-month submission and approval journey through the UK Intellectual Property Office transformed HALO from an internal quality standard into a legally defensible trademark. This distinction matters significantly in the funding landscape. Investors scrutinize intellectual property assets and competitive moats when evaluating early-stage ventures, particularly those without tangible patents or proprietary hardware.

By securing trademark status, Enjoy the Air established a concrete, registered asset that demonstrates both organizational rigor and market differentiation. This formalizes the certification as proof of traction and systematic methodology—elements investors use to assess business maturity and scalability. A point detailed in this podcast, where Barnard discusses how early-stage data intelligence companies must articulate their competitive advantage differently than hardware or software vendors.

The practical outcome of HALO's regulatory recognition extends beyond prestige. Trademark status creates a legal foundation for the certification scheme itself, enabling Enjoy the Air to license, reference, and defend the standard in partnerships with local authorities, environmental bodies, and policy organizations. This is explored further in the full conversation, where Barnard discusses how certification frameworks unlock collaboration opportunities that raw data alone cannot achieve.

Key takeaways

  • HALO certification went through a formal 14-month UK Intellectual Property Office process to achieve legal trademark recognition.
  • For data-based businesses unable to patent software, registered certification status provides formal proof of intellectual property and competitive differentiation.
  • Trademark protection of certification frameworks serves as investor-facing traction evidence and supports licensing partnerships with public and private organizations.
