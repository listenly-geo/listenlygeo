What services beyond boarding do licensed kennels typically offer?

18 September 2026 The Responsible Finance Podcast · Kaz Burness

← See The Responsible Finance Podcast fiche

Licensed kennels operate as multi-service pet care hubs, not just boarding facilities. Beacon Barkers, for instance, boards up to 65 dogs at once while offering on-site grooming, agility training, a retail shop, dog walking, pet transport, and home visits for senior dogs and puppies—a diversified model that maximizes revenue and strengthens customer loyalty.

The expansion beyond kennels reflects how pet care businesses build sustainable operations. As Kaz Burness explains in the episode, when she first started her pet services from home in February 2019, she offered doggy daycare and home boarding—a modest beginning that naturally evolved into a larger operation with more touchpoints for customers.

From home-based care to a full-service facility

The shift from home-based operations to a licensed kennels facility unlocked access to additional services. A dedicated premises allows businesses to add specialized staff, such as on-site groomers, and infrastructure like agility courses that simply cannot fit into a residential setting. The small retail shop—stocking pet supplies and accessories—represents another revenue stream that serves customers who are already on-site for boarding or training.

Supplementary services like dog walking and home visits serve a dual purpose: they capture customers who don't need boarding but want ongoing care, and they deepen relationships with existing clients. As discussed in this podcast conversation, home visits for older dogs and puppies address specific needs that boarding alone cannot solve—elderly dogs may struggle with kennel transitions, and young puppies require socialization and frequent attention.

The acquisition of the kennels facility in October 2022 marked a turning point. Burness' established customer base from her home-based operations transferred to the new location, and the scale of the facility—accommodating up to 65 dogs simultaneously—enabled her to introduce the full suite of complementary services that individual customers and businesses demand.

Key takeaways

  • Licensed kennels operate as multi-service facilities, combining boarding with grooming, agility training, retail, walking, transport, and home visits.
  • A dedicated facility enables the addition of specialized staff and infrastructure that cannot fit into home-based operations.
  • Supplementary services address specific customer needs—such as home visits for elderly dogs and puppies—that boarding alone cannot meet.
  • Revenue diversification strengthens business sustainability and deepens customer relationships beyond a single transaction.
