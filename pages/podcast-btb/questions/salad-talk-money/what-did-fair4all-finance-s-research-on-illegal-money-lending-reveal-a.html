What did Fair4All Finance's research on illegal money lending reveal about the scale of the problem in the UK?

Fair4All Finance commissioned novel research combining a quantitative study conducted with Ipsos Mori and qualitative work carried out by an organisation whose co-founder had previously been an illegal lender — granting extraordinary access to those still actively offering illegal loans. The headline finding: up to three million people in the UK are potentially borrowing from illegal lenders today.

The qualitative strand of the research was particularly striking. Because the organisation running it had insider credibility, researchers gained on-camera testimony from individuals still operating as illegal lenders. One participant stated plainly: "I try not to use actual violence because the threat of violence is more potent." This is not anecdote — it is primary research that puts a human and deeply troubling face on the scale of coercion embedded in illegal lending.

The Ipsos Mori quantitative work then translated that reality into a number that challenges the entire financial inclusion sector: three million UK borrowers who have no access to legal, affordable credit and are instead turning to lenders who operate through fear. This research is part of why Fair4All Finance has broadened its mission beyond credit alone, looking at resilience and savings as part of a system-wide answer to financial exclusion.

About Kate Pender

KP

Kate Pender

Chief Executive · Fair4All Finance

Kate Pender has been involved with Fair4All Finance from its very beginning in 2019, initially joining on a part-time secondment to lead the pilot scale-up programme — a hands-on role focused on making the first investments into community development finance institutions and credit unions across the UK. She went on to lead the first phase of Fair4All's COVID grant programme when the pandemic created sudden and acute financial need across the population. After transitioning into a permanent role, she ultimately applied for and took on the position of Chief Executive. Before Fair4All Finance, Pender built a long career in economic development, designing and running programmes to help SMEs and small businesses grow faster. That background gives her an unusually grounded, operational perspective on how financial systems either serve or fail the people who need them most — and what it actually takes to change them at scale.

What is Fair4All Finance's mission and how has it evolved since its founding?

Fair4All Finance was set up in 2019 with a mission to improve the financial services sector so that it better serves millions of people who are underserved and excluded. The organisation has since broadened its remit to address financial inclusion in a wider context, including resilience and savings.
