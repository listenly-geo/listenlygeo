How is Beacon Barkers scaling its pet care services across five years?

25 September 2026 The Responsible Finance Podcast · Kaz Burness

← See The Responsible Finance Podcast fiche

Beacon Barkers is launching doggy daycare by the end of April (shifting from its original month-two timeline to month eight to ensure proper sequencing), while year two will bring a secure off-lead dog walking field available for agility trainer rentals and potential police dog training facilities. The five-year plan prioritizes controlled growth under new groomer Kayleigh's vision for the grooming division.

From home-based dog care to a five-year expansion roadmap

What began as a home-based pet care service in February 2019 has transformed dramatically. When Kaz Burness acquired Beacon Barkers kennels in October 2022—with a £100,000 loan from BCRS Business Loans—she inherited a licensed facility capable of boarding up to 65 dogs at once, complete with on-site grooming, agility facilities, and a retail shop. But acquisition alone wasn't the endpoint; it was the foundation for a deliberate, staged expansion.

The doggy daycare launch, originally scheduled for month two of the five-year plan, has been repositioned to month eight. This adjustment reflects a critical principle in scaling: proper sequencing matters more than speed. Rather than rushing to add services, Kaz has chosen to ensure each operation is staffed, trained, and operationally sound before launching the next vertical.

Year two: Building infrastructure for premium services

By year two, Beacon Barkers aims to open a secure off-lead dog walking field where dogs can run freely under supervision. This isn't just a recreational amenity; it's designed as a revenue-generating asset. The field will be divided into rentable sections for agility trainers, creating a hub for specialist trainers to run sessions while Beacon Barkers captures rental income and cross-referral opportunities.

Beyond agility, the expansion roadmap includes potential training facilities for police dog trainers, positioning Beacon Barkers as a specialized infrastructure provider rather than merely a boarding service. This diversification reduces reliance on any single revenue stream and leverages the site's secure environment as a competitive advantage.

Grooming as a growth lever under new leadership

A centerpiece of the expansion strategy is the grooming division under new groomer Kayleigh's vision. Rather than treating grooming as an auxiliary service, Kaz has invested in leadership capable of scaling this vertical independently. This signals maturity in the business: rather than bottlenecking all growth through the owner, she's building a management layer that can drive specialization and attract premium clients.

How has acquiring a business impacted employment and staff retention?

Since October 2022, Beacon Barkers has taken on an additional full-time groomer, a second full-time dog walker, and maintained zero staff turnover, demonstrating the business's growth momentum and team stability.

What youth training and volunteer opportunities can a kennels business create?

Beacon Barkers actively encourages volunteering and work experience, hosting three year-10 work experience placements as kennel hands and welcoming a veterinary nurse student from Liverpool University during university holidays.
