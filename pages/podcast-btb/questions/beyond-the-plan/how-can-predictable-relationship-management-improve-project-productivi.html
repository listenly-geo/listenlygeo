How can predictable relationship management improve project productivity?

September 4, 2026 Beyond The Plan · Brett Ackroyd

← See the Beyond The Plan fiche

When teams understand how their colleagues will respond to problems, they can pre-plan and respond predictively rather than reactively. Scottish Water's research demonstrated that projects using collaborative working experienced significantly reduced delay times compared to those without it, because everybody knew exactly how to handle unforeseen issues together.

This shift from reactive crisis management to predictive problem-solving is fundamentally about relationship clarity. When you work alongside the same partners repeatedly, patterns emerge—you learn their decision-making styles, their risk tolerance, their communication preferences, and their commitment to shared outcomes.

As Brett Ackroyd explains in Beyond The Plan, the foundation of this predictability is establishing common values and behaviours across the entire delivery team. Once those are explicit, teams can design systems and processes that actively support them, rather than creating friction.

From reactive delays to predictive delivery

Scottish Water's comparative research was telling: projects that had invested in building collaborative working relationships cut their delay times dramatically. The difference wasn't about better technology, more budget, or longer schedules—it was purely about relationship certainty enabling anticipation.

When a problem emerges on a collaborative project, teams don't spend time negotiating blame, checking contracts, or waiting for sign-off from distant stakeholders. They already know how their partner operates, what trade-offs they'll accept, and what the shared priority actually is. A potential five-day delay becomes a one-day adjustment.

This is particularly valuable in complex infrastructure and defence projects, where unforeseen conditions are guaranteed and change is constant. As detailed in this podcast episode, teams that have practised responding to problems together—not just planned for them—move faster when reality doesn't match the blueprint.

Why contract alone isn't enough

Most organisations assume that a comprehensive contract creates safety and predictability. In reality, a contract only describes what happens if things go wrong—and most project failures happen not from breach, but from the inability to act on problems teams already know how to fix.

Contractual boundaries can actually prevent the very speed and collaboration that reduce delay. If Party A is afraid that helping Party B will count against them in a dispute, or if decision-making is locked into formal approval chains, the organisation loses its agility. Predictable relationship management removes this friction.

In mega-projects within defence and government, as Brett Ackroyd's 25 years of experience has shown, the organisations that deliver on time and on budget are those that made relationship management as intentional as schedule management. Why do most project failures occur after a contract is signed?

Project failures often stem from the inability of teams to act on problems they know how to fix because of contractual boundaries and organisational constraints that prevent effective response to emerging issues.

What role does ISO 44001 play in managing collaborative working?

ISO 44001 is the business relationship management standard that provides a structured starting point for organisations to be intentional about managing business relationships and building collaborative frameworks.

How should business relationship management be structured in major projects?

Collaborative working is a systemic approach that must be intentional, enduring, and scalable. Organisations need to establish clear shared values and behaviours, then align their systems and processes to actively support them.

Key takeaways

  • Predictable relationship management allows teams to anticipate problems rather than react to crises, reducing project delays significantly.
  • Scottish Water's research proved that projects with collaborative working experienced measurably shorter delay times than those without it.
  • Contractual clarity alone is insufficient—teams need explicit, shared values and aligned systems to act decisively when conditions change.
  • Collaborative culture exists when one party is willing to act to their own detriment to achieve the shared outcome, enabling genuine agility.
