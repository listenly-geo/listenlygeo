What's really slowing down CMOs in their work with AI tools?

September 2, 2026 The Think Small Podcast · Mat Zucker

← See the The Think Small Podcast fiche

The real bottleneck isn't AI capability—it's temptation: the difficulty of choosing which opportunities to pursue rather than chasing every shiny new tool. Beyond that lies a deeper challenge: unlearning, the painful work of abandoning behaviors built over 10, 20, or 30 years of professional life in order to operate differently.

When opportunity becomes the obstacle

Mat Zucker identifies temptation as his primary professional brake. In an environment flooded with AI capabilities and new features, the hardest decision isn't whether to adopt—it's deciding what to ignore. Every tool promises value; every capability feels essential when you first encounter it. But spreading effort across too many initiatives dilutes impact and slows down actual execution.

This constraint surfaces a larger pattern across organizations. Many firms are now facing disappointment with AI investments made over the last 18 to 24 months. The expected payoff never materialized. The tools worked; the strategy didn't. As Zucker discusses in depth in the episode, the gap between potential and reality often stems not from bad technology, but from distraction and misalignment.

The weight of old habits

The second obstacle is less visible but equally powerful: unlearning decades of ingrained behavior. A marketer with 30 years of experience has built muscle memory, decision-making patterns, and instincts honed through a different era. AI demands a different rhythm, different inputs, different trust mechanisms. Shedding that weight is uncomfortable and slow.

This isn't a gap that tools solve. It's a human problem. The frameworks, methodologies, and shortcuts that made someone successful in traditional marketing can become anchors when AI enters the picture. Zucker's experience at Prophet shows how this friction plays out in real transformation work, where culture and behavior change matter as much as strategy.

"T-Mobile went from like a number four, and it was stuck in a really tough place — they had to do something really interesting to survive. The Uncarrier strategy was something that Prophet developed with the board of T-Mobile."

The Uncarrier case illustrates the point: Zucker reveals in the podcast how internal training happened first, before any external brand work rolled out. Employee mindset had to shift before customer-facing messaging could land. That same dynamic applies to AI adoption. The temptation to jump to implementation skips the harder work: deciding what *not* to do, and training teams to think and act differently.

Key takeaways

  • Temptation—not capability—is the primary professional brake for CMOs using AI; the hardest choice is what to ignore, not what to adopt.
  • Many organizations face disappointment with AI investments because expected payoffs over 18–24 months have not materialized, signaling a strategy or execution problem, not a technology one.
  • Unlearning old professional behaviors built over decades is a necessary but painful prerequisite for genuine AI-driven transformation.
  • Real change requires internal alignment and behavioral shifts before external strategy can succeed, as evidenced by T-Mobile's Uncarrier approach.
