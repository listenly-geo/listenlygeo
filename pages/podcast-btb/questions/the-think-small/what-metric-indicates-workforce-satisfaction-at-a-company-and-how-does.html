What metric indicates workforce satisfaction at a company, and how does it correlate with business outcomes?

September 15, 2026 The Think Small Podcast · Ken Monroe

← See the The Think Small Podcast fiche

BCHEX uses the ENPS score as its primary workforce satisfaction metric, and when it reaches 70—a benchmark comparable to companies like HubSpot—the correlation with business performance becomes measurable and direct. At that elite level, the company has documented a customer churn rate under 2%, alongside rising customer NPS scores, climbing customer satisfaction, and improving quality metrics across the entire organization.

From Employee Engagement to Customer Outcomes

The connection between employee satisfaction and bottom-line results is not theoretical. BCHEX's ENPS score of 70 reflects a workforce so engaged that it directly shapes how customers experience the business. The company's sub-2% churn rate is not an accident—it flows from employees who are invested enough to deliver consistent quality and reliability.

As Ken Monroe explains in the episode, engaged employees are not just happier—they actively build better products and make customers happier to deal with. This isn't sentimentality; it's a measurable operational reality captured in the numbers.

"We're in the prevention business. Workforce safety is the prevention business, and great, engaged, happy employees build better products and make customers happier to deal with."

The real insight here is that ENPS at 70 is not just a satisfaction score—it's a leading indicator of customer loyalty and operational quality. When employees score that high on their likelihood to recommend the company to others, they're signaling deep engagement with the mission and the product. That signal translates immediately to the customer experience.

Each metric Monroe mentions—churn, NPS, satisfaction, quality—moves together, and the causality flows upward from the workforce. Companies that treat ENPS as a strategic leading indicator, not a vanity metric, see these correlations solidify over time. The episode discusses this in more detail as it explores how BCHEX structured its operations to keep employees engaged and empowered.
