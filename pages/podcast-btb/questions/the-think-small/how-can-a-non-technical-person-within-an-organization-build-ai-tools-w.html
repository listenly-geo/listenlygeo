How can a non-technical person within an organization build AI tools without engineering expertise?

September 12, 2026 The Think Small Podcast · Ken Monroe

← See The Think Small Podcast fiche

Organizations can empower non-technical employees to build AI tools by providing access to platforms like Claude, Grok, or ChatGPT, then connecting those tools to existing business systems such as HubSpot, AWS databases, and meeting recorders, paired with practical training on building AI agents. One sales leader with zero AI experience six months prior built an agent that listens to sales calls, scores performance, and automatically delivers coaching documents to salespeople after each call.

A Framework That Removes Technical Barriers

The key insight is that non-technical employees don't need to write code — they need access to the right platforms and a clear pathway to learn how to connect them. Ken Monroe implemented this at his organization by giving employees direct access to AI platforms and then showing them how to wire those platforms into the tools they already use every day.

As detailed in The Think Small Podcast episode, the framework starts with selection of the right AI platforms — Claude, Grok, and ChatGPT are all viable entry points. The critical next step is connecting those platforms to business infrastructure that employees already understand: CRM systems like HubSpot, databases on AWS, or tools like Fireflies AI that record and transcribe meetings.

From Zero AI Experience to Building Production Agents

The proof point is concrete: a sales leader with no prior AI experience became capable of building a fully functional agent in just six months. That agent now automates a complete coaching workflow — it listens to recorded sales calls, scores individual performance against predetermined criteria, and generates personalized coaching documents that land in each salesperson's inbox after every call.

This isn't a toy project or proof-of-concept. It's a production system that runs every single day, saving the sales organization hours of manual review and coaching document creation. The employee didn't learn machine learning or Python; they learned how to compose AI tools into a workflow that solved a real business problem. As Monroe explains in this episode, the training itself focused on the practical mechanics of building agents within workflows rather than on coding theory.

"We're in the prevention business. Workforce safety is the prevention business, and great, engaged, happy employees build better products and make customers happier to deal with."

The curious detail worth exploring: Monroe's team didn't stop at one agent — they've built multiple AI-driven workflows across different departments. The episode reveals how other departments have adapted the same framework to solve problems in their own domains, suggesting the model is broadly replicable across an organization.

Key takeaways

  • Non-technical employees gain AI capability by using no-code platforms (Claude, Grok, ChatGPT) rather than learning to code.
  • Connecting those platforms to existing business systems (HubSpot, AWS, Fireflies) makes them immediately useful and familiar to end users.
  • Training should focus on practical workflow composition and agent design, not machine learning theory or programming.
  • A real-world result: a sales leader with zero prior AI experience built an agent that automates call scoring and coaching document generation in under six months.
