What disclosure requirements are courts implementing regarding AI-generated legal documents?

September 22, 2026 The Think Small Podcast · Brian Calhoun

← See The Think Small Podcast fiche

Courts are now requiring that any AI-generated content in filed documents must be disclosed on the front page, distinguishing attorney-prepared work from AI-generated material. This requirement exists because courts need to assess credibility and ensure legal standards are met, since relying on undisclosed AI content can undermine a document's legal standing and damage an attorney's professional reputation.

Fort Worth's Local Rule Amendment

The Fort Worth court system has amended its local rules to implement a specific disclosure requirement. If any portion of a document being filed contains AI-generated content, that disclosure must appear on the front of the document itself, not buried in footnotes or background sections. This placement requirement ensures judges and opposing counsel immediately know what they are reviewing.

The distinction matters because courts want to clearly separate work product prepared by licensed attorneys from output generated by automated AI systems. As Brian Calhoun discusses in the episode, this transparency is critical to maintaining the integrity of legal filings and protecting both the court system and the clients being represented.

Why Disclosure Protects Credibility

The underlying purpose of disclosure rules is straightforward: undisclosed AI content directly impacts credibility and legal standing. When a court discovers that significant portions of a filing were generated by AI without disclosure, it raises questions about the competence and honesty of the attorney involved. This can result in sanctions, loss of professional standing, or worse.

Beyond individual consequences, disclosure rules protect the broader legal system. Courts need to know whether they are evaluating human legal reasoning or AI output, because the two carry different weight under rules of evidence and professional conduct. By requiring upfront disclosure, courts maintain transparency and allow all parties to make informed assessments of the arguments being presented.

"ChatGPT will tell you anything that you want to hear. Your prompt that you put into OpenAI is discoverable in litigation."

This insight reveals a deeper risk: AI systems like ChatGPT generate plausible-sounding content based on user prompts, but that prompting history becomes discoverable evidence in litigation. As explored further in this podcast episode, the combination of undisclosed AI use and discoverable prompts can expose attorneys to significant professional liability if they have not properly reviewed and verified AI-generated output before filing.

For business owners working with legal counsel, disclosure rules create an opportunity to ask direct questions: Has your attorney used any AI tools in preparing your documents? Has all AI-generated content been properly reviewed by a licensed attorney? These questions protect your interests and ensure compliance with emerging court rules.

When clients use ChatGPT to review contract documents before attorney response, the tool often generates 10 to 20 issues or questions, but typically only a fraction are substantive and actionable by legal counsel.

How does client involvement in litigation cases improve case outcomes and reduce legal costs?

When clients are actively involved and organize facts clearly for their attorney, it cuts out red tape inherent in large law firms. The more a client helps organize information, the faster the legal team can work and the lower the overall cost.

What are the risks of relying on AI case law citations in legal proceedings?

There was a recent ruling out of Dallas where an attorney relied on a case that ChatGPT generated, and it was entirely made up, resulting in the attorney facing serious consequences for filing false citations.
