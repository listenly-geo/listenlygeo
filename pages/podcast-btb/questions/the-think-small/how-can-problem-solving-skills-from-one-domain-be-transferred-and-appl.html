How can problem-solving skills from one domain be transferred and applied to solve problems in a completely different industry?

September 25, 2026 The Think Small Podcast · Bryant Wallace

← See The Think Small Podcast fiche

The key is to extract the underlying concepts and principles from your original domain rather than the surface details, then apply those abstract frameworks to new problems. This works because problem-solving methodology transcends industry boundaries—understanding how to diagnose and fix one type of system teaches you how to approach any other.

Bryant Wallace demonstrates this principle through a concrete example from his teenage years. His father gave him a manual for a Ford Granada to help him repair his Pontiac Ventura, teaching him that despite the different car models, the underlying mechanical concepts were transferable. He didn't need to memorize every detail specific to each vehicle; he needed to understand the fundamental principles of how cars work.

This early lesson became the foundation for Wallace's entire career trajectory. As he explains in the Think Small Podcast episode, he later applied this exact same cross-domain transfer methodology when he moved from studying sports psychology to founding WallScott Solutions, a cybersecurity and compliance company. The problem-solving mindset, the goal-setting frameworks, and the behavioral principles he learned in sports psychology became powerful tools for understanding business challenges.

From abstract principle to business application

The real value lies in identifying what remains constant across domains. Whether you're optimizing team performance in athletics or hardening security protocols in IT, you're still solving problems around human behavior, systems thinking, and resource allocation. The surface language changes; the underlying logic does not.

Wallace founded WallScott Solutions in 1999, more than 27 years ago, and has consistently applied this principle of conceptual transfer throughout his entrepreneurial journey. His early exposure to entrepreneurship—starting at age nine working in his cousin's health food store called Greatest Grains—taught him how to observe systems, talk to diverse people, and solve practical problems. That foundational mindset then enabled him to succeed in completely different industries, from sports psychology to cybersecurity.

A deeper look at how Wallace orchestrates team dynamics in a high-stakes IT environment is covered in detail in this full podcast conversation, where he shares specific methodologies for translating behavioral science into business strategy.

"I can go fast by myself or I can go further with others, and now I can go further faster with the right others."

The practical implication is straightforward: invest in understanding principles, not just procedures. When you solve problems, ask yourself what fundamental principle you're using, not just what steps you're following. That principle becomes your portable toolkit for new domains. Key takeaways

  • Extract abstract principles and frameworks from your original field rather than memorizing surface-level details or procedures.
  • Look for what remains constant across different domains—behavioral patterns, systems thinking, and problem diagnosis methods transfer universally.
  • Start young with entrepreneurship and diverse experiences; early exposure to multiple problem-solving contexts builds a more flexible mental toolkit.
  • Test your transferable frameworks actively in new domains instead of assuming they won't apply; mechanical concepts, sports psychology, and business strategy are closer than they appear.
