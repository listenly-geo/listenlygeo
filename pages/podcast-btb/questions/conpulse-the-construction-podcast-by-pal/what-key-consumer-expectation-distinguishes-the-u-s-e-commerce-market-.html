What key consumer expectation distinguishes the U.S. e-commerce market from other markets?

September 24, 2026 ConPulse - The Construction Podcast · Jennifer Alexander

← See the ConPulse - The Construction Podcast fiche

Authenticity is the most critical consumer expectation when launching a brand in the U.S. market. Buyers want to know who is behind the brand, where products are made, and the genuine story of how the product came to market—making your About Us page critically important to conversion and trust.

The U.S. Market's Unique Demand for Transparency

When countless new brands launch daily across platforms like Amazon, Etsy, and direct-to-consumer channels, standing out requires more than product quality or competitive pricing. U.S. consumers have become skeptical of faceless brands and demand to understand the people and story behind what they're buying.

This expectation for authenticity shapes how successful brands present themselves. As Jennifer Alexander explains in the episode, your About Us page isn't a secondary detail—it's a conversion driver. Customers visiting your site are asking: "Who made this? Where did it come from? Why should I trust this brand?"

"Authenticity is probably the most critical piece of the pie when you're thinking about launching something in the U.S. market."

Why This Matters More in the U.S. Than Elsewhere

The U.S. e-commerce ecosystem is saturated—new brands enter Amazon, Etsy, and the D2C space constantly—creating a trust problem that authenticity solves. Consumers in this market have learned to be selective, and they use brand story and transparency as filters for legitimacy.

Whether your products are manufactured locally or overseas matters to U.S. buyers, and this distinction is discussed in detail in the full episode, where Alexander shares specific strategies for communicating your sourcing and production story to build consumer confidence.

For any brand targeting the U.S. market, investing time in a compelling, truthful About Us page and clear brand narrative is as essential as product development itself. It's the difference between a browser and a buyer.

What percentage of Americans shop online and what is the penetration of internet access in U.S. households?

90% of U.S. households have internet access, which is among the highest in the world. However, only 70% of Americans actually shop online, indicating a gap between connectivity and actual purchasing behavior.
