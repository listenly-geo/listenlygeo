Why AgTech is the Most Challenging Software for Earthwork Modeling?

September 6, 2026 ConPulse - The Construction Podcast · Aurel

← See the ConPulse - The Construction Podcast fiche

Aurel recommends learning AgTech, an earthwork modeling program, because it is the most challenging software he has ever worked with. He deliberately chose this goal because mastering difficult tasks builds capabilities that make easier ones manageable, and the program's complexity has taught him persistence, patience, and problem-solving that extends far beyond simply asking questions.

The software is notoriously finicky and lacks user-friendly design. Rather than viewing these shortcomings as drawbacks, Aurel framed them as deliberate learning opportunities. When a tool resists easy answers, you are forced to develop resilience and troubleshoot independently, skills that transfer across all technical and professional challenges.

As discussed in the episode, Aurel's philosophy of tackling the hardest problems first stems from his early work ethic. His father sent him to work for a landscaper specifically to instill the value of hard work. That same principle—that difficulty builds character and competence—guided his professional development in construction engineering.

Building Problem-Solving Beyond Software

Learning AgTech teaches more than just how to operate earthwork modeling tools. The struggle with the software develops genuine problem-solving instincts that do not rely on quick fixes or external help. Aurel emphasizes that when you work through frustration and non-intuitive interfaces, you learn to think critically and persist when faced with ambiguity.

This approach contrasts with the modern tendency to reach for support documentation or ask colleagues at the first sign of confusion. Aurel's point is not that asking questions is wrong, but that the willingness to struggle through a difficult tool rewires how you approach obstacles altogether. That mindset becomes an asset in project management, design coordination, and any role demanding adaptability.

"Don't do it for the money. The money will be there, believe me, because it's a difficult industry, but do it because you're passionate, but do most things in life because you're passionate and you will, your life will improve tremendously."

What is the importance of clear communication with vendors and subcontractors on construction projects?

Having a strong relationship with the foreman and material suppliers allows for precise ordering and delivery. When the foreman needs half a truck of specific materials, clear communication ensures the right quantities arrive on time.

What operational challenge did Aurel face regarding public safety on a construction project?

A retired gentleman regularly rode his bike through an active construction zone without a helmet, despite traffic control redirecting pedestrians to a safer route, creating an ongoing safety concern for the project team.
