How long does trademark application processing take across different countries?

September 11, 2026 ConPulse - The Construction Podcast · Steve Weibler

← See the ConPulse - The Construction Podcast fiche

Trademark application processing varies significantly by jurisdiction, with timelines typically ranging from one year to two years depending on the country. In Canada, applicants currently face processing times of 18 to 24 months, but remain protected throughout the entire examination period while attorneys in each jurisdiction review the application.

The variation in processing timelines reflects different governmental workloads and procedural requirements across jurisdictions. As Steve Weibler explains in this episode, entrepreneurs often underestimate how critical early trademark filing becomes when building e-commerce companies intended for rapid growth and eventual exit.

A key advantage during the application period is that applicants maintain legal protection while attorneys conduct their examination. This means your brand remains safeguarded throughout the processing phase, even though the formal registration hasn't yet been granted. The protection applies as long as the application remains active and under review.

Planning for international trademark expansion

If you're scaling beyond a single market, timing matters enormously. The Madrid Protocol offers a strategic window: entrepreneurs have six months from their home country filing to extend protection to other jurisdictions under a unified application system, rather than filing separately in each nation.

This coordinated approach prevents the compounding effect of processing delays. Rather than waiting for approval in one country before filing elsewhere—which could add years to your total timeline—you can submit all applications simultaneously and have them examined in parallel. Discussed at length in the ConPulse podcast, this strategy is especially valuable for e-commerce founders who operate across multiple geographies from inception.

"If you don't have a registered trademark or intellectual property strategy, they're going to ding you at exit and say the company doesn't have any value."

The cost of delays compounds when exit arrives. Acquirers routinely devalue companies lacking formal trademark protection, viewing unregistered marks as unproven assets. Filing early—even when cash is tight—costs far less than remedying the absence during due diligence. As Steve Weibler notes in the episode, trademark protection is "hugely inexpensive" compared to the damage unregistered brands suffer at valuation time.

Why processing timelines matter for your business plan

Understanding these timelines helps you build realistic roadmaps for brand expansion. If you're launching in North America and planning European entry within 18 months, you must initiate trademark filings immediately—the examination periods alone could consume your entire growth window. Waiting until you "feel ready" often means your brand enters new markets without formal protection.

The lesson is straightforward: file early, file broadly, and understand that protection begins immediately, even though formal approval takes 12–24 months depending on geography.
