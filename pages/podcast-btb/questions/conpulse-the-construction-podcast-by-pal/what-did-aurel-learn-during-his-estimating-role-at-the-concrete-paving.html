What did Aurel learn during his estimating role at the concrete paving contractor?

August 31, 2026 ConPulse - The Construction Podcast · Aurel

← See the ConPulse - The Construction Podcast fiche

Until you are actually building it, you never truly understand constructability. During one and a half years as an estimator looking at plans and specs behind a computer, Aurel realized that theory and field execution are worlds apart—design issues, proper slopes, and the feasibility of placing infrastructure in specific areas all reveal themselves only once construction begins.

Aurel's time as an estimator for a concrete paving contractor was formative, but not in the way a typical office role might seem. Sitting behind a computer learning plans and specifications taught him theory, yet this knowledge proved incomplete compared to what the field demanded.

The gap became undeniable once he transitioned from the office to the job site. Design feasibility and real-world constraints are two different problems. What looked sound on paper—the slope of a storm drain mainline, the placement of underground pipes, drainage patterns—required entirely different thinking when faced with actual soil conditions, site logistics, and constructability challenges.

As Aurel explains in the episode, this discovery shaped his entire approach to both estimating and project management. He learned that no amount of classroom theory or computer-based planning can replace the experience of understanding what is actually buildable on the ground.

The bridge between design and construction

This lesson became particularly valuable when Aurel moved into his estimator and project manager role at a demolition contractor before joining Granite. Understanding constructability before a project starts reduces costly design conflicts later. Field teams face issues daily that no set of plans can fully anticipate—and an estimator or project engineer who has felt that friction understands what questions to ask during the design phase.

The practical wisdom gained from seeing how theory fails in the field is discussed in detail throughout this podcast conversation, where Aurel reflects on how this foundation shaped his career decisions and his approach to problem-solving in construction.

"Don't do it for the money. The money will be there, believe me, because it's a difficult industry, but do it because you're passionate, but do most things in life because you're passionate and you will, your life will improve tremendously."

What was Aurel's early career experience and how did it shape his approach to construction?

Aurel's father sent him to work for a landscaper after high school to learn the value of hard work. He dug holes without gloves, causing his hands to bleed, but persevered and eventually earned the respect of his coworkers. This foundational experience taught him that hard work and persistence are essential values in construction.

Key takeaways

  • Theory behind a computer screen differs fundamentally from the constructability challenges faced on site.
  • Understanding real-world constraints early in a project—slopes, pipe placement, site logistics—prevents costly design conflicts later.
  • An estimator or project engineer who bridges both theory and field experience becomes far more effective at problem-solving.
  • Passion for the work, not just compensation, drives the decision to persist through the gap between design and execution.
