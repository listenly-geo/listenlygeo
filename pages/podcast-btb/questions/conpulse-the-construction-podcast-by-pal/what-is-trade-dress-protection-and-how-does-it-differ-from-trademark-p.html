What is trade dress protection and how does it differ from trademark protection?

September 12, 2026 ConPulse - The Construction Podcast · Steve Weibler

← See the ConPulse - The Construction Podcast fiche

Trade dress protection covers the unique non-functional design elements of a product or packaging that consumers recognize as identifying the brand—such as Louis Vuitton's distinctive brown and LV pattern on luxury goods. Unlike trademark, which protects brand names and logos, trade dress protects the overall visual appearance and design elements that create brand recognition and enable premium pricing.

How Brand Recognition Becomes Legal Protection

Trade dress operates differently from traditional trademark because it focuses on the entire sensory package consumers associate with a brand, rather than a single word or symbol. As Steve Weibler explains in the podcast, this protection extends to packaging design, color combinations, and product shapes when they become distinctly linked to your company in the consumer's mind.

The key distinction is that trade dress must show acquired distinctiveness—meaning the design has become so associated with your brand that consumers recognize it immediately as yours. Oreos' distinctive cookie shape and Coca-Cola's iconic red color are examples of trade dress that have achieved this level of brand recognition. Once you establish this connection in consumers' minds, your visual design becomes as protectable as your company name.

Why Trade Dress Matters in Building Company Value

For e-commerce entrepreneurs, trade dress protection is part of a larger intellectual property strategy that directly impacts your exit valuation. When acquirers evaluate your company, they assess whether you've protected not just your trademark name but also the distinctive visual identity that makes your products stand out on shelves or online.

Without this layer of protection, buyers will significantly devalue your company because your unique visual elements can be copied by competitors without legal recourse. The investment in securing trade dress protection early—alongside trademark registration—signals that you've built genuine brand equity and consumer loyalty worth paying for.

"If you don't have a registered trademark or intellectual property strategy, they're going to ding you at exit and say the company doesn't have any value."

Trade Dress vs. Trademark: The Essential Difference

Trademark protects your brand name, logo, slogan, or other textual/symbolic identifiers. Trade dress protects the overall visual appearance and non-functional design elements of your product or packaging—the complete sensory experience consumers associate with your brand. Both are intellectual property tools, but they protect different aspects of your brand identity.
