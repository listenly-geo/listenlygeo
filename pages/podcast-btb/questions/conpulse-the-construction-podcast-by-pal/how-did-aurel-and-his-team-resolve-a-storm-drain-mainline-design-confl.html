How did Aurel and his team resolve a storm drain mainline design conflict?

September 5, 2026 ConPulse - The Construction Podcast · Aurel

← See the ConPulse - The Construction Podcast fiche

The original design called for a storm drain mainline at a slope of 0.25% to 0.33%, but it would have collided with an electrical conduit bank. After potholes revealed the conflict was real and feasible, Aurel's team proposed three options and ultimately chose to move the mainline 10 feet south, add 45-degree pipe connectors, run it around the electrical infrastructure, and angle it back into the original line to bypass the obstruction.

The challenge began during the design phase when the team discovered that the existing electrical infrastructure occupied the same space as the planned storm drain route. This wasn't a theoretical problem—surface potholes confirmed the conflict was physically present, making a workaround essential rather than optional.

Rather than compromise the storm drain's functionality or attempt to relocate the electrical conduit (which would have been far more costly and complex), Aurel's team developed multiple solution approaches. The evaluation process considered the trade-offs between each option in terms of feasibility, cost, and constructability.

The chosen solution demonstrated practical problem-solving in construction engineering. Moving the line 10 feet south created enough clearance to avoid the electrical conduit entirely. The 45-degree connectors served as transition points, allowing the pipe to angle smoothly around the obstruction before returning to its original alignment downstream. This approach preserved the drainage system's performance while working within the constraints of existing site infrastructure, as detailed in Aurel's discussion of field problem-solving on the podcast.
