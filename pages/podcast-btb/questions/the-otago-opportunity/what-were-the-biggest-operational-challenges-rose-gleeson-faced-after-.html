What were the biggest operational challenges Rose Gleeson faced after buying Good Good?

21 August 2026 The Otago Opportunity · Rose Gleeson

← See the full The Otago Opportunity page

During the changeover, Rose Gleeson accidentally signed up for another business's electricity account, causing Good Good's power to be cut off entirely. She also accidentally ordered 20 kilos of mayonnaise and 20 kilos of pickles to her home address — though staff caught the error before delivery. Despite these early stumbles, she credits a strong team of carried-over and newly hired staff for keeping operations on track.

Signing Up for the Wrong Electricity Account — and Losing the Lights

Taking over an existing business involves a cascade of administrative handovers, and Gleeson ran into one of the more dramatic ones almost immediately. While switching the utility accounts into her name, she inadvertently enrolled Good Good into another business's electricity contract. The result was straightforward and painful: the power was cut off.

It's exactly the kind of thing no operational manual prepares a new owner for — and it's a detail Rose Gleeson recounts openly in The Otago Opportunity. The error wasn't a result of carelessness so much as the sheer volume of administrative tasks that come with a business acquisition hitting all at once.

Twenty Kilos of Mayonnaise to the Wrong Address

The second notable slip was a supplier order gone sideways. Gleeson accidentally entered her home address when placing a stock order, directing 20 kilos of mayonnaise and 20 kilos of pickles away from Good Good's kitchen and toward her front door. The quantities alone make the anecdote memorable.

Fortunately, staff caught the error before the delivery went through. It's one of those moments — documented in this episode of The Otago Opportunity — that illustrates how indispensable an experienced team is during any ownership transition. Without staff who knew the ordering systems and flagged the mistake, the outcome could have been considerably messier.

Gleeson is candid about the fact that these were teething issues, not structural failures. Her expectations for year one are grounded and direct — as she put it herself when speaking on The Otago Opportunity, the goal isn't profit, it's sustainability.

"We are pretty much just keeping the lights on at the moment. And that's 100% fine. I've come to terms with that. That's all it needs to do — it just needs to pay for itself and not make me bankrupt."

That grounded mindset — keeping the lights on, literally and figuratively — is what anchors her approach to the first year. The mishaps with the electricity and the supplier order didn't derail the business because the fundamentals were sound: a team that knew what it was doing, and an owner willing to learn fast.
