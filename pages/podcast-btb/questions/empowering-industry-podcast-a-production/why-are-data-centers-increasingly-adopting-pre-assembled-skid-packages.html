Why are data centers increasingly adopting pre-assembled skid packages instead of assembling equipment in the field?

September 6, 2026 Empowering Industry Podcast - A Production of Empowering Pumps & Equipment · Rick Trammell

← See the Empowering Industry Podcast - A Production of Empowering Pumps & Equipment fiche

Data centers are racing against tight construction schedules, and pre-assembled skid packages eliminate the bottleneck of on-site assembly by delivering fully engineered, piped, and tested equipment ready to bolt into place. This approach reduces costs, cuts down on the need for highly skilled field technicians, and ensures equipment specifications are locked in before installation—eliminating delays caused by design changes or field troubleshooting.

Speed as the Core Driver

Data center construction moves at a relentless pace. In this competitive environment, equipment delivery and installation cannot be the constraint. By pre-purchasing pumps, tanks, and heat exchangers and assembling them into fully integrated skids in a controlled facility environment, developers know exactly what they're getting and how it will fit before the first component arrives on site.

As Rick Trammell explains in the episode, the chiller and skid are ready to bolt up immediately, which solves the time problem while also dramatically reducing expense. There is no waiting for components, no on-site fabrication delays, and no last-minute design modifications that would halt progress.

The Skills Gap and Labor Reality

The traditional field-assembly model relied on teams of highly qualified HVAC and mechanical technicians to configure complex piping, routing, and integration on-site. That labor pool is shrinking. Skid packages address this directly by moving assembly work into factories where skilled fabricators and engineers control every step, reducing dependency on hard-to-find specialized field personnel.

This shift doesn't eliminate skilled work—it relocates and concentrates it. Facilities like TACO's operation in Lubbock, Texas have become hubs of expertise where welding, engineering, and precision assembly happen under controlled conditions, then the completed product travels to the data center site where installation crews focus purely on placement and connection.

For a deeper look at how this operational shift plays out across the industry, including how teams at TACO are handling the expansion and why they chose Lubbock as the hub for custom skid manufacturing, the full episode offers rich context on facility strategy and equipment sourcing decisions.

Key takeaways

  • Pre-assembled skids compress data center construction timelines by eliminating on-site assembly work and design delays.
  • Pre-engineering and testing in a factory environment reduces unexpected failures and change orders during the critical construction phase.
  • Skid packaging addresses the shortage of specialized HVAC technicians by centralizing complex fabrication work in controlled manufacturing facilities.
  • Integrated packages reduce total project cost by lowering labor hours, minimizing rework, and ensuring equipment compatibility before installation.
