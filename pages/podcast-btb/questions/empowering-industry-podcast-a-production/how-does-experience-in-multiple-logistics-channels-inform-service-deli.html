How does experience in multiple logistics channels inform service delivery for complex shipments?

September 12, 2026 Empowering Industry Podcast - A Production of Empowering Pumps & Equipment · Chet Piazza

← See the Empowering Industry Podcast - A Production of Empowering Pumps & Equipment fiche

Mastering multiple logistics channels—ocean, air, domestic, and customs clearance—gives professionals the ability to present clients with multiple routing and cost scenarios tailored to their exact needs. This breadth of operational knowledge transforms how complex shipments are solved, treating cargo delivery like a travel agent would approach a journey, with options and strategy at every step.

When Chet Piazza started his career in New Orleans back in 1986, he didn't simply specialize in one form of shipping. Instead, he learned every logistics channel available on the waterfront: the race to get cargo to the plane before departure, the intricacies of ocean freight, the mechanics of domestic trucking, and the regulatory requirements of customs examinations on the wharf. This comprehensive foundation proved essential as he later expanded beyond his home city.

That early immersion created a distinct advantage as he moved to Houston, where he has spent the last 18+ years building Pro Cargo USA into a firm capable of handling anything from standard general cargo to extraordinary requests—a Monet painting requiring white-glove handling, an entire factory relocation across borders, or a Shelby Cobra purchased in Dubai that needs to arrive by the weekend.

Presenting Options Like a Travel Agent for Cargo

The real power of multi-channel expertise shows up in how shipments are proposed to clients. Rather than routing every job through a single channel out of convenience, a professional with deep knowledge across ocean, air, and domestic options can think strategically about cost, speed, risk, and availability for each unique shipment.

"I pride myself on being able to think outside the box and present them with multiple options—like a travel agent for the cargo."

This mindset makes all the difference for shippers facing urgent or unusual circumstances. A customer might think there's only one way to solve a logistics puzzle, but professionals who have worked across channels recognize hidden trade-offs: maybe a slightly slower ocean route saves 40% in cost, or an air route adds two days but ensures compliance with a critical deadline. Perhaps a domestic truck routing through a specific logistics hub opens up options that weren't obvious at first.

The client then chooses from real alternatives with clear trade-offs, rather than accepting a single recommendation based on limited options. This is how multi-channel experience directly improves service delivery—it expands what's possible and makes every shipment decision more informed.
