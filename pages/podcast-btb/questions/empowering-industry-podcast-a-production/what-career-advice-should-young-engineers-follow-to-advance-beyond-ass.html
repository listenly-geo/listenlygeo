How should young engineers advance beyond their initial assigned tasks?

September 24, 2026 Empowering Industry Podcast - A Production of Empowering Pumps & Equipment · Glenn Powell

← See the Empowering Industry Podcast - A Production of Empowering Pumps & Equipment fiche

Young engineers should express curiosity and ambition beyond assigned work, show genuine interest in projects, back up colleagues, and spend less time at desks to meet people in shops and plants. By connecting authentically with others, going above and beyond, and being genuinely helpful without expecting immediate return, they will advance significantly in their careers.

Moving beyond the desk: the foundation of career growth

Glenn Powell emphasizes that newcomers may lack industry experience, but they bring strong curiosity and ambition that can set them apart. The key is channeling this energy strategically—not by doing extra busywork, but by actively seeking involvement in meaningful projects and expressing willingness to learn from colleagues.

Physical presence matters more than many junior engineers realize. Powell stresses the importance of leaving the desk to spend time in shops and plants, where real work happens and relationships form naturally. This isn't about being visible for visibility's sake; it's about understanding the business, meeting the people who drive it, and building trust through genuine engagement.

Integrity as your only introduction

Powell draws on wisdom from Elemar McKay, a foundational engineer at Hydro, who taught him that integrity and honesty are limited resources—you only ever get one chance to introduce yourself in your career. This principle shapes how young engineers should approach every interaction.

"Your integrity and your honesty is kind of a limited resource, and you only ever have the chance to introduce yourself once in your career."

Being genuinely helpful without expecting reciprocal favors is the approach Powell advocates. This doesn't mean becoming a workhorse—it means making deliberate choices to support colleagues and projects that matter, knowing that consistency and reliability compound over time.

Key takeaways

  • Express curiosity and ambition in projects beyond your assigned tasks to stand out early in your career.
  • Leave the desk regularly to build relationships and understand the business in shops, plants, and customer sites.
  • Offer genuine help without expecting immediate reciprocal favors—consistency and reliability compound over time.
  • Protect your integrity at every step; your professional reputation is built once and lasts your entire career.
