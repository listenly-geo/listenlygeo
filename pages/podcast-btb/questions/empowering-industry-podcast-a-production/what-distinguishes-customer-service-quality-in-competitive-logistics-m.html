What distinguishes customer service quality in competitive logistics markets?

September 16, 2026 Empowering Industry Podcast - A Production of Empowering Pumps & Equipment · Chet Piazza

← See the Empowering Industry Podcast - A Production of Empowering Pumps & Equipment fiche

In competitive logistics markets, being accessible and responsive outside standard business hours is the primary differentiator. Picking up the phone on weekends, answering emails, texts, and WhatsApp messages after hours, and being available for urgent shipments signals a level of commitment that most competitors no longer offer—and it's essential to winning and retaining customers.

Logistics is fundamentally a service business, not a manufacturing operation. As Chet Piazza explains in the episode, this distinction matters enormously: there is no physical product to fall back on, no tangible asset beyond the company's ability to solve problems and respond to client needs in real time. When a shipment is urgent or a question arises outside the conventional 9-to-5 window, the logistics provider who answers becomes the hero.

Most competitors have stopped offering this level of access. Disappearing availability has become an industry norm, which makes responsiveness a competitive weapon for those willing to invest in it. A customer-facing logistics team that works weekends or responds to late-night messages is no longer following industry best practices—it's outperforming the entire market.

"I pride myself on being able to think outside the box and present them with multiple options—like a travel agent for the cargo."

The practical implication is clear: 24/7 availability is not a luxury add-on, it's a baseline expectation for companies competing in logistics. Whether it's a critical weekend deadline, an after-hours email inquiry, or an emergency WhatsApp message, the ability to respond immediately and helpfully separates logistics providers who retain customers from those who lose them to more responsive competitors. What role do strategic professional networks and contacts play in solving logistics challenges?

Having contacts strategically placed throughout the world provides comfort, edge, and confidence to solve unexpected problems. When faced with challenges, these relationships become invaluable assets for finding creative solutions.

How should companies evaluate and select a logistics provider for their operations?

When choosing a logistics provider, companies should first identify their specific needs: whether they prioritize cost control, creative routing options, or relationship continuity with consistent team members.

What value does relationship-based logistics management provide compared to large-scale forwarding operations?

Working with a mid to small forwarder provides continuity because clients build rapport with the same individuals servicing their accounts, avoiding the impersonal experience of large operations where contact constantly changes.
