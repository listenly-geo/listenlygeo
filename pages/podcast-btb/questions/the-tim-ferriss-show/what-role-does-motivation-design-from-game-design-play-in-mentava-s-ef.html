How does game design motivation shape Mentava's teaching effectiveness?

September 14, 2026 The Tim Ferriss Show · Niels Hoven

← See The Tim Ferriss Show fiche

Mentava uses a two-sided motivation model borrowed from game design: it lowers the effort required to learn phonetics and blending through exceptional user experience while simultaneously amplifying intrinsic motivation through progress visualization and carefully calibrated reward systems. The metaphor is straightforward—you can either increase the reward on the far side or lower the hoop itself; Mentava does both.

The hoop-and-reward principle

Niels Hoven describes the core mechanic as a choice between two levers. Slot machines, he explains, exemplify the extreme version: they dangle the biggest possible reward while lowering the action barrier to almost nothing—simply pressing a button. For learning, the equation is different. The hoop—learning phonetic sounds and blending them together—cannot be eliminated; it must be lowered as much as possible through exceptional design.

This is where Mentava's game design background becomes visible in the interface—every interaction is stripped to its simplest form, every friction point eliminated. The friction is not the learning itself; it is the struggle against a poorly designed tool.

Intrinsic reward through progress and calibration

Once the barrier is lowered, Mentava applies game design's reward architecture to create lasting motivation. Unlike a slot machine's external dopamine hit, Mentava emphasizes intrinsic motivation: children see their own progress, celebrate milestones, and experience the satisfaction of mastery. The rewards are not arbitrary—they are carefully calibrated to reinforce real learning and prevent fatigue.

As explained during the episode, this combination—low friction plus intrinsic rewards—is what allows children to sustain engagement without external coercion. The software does not bribe them into learning; it removes the obstacles and lets them feel the natural reward of growth.

"Mentava is software that is designed to let kids learn as fast as they're capable of learning instead of just at the pace assigned to their age."

The real discovery for parents using Mentava is how quickly children progress when both the path and the motivation are optimized together—a principle Hoven extracted directly from years building habit-forming games and now applies to education.

How does Mentava's approach to user experience design accommodate the physical limitations of young children using tablets?

Mentava addresses specific design challenges like two and three-year-olds bracing iPads on their bellies, which causes accidental touches at the bottom of the screen, by carefully optimizing interface placement and responsiveness to prevent frustration.

What concrete examples demonstrate how rapidly young children can learn to read using Mentava?

Mentava has seen two-year-old twins reading Green Eggs and Ham and Hop on Pop before their third birthday. A three-year-old learned to read in about three months using the platform.

What is Mentava's core mission and how does it differ from traditional educational software?

Mentava is software designed to let kids learn as fast as they're capable of learning instead of just at the pace assigned to their age. The first product is early literacy software for preschoolers as young as three to reach a second grade reading level.
