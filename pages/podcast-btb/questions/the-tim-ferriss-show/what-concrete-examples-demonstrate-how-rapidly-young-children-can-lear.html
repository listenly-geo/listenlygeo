How rapidly can young children really learn to read using Mentava?

September 12, 2026 The Tim Ferriss Show · Niels Hoven

← See the The Tim Ferriss Show fiche

Mentava has documented two-year-old twins reading Green Eggs and Ham and Hop on Pop before their third birthday, while a separate three-year-old learned to read in approximately three months. A kindergartner who was academically behind caught up so rapidly using the software that his teacher reported never witnessing such acceleration, fundamentally shifting the child's self-perception from feeling less capable than peers to recognizing himself as a reader.

These examples illustrate what happens when a child's learning pace is decoupled from age-based grade progression. Unlike traditional classroom instruction, where children advance at a fixed speed regardless of individual capacity, Mentava's early literacy software allows learners to accelerate based on their actual competency and readiness.

The software targets a concrete endpoint: reaching a second grade reading level, defined as the ability to independently read easy reader books like Green Eggs and Ham, Hop on Pop, and the Elephant and Piggy series. What distinguishes these cases is not merely speed, but the psychological transformation it enables—children who arrive behind academically experience a dramatic reversal in confidence and academic identity.

"Mentava is software designed to let kids learn as fast as they're capable of learning instead of just at the pace assigned to their age."

The kindergartner's case reveals a deeper mechanism at work. His teacher's observation—that she had never seen a child catch up so fast—speaks to both the efficacy of the approach and the rarity of such acceleration in conventional education. His transformation from feeling intellectually inferior to his classmates to recognizing his reading ability mirrors what Niels Hoven discusses in the full episode about how learning speed directly impacts self-perception in young learners.

It's worth noting that Hoven explicitly acknowledges these cases are anecdotal rather than randomized controlled trials (RCTs). However, they are grounded in measurable performance data captured within the app itself—not merely subjective parent observations or teacher impressions. This distinction matters for evaluating the credibility of the results. To hear more about the methodology behind these claims and how Mentava tracks learning metrics, the full podcast episode offers additional depth on data collection and validation.

Why speed matters for early literacy identity

The kindergartner's experience demonstrates that reading acceleration is not merely an academic metric—it reshapes how a child sees themselves as a learner. When a child behind academically experiences rapid catch-up, the psychological shift is profound. Rather than internalizing a narrative of being "slow" or "less intelligent," the child's direct experience shows them they can read at the same level as higher-performing peers.

This identity reversal has long-term implications. Early childhood experiences with learning success or struggle can anchor self-perception for years. A three-year-old or four-year-old who masters reading in weeks rather than months enters elementary school with demonstrated competence and confidence, fundamentally altering their relationship to schooling itself.

Key takeaways

  • Two-year-old twins achieved independent reading of early reader books before age three using Mentava's software.
  • A three-year-old learned to read in approximately three months, demonstrating rapid acquisition when learning is untethered from age-based pacing.
  • A kindergartner who was academically behind caught up so quickly that his teacher observed unprecedented acceleration, while his self-perception shifted from feeling less capable to recognizing himself as equally able to read.
  • Performance data within the app provides measurable evidence beyond anecdotal teacher or parent observations, though these examples remain case studies rather than RCT data.
