What innovative technologies and prefabrication methods are being used in modern hospital construction to improve safety and schedule efficiency?

September 25, 2026 Forum Focus · Joe Lewandowski

← See the Forum Focus fiche

McCarthy Building Company is deploying Revisto, a 3D coordination platform, combined with prefabricated bathroom pods and modular piping/ductwork racks to identify design conflicts before construction and reduce on-site material stockpiling. These methods cut congestion, accelerate installation, and tighten schedule delivery on the BJC Healthcare Campus Renewal Phase 3 project.

3D Coordination: Finding Problems Before the Crew Arrives

The core innovation is Revisto, a 3D coordination platform similar to Navisworks, which allows the team to work in both 3D and 2D combined fashion. This hybrid approach enables rapid identification and resolution of design conflicts—clashes between structural elements, MEP (mechanical, electrical, plumbing) systems, and architectural components—long before crews step on site.

By catching these issues in the virtual model, McCarthy avoids costly rework, delays, and safety hazards that would otherwise emerge during framing or finishing phases. The platform reduces field conflicts by allowing all trades to coordinate in a single visual environment, dramatically compressing decision cycles and keeping the project on schedule.

Off-Site Prefabrication: Speed and Control

McCarthy and its trade partners are prefabricating entire bathroom pods through a joint venture of local contractors, then flying them in to the site. This method removes one of the slowest and most error-prone trades from the congested job site—bathroom assembly now happens in a controlled factory environment with quality checks before delivery.

Beyond bathrooms, racks for piping, ductwork, and electrical conduit are also prefabricated and brought to site just-in-time, dramatically reducing the number of materials and workers crowding the site at any given moment. This lean approach minimizes congestion in an extraordinarily tight urban setting at Kings Highway and Barnes Place, where the hospital must remain operational throughout construction.

"We not only take pride in building for BJC, who's a national leader among health care organizations, but also feel special to be building a hospital that's so important to the people in and around St. Louis."

The podcast also explores how McCarthy manages logistics in a congested urban hospital environment where patient access and existing operations cannot be interrupted—a challenge unique to healthcare replacement projects in dense urban centers.

Key takeaways

  • Revisto 3D coordination allows combined 3D/2D workflow to identify and resolve design conflicts before construction begins, preventing costly rework and delays.
  • Bathroom pods are prefabricated off-site by local trade partners through a joint venture and flown in, removing bathroom installation from the congested site.
  • Piping, ductwork, and electrical racks are prefabricated and delivered just-in-time, minimizing material stockpiling and reducing peak workforce pressure on site.
  • This combination of virtual coordination and lean logistics is essential in congested urban hospital settings where ongoing patient care must be maintained.

How did the COVID-19 pandemic impact the timeline of a major healthcare campus construction project?

McCarthy was brought onto the project in December 2019 for design and pre-construction services. The pandemic caused BJC leadership to pause the project for evaluation, creating approximately a nine-month delay before construction rescheduled to November 2021.

What makes healthcare construction project logistics in congested urban hospital settings particularly challenging?

The intersection of Kings Highway and Barnes at the hospital plaza is an extraordinarily congested space. Lewandowski's team must coordinate workflow carefully to manage traffic, maintain patient access to the existing medical center, and execute construction safely in an active hospital environment.

What is the current workforce size and trajectory for a growing minority-owned construction contractor?

The BJC Phase 3 project involves 100 to 150 trade partners and reaches peak workforce of 300 to 325 workers in 2023, with 25% minority-owned and 5% women-owned business enterprises participation, plus 25% minorities and 5% women in the workforce.
