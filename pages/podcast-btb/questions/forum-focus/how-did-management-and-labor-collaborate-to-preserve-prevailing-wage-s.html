How did management and labor collaborate to preserve prevailing wage standards in Missouri?

September 4, 2026 Forum Focus · Doug Martin

← See the Forum Focus fiche

A coalition of management and labor leadership spent a decade modernizing Missouri's Preventive Wage Law, preserving core protections for construction workers' safe and fair wages while making the law more receptive to competition. The effort ensured that tax dollars support construction work conducted by skilled, properly paid workers rather than undercutting standards through cheaper, less skilled providers.

The preservation of prevailing wage standards in Missouri represents a rare moment of unified advocacy across two traditionally opposing sides of the construction industry. Rather than fighting over which side would win or lose, management and labor recognized a shared interest in protecting the integrity of the construction market itself.

Doug Martin, who spent over 40 years building these relationships as part of his work with the electrical contractors industry, explains how this collaboration took shape. As he describes it in the Forum Focus episode, the challenge was not choosing between protecting workers or enabling business growth—it was designing a system that could do both.

Ten years of modernization without compromise

The 10-year scrutiny of Missouri's Preventive Wage Law was not a quick agreement. Instead, it required sustained dialogue and incremental refinement. Both sides agreed to modernize the law in ways that addressed concerns about regulatory flexibility while defending the fundamental protection: no public dollars would fund substandard labor practices.

This approach created a model where competition could flourish—but not through a race to the bottom on wages. Companies could still compete on efficiency, innovation, and service quality. The wage floor, however, remained non-negotiable. The specific protections that emerged from this process reflected a pragmatic understanding: when tax dollars are involved, society has a right to expect both fair wages and skilled work.

"I've worked my whole career with an organization that simply wanted to build an appropriate and productive relationship with our labor partners."

The preservation effort also demonstrates how institutional memory and trust matter. A coalition built over decades, with leaders who had worked together through multiple cycles of negotiation and crisis, could navigate complex policy issues without defaulting to zero-sum thinking. The outcome was neither a labor victory nor a management victory—it was an industry victory.

What new diversity and inclusion infrastructure did NECA create to address historical barriers in electrical contracting?

NECA created a Director of Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion position for the first time in the last 18 months and hired a confident electrician to ensure commitment to addressing systemic barriers in the electrical contracting industry.

What structural changes to apprenticeship recruitment doubled the number of electricians entering training?

NECA changed from recruiting annually and placing one class based on classroom capacity to a continuous probationary apprenticeship model that recruits and places candidates year-round, significantly increasing the pipeline of new electricians.

How did a shift from adversarial labor-management conflict to productive partnership transform a regional electrical contractors industry?

Over 40 years, NECA St. Louis evolved from a time where contractors and labor could barely sit in a room to speak amiably, with negotiations conducted through structured dialogue and mutual commitment to industry advancement and worker welfare.

Key takeaways

  • Management and labor can align around shared interests—in this case, protecting the integrity of the construction market from undercutting through substandard wages and skill levels.
  • Modernizing a prevailing wage law does not require abandoning worker protections; it means redesigning them to function in a competitive environment without compromising core safeguards.
  • A decade of sustained dialogue and incremental refinement produced a compromise that neither side had to view as defeat, transforming a regulatory defense into an industry standard.
  • Trust built over decades of relationship-building enabled both sides to negotiate complex policy without reverting to adversarial tactics.
