Why are homeowners pulling listings from the market at record rates despite favorable selling conditions?

September 1, 2026 Real Estate Intelligence Daily — PropTech, Finance & Commercial Market Insights

← See the Real Estate Intelligence Daily — PropTech, Finance & Commercial Market Insights fiche

Homeowners are unwilling to trade ultra-low pandemic-era mortgage rates locked in at 3% or 4% for mortgages above 6%. Rather than accept lower-than-expected prices or higher borrowing costs, many are staying put—creating an unprecedented housing standoff where 5.8% of home listings were pulled in April 2025, the fastest pace since March 2020.

The core issue is straightforward: a homeowner who secured a 30-year mortgage at 3% or 4% during the pandemic faces an impossible trade-off. Selling means borrowing at today's higher rates—a move that makes little financial sense. As rates climbed above 6%, the mental calculation shifted from "Should I sell?" to "Can I afford to sell?"

This rate lock-in effect is creating genuine market paralysis. Many sellers unable to get the lofty prices they expected in 2021–2022 have chosen to withdraw their listings rather than accept the new market reality. The result: a dearth of inventory precisely when spring should bring peak activity. As discussed in this podcast, this dynamic is intensifying competition for buyers and stymieing sales during what is normally a busy season.

When the Math No Longer Works: The Delisting Surge

Delisting rates have reached levels not seen since the early pandemic lockdown. In April 2025, nearly one in 17 listings were removed from the market—a signal that homeowners are making active, deliberate decisions to stay put rather than accept the terms offered. Atlanta experienced even sharper pressure, with roughly one in 10 home sellers withdrawing listings.

Price expectations have become a secondary concern to rate protection. Even in markets where prices remain healthy, sellers are reluctant to move if it means exchanging a 3% mortgage for a 6%+ alternative. This psychological friction—the "rate shock" of refinancing at double the original rate—is powerful enough to override traditional selling incentives like neighborhood demand or family relocation.

The delisting surge reflects a deeper truth: mortgage rates are no longer just a transaction cost, they are a long-term wealth anchor. Homeowners understand that locking in a sub-4% rate in today's environment is an asset. Surrendering it requires genuine hardship or extraordinary opportunity, not merely favorable market conditions.

Key takeaways

  • Rate lock-in is the primary driver of delisting, not price or market conditions—homeowners prefer to stay put rather than refinance into higher borrowing costs.
  • 5.8% of listings pulled in April 2025 marks the fastest pace since March 2020, signaling a historic inventory squeeze driven by rational financial self-interest.
  • This stalemate paradoxically leaves buyers with less choice and sellers unable to move, creating mutual frustration despite what nominally appear to be favorable market signals.
  • Mortgage rates have become a long-term wealth asset in homeowners' eyes, outweighing traditional selling motivations like relocation or market demand.
