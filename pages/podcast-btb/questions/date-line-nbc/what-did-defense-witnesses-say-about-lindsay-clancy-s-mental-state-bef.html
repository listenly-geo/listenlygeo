What did defense witnesses say about Lindsay Clancy's mental state before the killings?

August 22, 2026 Dateline NBC

← See the Dateline NBC podcast page

Defense witnesses painted a picture of a woman in severe and escalating psychiatric crisis. Lindsay Clancy's sister testified that by December 2022, Clancy had depression, insomnia, and suicidal ideations every single day. Her mother revealed that Clancy had privately confided she was having thoughts of harming her children — a disclosure made public for the first time at trial.

A sister's testimony: daily suicidal ideations through Christmas 2022

Alison Osga, Lindsay Clancy's sister, took the stand to describe how Clancy's condition deteriorated sharply in the weeks leading up to January 24, 2023 — the night Clancy is accused of strangling her three young children. Osga testified that by Christmas 2022, her sister's mood was worsening every day. She described depression and insomnia as constants, not episodes.

Most strikingly, Osga testified that Clancy had suicidal ideations every single day that December. This testimony directly challenges the prosecution's assertion — built on roughly 70 witnesses over three weeks — that Clancy was of sound mind on the night of the killings. The defense account, covered in detail on Dateline NBC, positions Clancy not as a calculated killer but as someone who had already lost control of her own mind.

"Something is wrong" — a mother reads her daughter's texts aloud in court

Paula Musgrove, Clancy's mother, delivered what may be the most emotionally charged testimony of the defense phase. She read aloud a text message Lindsay Clancy sent on October 20, 2022 — months before the killings — in which Clancy wrote: "Something is wrong" and "I think it's made things worse."

The messages suggest Clancy was already aware something was deeply wrong with her mental state, and was reaching out to her mother in distress. As reported in this episode of Dateline NBC, Musgrove also revealed for the first time at trial that Clancy had confided to her that she was having thoughts of harming her children. This disclosure had never been made public before the trial.

The combination — a written cry for help in October, escalating daily suicidal ideations in December, and a private confession about intrusive thoughts toward her children — forms the core of the defense's argument that Clancy was not criminally responsible for her actions.

"She was begging for help."

The case has drawn comparisons to that of Andrea Yates, the Texas mother who drowned her five children in 2001 and was ultimately found not guilty by reason of insanity after a second trial. The defense team in the Clancy case is pursuing a similar strategy, arguing that severe postpartum psychiatric illness robbed Clancy of the ability to understand the nature of her actions. The full context of these arguments is explored in Dateline NBC's coverage of the trial.

Clancy was 36 years old and a former labor and delivery nurse — a detail that adds a layer of tragic irony to the case. She had professional knowledge of perinatal care, yet apparently could not access the help she needed for herself. The jury, which skews older with more than half of the 18 jurors believed to be in their 50s or above, will ultimately weigh this evidence against the prosecution's case, which rested after approximately three weeks of testimony.
