How much did a paramedic receive from life insurance policies after his wife's death?

September 23, 2026 Dateline NBC · Marissa Meyer

← See the Dateline NBC fiche

Joshua Hunsucker received over $200,000 from two life insurance policies belonging to his wife Stacey, according to the North Carolina Department of Insurance. The substantial payout became a key piece of evidence in the case against the former paramedic, who faced accusations far more complex than a simple wrongful death scenario.

A Motive Emerges From Insurance Records

The $200,000 figure represents far more than just a financial transaction. When combined with other evidence, the life insurance payout formed part of a troubling pattern examined in the investigation into Stacey Hunsucker's death. In September 2018, the preschool teacher died suddenly at just 32 years old, leaving two daughters behind.

The circumstances surrounding Stacey's death grew increasingly suspicious over time. Investigators discovered that tetrahydrosoline—a chemical found in eye drops—was present in her blood at 30 to 40 times the therapeutic dose. This discovery, detailed in a Dateline NBC episode, transformed what appeared to be a medical tragedy into a potential poisoning case.

Joshua Hunsucker remained free for over a year after his wife's death. He was arrested in late 2019, more than 12 months after September 2018. What began as questions about Stacey's sudden illness evolved into a full criminal investigation when the insurance records and toxicology results intersected.

A Case With Layers of Bizarre Accusations

The Hunsucker case became unusual not only for the alleged poisoning but for the extraordinary range of charges that followed. Beyond the death itself, Joshua Hunsucker faced accusations of faking his own kidnapping and allegedly committing arson on a medical helicopter. In 2021, he was arrested on the arson charge related to an alleged incident during flight in 2019.

The $200,000 insurance payout, while a meaningful motive in isolation, became one element in a series of actions that prosecutors argued revealed a pattern of deception. As Marissa Meyer reports in the Dateline episode, the case drew further complexity from Hunsucker's subsequent behavior after his arrest, including the staged kidnapping claim in 2023.

The trial itself faced multiple delays before being scheduled for January 2027. The case highlights how financial motive, forensic evidence, and behavioral patterns can weave together in a criminal investigation—and why the insurance payout alone might not have raised sufficient alarm without the chemical evidence that followed.

What are the physiological effects of ingesting tetrahydrosoline from eye drops?

When ingested, tetrahydrosoline can cause vomiting, nausea, diarrhea, seizing, and even cardiac arrest. This toxic potential made the chemical found in Stacey Hunsucker's blood a critical piece of evidence.

What chemical found in eye drops was detected in a victim's blood at 30 to 40 times the therapeutic dose?

According to the probable cause affidavit, tetrahydrosoline—a chemical found in eye drops—was detected in Stacey's blood at concentrations 30 to 40 times higher than the therapeutic dose, indicating poisoning rather than accidental exposure.

What are ghost guns and why do they complicate criminal investigations?

Ghost guns are homemade firearms that do not have a serial number. Without serial numbers, they cannot be traced through standard firearm databases, making them difficult for investigators to link to suspects or establish ownership.
