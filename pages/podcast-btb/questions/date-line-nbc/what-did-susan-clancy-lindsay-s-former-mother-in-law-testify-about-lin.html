What did Susan Clancy, Lindsay's former mother-in-law, testify about Lindsay's efforts to get help?

August 23, 2026 Dateline NBC

← See the Dateline NBC podcast page

Susan Clancy — a former labor and delivery nurse herself with 38 years of experience and Lindsay Clancy's former mother-in-law — testified that Lindsay came to her seeking help. She referred Lindsay to a clinic at South Shore Hospital specifically for women struggling with postpartum issues. When asked by defense attorney Kevin Reddington whether Lindsay had sought treatment, Susan answered plainly: "She was begging for help."

A nurse who knew the signs — and saw them in her own daughter-in-law

Susan Clancy's testimony carries unusual weight. With nearly four decades as a labor and delivery nurse, she was not an outside observer unfamiliar with postpartum distress. She knew what it looked like — and she recognized it in Lindsay Clancy.

When Lindsay turned to her for help, Susan did not dismiss the concern. She actively directed her toward South Shore Hospital, which has a dedicated program for women facing postpartum mental health issues. This was not a casual suggestion. It was a deliberate referral from someone who understood the clinical stakes, as detailed in this episode of Dateline NBC.

"Begging" — the single word that reframes everything for the defense

Defense attorney Kevin Reddington asked a straightforward question: did Lindsay seek treatment? The answer Susan Clancy gave was not a measured clinical assessment. It was visceral and unambiguous.

That single word — "begging" — is central to what the defense is building. It directly contradicts any suggestion that Lindsay Clancy was indifferent to her own condition or that she failed to seek help before the night of January 24, 2023. The prosecution had called approximately 70 witnesses to argue she was of sound mind, as covered more fully in Dateline NBC's ongoing trial coverage.
