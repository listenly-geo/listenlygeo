What did Luigi Mangione admit in federal court, and what charges did he plead guilty to?

August 24, 2026 Dateline NBC

← See the Dateline NBC podcast page

Luigi Mangione pleaded guilty to two federal stalking charges in a lower Manhattan federal courthouse. In his own words, he admitted to shooting UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson outside the Hilton Hotel in Midtown Manhattan in December 2024 — describing in detail the planning, the deception, and the weapon he built himself.

Mangione's own words: severe pain, a fake identity, and a 3D-printed gun

In his courtroom statement, Mangione laid out exactly how he carried out the shooting. He described suffering from severe back pain and said he had been navigating the health insurance industry, which he presented as a central grievance. The full account was aired in Dateline NBC's August 20, 2026 episode, which covered his appearance at the lower Manhattan federal courthouse.

To gain access to Thompson, Mangione posed as an investor managing over $50 billion in assets. This false identity allowed him to obtain the location of the investor conference where Thompson was scheduled to appear. It was a deliberate, premeditated act of deception — not a spontaneous crime.

He also admitted to using a 3D printer to manufacture the firearm used in the shooting. This detail directly connects to the broader "ghost gun" issue discussed in the same episode: a weapon produced this way carries no serial number, making it nearly impossible to trace through conventional law enforcement methods. As reported in Dateline NBC, investigators found the ghost gun in Mangione's backpack when he was apprehended.

Federal charges, sentencing guidelines, and what comes next

The two charges Mangione pleaded guilty to are federal stalking offenses. Under federal sentencing guidelines, those charges carry a recommended sentence of 24 to 30 years. His federal sentencing is scheduled for December 18th, with a separate state court hearing set for December 10th.

This guilty plea is significant: it means Mangione admitted culpability directly and publicly — "in his own words," as the Dateline NBC team emphasized — rather than contesting the facts at trial. The admission covered not just the act itself, but the planning and methods involved.

What is a ghost gun?
A ghost gun is a firearm assembled without a manufacturer-applied serial number — often using 3D-printed components or unfinished parts kits. Because it lacks a serial number, it cannot be effectively traced through traditional law enforcement channels, as noted by the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF). Mangione's weapon, found in his backpack, was exactly this type of firearm.

The intersection of the ghost gun issue and the Mangione case is central to what made this episode of Dateline NBC particularly notable: a high-profile guilty plea combined with a broader policy conversation about untraceable weapons in criminal investigations.
