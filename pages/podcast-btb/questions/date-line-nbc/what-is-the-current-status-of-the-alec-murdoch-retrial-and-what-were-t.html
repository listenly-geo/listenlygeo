What is the current status of the Alec Murdoch retrial and what were the outcomes of the recent pretrial hearing?

September 18, 2026 Dateline NBC

← See the Dateline NBC fiche

Alec Murdoch appeared for his second pretrial hearing after the South Carolina Supreme Court overturned his double murder convictions in May. Both sides agreed to change the trial venue, though the judge must still decide the new location—and in a major prosecution victory, the judge ruled that portions of Murdoch's first trial testimony will be admissible in the retrial.

The retrial represents a critical next phase for Murdoch after his original conviction was set aside. His appearance at the pretrial hearing brought the dual murder case back into active court proceedings following the appellate ruling that had invalidated his May conviction.

A central issue resolved at the hearing concerned the trial location itself. As covered in this episode of Dateline NBC, both the prosecution and defense agreed that a venue change was necessary—but they remained at odds over where the new trial should take place. The judge deferred a final decision on the location to a later ruling.

The most consequential development for prosecutors came with the judge's decision on Murdoch's prior testimony admissibility. Allowing portions of his first trial testimony to be introduced in the retrial significantly strengthens the state's position, as it means previously recorded statements from Murdoch himself will be available as evidence without requiring him to testify again in open court.

Keith Morrison detailed these developments and their implications in Dateline's Roundup segment, where the Alec Murdoch saga continues to unfold with each court appearance.

Why This Ruling Matters for the Prosecution

The admission of Murdoch's prior testimony is understood as a major tactical advantage for prosecutors. By allowing his statements from the first trial into evidence, the court has essentially preserved the core of his earlier testimony without requiring him to repeat it—a procedural win that eliminates the risk that he might alter his story or behave differently under cross-examination in the retrial.

Defense teams typically oppose the use of prior testimony precisely because it forecloses the possibility of a fresh narrative or revised account. The judge's ruling signals confidence in the strength of those earlier statements and their relevance to proving the charges again.

The Venue Question Still Pending

While both sides agreed a venue change was needed—likely because pretrial publicity and local sentiment may have made a fair trial difficult in the original jurisdiction—they could not agree on a destination. This disagreement leaves the judge to select the new venue unilaterally, a decision that could significantly shape jury composition, media coverage, and the overall courtroom environment for the retrial.

The Murdoch case has attracted national attention, and venue selection in high-profile cases often becomes a strategic battleground. Where the trial is held can influence jury pools, expert witness availability, and local attitudes toward the defendant.
