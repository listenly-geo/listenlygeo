After pleading guilty to the University of Idaho murders, what is Brian Koberger now trying to do?

More than a year after pleading guilty to the 2022 killings of Kaylee Goncalves, Madison Mogen, Ethan Chapin, and Xana Kernodle, Brian Koberger filed a petition challenging his guilty plea and conviction. He is claiming innocence and alleging that his former attorneys provided ineffective assistance of counsel and coerced him into accepting the plea. Koberger is currently serving four life sentences.

The case was covered as a major development in an August 2026 episode of Dateline NBC, which reported on the petition alongside several other high-profile criminal proceedings. The ineffective assistance of counsel claim — the argument that his legal representation fell below acceptable professional standards and that the resulting plea was therefore involuntary — is a recognized but demanding legal standard to meet in post-conviction challenges. Koberger's attempt to walk back a guilty plea he has already entered means he faces a significant legal burden to demonstrate that his original counsel's conduct materially affected the outcome.

The University of Idaho murders drew sustained national attention when the four students were found killed in November 2022. Koberger was arrested weeks later, and his eventual guilty plea appeared to close the case — making his current challenge a notable development in an already closely watched legal story.
