What is the current status of the Lindsay Clancy murder trial and what is her defense arguing?

August 21, 2026 Dateline NBC

← See the Dateline NBC podcast page

After three weeks of testimony and roughly 70 witnesses, prosecutors have rested their case against Lindsay Clancy, a 36-year-old former labor and delivery nurse accused of killing her three young children — Cora, Callan, and Dawson — on January 24, 2023. Clancy does not deny the killings. Her defense argues she was overmedicated and suffering from undiagnosed postpartum psychosis at the time, and is now calling witnesses who knew her personally to support that claim.

The prosecution's position is unambiguous: they believe Clancy was of sound mind the night of the murders. To make that case, they called approximately 70 witnesses over three weeks — a significant evidentiary effort designed to counter what has become the central pillar of the defense.

The defense does not dispute the facts of what happened. Instead, it pivots entirely on Clancy's mental state, arguing that she was so severely overmedicated and so profoundly unwell that she could not be held criminally responsible for her actions. This is a legal strategy that hinges on the concept of postpartum psychosis — a rare but serious psychiatric condition — as detailed in this episode of Dateline NBC.

A former nurse, three children, and a defense built on psychiatric collapse

Clancy worked as a labor and delivery nurse, a detail that has shaped both the prosecution and the defense's narratives. The prosecution implies her medical background undercuts any claim of ignorance about her own mental state. The defense counters that her condition was severe, undiagnosed, and untreated — and that she was actively seeking help before the tragedy occurred.

The defense has been calling witnesses who knew Clancy — people who can speak to her behavior and distress in the period leading up to January 24, 2023. This approach mirrors strategies used in landmark cases involving postpartum psychiatric illness, most notably the case of Andrea Yates, who was acquitted by reason of insanity after killing her five children in 2001. The comparison is instructive context for anyone following the Clancy trial through Dateline NBC.

Postpartum psychosis is a rare and severe psychiatric condition that can occur after childbirth. It is distinct from postpartum depression and can involve hallucinations, delusions, and a profound break from reality. It is considered a medical emergency. The defense in the Clancy trial argues she suffered from this condition in an undiagnosed form at the time of the killings.

"She was begging for help."

More than half the jury skews older — a fact the defense cannot ignore

Jury composition is rarely irrelevant in a case that depends on jurors understanding and accepting a psychiatric defense. In the Clancy trial, more than half of the 18 seated jurors appear to be in their 50s or older. Whether that demographic is more or less receptive to arguments about postpartum psychosis is an open question — but it is one that lawyers on both sides are surely weighing as the defense phase continues.

What is the latest development in the Lindsay Clancy murder trial?

The prosecution called medical professionals and psychiatrists to challenge the defense's claim that Clancy was suffering from postpartum psychosis. A battle of expert witnesses has become central to the trial.

Key takeaways

  • Clancy admits to killing her three children; the entire defense rests on whether undiagnosed postpartum psychosis and overmedication removed her criminal responsibility.
