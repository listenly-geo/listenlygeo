Why did it take nearly 30 years to charge someone with Tupac Shakur's murder?

The case stalled for three converging reasons: the alleged trigger man, Orlando Anderson, died in an unrelated gang shooting just years after Tupac Shakur's murder; a 2009 proffer agreement between Dwayne Keith Davis — also known as Keefe D — and the LAPD legally shielded him from prosecution in exchange for information; and prosecutors simply had no usable evidence against him until Davis chose to repeat his own account publicly, in a memoir and in media interviews.

Tupac Shakur was shot four times on September 7, 1996, near the MGM Grand Casino in Las Vegas, and died six days later at age 25. Anderson — a Crips member and the man authorities believed pulled the trigger — was killed in a separate gang-related incident before charges could ever be filed against him. With the alleged shooter gone, the investigation had no clear path forward.

What is a proffer agreement? A proffer agreement (sometimes called a "queen for a day" agreement) is a deal in which a suspect or witness shares information with law enforcement under a promise that their statements cannot be used directly against them in court. It is a standard investigative tool — but it can create legal barriers to prosecution if the protected party later changes the terms by going public.

The 2009 proffer agreement between Davis and the LAPD was exactly that kind of barrier. Davis provided information, received protection from prosecution, and the case effectively went cold again. What changed everything was Davis's own decision to go on the record: he described events surrounding the murder in his memoir and gave interviews repeating his account. Prosecutors from the Clark County DA's office determined that those voluntary, public statements — made outside the scope of any protected agreement — could be used against him.

"So what authorities are saying is, you said it in your own words. It's kind of tantamount to a confession."

— Dateline NBC, August 6, 2026 episode

Davis was arrested in September 2023 at age 60, nearly 27 years after the murder — charged not as the shooter, but as the man accused of orchestrating it. His trial marks the first time anyone has faced formal prosecution for one of the most notorious unsolved homicides in American music history. Who is Duane Keefe D Davis and why was he arrested for Tupac Shakur's murder?

Dwayne Keith Davis, also known as Keefe D, is the uncle of Orlando "Baby Lane" Anderson, who is believed to be the trigger man in Tupac Shakur's 1996 murder. Davis was arrested in September 2023, nearly 27 years after the killing.

What plea deal did Courtney Clenny agree to and what are the potential sentencing implications?

Courtney Clenny agreed to a plea deal whose specific terms were set to be made public on August 10th. She had been charged with second-degree murder, but the deal appears to involve a lesser charge — with Florida manslaughter guidelines carrying just over nine years, and Clenny having already served four.

What evidence did Courtney Clenny's defense team present to support her version of events?

The defense conducted an experiment using a pig carcass where someone threw a knife at it, arguing the angle and depth of the wound was consistent with a thrown knife — directly contradicting the medical examiner's account.
