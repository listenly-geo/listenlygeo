What is the latest development in the Lindsay Clancy murder trial?

The prosecution called medical professionals and psychiatrists to directly challenge the defense's claim that Lindsay Clancy was suffering from postpartum psychosis when she killed her children. A doctor who treated Clancy in the ICU testified that when asked about her mood, Clancy wrote the word "horrified" on a whiteboard — and also asked, "Do I have an attorney?" Prosecutors argued that both responses demonstrated she retained the mental awareness to understand what she had done and to protect her own legal rights.

The significance of the whiteboard exchange is central to the prosecution's strategy. By asking for an attorney, Clancy showed — in the prosecution's reading — that she understood the gravity of her situation and was capable of rational self-interest in the immediate aftermath. That is a direct rebuttal to the defense's assertion that a severe postpartum psychotic episode had completely severed her grasp on reality at the time of the killings.

"I did ask her her mood and what did she write? She wrote horrified."

— ICU physician testimony, as reported on Dateline True Crime Weekly, August 6, 2026

The Clancy case is one of several high-profile trials being tracked in real time on Dateline NBC's weekly true crime coverage. The postpartum psychosis defense remains the core battleground: if the jury accepts it, Clancy could avoid a murder conviction; if the prosecution's medical witnesses persuade jurors that she understood the nature of her actions, the defense collapses. The testimony from the ICU doctor is among the most direct evidence yet on that precise question.

What is the defense strategy for Dwayne Davis at the Tupac Shakur murder trial?

Davis's defense team argues that the case lacks physical evidence — no gun, no ballistics, and no DNA — which are the types of evidence juries typically expect in a murder case.

Why did it take nearly 30 years to charge someone with Tupac Shakur's murder?

The case stalled partly because Orlando Anderson, the alleged shooter, died in an unrelated gang-related shooting a few years after Tupac's murder, removing the most direct line of evidence.

Who is Duane Keefe D Davis and why was he arrested for Tupac Shakur's murder?

Dwayne Keith Davis, also known as Keefe D, is the uncle of Orlando "Baby Lane" Anderson, who is believed to be the trigger man in Tupac Shakur's 1996 murder.
