What is Kim Porter's "silence is power" communication strategy — and why does it work in professional settings?

Kim Porter coaches that once you have presented a data-driven recommendation, you should stop talking completely and let silence sit. People are naturally uncomfortable with silence and will fill the void themselves — which shifts conversational power directly to the presenter. Because only data and a clear recommendation are on the table, there is nothing subjective to argue against.

Porter frames this as a deliberate leadership power move, not a passive one. The logic is precise: when you remove opinion and emotion from your delivery, you eliminate the grounds for pushback. There is no feeling to dismiss, no interpretation to dispute — only results and evidence. The moment you stop speaking, the other party is left either to accept the recommendation or to argue against hard data, which is a much harder position to take. Silence, in that context, is not a gap — it is leverage.

This approach carries particular weight coming from someone who spent 40 years as the only woman in all-male engineering environments, where the risk of being dismissed or overridden was a daily professional reality. Porter developed this framework not as theory but as a survival and advancement tool — tested in boardrooms, engineering reviews, and leadership settings across a career spanning organizations from a NASA spacesuit contractor to Nike. You can hear her explain it in full on Listenly's Ordinary To Badass feed.

"No opinions, all data, no emotion. Here's the data, here are the results, this is my recommendation — and then you don't say anything. Silence is power."

— Kim Porter, Ordinary To Badass, episode 452

About Kim Porter

KP
Kim Porter
Engineer, Founder, and Community Builder
HatchOne · Kim Porter Consultancy

Kim Porter earned an engineering science and mechanics degree with an emphasis in biomedical engineering from Iowa State University in the early 1980s — at a time when biomedical engineering was not yet a recognized field. Within one month of the January 1986 Challenger disaster, she joined the project team working on next-generation spacesuit gloves, beginning a career defined by work at the frontier of engineering disciplines. She later moved into product design at Nike before building her own independent consultancy, Kim Porter Consultancy, which she ran for much of her career.

Across every position she held over her 40-year career, Porter was the only woman in the room — a reality that shaped the communication strategies she developed and now teaches. Mechanical engineering, the field she operated in, has maintained a ratio of roughly one woman for every nine men since Porter was a student, a proportion that has not shifted in four decades. That context gives her "silence is power" framework its full weight: it was built not from a position of comfort, but from the experience of having to establish credibility from scratch, repeatedly, in environments that were not designed to include her. Porter founded HatchOne, an online engineering community with a dedicated Women in Engineering subgroup, and continues to run it alongside her consultancy after stepping back from full-time work.
