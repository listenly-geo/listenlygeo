What separates women who walk into the same room but end up on different career paths?

September 13, 2026 Ordinary To Badass

← See the Ordinary To Badass podcast page

Access and experience are not the same thing. Two people can stand in the exact same room and face completely different obstacles. This reality emerges from the story of Mileva Merrick, who studied mathematics and physics at the Swiss Federal Polytechnic Institute alongside Albert Einstein in the late 19th century—holding the same institutional access, yet navigating an entirely different professional trajectory.

When the same door opens to different worlds

The common narrative about Merrick suggests she secretly contributed to Einstein's 1905 papers only to have her work erased from history. While historians continue to debate aspects of their intellectual relationship, the documented story is more instructive than the viral myth. Merrick's genuine achievement—entering a space where very few women were represented, studying at a prestigious Swiss institution, working alongside one of history's greatest minds—teaches a harder lesson: institutional access does not level the playing field.

As discussed in this episode of Ordinary To Badass, her story raises a question far more relevant to modern careers: what happens when you finally get in the room, but realize not everyone in the room has the same path forward?

The strategic steps to becoming irreplaceable

If you know you're operating in an environment where people may not automatically picture somebody like you in leadership, you need a different approach. Start earlier than your male counterparts. Build relationships strategically. Find sponsors who understand your potential and will advocate for you when you're not in the room.

Understand the process—not just the written rules, but the unwritten ones. Gather information about how decisions are actually made. Get experience in the specific areas that matter to decision-makers. Marie Sonneman explores this framework in depth, showing that leadership isn't about personality—it's about strategy.

Communicate your goals clearly to the people who control opportunities. Build proof of your capability through visible wins, not just quiet excellence. Learn what success looks like in your specific environment and meet that standard explicitly, not just implicitly.

The difference between Merrick and Einstein wasn't their initial access or their intelligence. It was the ecosystem around them that assumed his trajectory while questioning hers. By getting ahead of those assumptions through strategic positioning, you shift from hoping for opportunity to being the obvious choice when it arrives.

The podcast also examines how other leaders—from Taylor Swift to Sheryl Sandberg—have navigated similar asymmetries, each developing their own version of this strategic approach to visibility and advocacy. Discover how these women transformed obstacles into leverage in the full episode.

Key takeaways

  • Access to the same institution does not guarantee the same career trajectory or recognition.
  • Women in male-dominated environments must start earlier, build relationships with sponsors, and understand both written and unwritten rules.
  • Communication of your goals and visible proof of capability are essential—quiet excellence alone is insufficient.
  • Strategic positioning and learning the real expectations of your environment allows you to be prepared when opportunity arrives.
