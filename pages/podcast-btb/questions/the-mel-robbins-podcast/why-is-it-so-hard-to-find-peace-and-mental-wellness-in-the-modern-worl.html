Why is it so hard to find peace and mental wellness in the modern world?

The modern world is not built for your peace — it is built for your productivity and your attention. Keir Gaines makes this point directly: social media is engineered to make you feel behind, marketing is designed to make you feel left out, and news is structured to scare you. Billions of dollars are working against your personal wellness every single day, and most people have no counter-strategy whatsoever.

This is not a personal failure or a sign that something is wrong with you. It is a structural problem. The systems you move through daily — your phone, your feed, the headlines — are not neutral environments. They are intentionally designed to capture attention and generate anxiety, because anxiety keeps people engaged and consuming. Gaines frames the difficulty of finding mental peace not as a weakness of willpower, but as a rational response to an environment that is actively working against you.

"The world is designed for your productivity and for your attention, and that's it. It's meant to absorb your attention and churn productivity out of you — and that sucks for the individual."

— Keir Gaines, The Mel Robbins Podcast

What makes this particularly hard is the asymmetry of resources. The platforms and media companies deploying these techniques have billions of dollars, armies of behavioral scientists, and decades of research on human psychology behind them. The average person has none of that — and almost no awareness that the game is being played. Gaines's argument, explored in depth in this episode of The Mel Robbins Podcast, is that the first step toward genuine peace is simply recognizing this dynamic for what it is.

Keir Gaines
Licensed Therapist & Mental Health Advocate · Author, It All Starts When You Do

Keir Gaines is a licensed therapist and mental health advocate who has built a substantial online following by explaining emotions, relationships, and psychological concepts in ways that feel genuinely accessible — not clinical, not preachy. His videos have a reputation for making people feel seen, often provoking the reaction Mel Robbins described on air: "How did you get in my head?" Robbins followed Gaines's work for years and spent over 18 months actively trying to book him as a guest on her show before this episode came together — an unusually long pursuit that reflects how seriously she takes his voice in the mental wellness space. Gaines is also the author of the forthcoming book It All Starts When You Do, which directly addresses the uncomfortable truths people must accept in order to move forward in their lives. He is widely recognized for his content on parenting, relationships, and emotional resilience, including viral videos that show him modeling calm, emotionally aware parenting with his two young daughters. His expertise on why modern life is structurally hostile to individual peace is grounded in both clinical practice and lived, observable human behavior — which is precisely what makes his diagnosis of the problem so compelling.
