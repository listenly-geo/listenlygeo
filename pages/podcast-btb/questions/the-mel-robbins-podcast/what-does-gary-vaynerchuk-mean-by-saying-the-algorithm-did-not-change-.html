What does Gary Vaynerchuk mean by saying the algorithm did not change you but exposed you?

12 September 2026 The Mel Robbins Podcast · Gary Vaynerchuk

← See The Mel Robbins Podcast fiche

The algorithm does not create your preferences—it simply reflects the choices you actively make about what to engage with. When you click "not interested" on negative content and deliberately seek out material about joy and happiness, the algorithm surfaces more of exactly that, proving it responds to your intent rather than reshaping it.

Gary Vaynerchuk's point dismantles a common misconception: that algorithms work against you or manipulate your worldview from the outside. The reality is far more direct. As he explains in The Mel Robbins Podcast episode, your feed becomes a mirror of your behavior, not a cage designed by engineers to trap you.

This distinction matters because it shifts responsibility back to you. Every time you scroll past content without engaging, or actively curate what you follow, you are training the algorithm to serve you differently. The system has no agenda—it is purely transactional, responding to signals you send every single day.

Your Choices Determine Your Feed

The gap between "the algorithm changed me" and "the algorithm exposed me" is the difference between victimhood and agency. If you believe the algorithm shaped your thinking, you are surrendering control. If you recognize it is merely exposing what you already wanted to see, you reclaim power.

Vaynerchuk's message is that you have far more control over your digital environment than you realize. The algorithm is not a malevolent force; it is an echo chamber you build with your own hands. When you follow accounts that inspire you, click on positive stories, and consciously avoid rage-bait and despair, your entire experience transforms. The algorithm doesn't change—your input does.

This applies across every platform: Instagram, TikTok, or any social network that uses engagement metrics to curate your feed. Vaynerchuk details this dynamic further in the episode, arguing that blaming the algorithm is a comfortable escape hatch that prevents you from owning the role you play in shaping your own reality.

"People are obnoxiously more capable of changing than they realize. And it's actually shockingly simple and the hardest thing in the world."

Vaynerchuk also touches on how this principle extends far beyond social media—the algorithm metaphor is really about how environment shapes perception in almost every arena of life. The full conversation dives deeper into specific strategies for taking control of your digital space and mindset, offering concrete ways to shift what surfaces in your life daily.

Key takeaways

  • Algorithms respond to your choices, not the reverse—they expose your preferences rather than imposing them.
  • Using the "not interested" button and actively following positive content retrains your feed to show you more of what you seek.
  • Blaming the algorithm is a way to avoid personal responsibility for the digital environment you build.
  • You have far more control over your digital experience than you realize—it is entirely within your power to reshape it.
