How should you handle difficult relationships you're worried about or frustrated with?

September 23, 2026 The Mel Robbins Podcast · Mel Robbins

← See The Mel Robbins Podcast fiche

The framework consists of three distinct parts: first, focus on 'me'—creating space for yourself by identifying what is going well and what support you need; second, address 'we' by having an honest conversation with the person about how the relationship impacts you and what you need from them; and third, embrace 'them' by accepting their right to make their own choices and respecting their dignity, even when you disagree with their decisions.

The three layers of reclaiming your power

The 'me' layer starts with you, not the other person. This means shifting your focus from trying to control or change someone else to understanding your own boundaries and needs. What support do you actually need, and what parts of your life are working well despite the friction in this relationship? By answering these questions honestly, you stop waiting for the other person to change before you can feel better.

The 'we' layer requires courage. It's the conversation where you tell the person exactly how their behavior or choices are affecting you—not to shame them, but to be truthful. This honest dialogue creates space for real connection, even in difficult relationships, by shifting away from blame and toward your actual experience. You're not demanding they change; you're sharing what you need.

The 'them' layer is often the hardest: accepting that they get to make their own choices, even ones you believe are wrong. If they understand the consequences and are capable of deciding for themselves, your job is to let them own their decisions. This doesn't mean you stop caring—it means you stop carrying responsibility for their outcomes.

As Mel Robbins explains in her framework, this approach removes the stress and friction from relationships by redirecting your energy toward the one thing you actually control: yourself.

"If you change nothing, nothing changes. But if you change one thing, everything could change."

The power of this framework lies in its simplicity and clarity. In the episode, Robbins walks through real-life scenarios—from frustrated parents to exasperated partners—showing exactly how to apply 'me, we, and them' to situations where you feel powerless. The framework moves beyond the popular "let them" concept to give you concrete language and steps for managing the relationships that weigh on you most.

One of the most encouraging details covered in the full episode is how this approach shifts the dynamic entirely: when you stop trying to change the other person and focus on your own clarity and needs, the entire relationship often transforms—not because they've changed, but because you have.

Key takeaways

  • 'Me' means establishing your own boundaries and identifying what you need and what is working in your life, independent of the other person's choices.
  • 'We' involves having an honest, direct conversation about how the relationship is impacting you and what specific support or change you need from them.
  • 'Them' requires accepting their autonomy to make their own decisions, even ones you believe are harmful, as long as they understand the consequences.
  • Shifting focus from controlling others to controlling yourself is where real power and peace come from in difficult relationships.

What specific recommendation does research suggest for protecting young people from social media harm?

Research indicates that young people should avoid social media until age 16. This recommendation stems from evidence showing that early social media exposure increases anxiety and harm among children.

Platforms like Instagram and Roblox allow children to interact with anonymous adults who can hide their true identities with no accountability, creating significant safety risks for young users.
