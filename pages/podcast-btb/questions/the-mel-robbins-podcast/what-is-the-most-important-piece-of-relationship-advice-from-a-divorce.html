What is the most important piece of relationship advice from a divorce attorney to keep a marriage lasting?

August 25, 2026 The Mel Robbins Podcast · Christopher Robbins

← See the full The Mel Robbins Podcast page

According to world-renowned divorce attorney James Sexton, the most important relationship advice fits into four words: "You're my favorite person." If a partner can genuinely say and mean that phrase — looking back from the end of life — that is the single greatest asset a long-term relationship can have. Everything else, Sexton argues, can be reverse-engineered from that truth.

Four words that a divorce attorney uses to measure every marriage

James Sexton has spent his career watching marriages end. That vantage point gave him a clear-eyed view of what separates the couples who stay from the ones who don't. His answer isn't a communication framework or a set of rules — it's a single question: can you look at this person and call them your favorite?

The phrase is deceptively simple. It isn't about romance or chemistry. It's about accumulated choice — deciding, over and over, across decades of bills, disagreements, career failures, and growing up together, that this person is still the one you'd choose. That's the standard Sexton sets, and it's explored in depth in The Mel Robbins Podcast.

Thirty years of marriage as a real-world test case

Mel Robbins and Christopher Robbins bring this idea to life not as a theory but as lived experience. At the time of recording, they had been together for 30 years and were raising three children, ages 27, 26, and 21. Their marriage survived unemployment, business failure, debt, and extended periods of emotional disconnection — including a decade during which Mel Robbins herself described exhibiting dismissive behavior toward Christopher.

That honesty is exactly what makes Sexton's four-word test feel credible in this episode. It isn't applied to an ideal marriage — it's applied to a real one, with real damage and real repair. Christopher Robbins, who tends to process things internally rather than outwardly, joins this episode of The Mel Robbins Podcast to reflect on what those 30 years actually looked like from the inside.

The deeper point Sexton makes is about authenticity, not perfection. A partner who helped you become the most honest version of yourself — and who you still want to be around — is the benchmark. As he frames it, that's the only wedding toast that actually matters.

This reframe — from "did we have a smooth marriage" to "are you still my favorite person" — is one of the sharpest insights in this conversation on The Mel Robbins Podcast. It shifts the measure of a successful relationship from absence of conflict to presence of genuine preference.

"At the end of your life, if you could look at another person and say, you helped me become the most authentic version of myself, and you're still my favorite person, that's the greatest wedding toast you could ever have."
