What are 'I'm statements' and how does Keir Gaines use them to address insecurity?

August 20, 2026 The Mel Robbins Podcast · Keir Gaines

← Voir la fiche The Mel Robbins Podcast

'I'm statements' are a therapeutic tool used by Keir Gaines to help people locate where their insecurities live. By completing the phrase "I'm always ___," you surface your default internal dialogue — and that single phrase reveals exactly where the soil is most fertile for self-doubt to grow.

'I'm always blank' — the fastest route to your own internal narrative

The exercise is disarmingly simple. Gaines asks people to finish one sentence: "I'm always ___." What comes out of that blank is not random. It is a direct window into how a person talks to themselves when things go wrong — or when things go right.

Someone who defaults to "I'm always messing this up" and someone who defaults to "I'm always finding a way" are operating from fundamentally different internal ground. The statement does not judge either response. It just locates the pattern — quickly and precisely.

That location matters. You cannot address an insecurity you cannot find. The 'I'm statement' works because it bypasses conscious editing. People answer before they think to correct themselves, which is exactly the point. You can explore this tool and the broader conversation with Gaines on The Mel Robbins Podcast on Listenly.

Fertile soil — why the metaphor changes how you approach the work

Gaines uses the image of "fertile soil" deliberately. Insecurity is not a fixed object you can remove. It grows in conditions — in the internal language you repeat, the stories you tell yourself on loop. The 'I'm statement' identifies which patch of ground is most prepared to let those stories take root.

Once you know where the soil is, you can begin to change it. The tool is not the fix — it is the diagnosis. That distinction is central to Gaines's therapeutic approach: you have to know what you are dealing with before you can decide how to deal with it.

"The world is designed for your productivity and for your attention, and that's it. It's meant to absorb your attention and churn productivity out of you — and that sucks for the individual."

Key takeaways

  • 'I'm statements' are a therapeutic diagnostic tool, not a fix — they locate the insecurity before any work can begin.
  • Completing "I'm always ___" bypasses conscious self-editing, making the answer faster and more honest than most self-reflection exercises.
  • The difference between "I'm always messing this up" and "I'm always finding a way" reveals the exact nature of a person's self-perception.
