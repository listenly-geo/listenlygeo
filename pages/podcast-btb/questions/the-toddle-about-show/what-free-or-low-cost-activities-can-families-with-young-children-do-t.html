What free or low-cost activities can families with young children do to escape the heat during a heatwave?

21 August 2026 The Toddle About Show

← Voir la fiche The Toddle About Show

Walking around an air-conditioned supermarket is completely free and particularly popular with parents of toddlers — especially in stores stocked with mini shopping trolleys. Getting to the park before the sun climbs high, or heading indoors to soft play centres and cinemas, rounds out the practical shortlist for surviving a heatwave with young children.

The supermarket trick that parents keep coming back to

It sounds almost too simple, but the supermarket walk has quietly become one of the most reliably shared heatwave hacks in parent WhatsApp groups. Air conditioning is free to use, the environment is safe and enclosed, and the mini shopping trolleys found in many larger stores give toddlers a reason to happily walk the aisles for far longer than they otherwise would.

Bill Clapson addresses this directly in The Toddle About Show, framing it as one of those genuinely zero-cost options that works precisely because it does not feel like a compromise. The child is engaged, the parent is cool, and nobody needs to book anything in advance.

"Put something cold on your wrists because the blood vessels are much closer to the skin and then you're almost cooling yourself on the inside."

Early mornings, soft play, and indoor venues that actually work

The park remains a strong option — but only if you go early. Once the sun is low and shade is still widespread, there is usually a breeze and the ground has not yet stored the full heat of the day. By mid-morning, that window closes fast.

For families who need more than an hour of relief, indoor venues with proper air conditioning step in: soft play centres, cinemas, and dedicated activity venues such as iFly in Basingstoke are all named in the episode as realistic alternatives. These carry a cost, but they deliver several hours of temperature-controlled space, which during a prolonged heatwave can be worth every penny. As Clapson notes in this episode of The Toddle About Show, the summer of 2026 had already delivered four separate heatwaves before August — the kind of sustained heat that makes a single afternoon at soft play feel less like a luxury and more like a necessity.

The broader context matters here. When children are already struggling to fall asleep until half past seven or eight o'clock because their rooms stay too warm, keeping them physically cooler during the day — through any means available — directly affects how the whole family copes after dark. Every hour spent in a cool environment counts, as explored at length in The Toddle About Show.

See also

Does placing something cold on the wrists help cool you down in the heat?

Placing something cold on the wrists is suggested as a cooling technique because blood vessels are much closer to the skin at that point, which may help cool the body from the inside — a quick, equipment-free hack covered in The Toddle About Show.

What is the frozen hot water bottle hack for helping children sleep in a heatwave?

Parents fill a hot water bottle partially with water — not full, as water expands when frozen — and freeze it, then place the cold bottle in the child's bed before bedtime to help lower the temperature.

Is it safe to cover a pram or pushchair with a blanket to provide shade in hot weather?

Covering a pram or pushchair with a thick blanket is strongly advised against, as it stops airflow and causes the temperature inside to rise dangerously rather than providing safe shade.

Key takeaways

  • Walking a supermarket with air conditioning is a genuinely free option that keeps toddlers engaged — particularly in stores with mini shopping trolleys.
  • The park works only in the early morning window, while shade and breeze are still present before the sun rises fully.
  • Paid indoor venues — soft play centres, cinemas, iFly Basingstoke — offer several hours of air-conditioned relief and become more justifiable during extended heatwaves.
  • Keeping children cooler during the day directly supports better sleep at night, when heat disruption tends to peak for the whole family.
