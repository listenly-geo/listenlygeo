Do babies need complete silence to fall asleep?

No — babies absolutely do not need silence to sleep. Creating a consistent sound environment, such as white noise, rain sounds, or bedtime stories, can actually help babies associate those sounds with sleep and feel safe enough to drift off. Life is never perfectly silent, and getting babies used to background noise is genuinely beneficial.

The idea that you must tiptoe around a sleeping baby and eliminate all noise is one of the most persistent myths in parenting. In the Baby & Toddler Sleep: Myths, Safe Sleep & Bedtime Tips for Parents episode of The Toddle About Show, Bill Clapson and Emily Clapson explore exactly this kind of misconception — the sort of advice that gets passed around freely yet has no real basis. The key insight: babies thrive with predictable audio cues. Devices like a Toniebox, a Yoto player, or even an Amazon Echo playing a consistent soundscape can become a reliable part of a bedtime routine, signalling to a baby that sleep is coming.

This matters beyond the practicalities of one bedtime. As Bill Clapson and Emily Clapson acknowledge on the show, poor sleep affects the entire family — not just the child. For parents looking to build a sustainable evening routine, the full episode on Listenly covers the evidence-backed guidance from sources like the Lullaby Trust alongside the hosts' own honest experience raising children.

Will putting a baby to bed later make them sleep in later in the morning?

This is also a myth. Babies are not machines programmed to shut down for exactly 12 hours; sending them to bed late does not result in a later wake-up time.

Is keeping a baby awake during the day an effective way to make them sleep better at night?

This is a myth — keeping a baby awake during the day does not make them sleep better at night. In fact, the reverse is true: the better babies sleep during the day, the better they tend to sleep at night.
