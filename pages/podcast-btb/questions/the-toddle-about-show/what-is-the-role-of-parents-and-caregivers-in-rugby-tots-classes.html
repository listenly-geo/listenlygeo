How do parents and caregivers participate in Rugby Tots classes?

17 September 2026 The Toddle About Show · Tom Brown

← See The Toddle About Show fiche

Rugby Tots actively encourages parents and caregivers to participate in sessions alongside their children, giving grown-ups the chance to witness their child's breakthrough moments — the lightbulb instances that typically happen at nursery or school but rarely in front of a trusted adult at home. As children gain confidence, the programme progressively supports independence while always allowing them to return to their parent for reassurance.

Active participation, not sidelines

The entire design of Rugby Tots rests on having mum and dad directly involved in the fun, not watching from the edge. This participation is not incidental — it is central to how the programme builds confidence and creates that safe space for learning.

As children move through sessions, coaches intentionally guide the transition toward working with peers and the coaching team, but the door back to their grown-up remains open at all times. This balance between independence and accessible support lets each child move at their own pace without feeling abandoned or unsupported.

Witnessing growth at the moment it happens

One of the defining features of parental involvement in Rugby Tots is the opportunity to see your child succeed in real time. Parents often miss the small victories that happen during school hours — the first time a child completes an obstacle course, the moment they gain the confidence to try something new, or when they suddenly understand a skill they had struggled with the week before.

Rugby Tots creates a space where parents are present for these breakthroughs, which strengthens both the child's confidence and the parent's understanding of their growing abilities. As Tom Brown explains in the episode, the goal is to build that supportive platform without the child even realizing what they are developing.

"We want to build confidence in young children, we want to give opportunity for those lightbulb moments, we want to work on movement literacy to really develop a really supportive platform that children can grow in without really knowing what they're developing."

Rugby Tots sessions run on a two-week cycle, which gives children the chance to see an activity one week and excel at it the next. This structure, combined with parental presence, creates a rhythm where grown-ups can genuinely observe their child's learning arc — not as one-off moments, but as a progression.

How does Rugby Tots use imaginative play to develop physical skills in young children?

Rugby Tots uses imaginative framing to teach movement skills without children realizing they are learning. For example, asking children to stomp like animals or move through courses as if on adventures makes physical development feel like play rather than instruction.

What does a typical Rugby Tots session look like and how is it structured?

Rugby Tots sessions follow a structured spine: a starting activity, a catch and pass activity or game, a movement skill such as an obstacle course or relay, a kicking activity or game, and a finishing activity. This structure gives children familiarity and learning rhythm across the two-week programme.

What hidden hot-weather hazards should parents be aware of beyond direct sun exposure?

Water left in a garden hose pipe that has been sitting in the sun can become dangerously hot, hot enough to scald, and should always be run off before use with young children.

Key takeaways

  • Parents are actively encouraged to participate in Rugby Tots sessions alongside their children, not as observers.
  • Parental presence creates opportunities for children to experience breakthroughs with a trusted adult present, moments that often happen at school but rarely at home.
  • The programme intentionally supports the transition toward peer interaction and independence while keeping the door open for children to return to their grown-up at any point.
  • Two-week structured cycles allow parents to witness their child's learning progression from one session to the next.
