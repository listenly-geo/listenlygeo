Is the baby and toddler sleep consultant industry regulated in the UK?

No — the baby and toddler sleep consultant industry in the UK is not regulated, and there is no formal qualification required to practise. Anyone can set themselves up as a sleep consultant. Parents are advised to do their research carefully, ask plenty of questions, and choose a consultant whose approach and personality feel like a good personal fit — especially given how vulnerable the early stages of parenthood can be.

Bill Clapson and Emily Clapson discussed this openly on The Toddle About Show, noting that the sheer volume of sleep advice available to parents — online, in magazines, from consultants, and via word of mouth — makes it genuinely hard to know who to trust. The episode points to the Lullaby Trust as the gold-standard evidence-based resource that both parents and professionals should be turning to first.

The hosts also flag the importance of fit when selecting a sleep consultant: because parents are seeking support during a period of stress and exhaustion, the dynamic between consultant and family matters as much as the method itself. Finding someone whose style, tone, and values align with your own is not a luxury — it is part of what makes the support effective.

How does parental sleep deprivation affect families and what advice is given?

Broken sleep does not just cause tiredness — it makes parents short-tempered, more anxious, and less enjoyable as a parent. When working on children's sleep, it is really important to focus on the whole family.

How important is a consistent bedtime routine for babies and toddlers?

A predictable wind-down routine is very important because babies and toddlers cannot tell the time but can understand rhythm and pattern. A sequence such as bath, feed, and story helps signal to a young child that sleep is coming.

Is night waking in babies a sign that something is wrong?

Night waking is completely normal and is not a sign that a parent has done anything wrong or that there is something wrong with the baby. Babies wake to be comforted, fed, or reassured, and this is a natural part of early development.
