How can parents encourage children to drink enough water in the heat?

Getting children to drink plain water is genuinely difficult, and that's okay to acknowledge. Adding a small amount of squash to make the drink more appealing is a practical solution — some fluid intake is far better than none. Offering fruit with a naturally high water content, such as watermelon, melon, and cucumber, is another effective strategy. Keeping cold water ready in the fridge and modelling good drinking habits at family mealtimes also makes a real difference.

In the episode of The Toddle About Show dedicated to surviving a heatwave, Bill Clapson and Emily Clapson are candid about the reality of parenting through extreme heat — and hydration sits at the heart of it. The advice is refreshingly pragmatic: don't hold out for the perfect outcome. If squash gets the drink down, use it. The goal is keeping children hydrated, not keeping the drink pristine.

The episode was recorded during a summer in which the hosts had already experienced four heatwaves before August — a context that makes these tips feel earned rather than theoretical. What are the signs of dehydration to watch for in young children during hot weather?

Signs of dehydration in children include going to the toilet less often, fewer wet nappies than expected, being visibly upset but unable to cry, and being lethargic or unusually irritable.

What is the best way to check if a baby or toddler is too hot?

The most reliable way to check if a child is too hot is to touch their chest or their back, as this reflects their core body temperature. Checking hands and feet can be misleading as they are often cooler than the rest of the body.

How can parents keep babies and toddlers cool during a heatwave?

Parents can use cold showers, paddling pools, wet towels placed in the bed, and frozen flannels to help children regulate their temperature. Keeping fans in the right position and adjusting bedtime routines also helps during a heatwave.
