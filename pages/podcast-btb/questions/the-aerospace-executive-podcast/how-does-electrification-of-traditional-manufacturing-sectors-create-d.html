How does electrification of traditional manufacturing sectors create demand for electronics supply chain solutions?

September 15, 2026 The Aerospace Executive Podcast · Sebastian Shaw

← See the The Aerospace Executive Podcast fiche

When mechanical companies add electronics to their products, their supply chain suddenly works by entirely different rules. Electronics follow fundamentally different laws of physics than plastics or mechanical components—the connection between design and procurement is so tight that you cannot simply transfer a mechanical blueprint to the electronic supply chain. This mismatch forces mechatronic companies (hybrid mechanical-electronic businesses) to rebuild their supply chain strategy from the ground up.

The aerospace and defense industry is experiencing extreme demand acceleration with electrification, making companies desperate to gain control over their electronic supply chain. As detailed in The Aerospace Executive Podcast episode, this shift represents far more than a simple product enhancement—it is a structural transformation of how procurement, design, and operations interact.

Why mechanical companies struggle with electronics procurement

Mechanical companies historically optimized their supply chains around plastics and mechanical components—categories with predictable lead times, stable sourcing, and straightforward logistics. Electronics procurement operates under completely different constraints. Component availability, obsolescence cycles, design-to-procurement timing, and supplier qualification all follow different timelines and rules.

When a company transitions from pure mechanical manufacturing to becoming a mechatronic company, the procurement team cannot simply apply the same processes. The design engineer's choices about which semiconductor to use directly impact component availability, cost, and supply chain risk in ways that have no parallel in mechanical design. As Sebastian Shaw explains in the episode, this disconnect creates immediate operational shock: "They're kind of in shock and need like support."

The aerospace and defense electrification wave

The aerospace and defense sector is undergoing rapid electrification of traditionally mechanical platforms—from hydraulic systems to electric alternatives, from mechanical flight surfaces to electronic controls. This transition is not gradual; it is driven by regulatory pressure, performance demands, and the competitive need to modernize aging platforms.

Companies in this sector are now competing not just on mechanical design excellence but on their ability to manage electronics supply chain complexity. Those without dedicated electronics procurement infrastructure face serious supply shortages, extended lead times, and design-cycle delays. The result is urgent demand for software platforms and expert support to manage this new electronic supply chain reality, as discussed at length in this podcast.

"Innovation diffuses at the rate of trust and the industry is sometimes very much a laggard when it comes to their own processes."

Key takeaways

  • Electronics supply chains operate under fundamentally different physics and constraints than mechanical or plastic component procurement.
  • The connection between design and procurement in electronics is so tight that mechanical blueprints cannot simply transfer to the electronic supply chain.
  • Mechanical companies transitioning to mechatronic status experience immediate operational disruption and require dedicated electronics supply chain management support.
  • Aerospace and defense companies are experiencing extreme electrification demand, creating urgent need for electronics supply chain control and expertise.
