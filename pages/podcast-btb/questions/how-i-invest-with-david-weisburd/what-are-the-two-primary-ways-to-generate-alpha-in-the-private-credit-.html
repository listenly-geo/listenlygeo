How do you actually generate alpha in the private credit asset class?

September 25, 2026 How I Invest with David Weisburd · David Weisburd

← See the How I Invest with David Weisburd fiche

Alpha in private credit boils down to two straightforward approaches: making more money than your peers or losing less than they do. Private credit is fixed income, not a venture-scale asset class chasing 2x, 3x, or 5x returns—it's engineered to generate safe, stable, consistent performance year after year for institutional investors like pension funds and insurance companies.

Why private credit is fundamentally different from growth investing

Private credit has a structural mandate around capital preservation that distinguishes it from private equity or venture capital. Institutional investors—pension funds, insurance companies, endowments—depend on this asset class to deliver predictable cash flows and minimize volatility. That's the opposite of a search for moonshot returns.

When Weisburd founded Monaro Capital in 2001, this reality shaped everything about how the firm approached underwriting and deal structure. As he explains in the episode, the entire thesis was built on doing the opposite of leverage play: finding companies with strong cash flow, structuring loans that could be repaid, and ensuring the lender got paid first.

The two dimensions of competitive advantage

Earning more than competitors typically means better deal selection, tighter credit analysis, or smarter structuring—finding borrowers others miss, or negotiating better terms and pricing. This is the traditional alpha game in fixed income.

Losing less than competitors is equally important. A discussion covered at length in this podcast centers on rigorous credit discipline, early warning systems for portfolio stress, and the ability to restructure loans before they fail. In a $2 trillion market projected to hit $5 trillion within five years, the firms that defend their capital most effectively compound that advantage year after year.

"Don't focus on your accomplishments. Focus on the work that you've done, the effort you've put in to get there, and then keep moving your goals."

Weisburd's approach has been tested across market cycles—from the 2008 financial crisis through today's tightening environment. The discipline of picking credits carefully and defending them harder pays compounding returns that quietly outpace the asset class average.

The real curiosity for listeners: Weisburd walked into a market where nearly 90% of financing was still done inside the banking system just 24 years ago—and through methodical execution of these two alpha pillars, helped reshape an entire industry. That transformation holds a lesson for anyone thinking about competitive edge in mature markets.

What percentage of transaction financing now occurs outside the regulated banking system compared to 24 years ago?

When Weisburd started his business 24 years ago, 90% of financing for transactions and buyouts was done in the banking system and 10% outside it. Today, that ratio has completely inverted—90% now occurs outside the banking system through private credit vehicles.

How did regulatory changes after the 2008 financial crisis reshape the private credit market?

After the financial crisis, regulators imposed high capital requirements on banks for leveraged buyout loans but low requirements for asset-backed securities and private credit vehicles. This regulatory asymmetry accelerated capital flight from traditional banking to private credit.

What market conditions and regulatory gaps inspired the creation of a non-bank private credit firm in the early 2000s?

Weisburd observed that banks were inflexible due to regulations, while the only non-banks operating at the time—GE Capital and Heller Financial—could structure deals more creatively. He founded Monaro Capital in 2001 to fill the gap for lower middle market companies underserved by traditional banking.

Key takeaways

  • Private credit alpha comes from either generating higher returns or suffering smaller losses than competitors—not from venture-scale multiples.
  • The asset class is designed for institutional investors requiring stable, predictable cash flows and capital preservation, not growth-at-all-costs.
  • Competitive advantage flows from rigorous credit selection and disciplined portfolio defense across market cycles.
  • Monaro Capital's $24 billion in managed assets and $10 billion in annual deployment demonstrate the scale possible through consistent application of these two alpha principles.
