How do CRE advisory firms turn data into competitive advantage while sharing it openly?

September 24, 2026 Coffee & Cap Rates: Commercial Real Estate Podcast · Shimon Shkury

← See the Coffee & Cap Rates: Commercial Real Estate Podcast fiche

Ariel Property Advisors consolidated 15 years of market intelligence and 30 annual reports into a centralized Salesforce database accessible to all 35-plus support and analysis staff, then shared this information openly with competitors and the industry because they believe transparency benefits the greater good. They maintain competitive advantage because their methodology cannot be easily replicated, and artificial intelligence now helps them connect data dots in ways that deepen analysis beyond the raw information itself.

This approach sounds counterintuitive in a competitive industry. Why would a firm openly distribute market intelligence that took 15 years and significant resources to build? Shkury's answer is rooted in culture and philosophy rather than strategic naivety.

The firm recognized early that information accessibility across all staff members — not just senior partners — created internal velocity and better client service. By centralizing everything in Salesforce rather than siloing data in individual brokers' heads or email folders, Ariel created an institutional asset that scales with team growth.

When they chose to share market reports and intelligence openly, competitors didn't gain the ability to replicate Ariel's core strength. The methodology — how data is collected, analyzed, and synthesized — remained proprietary. As Shkury explains in the episode, sharing raw information does not expose the process or the expertise that transforms it into actionable insight.

The introduction of artificial intelligence to their workflow added a new competitive layer. AI helped them identify patterns and connections across their 15-year data archive that human analysis alone might miss or take much longer to surface. This is where the real differentiation lives now — not in who has the data, but in how intelligently it is processed and applied.

The culture of working together, not in silos

Shkury was explicit about culture driving this openness. He emphasized that Ariel operates in a competitive environment that supports collaboration rather than internal competition between team members. This runs counter to the traditional brokerage model where individual brokers hoard client relationships and data as personal competitive moats.

By breaking that siloed model, Ariel enabled its 65 employees and seven partners to build on each other's work. A junior analyst accessing the shared Salesforce database can leverage 15 years of institutional knowledge immediately. A support staffer can contribute findings that inform senior advisors' market reports. The methodology becomes stronger because more minds are working on it.

The decision to share market intelligence publicly — with reporters, competitors, and the broader industry — extended this philosophy outward. If transparency strengthens the industry as a whole, Ariel benefits from operating in a smarter, more informed market, even if some competitors gain access to the same raw reports. The episode dives deeper into how this philosophy shaped hiring and partnership decisions over Ariel's 15-year growth.

"We're breaking that traditional model. And so going back to culture, we work in a competitive environment that supports you, and I think a lot of people in our industry don't know what it means to work not in a silo or in a silo."

What many firms find difficult to replicate is not the information itself, but the discipline and investment required to maintain such a database and the culture required to staff it with collaborative talent. The episode also covers specific market metrics and transactions that illustrate why this intelligence infrastructure matters in real deal scenarios.

Key takeaways

  • Ariel Property Advisors stores 15 years of market data and 30 annual reports in a centralized Salesforce system accessible to all 35-plus staff members, not siloed to individual brokers.
  • The firm openly shares market intelligence with competitors and the media because they believe transparency strengthens the broader industry, not just their clients.
  • Competitive advantage lives in methodology, not raw data — how information is collected, analyzed, and synthesized remains proprietary and difficult to replicate.
  • Artificial intelligence now helps Ariel identify patterns and connections across their data archive, deepening analysis beyond what manual review can achieve.
  • Operating in a collaborative, non-siloed culture attracts talent and accelerates institutional knowledge across the team, which is harder to copy than any single report.
