How is AI fundamentally changing the role of internal communicators beyond just creating content?

September 18, 2026 Unprompted: Real AI in IC · Rebecca Lindsay

← See the Unprompted: Real AI in IC fiche

AI is shifting internal communicators from being 'post boxes' that distribute messages to enablers who give others the tools to communicate well themselves. Rather than creating all content centrally, communicators can now guide managers and teams through AI-powered agents that help them craft their own messages, turning the role into one of strategic support at scale.

The transformation addresses a real challenge facing internal comms teams today. As explained in Unprompted: Real AI in IC, people across organizations are time-poor, yet they still need to communicate effectively with their teams. Instead of bottlenecking all requests through a central comms function, AI agents can distribute the capability to communicate well directly to the people who need it most—managers, team leads, and individual contributors.

This shift means internal communicators stop being the gatekeepers of every message. Instead, they become architects of systems that empower others. As Rebecca Lindsay describes it: "We want to be giving you the tools to do great comms yourself, and that is precisely what AI can enable us to do at scale."

From bottleneck to enabler: redefining the internal comms role

Traditional internal communications operates as a centralized function—messages flow through the comms team, creating inevitable delays and a dependency on their capacity. This model breaks down when organizations need rapid, localized communication from managers and team leads. AI agents solve this by placing communication tools directly in the hands of those who need them, guided by frameworks and guardrails set by the internal comms team.

The role becomes less about authoring every message and more about ensuring that those messages—written by dozens or hundreds of people across the organization—align with brand voice, tone, and strategic intent. Rebecca Lindsay's work at Hyper Exponential demonstrates this in practice through AI agents that support everything from annual leave messaging to emotionally intelligent communications checking, freeing internal comms to focus on strategy rather than production.

This also enables better conversations between managers and their teams. When managers have tools to craft thoughtful, considered messages—rather than sending off-the-cuff replies—the quality of internal dialogue improves. The internal comms team becomes the strategic guide rather than the sole creator, multiplying their impact across the entire organization without requiring proportional growth in headcount.

Key takeaways

  • Internal communicators shift from being content creators to strategic enablers who build systems and tools for others to use.
  • AI agents distributed across the organization allow managers and teams to create their own communications with guidance, multiplying the impact of the internal comms function.
  • The internal comms role becomes about setting frameworks, guardrails, and quality standards rather than producing every message centrally.
  • This model addresses the time-poverty challenge in organizations by removing bottlenecks and empowering rapid, localized communication.
