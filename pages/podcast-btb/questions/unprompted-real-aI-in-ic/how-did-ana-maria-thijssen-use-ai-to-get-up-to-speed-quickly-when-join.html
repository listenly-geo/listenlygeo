How did Ana-Maria Thijssen use AI to get up to speed quickly when joining a mid-flight SAP transformation programme?

20 August 2026 Unprompted: Real AI in IC · Ana-Maria Thijssen

← See the Unprompted: Real AI in IC podcast page

Thijssen arrived on a global SAP transformation programme roughly a year and a half after it had launched — with no formal onboarding and a team already fluent in dense IT and programme-specific jargon. She used AI to rapidly decode that language, eventually creating her own personal file she called a "Jargon Buster" — translating complex SAP and OCM terminology into plain, digestible content so she could contribute meaningfully from her very first days.

Arriving 18 months late — with no time to catch up the slow way

The programme Thijssen joined had been running since January 2015. Her colleagues carried deep institutional knowledge built over years. She had none of it — and there was no structured induction waiting for her.

Rather than spending weeks shadowing colleagues or working through documentation, she turned to AI as an accelerator. The tool let her query unfamiliar terms in real time, surface definitions, and cross-reference SAP-specific concepts against broader change management practice — at her own pace, without burdening a team that was already mid-delivery.

The result was the Jargon Buster: a personal reference file built with AI's help, capturing the programme's specific vocabulary in plain language. It was not a generic glossary. It was tailored to her programme's context, and she built it herself through iterative AI-assisted translation of real content she encountered on the job. You can hear her explain the approach in full on Listenly's episode page for Unprompted.

AI as the colleague who already knew everything — without making her feel behind

One of the subtler points Thijssen makes is about psychological safety. As the sole communications professional embedded in an Organizational Change Management team, she had no peer to turn to with a basic question about SAP terminology without risking looking uninformed in front of programme stakeholders.

AI removed that friction entirely. She could ask it anything — however elementary — and receive a clear, patient explanation with no social cost. It translated technical IT language into content she could actually use in communications, allowing her to produce relevant, credible materials even before she had fully absorbed the programme's history.

The programme itself was large: ultimately set to impact 6,000 users across the company, with the next wave alone covering 30 locations worldwide. Getting fluent fast was not optional — it was a condition for being useful.

"AI became my virtual colleague. It's not a person that does my job. It's more like an entity that helps me bounce ideas and not feel so alone."

Key takeaways

  • Joining a programme mid-flight without onboarding is a real scenario — AI can substitute for the structured induction that doesn't exist.
  • Building a personal "Jargon Buster" with AI is a concrete, repeatable tactic: it captures programme-specific vocabulary in plain language you actually understand.
  • AI removes the social friction of asking basic questions — critical when you're the only communications professional on a large team and can't afford to appear uninformed.
