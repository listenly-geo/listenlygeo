How should internal communicators approach using AI for writing and content creation?

31 August 2026 Unprompted: Real AI in IC · Amanda Atkinson

← See the Unprompted: Real AI in IC fiche

Don't start from scratch with AI—it produces poor results. Instead, begin with your own core narrative and use AI to adapt and scale it into supporting pieces like event invitations, Slack posts, and promotional copy. The real value emerges when you start with human work and use AI to enhance it, not the other way around.

Why starting with AI leads to a mess

Using AI as your first step in the writing process tends to generate generic, uninspired output that requires heavy reworking. Where you start with AI, you find yourself just getting a mess. The problem isn't that AI lacks capability—it's that without human direction rooted in your organisation's voice, values, and context, AI produces content that feels disconnected and requires substantial revision. This approach wastes time rather than saves it.

The winning approach: human foundation, AI acceleration

The most effective workflow begins with you writing the core message yourself. This might be a key memo, a strategic narrative, or your authentic take on a topic. Then use AI to enhance and challenge that work, multiplying it across different formats and channels. As discussed at length in this episode of Unprompted: Real AI in IC, internal communicators report that this sequence—human insight first, AI adaptation second—unlocks genuine productivity gains.

The practical application is immediate: write your all-hands talking points, then ask AI to turn that into a Slack announcement, an event invitation, or a calendar reminder in your preferred tone. AI becomes a scaling engine for work already grounded in human judgment and organisational knowledge. You maintain control, authenticity, and context while eliminating repetitive rewriting.

"Where I start with AI, I find myself just getting a mess. Where I start with my own work and then use AI to enhance and challenge, that's where it works best for me."

In conversations about AI adoption in internal comms, context and confidence matter enormously. Amanda Atkinson explores in detail how managers who openly support and model AI use increase employee adoption dramatically—employees with managerial AI support are 7.4 times more likely to say it helps them do their work. This same principle of human leadership applies to your writing: your human judgment must lead, and AI must follow.

Key takeaways

  • Never use AI to generate content from zero—it produces generic, contextless output that requires heavy reworking and wastes time.
  • Start every piece with your own core narrative, voice, and organisational insight, then use AI to adapt and multiply it across formats.
  • Scale internal comms efficiently by using AI to convert one well-crafted message into event invites, Slack posts, emails, and promotional copy.
  • Maintain authenticity and control by keeping human judgment at the start of your workflow, not at the end.
