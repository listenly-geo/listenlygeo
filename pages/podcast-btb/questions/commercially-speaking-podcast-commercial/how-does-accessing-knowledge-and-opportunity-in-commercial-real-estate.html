How does accessing knowledge and opportunity in commercial real estate create wealth and break cycles of poverty?

September 4, 2026 · Commercially Speaking Podcast · Daymond Kirby

← See the Commercially Speaking Podcast fiche

Access to knowledge and opportunity in commercial real estate is the core mission of the Black Foundation, which connects talented, driven people with the resources they need to succeed. By democratizing access to commercial real estate knowledge and connections, individuals can become brokers, investors, or developers—giving them control over their income, the ability to build wealth, and ultimately leave a family legacy that breaks cycles of poverty.

The Hand-Up Model: From Disconnection to Opportunity

The Black Foundation operates as a 501c3 private foundation focused on connecting parties that have traditionally been disconnected by design. Rather than offering charity, Kirby emphasizes the foundation works with people who are already showing they have the motivation and willingness to put in the work. The foundation then equips them with all the resources needed to succeed in commercial real estate.

This model directly targets wealth-building rather than subsistence. When someone controls their own paycheck as an investor, broker, or developer—rather than working in a time-based employment model—the income potential and path to generational wealth shift fundamentally.

Why Knowledge Removes the Barrier to Entry

Kirby started his own commercial real estate career in June 2020, achieving his CCIM designation just three years later in 2023. His rapid progression demonstrates that career trajectory in commercial real estate depends primarily on knowledge and connections, not inherited advantage. The Black Foundation replicates this pathway systematically for minorities and veterans by removing information barriers and creating professional networks.

As a resource detailed in this episode of Commercially Speaking, once individuals understand the different pathways available—whether as brokers, investors, developers, or property managers—they can position themselves strategically rather than drifting into whatever employment is available.

"Access knowledge and opportunity is really what we focus on at the Black Foundation."

The specific mechanism here is worth noting: Kirby's episode also explores the two distinct economic models—time-and-effort versus results-based income—and how they apply directly to roles within commercial real estate, showing listeners exactly why knowledge of these distinctions matters for wealth-building decisions.

Scaling Access Across Career Stages

The Black Foundation does not operate with a one-size approach. Education and mentorship offerings are structured by career stage—from high school students receiving career day exposure through to professionals seeking investor or developer pathways. This tiered model ensures that the foundation meets people where they are and provides the specific knowledge and connections relevant to their next step.

Currently operational in Tennessee with Arizona expansion planned for the coming year, the foundation's growth reflects both demand for these pathways and the scalability of the knowledge-transfer model at its core.

Key takeaways

  • Access to knowledge and professional connections in commercial real estate removes the primary barrier to entry for minorities and veterans seeking wealth-building careers.
  • The Black Foundation operates as a "hand up, not a handout," working specifically with motivated people and providing them comprehensive resources to become brokers, investors, or developers.
  • Income control through real estate roles—particularly results-based positions—creates pathways to generational wealth unavailable in traditional time-and-effort employment models.
  • Structured education and mentorship at different career stages—from high school through professional—ensures scalable access regardless of starting point.

What educational pathways does the Black Foundation offer across different career stages?

Education offerings depend on career cycle: middle and high school students receive career day representation, mindset manifestation, and thinking bigger, while professionals seek education aligned with investor, broker, or developer pathways.

What are the two distinct economic models for career paths, and how do they apply to commercial real estate roles?

There is the time and effort economy where people are paid for showing up and doing the work, and the results economy where income depends on producing outcomes. In commercial real estate, broker, investor, and developer roles operate on results-based models, creating wealth-building potential absent in traditional employment.

How should someone with no connections break into commercial real estate as a career in their 20s?

The first step is education and understanding the different pathways available—broker, investor, developer, property manager, asset manager, or other roles—so you can choose strategically and position yourself for mentorship and opportunity.
