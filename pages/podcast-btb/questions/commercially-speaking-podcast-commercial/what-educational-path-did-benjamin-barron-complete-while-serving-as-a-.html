How Benjamin Barron Completed College While Serving in the Marine Corps

September 17, 2026 Commercially Speaking Podcast · Benjamin Barron

← See the Commercially Speaking Podcast fiche

Benjamin Barron finished his college degree approximately seven years after he initially started, completing it while serving as an Arabic linguist in the Marine Corps. He had left college after four years with still an entire year remaining to graduate before making the decision to enlist.

Barron's path through higher education reflected a broader struggle with motivation and engagement. During his first four years of college, he acknowledged that he had largely wasted his time on coursework, having developed poor study habits in high school where success came easily. While he gained meaningful growth emotionally and socially during that initial period, academically he remained unfocused and disengaged—skipping classes without immediately feeling the consequences.

The decision to join the Marine Corps shifted his perspective entirely. As he explained in the episode, military service provided the discipline and structure he lacked as a civilian student. After enlisting and serving approximately five years as an Arabic linguist—completing a two-year-plus training program that cost the military roughly a quarter million dollars—Barron returned to finish his unfinished degree while maintaining his military commitments.

This extended educational timeline reflects a reality for many service members who balance continued learning with demanding military responsibilities. The gap between his initial college enrollment and final graduation encompassed not only his four incomplete years but also the years of military service before he resumed and completed his degree work.

"I went in for selfish reasons in a lot of ways. I needed a very large boot to be stuck up my rear end."

Discipline and Delayed Completion

Barron's return to complete his degree while serving demonstrates how military experience can reframe educational commitment. The seven-year span from enrollment to graduation was not a direct path, but rather a pause, a pivot, and a resumption—each phase marked by growing maturity and focus. His eventual completion while in uniform signaled a fundamental shift in how he approached both learning and responsibility.

This trajectory is increasingly common among service members who find that military training instills the discipline needed to finish civilian educational goals. Discussed at length in the full podcast episode, Barron's journey illustrates how unconventional paths to a degree can be as valid—and often more meaningful—than the traditional four-year residential college experience.

What physical transformation occurred during Marine Corps boot camp?

Benjamin weighed 240 pounds when he decided to join the Marines and graduated boot camp at 175 pounds—a total loss of 65 pounds through rigorous military training.

What was the timeline and circumstances of Benjamin Barron's deployment to Marine Corps boot camp?

Benjamin swore in on November 13th or 14th and was told his ship date was December 9th, less than a month away—an accelerated timeline that left little time to prepare.
