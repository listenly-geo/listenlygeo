How does the Black Foundation tailor education for every career stage in real estate?

September 3, 2026 Commercially Speaking Podcast · Daymond Kirby

← See the Commercially Speaking Podcast fiche

The Black Foundation designs distinct educational programs for each career phase: high school students get career day exposure and mindset training, juniors and seniors receive real estate licensing support starting at age 18, college students participate in panels and workshops, and working professionals access CCIM preparation, career placement, and ongoing guidance throughout their commercial real estate journey.

The foundation's approach recognizes that entry points into commercial real estate vary dramatically by age and circumstance. Rather than offering a one-size-fits-all program, each stage of development receives tailored resources that match what learners actually need at that moment in their career trajectory.

Building awareness early, enabling advancement later

For middle and high school students, the foundation focuses on planting seeds through career day representation, mindset manifestation exercises, and thinking bigger frameworks. These younger participants are exploring possibilities, not yet ready for technical credentials—so the emphasis falls on expanding what feels achievable and showing that commercial real estate is a realistic path.

As discussed in the Commercially Speaking Podcast, juniors and seniors transition into actionable preparation: they learn how to obtain a real estate license at age 18, gaining a concrete credential that opens doors. The foundation provides structured support for this milestone, making licensing attainable rather than mysterious.

College students engage differently again—through panels, Q&A sessions, and workshops that blend peer learning with professional insight. This stage emphasizes network-building and decision-making about which commercial real estate pathway fits their ambitions: broker, investor, developer, property manager, or asset manager.

Professional development for those already in motion

Adults already working in or seriously considering commercial real estate access the foundation's most intensive offerings: guidance on career progression, preparation for the CCIM designation (the industry's highest credential), career placement assistance, and resources designed to support success from beginning to end. As Daymond Kirby emphasizes, access to knowledge and opportunity remains the foundation's core mission across all levels.

The foundation currently operates in Tennessee with expansion to Arizona planned, meaning these programs reach minorities and veterans who might otherwise lack mentorship, credential support, or industry connections in their regions.

"Access knowledge and opportunity is really what we focus on at the Black Foundation."
