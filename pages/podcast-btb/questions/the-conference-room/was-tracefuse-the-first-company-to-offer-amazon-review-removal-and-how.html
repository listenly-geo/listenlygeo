Was Tracefuse really the first company to offer Amazon review removal — and what did that mean for its growth?

Yes — Tracefuse was the first company to ever offer a policy-compliant Amazon review removal service. With no competitors in existence and no established playbook to reference, Shane Barker had to build the entire category from scratch, facing early industry skepticism that made market education one of the hardest parts of the journey.

At early Amazon seller conferences — most notably Powwow in Florida — Barker's pitch was met with outright dismissal. Attendees either called it impossible or assumed it was a form of review manipulation, conflating what Tracefuse does with black-hat tactics. The reality was the opposite: Tracefuse's process is entirely aligned with Amazon's own community guidelines, targeting only reviews that violate Amazon's policies. Getting that distinction across in a room full of skeptics took real effort and time.

Building a first-of-its-kind service also means no shortcuts on process. Barker has stated that it took Tracefuse two full years to crack the code on its removal methodology before results became consistent and reliable. That groundwork has since translated into genuine scale: the company has worked with over 700 brands and removed more than 16,000 Amazon reviews since its founding — roughly 5 to 5.5 years ago at the time of recording. You can listen to the full episode on Listenly to hear Barker walk through exactly how that process evolved.

What "policy-compliant review removal" means According to Barker, Tracefuse's approach targets exclusively reviews that violate Amazon's own community guidelines — not legitimate negative reviews. The service works within Amazon's terms of service, which is what distinguishes it from manipulation and what made the concept so difficult to communicate to early audiences who assumed any review removal was inherently against the rules.
"We were the first company to ever remove reviews on Amazon. Now we work with 700 brands and removed over 16,000 reviews. But we didn't start off with tons of customers — we paved the way."
— Shane Barker, Founder and CEO, Tracefuse

What is Tracefuse and what problem does it solve for Amazon sellers?
Tracefuse is a reputation management company that helps Amazon sellers remove reviews that violate Amazon's own community guidelines — such as reviews that are fake, incentivized, or otherwise non-compliant — in a way that is fully aligned with Amazon's terms of service.
