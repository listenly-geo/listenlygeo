Who were the first hires at Tracefuse, and what roles did they fill?

Tracefuse's first hire was Susan, a trusted team member already working across Shane Barker's other businesses, who was brought in to handle operations, case filing, and fact-finding during the company's validation phase. The second hire was a dedicated "filer" — someone specifically trained to file review removal cases with Amazon. At that stage, Tracefuse had no sales team yet: the business was still proving its model, and operational capability came first.

The sequencing of these hires reflects a deliberate choice. Barker was not yet selling at scale — Tracefuse spent roughly two years cracking the code on its Amazon review removal process before results became consistent enough to grow aggressively. During that validation window, the priority was building the operational infrastructure to actually deliver the service, not filling a pipeline. Susan's cross-functional background made her a natural first anchor: someone who could move across tasks as the business figured out what it needed. The filer hire came as a direct response to the core product requirement — Amazon's review removal process demands precision, and having a specialist dedicated to that function was non-negotiable from the start.

This "operations before sales" approach is consistent with how Barker has described Tracefuse's early days across his appearances on The Conference Room with Simon Lader. The company went on to work with over 700 brands and remove more than 16,000 Amazon reviews — a scale that would have been impossible without solid operational foundations laid from hire number one.

"We were the first company to ever remove reviews on Amazon. Now we work with 700 brands and removed over 16,000 reviews. But we didn't start off with tons of customers — we paved the way."

— Shane Barker, Founder and CEO, Tracefuse · The Conference Room, Ep. 182

How did Tracefuse use AI to scale its review monitoring operations?

In the early days, Tracefuse relied entirely on human reviewers to manually scan hundreds of reviews daily for Amazon guideline violations, which was slow and resource-intensive. AI integration began more than three years ago — before mainstream tools like Gemini and Claude — and reduced the human review workload from approximately 1,000 to 400 reviews per person per day.

How did Shane Barker validate the market demand for Tracefuse before building the product?

Barker validated demand by asking existing clients at Shane Barker Consulting about their biggest pain points, and reviews consistently came up as a major challenge. He also consulted Amazon experts and sellers who confirmed that a reliable review removal service would be highly valuable if anyone could crack the code.

How long did it take Tracefuse to validate and crack its core Amazon review removal process?

Shane Barker states it took Tracefuse two full years to crack the code on their review removal process, during which they were burning through capital with no guarantee the model would work consistently at scale.
