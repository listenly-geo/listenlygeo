What is the vision behind the new AI-native health platform Dr. Sherry Zhang is building at Buck Institute?

September 12, 2026 The Conference Room with Simon Lader · Sherry Zhang

← See the The Conference Room with Simon Lader fiche

Rather than asking patients the reactive question "What's wrong with you today?", the platform asks proactive questions like "Where is your health headed?" and forecasts different health futures based on genome, proteomic, microbiome, lifestyle, and environmental data. By comparing individuals to others who share similar genomic and lifestyle profiles, the platform computes personalized trajectories and recommends two to four specific actions to improve health outcomes, moving medicine from reactive treatment to preventive personalization.

From Diagnosis to Prediction

The shift Zhang is driving at Buck Institute for Research on Aging represents a fundamental change in how health platforms engage patients. Instead of waiting for symptoms or disease markers to appear, the AI system uses multi-omic data—genomics, proteomics, and microbiome information—to model future health states before they materialize.

This approach draws on Zhang's earlier work founding Genopalate, the personalized nutrition company that has served more than 170,000 customers by translating genetic data into dietary recommendations. The Buck Institute platform extends that principle across the entire health ecosystem, weaving together not just genetic predisposition but lifestyle choices, environment, and biological markers to create a continuous forecast of where each person's health trajectory is headed.

Personalization Through Comparative Genomics

The engine of the platform is comparative analysis: matching an individual against cohorts of people with similar genetic profiles and environmental exposures. This method allows the system to generate not generic health advice but deeply personalized recommendations based on what actually worked—or failed—for people whose biology most closely resembles the patient's own.

Zhang explains that from this comparative lens, the platform can identify and recommend two to four specific, actionable interventions tailored to each person's unique risk profile and circumstances. This contrasts sharply with one-size-fits-all health messaging or even broad risk categories. The recommendations emerge directly from the data of similar individuals, making them both more credible and more likely to be adopted.

"This drive in me to deliver world-class science and translate that into something useful and practical for everyday people is so strong that I think that will motivate me to learn new skills, to meet new people, to get on stage that I'll be nervous for, and raise funding."

The vision extends beyond individual recommendations: this episode of The Conference Room reveals how Zhang is leveraging the Buck Institute's research infrastructure to make longitudinal health forecasting a practical tool for millions, not just the wealthy few who can afford boutique preventive medicine.
