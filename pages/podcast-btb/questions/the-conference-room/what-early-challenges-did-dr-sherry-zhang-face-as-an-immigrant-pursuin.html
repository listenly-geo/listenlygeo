What early challenges did Dr. Sherry Zhang face as an immigrant pursuing higher education in the United States?

13 September 2026 The Conference Room with Simon Lader · Sherry Zhang

← See The Conference Room with Simon Lader fiche

Dr. Sherry Zhang's visa was rejected three times before she could start her PhD at Marquette University in Wisconsin, forcing her to arrive late to her program start date. Her parents purchased a one-way ticket to support her attempt, demonstrating the sacrifice and determination required to overcome immigration barriers to higher education in the United States.

Arriving in Wisconsin with minimal financial resources—just three bags and $3,000 from her parents—Zhang faced not only bureaucratic obstacles but also environmental challenges. Wisconsin's harsh winter winters presented a stark contrast to her expectations, yet she discovered the university community to be unexpectedly warm and welcoming.

As Zhang explains in the episode, this early environment became foundational to her scientific training and would later fuel her entrepreneurial journey. The acceptance and support she found at Marquette, despite arriving as a first-generation immigrant with limited resources, shaped her approach to building inclusive communities in her own work.

"This drive in me to deliver world-class science and translate that into something useful and practical for everyday people is so strong that I think that will motivate me to learn new skills, to meet new people, to get on stage that I'll be nervous for, and raise funding."

The visa rejection process itself reveals the systemic barriers many international students face. Three denials required persistence and family financial support that most aspiring students cannot access. Zhang's parents' decision to purchase a one-way ticket—rather than a round-trip ticket that might suggest her departure—represented both a vote of confidence and a risk, signaling their commitment to her success in the United States.

What makes Zhang's story particularly instructive is how she transformed these early hardships into fuel for ambition. The combination of immigration challenges, financial constraints, and environmental adaptation—arriving in one of the coldest American states—could have deterred her. Instead, as discussed at length in this podcast, she credits that difficult transition period with building the resilience and work ethic that would define her later achievements in both academia and entrepreneurship.
