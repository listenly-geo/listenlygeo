What are the visa priorities for non-EU citizens wanting to move to the Costa del Sol?

21 August 2026 The Costa Property Podcast · Warner

← Voir la fiche The Costa Property Podcast

For anyone coming from outside Europe, confirming visa eligibility is Priority One — it must happen before any property search, budget planning, or school research. Post-Brexit UK buyers face an additional hurdle: the rules changed after Brexit, and they must consult a Spanish lawyer first to confirm whether they are actually eligible to live in Spain.

Visa eligibility is not a formality — it is the starting gate

The framing is deliberate. Visa eligibility is not one item on a checklist among many — it is described as the first question that determines whether everything else is even worth pursuing. If you cannot legally live in Spain, no property purchase changes that.

This is why Warner and his co-host at The Costa Property Podcast structure their relocation guidance around this sequence: establish the legal right to be there first, then work through the lifestyle and financial questions. Skipping this step is the most common and costly mistake non-EU movers make.

Post-Brexit UK buyers: a changed landscape that catches people off guard

Before 2021, British citizens enjoyed free movement across the EU, including Spain. That changed with Brexit. UK nationals are now treated as non-EU citizens for residency purposes, which means the same visa-first logic applies to them just as it does to buyers coming from the US, Australia, or anywhere outside the European Union.

The specific recommendation is unambiguous: consult a Spanish lawyer before taking any other step. A qualified lawyer can assess individual circumstances — employment status, income sources, pension arrangements — and identify which visa route, if any, is available. This advice is detailed further in the episode, where Warner walks through each relocation question in turn rather than treating them as interchangeable.

Can foreign buyers get a mortgage in Spain on the Costa del Sol?

Foreign buyers can access Spanish mortgages, but banks will typically lend between 60% to 70% of the loan-to-value ratio. Understanding this lending ceiling is essential before planning any purchase budget.

How much deposit do you need beyond the property price when buying on the Costa del Sol as a foreign buyer?

Beyond the list price of the property, buyers should budget an additional 10% to 12% on top of the purchase price to cover taxes and fees. On a 350,000-euro property, that means setting aside an extra 35,000 to 42,000 euros before any negotiations begin.

Key takeaways
