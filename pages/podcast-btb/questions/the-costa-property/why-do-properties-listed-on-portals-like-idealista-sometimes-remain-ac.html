Why do properties listed on portals like Idealista sometimes remain active even after being sold?

10 September 2026 The Costa Property Podcast · Warner

← See the The Costa Property Podcast fiche

In Spain, the real estate market operates without a centralized system forcing agents to remove listings immediately after a sale. Property owners can list their property with multiple agents simultaneously, and when one agent closes a deal, the others may not receive notification, leaving listings active on portals like Idealista long after the property has sold.

Multiple agents, fragmented information

The core issue stems from how the Spanish property market is structured. Unlike some markets with centralized listing databases, agents in Spain operate largely independently, and each agent maintains their own information about a property's status. When you list with Agent A, Agent B, and Agent C simultaneously—a common practice—they may not communicate the moment one of them closes a sale.

This fragmentation creates a lag where the property portal reflects outdated information. The agent who sold the property updates their records, but the other agents may not. If a potential buyer searches Idealista days or weeks after the purchase completes, they still see an active listing because no one has instructed the portal to remove it.

Keeping listings alive to capture leads

Beyond communication delays, some agencies deliberately maintain listings even when a property has been sold or is in a reservation phase. As explained in The Costa Property Podcast, this happens because agencies face supply constraints in the market and use active listings as a lead generation tool.

When an agency keeps a sold property visible, they capture inquiries from interested buyers who then contact them looking for the advertised property. These agencies can then offer clients alternative properties, effectively using the sold listing to build their buyer pipeline. It's a gray-area practice born from real market pressure: demand often exceeds available inventory, so agencies stretch their resources by converting post-sale inquiries into new business.

For property hunters on portals, the practical takeaway is clear: an active listing does not guarantee availability. A property shown on Idealista today may have been under contract for weeks or already closed. This is why contacting the agent directly to confirm the property's true status—before investing time or emotion—is essential. The portal price you see is only reliable if you verify the listing status in real time with the agency handling the sale.

Understanding this dynamic, discussed in detail across this episode of The Costa Property Podcast, protects you from disappointment and helps you approach property hunting with realistic expectations about what the numbers on your screen truly represent.
