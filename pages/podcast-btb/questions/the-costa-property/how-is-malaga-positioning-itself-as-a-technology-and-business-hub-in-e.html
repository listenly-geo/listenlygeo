How is Málaga positioning itself as a technology and business hub in Europe?

August 24, 2026 The Costa Property Podcast · Warner

← See the full The Costa Property Podcast page

Málaga is becoming one of the biggest tech and AI hubs in Europe — and that changes the relocation equation entirely. The Costa del Sol is no longer just a destination for retirees or remote workers: it is now a place where careers and businesses can actively grow.

Beyond the beach: Málaga's emergence as a serious employment destination

For years, moving to the Costa del Sol was framed as a lifestyle trade-off — you gained sunshine and pace of life, but you left behind career opportunities. That framing is becoming outdated. As Warner discusses in The Costa Property Podcast, Málaga's growing tech and AI ecosystem is creating real employment options that didn't exist even five years ago.

This matters for anyone doing the financial maths on a relocation. When both partners in a household can realistically find work in the region — not just one person working remotely while the other puts their career on pause — the numbers look very different. The Costa del Sol stops being a beautiful compromise and starts looking like a genuine opportunity.

It is worth noting that Warner and his co-host still advise that one partner should plan for roughly three months focused on family integration before returning to full employment after a move. But the key shift is that returning to employment is increasingly viable locally — and in a high-growth sector — rather than requiring a full remote setup or an international commute.

"We knew that the life that we had in Ireland wasn't sustainable for the growth of our family and our children and we just made that decision."

325 sunshine days a year — and now a tech ecosystem to match

The lifestyle case for the Costa del Sol has always been strong. The region averages 325 sunshine days per year, and Málaga has long attracted international buyers drawn by climate, cost of living, and proximity to the rest of Europe. What is new is the professional infrastructure developing around it.

Málaga's positioning as a tech and AI hub means that relocators arriving today — whether from Ireland, the UK, or elsewhere — are entering a market that is growing, not stagnating. That is the argument Warner makes in this episode: the Costa del Sol is no longer a place you move to despite your career ambitions, but potentially because of them.

For buyers and movers who have been hesitant because of employment uncertainty, this development is significant. It broadens the pool of people for whom the move makes practical sense — including younger professionals and families at an earlier stage of their working lives, not just those approaching retirement or already set up for full remote work.

All of this context is unpacked step by step in The Costa Property Podcast, which addresses the full range of questions that come up when turning a Costa del Sol holiday into a real relocation plan.

The hosts initially rented when they moved 10 years ago, but now advise that if you can afford to buy, buying is often the better option — the rental market on the Costa del Sol has become increasingly tight and competitive since then.

For anyone coming from outside Europe, understanding visa eligibility is described as Priority One before taking any other steps. Post-Brexit UK buyers also need to assess their options carefully before proceeding with any relocation or property plans.
