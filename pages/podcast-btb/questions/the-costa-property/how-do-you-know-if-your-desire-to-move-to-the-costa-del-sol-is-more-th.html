How do you know if your desire to move to the Costa del Sol is more than just post-holiday blues?

30 Aug 2026 The Costa Property Podcast · Warner

← See The Costa Property Podcast fiche

The difference lies in persistence and depth of feeling. Normal end-of-holiday sadness fades once you return to routine and life resumes—but if that internal restlessness doesn't go away, it signals something deeper worth exploring seriously.

Every summer, thousands of people experience that moment on the last evening of their Costa del Sol holiday: standing in the warm sunset, surrounded by beaches and endless blue sky, they ask themselves the question—what if I didn't have to leave? What if this became my life? The challenge is distinguishing between the fleeting melancholy that follows any good vacation and the kind of persistent desire that points toward a real life change.

According to the hosts of The Costa Property Podcast, the test is simple but requires patience. Return home, let your normal routine take hold—work, family commitments, weather, daily obligations all return to their usual pace. Then wait and observe whether that feeling of longing stays with you or quietly fades away.

The Internal Noise That Won't Disappear

If after a few weeks or months of settling back into your regular life that deep internal questioning persists—if you find yourself still thinking about the Costa del Sol, still imagining what your life there would look like—that's the signal Warner and his co-host recognize as genuine. They describe it not as fleeting nostalgia but as a kind of knowing that something needs to change.

This is the moment to move from daydreaming to active exploration. Instead of dismissing the feeling as "just holiday blues," plan another trip back to the Costa del Sol—but this time with intentionality. Begin researching seriously: look into visa requirements for Spain, explore employment options, understand the financial realities, and spend time imagining the concrete details of living here rather than vacationing here.

"We knew that the life that we had in Ireland wasn't sustainable for the growth of our family and our children and we just made that decision."

Warner and his co-host speak from direct experience—they made exactly this move a decade ago, uprooting their young family from Ireland with all the questions and doubts that come with such a decision. They've since worked with many others who faced the same moment of wondering whether their Costa del Sol dream was real or just an echo of a good vacation. The pattern they've observed is clear: if the feeling is genuine, it doesn't disappear once you're back in your regular life, and that's your signal to explore further.

The key is not to suppress the feeling or dismiss it too quickly as impractical fantasy. As discussed in this episode of The Costa Property Podcast, taking it seriously—planning another visit with research intent, beginning to answer the practical questions about visas, finances, and employment—is how you move from "I wonder" to "I'm actually considering this."

Key takeaways

  • Post-holiday sadness typically fades within weeks as normal routine resumes; genuine relocation desire persists over months.
  • The real test is returning home and observing whether the internal longing to change your life stays with you or quietly disappears.
  • If the feeling persists, move from passive daydreaming to active research—plan another trip with intentionality and begin exploring practical details like visas, employment, and finances.
  • Personal experience and honest self-reflection are more reliable than logic alone when deciding whether to pursue such a significant life change.

How should couples or families manage the transition period when both partners need to work after relocating to the Costa del Sol?

The hosts advise that one partner should take the lead on employment while the other focuses on integrating the family, particularly settling children into school and establishing daily routines before returning to full employment.

What employment options exist for people relocating to the Costa del Sol who still need to work?

Relocators have several options: approaching their current international employer's HR department about a global mobility program if a legal entity exists in Spain, seeking remote work arrangements, or exploring opportunities with local companies and tech hubs.

How is Malaga positioning itself as a technology and business hub in Europe?

According to the hosts, Malaga is becoming one of the biggest tech and AI hubs in Europe, which opens up employment opportunities for relocators beyond traditional tourism and property sectors.
