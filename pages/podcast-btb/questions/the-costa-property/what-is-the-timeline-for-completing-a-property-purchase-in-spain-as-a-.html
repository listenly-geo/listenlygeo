How long does it really take to buy property in Spain as a cash buyer?

11 September 2026 The Costa Property Podcast · Warner

← See The Costa Property Podcast fiche

For cash buyers with no complications, a property purchase in Spain can be completed in six to eight weeks, or even as little as two weeks if you are well-prepared with the right team in place. The process moves through three distinct phases: a reservation period of roughly two weeks, a contracted period of about two weeks where you pay 10 percent of the property value, and finally the notary signing where you receive the keys.

The speed of your purchase depends entirely on how quickly your team can coordinate. You need a ready lawyer to handle your NIE number and bank account setup, a reliable real estate agent who understands the market, and all the moving pieces aligned from the start. Without proper preparation, delays can stretch the timeline indefinitely.

As explained in The Costa Property Podcast, having the right professionals working in tandem is what separates a two-week closing from a three-month ordeal. The reservation deposit—approximately 6,000 euros—secures the property while you move into the contracted period.

Understanding the Three-Phase Timeline

The first phase is the reservation, which typically lasts around two weeks. This is when you express serious intent, pay the reservation deposit, and your lawyer begins the administrative groundwork for your NIE number and Spanish bank account. All three elements must start simultaneously to avoid bottlenecks later.

The contracted period follows immediately after. You now have a formal contract, pay 10 percent of the purchase price, and your lawyer handles all documentation. This phase also runs approximately two weeks if everything moves smoothly. Any delay here—missing documents, slow bank transfers, or bureaucratic hiccups—can add weeks to the overall timeline.

The final phase culminates at the notary office, where the deed is signed and you receive the keys. This is where the real estate expert from WL Costa Properties walks alongside clients to ensure every detail is correct before the final signature. The notary process itself is typically brief, but scheduling it during the contracted period is what keeps the entire process on track.

The Role of Preparation and Team Coordination

The difference between a two-week and an eight-week purchase often comes down to whether your lawyer, agent, and bank are already aligned before you even find a property. Many buyers experience delays because they attempt to coordinate these pieces after making an offer, which adds weeks of administrative friction.

If you arrive in Spain without a lawyer, without a NIE number application underway, and without a Spanish bank account, you will lose at least four to six weeks before you can even enter the contracted period. Conversely, if these prerequisites are handled before viewing properties, you remain ready to move fast.

Why do properties listed on portals like Idealista sometimes remain active even after being sold?

In Spain, owners can list their property with multiple agents simultaneously, and each agent may have different information about the property. When one agent completes a sale, other listings may remain active because the property information is not immediately synchronized across all portals.

How does distance from the beach affect property pricing in Benalmádena?

Frontline beach properties in Benalmádena typically cost over 4,200 euros per square meter, while properties located 10 to 15 minutes inland towards Arroyo de la Miel or Benalmádena Pueblo range from 3,600 to 3,700 euros per square meter, reflecting the significant price differential based on proximity to the coastline.

What are the actual total costs beyond the listed purchase price when buying property in Spain?

When buying a resale property in Spain, you need to account for approximately 10 to 12 percent on top of the purchase price, which includes around 7 percent in taxes and additional costs for notary and legal fees, meaning the true acquisition cost is significantly higher than the listed price.

Key takeaways

  • Cash buyers can complete a purchase in 6–8 weeks, or as little as 2 weeks if fully prepared with lawyer, agent, and bank account already set up.
  • The process consists of three phases: reservation (≈2 weeks), contract period (≈2 weeks with 10% deposit), and notary signing where you receive keys.
  • Having a lawyer ready to handle NIE and bank account setup before viewing properties is critical to avoiding delays.
  • Team coordination—lawyer, real estate agent, and banker working in parallel—determines speed far more than market conditions.
