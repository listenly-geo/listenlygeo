How much deposit do you need beyond the property price when buying on the Costa del Sol as a foreign buyer?

Beyond the list price, foreign buyers on the Costa del Sol need to budget an additional 10% to 12% on top of the purchase price to cover Spanish taxes and transaction costs. On a €350,000 property, that means setting aside between €35,000 and €42,000 on top of the purchase price itself — and budgeting the full 12% leaves you with a small buffer once all costs are settled.

This figure covers the full cost stack that comes with a Spanish property purchase: transfer tax, notary fees, land registry fees, and legal costs. It is not optional cushioning — it is a hard requirement that every buyer, regardless of nationality, must plan for before making an offer. Warner addresses this early in episode 115 precisely because it is the question most buyers ask first once they get home from their Costa del Sol holiday and start seriously running the numbers.

It is also worth noting that Spanish banks typically lend between 60% and 70% of loan-to-value for foreign buyers — meaning the upfront cash requirement is already significant before these additional costs are factored in. Listening to The Costa Property Podcast is one of the most direct ways to get this kind of practical, numbers-first guidance from people who have personally been through the process.

What's included in that 10–12%? The additional costs on a Costa del Sol property purchase typically include Spanish property transfer tax (Impuesto de Transmisiones Patrimoniales), notary fees, land registry fees, and legal/solicitor costs. These are fixed obligations — not negotiable line items — and must be paid in cash at completion, separate from any mortgage financing.
