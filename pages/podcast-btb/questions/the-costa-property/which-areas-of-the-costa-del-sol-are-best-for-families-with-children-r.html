Which areas of the Costa del Sol are best for families with children relocating from abroad?

August 23, 2026 The Costa Property Podcast · Warner

← Voir la fiche The Costa Property Podcast

Benalmadena is the standout recommendation for families with children — especially if you need quick access to Malaga airport or have teenagers who need to get around independently, since Benalmadena sits directly on the train line. The approach is deliberate: shortlist three areas, visit each in both summer and winter, and use a structured quiz or relocation expert to match your family's specific needs to the right location.

Benalmadena: the train line that changes everything for teenagers

Most families arriving on the Costa del Sol fixate on proximity to the beach or proximity to an international school. What often gets overlooked is public transport — specifically, whether a teenager can get from home to a friend's house, a sports club, or a part-time job without needing a parent behind the wheel every time.

Benalmadena solves that problem directly. It is one of the few towns on the Costa del Sol served by the commuter rail network connecting the coast to Malaga city centre. For families with teenagers, that rail access translates into genuine independence — a factor that Warner flags as decisive when matching families to a location, as discussed in The Costa Property Podcast.

The proximity to Malaga airport is the second practical reason Benalmadena keeps coming up for relocating families. Early arrivals — especially those whose move is phased, with one partner working remotely while the other manages the family integration — benefit enormously from cutting the airport transfer to a short, predictable journey.

The three-area shortlist: why summer-only viewings mislead families every time

Warner is consistent on one point: never judge a Costa del Sol location from a single summer visit. The coast is uniformly attractive under August sun. The differences that actually matter to a family — school runs, Saturday activities, weekend crowds, restaurant availability — only become visible in winter.

The method he recommends is straightforward: identify three candidate areas, then visit each twice. The summer visit gives you the lifestyle feel. The winter visit reveals whether the infrastructure and community hold up when the tourists have gone home. It is a point developed in detail across episodes of The Costa Property Podcast.

Beyond the visits, Warner and his co-host offer a location quiz through WL Costa Properties — a tool that takes under 60 seconds to complete and returns a personalised location profile by email. The quiz is built around family dependencies: school proximity, transport needs, budget, lifestyle pace. It is designed to surface the mismatch between where people think they want to live and where their daily lives will actually work best.

"We knew that the life that we had in Ireland wasn't sustainable for the growth of our family and our children and we just made that decision."

The relocation Warner describes was not a calculated spreadsheet exercise. It was a deeply felt decision made with two babies in tow, with the couple asking themselves repeatedly whether they were making a mistake. Ten years on, that lived experience is precisely what makes their location advice credible — they are not advising from theory, but from having navigated exactly the family dependencies their clients bring to them today.

Warner also notes that one partner should expect to spend approximately three months focused on family integration after arriving — managing school enrolment, establishing routines, building a local network — before returning to full employment. That practical reality affects which areas work for families: a location with walkable infrastructure matters far more in those early months than it might appear on a map. It is one of the nuances explored regularly in this podcast.

Key takeaways

  • Benalmadena is the top recommendation for relocating families: rail access to Malaga city gives teenagers genuine independence, and the airport is close enough to make phased relocations manageable.
  • Never base a location decision on a single summer visit — each shortlisted area should be seen in winter too, when the tourist layer disappears and real daily infrastructure becomes visible.
  • A structured location quiz (under 60 seconds) or a relocation expert can match your family's specific dependencies — schools, transport, budget, lifestyle pace — to the right area before you commit.
  • Plan for roughly three months of family integration time after arrival, which makes walkable, well-connected locations like Benalmadena especially practical in the early phase of the move.
