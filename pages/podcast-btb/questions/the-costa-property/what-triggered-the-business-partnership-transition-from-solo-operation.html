What triggered the business partnership transition from solo operation to joint family venture?

September 21, 2026 The Costa Property Podcast · Warner Unknown

← See The Costa Property Podcast fiche

After seven years of building WL Costa Properties solo while his wife worked in the corporate sector, Warner's wife decided to step back from corporate travel and upskill in digital strategy, triggering the shift to a joint family venture. Her transition to part-time consulting with her previous employer (25 hours per week) created space for her to identify a critical gap: Warner was missing growth opportunities in online marketing and customer reach, which led directly to the launch of the podcast and digital resources.

The catalyst: Work-life balance meets business vision

For the first seven years after launching WL Costa Properties in 2017, Warner operated the business solo, focusing entirely on property sales and direct client relationships. Meanwhile, his wife was managing a demanding corporate role that involved extensive travel across Northern Europe, making family time with their two young children increasingly difficult.

When their son turned 13, the family reached a turning point. His wife's desire to be more present at home, combined with her recognition that Warner had built a strong foundation in sales, prompted her to engineer a career transition that would ultimately transform the business. She didn't leave the corporate world entirely—instead, she negotiated a part-time consulting arrangement with her previous employer at 25 hours per week, giving her the flexibility to shift focus.

Upskilling and identifying the gap

During her transition to part-time consulting, Warner's wife invested in upskilling herself in online marketing and digital strategy. This focused learning revealed something critical: Warner was reaching clients effectively through direct sales, but the business had no digital presence or content strategy to extend its reach into broader audiences.

She presented this insight directly to Warner—that significant growth opportunities were being missed because the business wasn't visible or accessible to people searching online. This conversation sparked the decision to launch the podcast and develop online resources, marking the formal shift from a solo-operator model to a true family partnership where both brought distinct expertise to the table.

As discussed in this episode, the partnership was not born from crisis but from a thoughtful realignment of family priorities with business potential. The podcast now serves as the centerpiece of that digital-first strategy, allowing the couple to reach international families considering relocation to the Costa del Sol—an audience Warner could never have reached through sales alone.
