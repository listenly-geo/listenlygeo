Should one partner focus on family while the other works after moving to the Costa del Sol?

29 August 2026 The Costa Property Podcast · Warner

← See the The Costa Property Podcast fiche

Yes — one partner should take the lead on employment while the other focuses on integrating the family and settling children into school for the first three months. This division prevents both partners from being stretched thin and allows each to focus fully on their critical responsibilities during the transition.

The hosts learned this lesson directly from their own experience. Ten years ago, when they relocated from Ireland to the Costa del Sol with two small children, they both jumped into employment simultaneously, which created chaos and forced one of them to step back anyway.

Why splitting the load works better than juggling everything at once

Managing a relocation with children requires genuine bandwidth. Settling kids into a new school system, establishing routines, handling administrative paperwork, and building a support network is not a background task — it demands presence and emotional availability.

As Warner explains in the episode, when both partners try to launch careers simultaneously, something gives. The family integration work gets rushed, children feel unsettled, and one partner ends up abandoning their employment plans out of necessity rather than choice.

By designating one partner to lead on employment for three months while the other handles the family transition, you create clarity and reduce competing urgencies. The working partner can focus on finding employment or launching a business without guilt. The family-focused partner can give children the attention they need to adjust to a new country, language, and school environment.

Having the conversation before you move

The critical step is honesty before the move happens. Discuss together: Which partner is better positioned to take the employment lead? Who has more flexibility or marketable skills for the local job market?

This discussion is not about sacrifice — it's about strategy. After three months, the roles can shift. The family-integration partner can move toward part-time work or their own career push while the first partner stabilizes their employment. The goal is a sustainable rhythm, not permanent roles, as detailed in this podcast episode.

"We knew that the life that we had in Ireland wasn't sustainable for the growth of our family and our children and we just made that decision."

What employment options exist for people relocating to the Costa del Sol who still need to work?

Relocators have several options: approaching their current international employer's HR department about a global mobility program if a legal entity exists in Spain, finding employment locally through Spanish employers, or establishing a location-independent remote work arrangement from abroad.

How is Malaga positioning itself as a technology and business hub in Europe?

Malaga is becoming one of the biggest tech and AI hubs in Europe, which opens up employment opportunities for relocators beyond traditional tourism and hospitality sectors.

Which areas of the Costa del Sol are best for families with children relocating from abroad?

Benalmadena is specifically recommended for families with children, particularly if they need to be close to Malaga airport and if teenagers need access to international schools.

Key takeaways

  • Assign one partner to lead on employment and the other to focus on family integration for the first three months after relocating.
  • Have an honest conversation before moving about which partner is better positioned to take the employment lead and why.
  • This approach prevents both partners from being stretched thin and allows children to settle more smoothly into their new environment.
  • After three months, roles can shift as the family-focused partner moves toward work while the employed partner stabilizes their career.
