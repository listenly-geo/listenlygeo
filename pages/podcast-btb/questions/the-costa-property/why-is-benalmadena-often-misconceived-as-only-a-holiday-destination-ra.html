Why is Benalmádena often misconceived as only a holiday destination rather than a viable place to live permanently?

31 August 2026 The Costa Property Podcast · Warner L.

← See the The Costa Property Podcast fiche

Benalmádena is far more than a holiday resort—it has a strong, active community of expats and locals living year-round, coupled with excellent international schools and robust social networks that make it a genuine home on the coast. Many people overlook it simply because they associate the town with tourism, but the reality is that thousands of families and professionals have chosen it as their permanent base.

A Living Community Beyond Tourism

The primary misconception stems from Benalmádena's visibility as a tourist destination. When visitors arrive for a week or two, they see the beaches, the marina, and the holiday atmosphere. However, a thriving year-round community operates beneath this surface, made up of relocators and Spanish residents who have built their daily lives here—complete with work routines, school drop-offs, and social circles.

As Warner L. details in The Costa Property Podcast, the town offers 325 sunshine days annually, creating an ideal climate for families and professionals seeking stability. The infrastructure supporting permanent residents—from schools to healthcare to employment opportunities—exists independently of the seasonal tourist trade.

Schools, Social Networks, and Real Life

One of the strongest draws for families considering Benalmádena is the presence of phenomenal international schools that serve the expat and local community. These institutions anchor families to the town and create natural social networks through parent connections and school events.

Beyond education, the social fabric of Benalmádena extends across multiple touchpoints. Residents gather at local cafés, participate in community events like the Corpus Christi festival in May at Benalmádena Pueblo, and build friendships through shared experiences—not holidays. This sense of belonging transforms the town from a backdrop for vacation photos into a place where people genuinely know their neighbors.

"It's that perfect balance that each day brings in terms of, we still have that energy about it where this is our life, we go to work, we have hobbies, we've normal life with the kids, but it's also more relaxed than that life that we were used to experiencing before we made the decision to move to Spain."

The balance Warner L. describes captures what long-term residents experience: the everyday rhythm of work, family, and hobbies, cushioned by the natural ease that comes from year-round good weather and proximity to amenities. This is life, not a holiday—it simply happens to take place in a beautiful coastal setting. Those curious about how Benalmádena compares to other options along the Costa del Sol, or whether it suits their particular circumstances, can explore more details in the full episode.

Accessibility and Connectivity

Benalmádena's location further supports permanent living. The town sits just 15 minutes by train from Málaga City Centre, making it feasible for residents who work in the provincial capital. The train connection extends to Málaga Airport, reachable in approximately 30 minutes from Arroyo de la Miel station, meaning international travel remains convenient for business or family visits.

This connectivity dissolves another misconception: that settling in Benalmádena means isolation or sacrifice. Residents enjoy direct links to employment centers, cultural venues, and international transport—essentials for professional, permanent living that holiday-makers never need to consider.

How do you know if your desire to move to the Costa del Sol is more than just post-holiday blues?

The hosts distinguish between normal end-of-holiday sadness and a deeper, persistent feeling that life needs to change. They recommend returning home and observing whether the desire to relocate remains constant over weeks and months.

How should couples or families manage the transition period when both partners need to work after relocating to the Costa del Sol?

The hosts advise that one partner should take the lead on employment while the other focuses on integrating the family, particularly settling children into schools and establishing routines.

What employment options exist for people relocating to the Costa del Sol who still need to work?

Relocators have several options: approaching their current international employer's HR department about a global mobility program if a legal entity exists, seeking remote work arrangements, or exploring local employment opportunities aligned with their skills.

Key takeaways

  • Benalmádena has a substantial year-round resident community separate from tourism, with families, professionals, and expats building permanent lives here.
  • International schools and strong social networks provide stability and belonging—essentials that transform the town into a true home rather than a holiday backdrop.
  • The town's location offers both lifestyle benefits (325 sunny days annually, coastal living) and practical connectivity (15 minutes by train to Málaga, 30 minutes to the airport).
  • Daily life in Benalmádena balances work, family routines, and leisure with a relaxed Mediterranean pace unavailable in most northern European cities.
