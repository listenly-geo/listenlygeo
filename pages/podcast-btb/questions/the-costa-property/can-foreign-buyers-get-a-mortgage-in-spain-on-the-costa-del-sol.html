Can foreign buyers get a mortgage in Spain on the Costa del Sol?

20 August 2026 The Costa Property Podcast · Warner

← Voir la fiche The Costa Property Podcast

Yes — foreign buyers can access Spanish mortgages, but banks will typically lend only 60% to 70% of the property's value. That ceiling is lower than most buyers expect. The first move is to confirm whether you actually qualify for that lending before it enters your budget calculations at all.

A lower LTV than you'd expect — and why it changes everything

In many northern European markets, buyers are accustomed to borrowing 80%, 85%, or even 90% of a property's value. Spain applies a stricter limit for non-resident foreign purchasers: Spanish banks cap their lending at 60% to 70% loan-to-value. That gap has to come from your own funds.

On a property listed at 350,000 euros, for example, a 60% LTV mortgage covers 210,000 euros. The remaining 140,000 euros — plus an additional 10% to 12% in taxes and purchase costs on top of the list price — must be in place before you can proceed. Running the numbers on this early is not optional; it defines your entire budget range.

How much deposit do you need beyond the property price when buying on the Costa del Sol as a foreign buyer?

Beyond the list price, buyers should budget an additional 10% to 12% on top of the purchase price to cover taxes and costs. On a 350,000-euro property, that means setting aside an extra 35,000 to 42,000 euros before the purchase can complete.

Is Benalmadena good for golf course properties on the Costa del Sol?

Benalmadena is specifically highlighted as the better option for buyers seeking a townhouse on a golf course, with this type of property being readily and consistently available there compared to other areas along the coast.

Should you try before you buy if you want a rural or village lifestyle in Spain?

Yes — the podcast recommends renting via Airbnb for a month or six weeks, and repeating this at different times of year, including outside the summer season, before committing to a purchase in a rural or village setting.

Key takeaways

  • Spanish banks lend between 60% and 70% of the property value to foreign buyers — significantly less than many buyers assume coming from markets with higher LTV ratios.
  • Mortgage eligibility must be confirmed before it factors into your budget; building a plan around lending you may not qualify for is one of the most common early mistakes.
  • The shortfall between the bank's contribution and the purchase price — on top of the 10%–12% in taxes and fees — must come from your own funds, making liquidity the real constraint.
  • Getting clear on financing first is the foundation of any realistic relocation or purchase plan on the Costa del Sol.
