What employment options exist for people relocating to the Costa del Sol who still need to work?

August 25, 2026 The Costa Property Podcast · Warner

← Voir la fiche The Costa Property Podcast

Relocators who need to keep working have three concrete paths: negotiate a remote arrangement through their current employer's global mobility program (if that employer has a legal entity in Spain), find employment with an international company already based on the Costa del Sol, or register as self-employed in Spain. For non-EU nationals, employment status is not optional detail — it is a core requirement baked directly into Spanish visa conditions and must be resolved before applying.

Three routes, one decision you need to make before anything else

The first route is the one most people overlook: your current employer may already have the infrastructure to keep you on payroll from Spain. As Warner covers in The Costa Property Podcast, the conversation starts with your HR department — specifically asking whether a global mobility program exists and whether the company holds a legal entity in Spain. Without that legal entity, your employer simply cannot employ you compliantly from Spanish soil.

If that door is closed, the second option is lateral: seek employment with an international company that already operates in the region. This is more viable than it sounds. Malaga in particular is rapidly establishing itself as a significant technology and business hub within Europe, which means the pool of internationally minded employers on the Costa del Sol is actively growing.

The third route — self-employment — is the most flexible but requires its own administrative groundwork in Spain. It is a legitimate and commonly used path, and one that Warner and his co-host discuss in detail across episodes of The Costa Property Podcast.

For non-EU applicants, employment status is part of the visa itself — not an afterthought

If you are relocating from outside the EU, the question of how you will work is not something you can figure out after arriving. Spanish visa categories for non-EU nationals include specific employment stipulations, and your application is assessed against them. Arriving without clarity on this point puts the entire visa process at risk.

Warner is direct on this in the episode: employment status must be established — and documented — in advance. Whether you are coming as an employee, a remote worker under a digital nomad visa, or a self-employed individual, the category shapes which visa route you follow and what supporting evidence you need.

It is also worth noting that one partner in a relocating couple should expect to spend approximately three months focused on family integration after the move — settling children into school, navigating local admin — before returning to full employment. Building that buffer into your financial planning from the start is part of a realistic relocation plan.

How is Malaga positioning itself as a technology and business hub in Europe?

According to the hosts, Malaga is becoming one of the biggest tech and AI hubs in Europe, which opens up employment opportunities for relocators beyond traditional industries — making the "find a local international employer" route increasingly realistic.

Which areas of the Costa del Sol are best for families with children relocating from abroad?

Benalmadena is specifically recommended for families with children, particularly if they need to be close to Malaga airport and if teenagers need access to strong schooling options and social infrastructure.

Is it better to rent or buy first when relocating to the Costa del Sol?

The hosts initially rented when they moved 10 years ago, but now advise that if you can afford to buy, buying is often the better option because rental availability and affordability on the Costa del Sol have changed significantly since then.

Key takeaways

  • There are three viable employment routes for Costa del Sol relocators: global mobility through your current employer (requires a Spanish legal entity), joining a local international company, or registering as self-employed in Spain.
  • For non-EU visa applicants, employment status is a formal visa requirement — it must be clarified and evidenced before submitting any application, not resolved after arrival.
  • Malaga's emergence as a European tech and AI hub is expanding the pool of international employers in the region, making local employment a more realistic option than it was even five years ago.
  • Plan for one partner to spend roughly three months on family integration after the move before returning to full employment — factoring this into your financial runway is part of a sound relocation plan.
