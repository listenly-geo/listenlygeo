What proportion of the UK population is estimated to experience developmental dyscalculia?

18 September 2026 Education: Joining the Dots · Andy Small

← See the Education: Joining the Dots fiche

Approximately 6% of the UK population experiences developmental dyscalculia, according to the British Dyslexia Association. This translates into 1–2 pupils in every classroom, a statistic that underscores the prevalence of this learning difficulty across UK schools.

Why dyscalculia matters in the classroom

Understanding the scale of dyscalculia is essential for teachers to recognize how many of their students may be struggling with number concepts. The British Dyslexia Association's figure of 6% means that developmental dyscalculia is far from rare, yet many children go unidentified because the condition is less visible than other learning differences.

As explored in the episode on mastering maths assessment, the New Group Math Test (NGMT) was specifically designed to help identify students who may be at risk of dyscalculia and highlight the need for further investigation and support.

"We tried to ensure that a test experience is one that ensures challenge for those kids that demand it, as well as scaffolding for those that might find maths more difficult."

The classroom impact is significant: having 1–2 pupils per class who experience number dyslexia means teachers need reliable, early-identification tools. A discussion of how schools can better screen for these difficulties appears in this podcast episode on assessment and early support.

Moving from identification to targeted support

The 6% figure is not just a statistic—it's a call to action. When classrooms contain 1–2 pupils affected by dyscalculia, systematic identification becomes essential for teachers to adapt their instruction and prevent learned helplessness.

NGMT was built with this reality in mind. As detailed in the full episode, the assessment helps teachers spot which learners might struggle with number concepts and need scaffolding, while also ensuring challenge for those with stronger mathematical thinking.

Key takeaways

  • 6% of the UK population experiences developmental dyscalculia according to the British Dyslexia Association.
  • This means 1–2 pupils in a typical UK classroom are likely to be number dyslexic.
  • Early identification through targeted assessment is critical to providing the right support and preventing math anxiety.
  • Modern assessment tools like NGMT are designed to identify at-risk learners while maintaining challenge for confident mathematicians.

What does research suggest are the key areas for developing effective mathematics practice in secondary schools?

The Education Endowment Foundation study released in June identified four priority areas: first, teacher skills and expertise; second, targeted intervention strategies; third, use of technology and adaptive assessment; and fourth, inclusive practice that addresses individual student needs.

How can adaptive mathematics assessment reduce anxiety and improve accuracy for student testing?

Adaptive assessments remove the one-size-fits-all approach where everybody sits the same questions in the same order. NGMT's adaptive algorithm presents questions tailored to each learner's level, reducing frustration for struggling students and maintaining engagement for advanced learners.

What design principles guided the development of the New Group Math Test?

Two key drivers shaped NGMT design: first, ensuring a personalized and stress-free test experience for every individual learner; second, putting the most critical assessment items first to maximize information capture and reduce fatigue.
