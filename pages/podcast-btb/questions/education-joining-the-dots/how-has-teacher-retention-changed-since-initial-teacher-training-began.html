How has teacher retention changed since initial teacher training began?

25 September 2026 Education: Joining the Dots

← See the Education: Joining the Dots fiche

Teacher retention has collapsed since initial teacher training expanded. In one cohort that started with 183 trainees, only about 90 completed the course—and staying in teaching beyond a few years has become the exception rather than the rule. What once looked like a lifelong career path now stretches across far shorter tenures.

The shift is striking when measured against living memory. Teachers who entered the profession decades ago often recall their colleagues as fixtures in schools—professionals who taught multiple generations of families and remained until retirement. That continuity has largely vanished. Those who stay past their first few years are now rarities rather than the norm, a seismic change in how the profession sustains itself.

This decline sits within a broader crisis. As discussed in this podcast episode, the TES School Wellbeing Report 2024 found that 74% of school staff have contemplated leaving the profession in the past year—a figure that underscores how unsustainable current conditions have become for teachers across all career stages.

The damage begins early. Training cohorts are halved before graduation, and many who do graduate never make it to their second year. The profession is haemorrhaging talent before teachers have even begun their careers in earnest.

From pipelines to leaks: the exodus at every stage

The problem is not confined to the early years. Workload, systemic pressures, and burnout affect teachers at every point in their journey. As the podcast reveals, 61% of nearly 7,000 respondents to the TES report said their workload is simply not manageable. The stress accelerates once teachers are established: 78% of SENCOs reported unmanageable workloads, as did 71% of middle leaders.

This cascading exit means schools are not just losing new teachers—they are losing experienced staff who might otherwise anchor a school's culture and mentor the next generation. Retention has become a constant uphill battle, with schools unable to build the stable, multi-generational staffrooms that once steadied the profession.

What happens when you lose not just numbers but institutional memory, mentorship capacity, and the lived experience of long-serving educators? The episode digs deeper into what leaders and early career teachers are experiencing as they navigate a profession in flux.
