How has Robert Vincent Taylor restructured his day-to-day work after his cancer diagnosis?

29 August 2026 Embracing Marketing Mistakes · Robert Vincent Taylor

When Robert Vincent Taylor was diagnosed with stage four prostate cancer in May 2024 and told he had six years to live, he completely redirected his career. All his work is now voluntary, and any payment he receives goes directly to charities—he accepts only travel expenses.

A Life Redirected Toward Service

Taylor's diagnosis at age 77 fundamentally changed how he spends his time and uses his decades of expertise. Rather than retreating from professional life, he doubled down on work that matters directly to others facing similar health crises.

He now holds the position of public governor at Airedale NHS Trust, where he participates in oversight of the organization's ambitious £1.5 billion new hospital project—one of seven hospitals being built across the country. This role places him at the intersection of governance and healthcare transformation during a period of significant NHS change.

The Four Pillars of His Current Work

His voluntary commitments span multiple organizations, each addressing a different health crisis. He works with Yorkshire Cancer Research on cancer prevention and treatment awareness, and supports stroke charities through a project called All Things Stroke—an initiative designed to help stroke patients and their relatives understand their condition in plain English, particularly those navigating return to work.

As detailed in the episode, Taylor has also partnered with Hope for Tomorrow, an organization that operates mobile cancer treatment centers. These units are engineered like Formula 1 motorhomes—highly specialized, compact, and mobile—bringing advanced treatment directly to patients who cannot easily travel to fixed hospital centers.

Key takeaways

  • Taylor's stage four prostate cancer diagnosis in May 2024 prompted an immediate career realignment toward full-time voluntary work with no personal financial gain.
  • He now serves across four distinct health and NHS organizations—Airedale NHS Trust, Yorkshire Cancer Research, All Things Stroke, and Hope for Tomorrow—leveraging his PR and communications background for patient advocacy.
  • His work emphasizes accessibility and clarity, particularly in helping patients understand their conditions and pathways back to employment after health crises.
  • The shift reflects a deliberate choice to apply lifetime expertise directly to solving real patient problems rather than pursuing corporate advancement.
