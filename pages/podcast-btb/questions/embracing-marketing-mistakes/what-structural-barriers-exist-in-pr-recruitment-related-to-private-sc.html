Why Is Private School Education a Hidden Barrier to PR Careers?

17 September 2026 Embracing Marketing Mistakes · Chris Norton & Will Ockenden

← See the Embracing Marketing Mistakes fiche

27 percent of PR professionals attended independent schools compared to just 7 percent of the general population—a stark disparity that blocks career entry for people from lower socioeconomic backgrounds. This structural inequality is precisely why Socially Mobile was created: to dismantle the unspoken barrier that makes the PR industry inaccessible to anyone without private school credentials.

The Numbers Behind the Inequality

The gap is not subtle. Among UK PR practitioners, those who attended fee-paying independent schools represent nearly four times the proportion found in the broader population. This isn't coincidence—it reflects how professional networks, family connections, and cultural capital operate as invisible gatekeepers in the communications industry.

What makes this barrier insidious is that it's rarely articulated explicitly. Job postings don't state "independent school graduates only," yet the expectation is embedded in hiring patterns, interview culture, and the informal networks through which most PR roles are filled. As discussed in detail on the episode, talent from disadvantaged backgrounds never gets the chance to prove itself because the entry points themselves are sealed off.

Socially Mobile: Addressing the Structural Problem

Recognizing this disparity, a senior PR professional and former CIPR board member founded Socially Mobile, a community interest company with a single mission: to create pathways for underrepresented people from lower socioeconomic backgrounds into PR and communications. Rather than accept the status quo, the organization tackles the problem head-on through mentoring, training, and direct support.

The initiative addresses what traditional recruitment cannot: the cultural confidence gap that emerges when candidates have been systematically excluded from spaces where PR practitioners typically emerge. Hear more about how this challenge is being tackled in the full episode.
