How did an unethical South Yorkshire Police PR request force Robert to confront his own ethical failings?

2 September 2026 Embracing Marketing Mistakes · Robert Vincent Taylor

← See the Embracing Marketing Mistakes fiche

When one of his students called with concerns about being asked to alter police statements at South Yorkshire Police, Robert Vincent Taylor directed her to the director of communications and moved on. Only later, when she confronted him after her internship, did he realize those altered statements related to the Hillsborough disaster—and that his failure to investigate or truly help had betrayed someone he was supposed to guide as an educator.

The moment a student forced him to see his own complicity

During an internship at South Yorkshire Police, one of Taylor's students reached out to him, worried. She explained she had been asked to alter police statements for media release—a direct ethical violation. Instead of digging deeper or standing with her, Taylor simply directed her to the director of communications and considered the matter closed.

Months passed. After her internship ended, the student returned to confront him directly. She told him he was complicit, that he had sold her down the river by failing to act, and revealed the full context: those altered statements were connected to the Hillsborough disaster, one of Britain's most catastrophic institutional failures.

The contradiction he could no longer ignore

This confrontation shattered something in Taylor's self-perception. He had spent two decades as a PR lecturer, teaching ethics to students and attending ethics lectures himself. He had talked about values, integrity, and doing the right thing. Yet when faced with an actual ethical crisis from someone under his mentorship, he had done nothing—no investigation, no support, no real action.

As discussed in the episode, Taylor recognized that he had operated from a position of institutional comfort rather than genuine conviction. He knew the right words to use about ethics, but had lacked the courage to demand better from himself when it counted.

"Honesty and integrity are on the wall but the industry does not have that. It's not the default position."

He spent six years at Townsend Taurus, a shipping company that operated ferries called Free Enterprise 1 through 5. He learned to physically walk events and understand logistics from the ground up.

Key takeaways

  • Teaching ethics and living ethics are not the same—knowing the right language does not guarantee acting on values when pressure or discomfort is involved.
  • Directing a student away from a problem without investigation is a form of complicity, especially when the adult in the relationship has power and responsibility.
  • Institutional comfort and established position can mask ethical cowardice; real integrity requires action when it matters most.
  • The Hillsborough disaster context transformed a routine internship issue into a symbol of how systems protect themselves through narrative control and suppress those who question it.
