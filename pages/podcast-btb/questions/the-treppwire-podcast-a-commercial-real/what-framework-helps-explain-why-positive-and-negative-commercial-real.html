What framework helps explain why positive and negative commercial real estate signals occur simultaneously?

16 September 2026 The TreppWire Podcast: A Commercial Real Estate Show · Stephen Bushbaum

← See the The TreppWire Podcast: A Commercial Real Estate Show fiche

Commercial real estate markets display contradictory signals—leasing improves while credit deteriorates, capital returns before fundamentals recover—because three independent cycles operate at different speeds. Stephen Bushbaum's three-clock framework isolates each cycle: the property clock (leasing, occupancy, fundamentals), the capital clock (refinancing conditions, market coupons, loan timing), and the credit clock (payment status through delinquency and foreclosure). These clocks synchronize only at maturity, creating the illusion of disconnected market signals until that event forces alignment.

Three cycles, three timelines

The property clock tracks leasing activity and occupancy rates—the tangible asset performance most investors watch daily. Simultaneously, the capital clock operates on market conditions: yield curves shift, refinancing rates change, and the appetite for real estate debt swings independently of whether buildings are actually occupied or generating stronger rent rolls. This is why Bushbaum explains in the episode that capital can return to the market while property fundamentals remain weak.

The third clock—credit—measures the path of a loan from performing status through watch lists, delinquency, and foreclosure. All three clocks tick at their own pace until a maturity date arrives. At that moment, all three must face reality simultaneously: the property's actual condition, the capital market's cost of refinancing, and the borrower's ability to pay or refinance. That convergence is when the framework's power becomes evident.

"Maturity is the great synchronization event that forces all of these clocks to come into sync."

The framework directly explains why headlines can seem to contradict: the property clock may still be in recovery mode, the capital clock may have already reset to higher rates, and the credit clock might still register low delinquency because borrowers haven't yet faced a maturity event. A deeper dive into the episode reveals how this framework applied to September 2026 maturities: 50% of the $2.74 billion in hard maturities with no extension options were already in special servicing, signaling that credit conditions had shifted even as broader property markets remained volatile.

Why maturity matters

Maturity forces all three clocks to align at once. A property whose fundamentals haven't fully recovered must refinance into a higher-rate environment. The borrower cannot postpone the moment when property performance, available capital, and credit capacity converge. This is why understanding where each market stands on its respective clock—not just looking at one headline—reveals the real risk picture.

As discussed in the full episode, the maturity wall arriving in 2026 and beyond creates exactly this synchronization pressure, which is why property type, region, and individual loan structure matter far more than any single data point in isolation.

Key takeaways

  • Three independent clocks operate at different speeds: property fundamentals, capital market conditions, and credit performance.
  • Maturity events force all three clocks to synchronize, creating the conditions for rapid repricing and credit events.
  • Contradictory market signals (improving leasing, deteriorating credit) are normal until maturity aligns all three cycles.
  • Understanding where a market stands on each clock separately reveals risk better than any single headline metric.
