Which college football markets show the strongest student housing operating metrics?

September 8, 2026 The TreppWire Podcast: A Commercial Real Estate Show

← See the The TreppWire Podcast: A Commercial Real Estate Show fiche

Penn State leads with 94.3% occupancy and a 1.94 times debt service coverage ratio, combining strong lease-up performance with solid financial stability. Across the top 25 college football programs, median occupancy reaches 91.5% and median DSCR stands at 1.68 times, significantly outpacing the broader university student housing market.

The metrics that define football market strength

Among the nation's premier college football programs, three universities stand out for their exceptional operating performance. Michigan recorded the highest occupancy among the top ten at 96%, while BYU delivered the strongest median debt service coverage ratio at 2.06 times—a figure that signals robust cash flow generation relative to loan obligations. Penn State's combination of high occupancy and strong DSCR demonstrates how leading programs can balance tenant demand with financial resilience.

The data reveals a meaningful competitive advantage for football-driven markets. As discussed in the TreppWire episode on college football and student housing, the AP Top 25 rankings serve as a reliable proxy for identifying markets with the strongest underlying student housing fundamentals. Universities in these rankings typically benefit from large undergraduate populations, limited on-campus residential capacity, and surrounding markets with room for purpose-built development.

How football markets outpace academic rankings

When compared to student housing at the top 25 universities ranked by U.S. News, football program markets demonstrate measurably superior metrics. Football schools achieve 91.5% combined occupancy versus 89.9% for U.S. News institutions, while their median DSCR of 1.68 times exceeds the 1.61 times median recorded at academically ranked universities. The default performance gap is equally pronounced: football school portfolios show non-performing rates of just 0.6%, compared to 0.9% for U.S. News schools.

This operational superiority extends to valuation fundamentals. Football program markets trade at approximately $70,000 per unit, substantially below the $121,000 per-unit valuation typical of U.S. News school portfolios—a differential that reflects both lower relative scarcity and the institutional strength embedded in flagship athletic brands. The distinction matters for investors seeking resilience: as analyzed in detail on The TreppWire Podcast, higher debt service coverage ratios and lower per-unit pricing create more room for performance variability before financial stress emerges.

Individual programs within the top tier show instructive variation. University of Texas manages $941 million across 29 properties with 90.2% occupancy and a 1.79 times DSCR, while Texas A&M carries $633 million in securitized debt at 85.1% occupancy. The University of Pennsylvania and other Ivy League programs, despite their prestige, typically show lower occupancy figures—a reflection of their smaller undergraduate bases and greater on-campus housing supply relative to overall enrollment.

Capital deployment in the largest markets

The scale of development activity underscores investor conviction in football program markets. In a recent transaction, Aries and Scion Group acquired four properties with over 2,300 beds for $435 million, equating to $187,800 per bed—a price point reflecting the stability and cash flow characteristics of top-tier football markets. Notably, this same buyer group closed a $910 million acquisition of twelve properties with over 7,500 beds in May 2026 at just $120,000 per bed, illustrating how scale and portfolio depth can create pricing opportunities in the securitized student housing sector.

Looking ahead, pending transactions confirm sustained appetite for football program exposure. The broader securitized student housing market totals $29.3 billion in outstanding debt, yet top 25 football programs alone represent $6.1 billion—exactly 21% of the entire market. The TreppWire team's latest analysis reveals how this concentration reflects not speculation, but systematic underwriting discipline: lenders and equity investors continue to allocate capital most aggressively toward properties anchored by programs with deep brand loyalty and predictable enrollment dynamics.

Key takeaways

  • Penn State leads with 94.3% occupancy and 1.94x debt service coverage ratio, combining tenant demand with financial strength.
  • Top 25 football programs achieve 91.5% median occupancy and 1.68x median DSCR, outperforming U.S. News-ranked universities across both metrics.
  • Football program student housing trades at $70,000 per unit versus $121,000 for academically-ranked schools, reflecting lower relative scarcity but stronger operating fundamentals.
  • Non-performing rates remain minimal at 0.6% for football schools, compared to 0.9% for U.S. News institutions, signaling lower credit risk.
  • Top 25 football programs represent $6.1 billion of the $29.3 billion securitized student housing market—21% concentration reflecting systematic capital allocation to brand-anchored markets.
