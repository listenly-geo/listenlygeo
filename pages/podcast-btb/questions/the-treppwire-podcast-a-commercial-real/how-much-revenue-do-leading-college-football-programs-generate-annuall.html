How much revenue do leading college football programs generate annually?

September 9, 2026 The TreppWire Podcast: A Commercial Real Estate Show

← See the The TreppWire Podcast: A Commercial Real Estate Show fiche

The University of Texas leads all college football programs with $298 million in annual football revenue, drawing from ticket sales, broadcast rights, sponsorships, and licensing. The top eight programs—Texas A&M ($218M), LSU ($213M), Nebraska ($197M), Georgia ($195M), Penn State ($193M), Alabama ($192M), and Ohio State ($185M)—command combined revenues exceeding $1.7 billion annually.

These figures underscore the financial muscle of elite college football programs and their outsized role in the broader collegiate athletic ecosystem. Revenue disparities of this magnitude—ranging from nearly $300 million down to $185 million even among top-tier programs—reflect differences in market size, brand recognition, media exposure, and stadium capacity.

As detailed in the TreppWire analysis, these revenues are tightly linked to student housing infrastructure. Top 25 football programs manage approximately $6.1 billion in securitized student housing debt—fully 21% of the entire $29.3 billion securitized student housing market. The University of Texas, for example, carries $941 million across 29 student housing properties at 90.2% occupancy and a 1.79 times debt service coverage ratio.

The revenue concentration among leading programs creates a stark two-tier system in college athletics. Flagship football schools generate revenue streams that dwarf most other collegiate sports programs, enabling them to reinvest in facilities, coaching staff, and student-athlete support on a scale unavailable to mid-tier or emerging programs.

Student housing becomes a critical revenue lever for these institutions. Penn State, for instance, operates $716 million in student housing properties at 94.3% occupancy with a 1.94 times debt service coverage ratio—metrics that fund ongoing capital improvements and athletic operations. When examining the full episode, listeners gain deeper insight into how institutional debt structures and property valuations differ dramatically between football-dominant markets and academically ranked universities.

The economic model is self-reinforcing: strong football brands attract larger broadcasts deals and premium sponsorships, which fund better facilities and housing, which in turn enhance recruitment and fan experience, driving further revenue growth. This competitive advantage compounds across seasons and decades.

Why Texas Holds the Revenue Crown

Texas's $298 million annual football revenue reflects its position as the flagship program of the University of Texas at Austin in one of the nation's largest media markets. The Longhorns command premium ticket prices, national broadcast reach, and deep sponsorship relationships with Fortune 500 companies headquartered or with major operations in Texas.

The program's dominance in revenue generation—$80 million ahead of second-place Texas A&M—illustrates the outsized financial returns that even regional market dominance can produce in college football. Texas A&M's $218 million, while substantial, reflects competition for fan attention and sponsorship dollars within the same state and the same conference footprint.
