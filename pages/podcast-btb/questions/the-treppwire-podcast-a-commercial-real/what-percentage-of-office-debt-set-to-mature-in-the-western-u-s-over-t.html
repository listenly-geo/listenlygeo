How much of the Western U.S. office debt maturing in the next two years has extension options remaining?

September 25, 2026 The TreppWire Podcast: A Commercial Real Estate Show · Stephen Bushbaum

← See The TreppWire Podcast: A Commercial Real Estate Show fiche

Slightly over half of the total outstanding office debt in the Western U.S. set to mature over the next two years consists of soft maturities with remaining extension options. However, most of these loans are far into their term, typically with only one or two one-year extension options left before requiring a full refinancing cycle.

The depth of the maturity ladder

The distinction between soft and hard maturities matters enormously in the current lending environment. In this live conference discussion, Bushbaum explains that while extension options provide temporary breathing room, they don't solve underlying property or market challenges—they simply delay the reckoning.

Most loans with remaining extensions are in their seventh, eighth, or ninth year of original terms, meaning the original loan structure anticipated only two or three one-year extension windows from the outset. When borrowers use these extensions, they're not buying time for fundamentals to recover; they're managing a compressed runway before hard maturity forces a refinance into a much less favorable capital environment.

What extension actually means in practice

The critical insight is that soft maturity doesn't mean the debt problem disappears—it means the timeline for resolution shifts, often to an even tighter refinance window. Many of these Western office loans are maturing into an environment where 10-year Treasuries have risen significantly, making the 6.44% median coupon rates achievable in 2026 refinancings appear increasingly tight compared to the sub-3% fixed rates locked in a decade ago.

For borrowers with only one or two extension options left, the decision tree becomes binary: extend and push the maturity wall forward by one year, or refinance now into current market rates. The full episode explores how this calculus varies across San Francisco, Seattle, and Denver office markets, each facing distinct leasing and occupancy headwinds.

"Maturity is the great synchronization event that forces all of these clocks to come into sync."

The Western office market faces a maturity wave concentrated across the next 24 months, and while extension options provide tactical flexibility, they're increasingly exhausted. Borrowers and lenders must now confront whether properties can support refinancing into 2026–2027 rate environments, or whether the maturity event will force a restructuring, sale, or distressed outcome.
