What drew Chris Charles to rugby league during Hull KR's golden era in the 1980s?

24 April 2026 Hull Trains – Moving Minds · Chris Charles

← See the Hull Trains – Moving Minds fiche

Chris Charles grew up in the shadow of Hull Kingston Rovers during the 1980s, when the club was experiencing a golden era with legendary players like Mike Smith, Gavin Miller, and Mark Broadhurst. Living near the ground, he attended matches regularly as a child, developing a passion for rugby league that would shape his entire sporting career.

Beyond watching Rovers, Charles also played amateur rugby with his stepfather, which deepened his connection to the sport. This foundation eventually led him to play for Hull Boys Club, where his talent caught the attention of scouts and resulted in an invitation to join the Rovers Academy—the pathway to professional rugby league.

As Charles reflects in the episode, his decision to pursue rugby league full-time was far from being made in isolation. It emerged from a combination of proximity to the club, exposure to its greatest players, and early involvement in competitive amateur rugby—a convergence of influences that made the choice feel almost inevitable rather than arbitrary.

What makes Charles's story particularly notable is that this immersion in rugby league occurred during a specific historical moment: the 1980s represented a peak era of excellence for Hull Kingston Rovers, featuring players whose performances made the sport tangible and inspiring to a young person living nearby. The opportunity to watch these athletes regularly transformed what could have remained a casual interest into a calling.

"I don't really see it as a pressure right like this is what I've always wanted to do I love it to bits and I'd die doing it but I've never thought of it all as a burden."

Charles's progression from young spectator to professional athlete to team captain demonstrates how early exposure and proximity to excellence can catalyze a lifetime commitment. The fact that he later brought his own son, Jack Charles, into the same organization underscores how the cycle of passion and mentorship within a sporting community can span generations. The full episode explores this family legacy in detail, touching on how both father and son have navigated the sport and the broader challenges of men's mental health within professional rugby league.

How has P.A.U.L For Brain Recovery evolved from a personal initiative into a structured organization with partnerships and staff?

The charity was commissioned by the NHS in 2017, which enabled Paul to develop a team. He brought on Lee North as operations manager to handle the growing organizational needs.

What role did mentorship and networking play in transforming a personal recovery story into an established NHS-commissioned charity?

Paul met local businessman Neil Hudgel at a networking event and was invited to be an ambassador for Hudgel Solicitors. Neil was instrumental in developing the charity's vision and partnerships.

What inspired the founding of a brain injury recovery charity, and how did community relationships drive its creation?

After running marathons and organizing fundraising events, Paul launched a Facebook page to give insight into brain injury and serve as a voice for others, which became the foundation for the charity.
