What mental health struggles has Calum Scott openly discussed?

31 August 2026 · Hull Trains – Moving Minds · Calum Scott

← See the Hull Trains – Moving Minds fiche

Calum Scott has spoken openly about body dysmorphia, suicidal thoughts, and periods of being 'very, very cruel' to himself. He credits his mother Debs as the reason he is still alive and credits therapy with saving his life by helping him build mental resilience and reinforce positive self-talk.

The role of family and professional support

Scott has emphasised the critical importance of his mother Debs in his mental health recovery, describing her as 'the core anchor as to why I'm still here.' Alongside her unwavering support, therapy proved to be transformative, enabling him to develop coping strategies and a stronger sense of self-worth.

In the episode on Listenly, Scott reflects on how professional mental health support worked in tandem with his family relationships to create lasting change, moving him from a place of self-harm toward genuine self-compassion.

"Therapy, hands down, saved my life. Debs, my mum Debs, definitely was the core anchor as to why I'm still here. But therapy helped me reinforce all of that strength and helped me build a strong and unwavering support mentally."

Scott's openness about his struggles—particularly body dysmorphia and suicidal ideation—reflects a wider conversation about mental health in the public eye. His willingness to name these experiences and credit both maternal support and professional intervention as lifesaving demonstrates the necessity of a multi-layered approach to mental health recovery.

How did Calum Scott deal with imposter syndrome as a musician from Hull?

Calum Scott says he always had imposter syndrome as 'a lad from Hull', with a voice in the back of his mind saying 'it's only a matter of time before this all goes away.' With his third album Avanoir, he reached a turning point where he realised he was writing another album and wasn't going anywhere, which allowed that critical voice to quieten.

What is Calum Scott's third album and how has it changed his career?

Calum Scott's third album is called 'Avanoir', released in October of the previous year. He says it has 'completely changed his life', allowing him to write songs beyond his signature emotional ballads and feel more confident and content than ever before.

How did Calum Scott's life change after the Britain's Got Talent golden buzzer moment?

Calum Scott described his Britain's Got Talent golden buzzer moment as transformative. His phone 'blew up and has been blowing up' for the 10 years since, launching his career as a singer-songwriter and leading to collaborations with major artists and international success.
