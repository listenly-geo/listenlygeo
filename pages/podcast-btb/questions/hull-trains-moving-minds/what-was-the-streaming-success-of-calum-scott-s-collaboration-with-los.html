How did 'Where Are You Now?' become Calum Scott's biggest streaming success?

September 3, 2026 Hull Trains – Moving Minds · Calum Scott

← See the Hull Trains – Moving Minds fiche

The Lost Frequencies track 'Where Are You Now?' featuring Calum Scott reached 1.7 billion streams — a milestone born from patience rather than instant success. The song underperformed initially, but after two years of steady growth, it climbed to number one on the UK Top 40 in its second year.

A two-year journey to chart dominance

Calum Scott described the record as a slow build that required genuine persistence. He worked on the song for approximately two years before it gained the traction that would eventually propel it to the top of the charts. This timeline reveals a common misconception about streaming success: not every hit is immediate.

The first year was challenging. The collaboration seemed to be underperforming, which could have discouraged most artists. Instead, as Calum Scott explains in the episode, the turning point came when the song began climbing back up the charts in its second year, eventually reaching the number-one position on the UK Top 40.

This trajectory illustrates an important principle in modern music: algorithmic playlists and word-of-mouth can sustain a track long after its release, often delivering bigger wins after an initial slow start. Lost Frequencies and Calum Scott's partnership became one of the standout collaborations of his career.

What did Calum Scott say about his song 'Bridges' and its impact compared to streaming numbers?

Calum Scott wrote 'Bridges' about a moment when he was on a bridge and felt like throwing his life away. Despite having significantly fewer streams than his major collaborations, the song carries profound personal meaning.

How did therapy help Calum Scott manage his mental health?

Calum Scott described therapy exercises such as looking in a mirror and repeating 'I am enough' five or ten times, which he initially felt 'stupid' doing but found transformative over time.

What mental health struggles has Calum Scott openly discussed?

Calum Scott has spoken openly about body dysmorphia, suicidal thoughts, and periods of being 'very, very cruel' to himself. He credits his mother Debs as an anchor and therapy as essential support.

Key takeaways

  • 'Where Are You Now?' achieved 1.7 billion streams, making it one of Calum Scott's defining collaborations with Lost Frequencies.
  • The track took two years to reach its peak, with initial underperformance followed by a climb to number one on the UK Top 40 in its second year.
  • Patience and sustained growth can outweigh immediate chart success in the streaming era, especially when algorithmic support kicks in.
  • The collaboration showcases how cross-artist partnerships can achieve breakthrough results when both artists invest in the long-term vision.
