What is Calum Scott's third album and how has it changed his career?

29 August 2026 Hull Trains – Moving Minds · Calum Scott

← See the Hull Trains – Moving Minds fiche

Calum Scott's third album is called Avanoir, released in October, and it has completely changed his life. The album gave him the creative confidence to write more diverse and upbeat songs beyond emotional ballads, while opening doors to bigger shows, festival headline bookings, and a newfound sense of artistic freedom.

For years, Scott carried a deep imposter syndrome rooted in his identity as a lad from Hull. Despite his rapid rise after the golden buzzer moment on Britain's Got Talent, he harboured a nagging voice telling him it was all temporary—that any moment it could vanish. Avanoir became the turning point where he finally silenced that doubt.

With this third album, Scott reached a psychological milestone: the realisation that he wasn't going anywhere. As discussed at length in this episode of Moving Minds, he could now write without the weight of constant self-doubt. This mental shift unleashed a broader range of songwriting—tracks like "Roots," "At Your Worst," and "Lighthouse" showcase a Scott who writes songs that aren't solely designed to make audiences cry.

Creative liberation and career momentum

The album's release sparked immediate professional growth. Scott's touring schedule exploded into a worldwide tour, festival headline slots materialised, and collaborations multiplied dramatically. As Scott shared with Gemma Oaten on the podcast, working with artists like Lost Frequencies (whose collaboration "Where Are You Now?" amassed 1.7 billion streams), Ronan Keating, and the Whitney Houston estate became possible in a way that reflected his new confidence.

What makes Avanoir transformative isn't just the commercial success—it's the emotional permission Scott gave himself to evolve. He moved beyond the narrow lane of "the emotional ballad guy" into a fuller artist identity, one that his audience had been waiting to hear.

"This album in particular has completely changed my life. I think with this album I just feel more confident and more content than I ever have."
