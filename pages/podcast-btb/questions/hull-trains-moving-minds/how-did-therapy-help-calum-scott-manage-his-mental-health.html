How did therapy help Calum Scott manage his mental health?

1 September 2026 Hull Trains – Moving Minds · Calum Scott

← See the Hull Trains – Moving Minds fiche

Therapy gave Scott concrete tools to rewire his self-perception through daily practice—simple exercises like looking in a mirror and repeating "I am enough" multiple times until the affirmation became real. He came to understand that negative self-talk repeated enough becomes belief, and the same principle applies to positive reinforcement, building what he calls "a strong and unwavering support" mentally.

From resistance to mental resilience

When Scott first encountered these mirror exercises in therapy, he felt initially uncomfortable with the practice, describing it as feeling "stupid." But he committed to the process anyway, recognizing a fundamental truth about how the mind works through repetition.

The turning point came when he realized that positive affirmations operate on the same neural principle as negative self-talk—when you hear something enough times, whether it's harmful or healing, your brain internalizes it as truth. For Scott, this wasn't abstract psychology; it was a practical path out of the self-criticism and imposter syndrome that had shadowed his career for years.

As Scott explains in the episode, therapy wasn't just about talking through problems—it was about installing new mental habits through consistent, deliberate practice.

"Therapy, hands down, saved my life. Debs, my mum Debs, definitely was the core anchor as to why I'm still here. But therapy helped me reinforce all of that strength and helped me build a strong and unwavering support mentally."

Scott's willingness to share the specific mechanics of his therapeutic breakthrough—the mirror work, the repeated affirmations, the gradual rewiring of belief—offers a concrete model for anyone struggling with similar mental health challenges. He also discusses how professional mental health resources like the NHS and Samaritans offer accessible support for those facing their own mental health battles.

What mental health struggles has Calum Scott openly discussed?

Calum Scott has spoken openly about body dysmorphia, suicidal thoughts, and periods of being 'very, very cruel' to himself. He credits his mother Debs as a core anchor in his recovery journey.

How did Calum Scott deal with imposter syndrome as a musician from Hull?

Calum Scott says he always had imposter syndrome as 'a lad from Hull', with a voice in the back of his mind saying 'it's only a matter of time before this all goes away'—until his third album gave him the confidence to silence that doubt.

What is Calum Scott's third album and how has it changed his career?

Calum Scott's third album is called 'Avanoir', released in October of the previous year. He says it has 'completely changed his life', allowing him to write songs beyond just making people cry and to feel more confident than ever.

Key takeaways

  • Therapy exercises like mirror work and daily affirmations rewire negative self-talk through consistent repetition—the same principle that makes harmful thoughts stick also applies to healing ones.
  • Scott's mother Debs served as a foundational emotional anchor, while professional therapy provided the practical tools to reinforce and build upon that support.
  • Mental health resources like the NHS and Samaritans offer accessible 24/7 support for anyone facing similar struggles.
  • The shift from dismissing positive affirmations as "stupid" to embracing them as a core part of mental maintenance required both vulnerability and commitment to the process.
