Why does Calum Scott stay rooted in Hull despite global success?

4 September 2026 Hull Trains – Moving Minds · Calum Scott

← See the Hull Trains – Moving Minds fiche

Hull is where Calum Scott was born, raised, and where his entire family still lives—and staying grounded there is essential to remaining 'just Calum' between tours. Beyond personal roots, he actively supports local charities and businesses in his home city, framing his connection as both a personal anchor and a civic responsibility.

For Calum Scott, the distinction between his global stage identity and his private self is rooted in geography. As he explains in the episode, 'Calum Scott goes all around the world, but Calum's heart belongs in Hull.' This isn't merely sentiment—it's a deliberate choice about who he is when the spotlight fades.

The permanence of family in Hull provides what he describes as essential grounding. With everyone he loves still living in his hometown, returning there between tour commitments keeps him connected to a life beyond the music industry. This stability serves as a counterweight to the pressures of international touring and the imposter syndrome Calum has openly discussed experiencing despite his significant achievements.

Beyond family ties, Calum Scott has made supporting his community a visible part of his identity, backing local charities and businesses. This civic engagement isn't separate from his music career—it's integral to how he understands his responsibility as someone with a platform. A point detailed in this podcast episode is how his own journey through mental health challenges has deepened his commitment to giving back locally.

"Therapy, hands down, saved my life. Debs, my mum Debs, definitely was the core anchor as to why I'm still here. But therapy helped me reinforce all of that strength and helped me build a strong and unwavering support mentally."

There's a deeper layer to his commitment to Hull. The full conversation reveals how Calum has drawn on his own experiences working for Hull City Council before his music career took off—a grounding experience that keeps him mindful of his home city's real needs and opportunities. His third album, 'Avanoir', marked a shift from writing primarily to move audiences emotionally to creating work that reflects his newfound confidence and contentment, partly because he no longer fears success will vanish. That stability radiates back to Hull, where he maintains roots that feel as real and necessary as any professional milestone.

What was the streaming success of Calum Scott's collaboration with Lost Frequencies on 'Where Are You Now?'

The Lost Frequencies track 'Where Are You Now?' featuring Calum Scott reached 1.7 billion streams. Calum described it as a slow build, noting he worked the record for two years before it reached number one on the UK Top 40.

What did Calum Scott say about his song 'Bridges' and its impact compared to streaming numbers?

Calum Scott wrote 'Bridges' about a moment when he was on a bridge and felt like throwing his life away. Despite having significantly fewer streams than his major collaborations, the song carries profound personal meaning related to his mental health journey.

How did therapy help Calum Scott manage his mental health?

Calum Scott described therapy exercises such as looking in a mirror and repeating 'I am enough' five or ten times, which he initially felt 'stupid' doing. Therapy, combined with strong family support, became instrumental in his mental health recovery.

Key takeaways

  • Hull remains Calum Scott's emotional and practical anchor, a place where his family lives and where he grounds himself away from the touring circuit.
  • He actively supports local charities and businesses in Hull, seeing civic responsibility as inseparable from his global success.
  • His past work for Hull City Council before his music career took off shaped his understanding of his hometown's real needs.
  • Staying rooted in Hull has contributed to the greater confidence and contentment reflected in his third album, 'Avanoir'.
