What Pave It Black covers

Pave It Black is the official conversation space of the National Asphalt Pavement Association, examining the people, technologies, and strategic shifts reshaping American infrastructure. Since 2020, hosts Richard Willis and Brett Williams have conducted deep interviews spanning decades of pavement research, historical road construction narratives, and contemporary industry innovation. The show connects foundational infrastructure knowledge—from the AASHO Road Test to the Smithsonian recognition of asphalt's cultural role—with forward-looking leadership and emerging materials science.

Key facts
  • Launched in 2020 and hosted by Richard Willis and Brett Williams from NAPA, the organization representing the asphalt pavement industry in North America.
  • Season 9, "Beyond the Pavement: Lessons in Leadership," pivots explicitly toward organizational and personal development narratives within the construction sector.
  • Recent episodes cover landmark historical infrastructure (Route 66, the National Road, NCAT research facility) alongside cutting-edge technologies featured by Forbes 30 Under 30 innovators.
  • The show bridges academic research methodologies, regulatory history, industry leadership profiles, and practical implementation challenges in modern pavement engineering.

What this podcast really covers

Pave It Black operates at the intersection of industry history, technical innovation, and leadership narrative. The show's trajectory reveals a progression from foundational knowledge—the 1950s AASHO Road Test that revolutionized pavement design, the Smithsonian's recognition of asphalt's cultural significance, historical road projects like Route 66 and the National Road—to present-day infrastructure challenges and emerging material technologies.

The podcast examines how American mobility and infrastructure philosophy have evolved. Early episodes establish historical context: how transportation worked before formalized road systems, how federal funding shaped national road infrastructure, and how pavement science developed as a discipline. Recent seasons accelerate into contemporary concerns: travel behavior shifts reshaping infrastructure priorities, multi-generational family businesses in construction, and advanced asphalt innovations from young technologists.

This structure creates a throughline from "how we got here" to "where we're heading." The show treats asphalt not as commodity material but as a technical field worthy of deep expertise, research investment, and strategic industry dialogue.

Who this podcast is essential for

Infrastructure and transportation professionals constitute the core audience. Civil engineers, highway planners, state DOT officials, and consulting firms focused on road construction rely on Pave It Black for research context and technical credibility. The show's emphasis on NCAT research, AASHO standards, and peer-reviewed pavement science makes it essential reference material for anyone designing or managing road infrastructure projects.

Construction industry leaders and business owners benefit from the podcast's focus on generational leadership and organizational strategy. Season 9's emphasis on leadership lessons positions the show as a management resource within the construction sector, offering insights into how family businesses, innovation practices, and industry challenges reshape organizational culture.

Policy and regulatory professionals working on infrastructure investment, sustainability reporting, or transportation planning use the show to understand industry perspectives on federal funding, research priorities, and technological readiness. The podcast translates between technical expertise and policy-level conversation, making it valuable for government and nonprofit infrastructure advocates.

What the episodes really reveal

The episode archive demonstrates NAPA's commitment to positioning asphalt as an innovation-driven, strategically important field rather than a commodity supply chain. The show consistently elevates pavement engineering to the status of research discipline: historical episodes document how scientific methodology shaped modern road design; recent episodes feature young innovators and advanced material research.

A second pattern emphasizes historical context and cultural legitimacy. Episodes on Route 66, the National Road, and the Smithsonian exhibition situate asphalt within American infrastructure narrative, positioning the material not as utilitarian background but as a deliberate historical achievement worthy of scholarly attention.

Leadership and succession emerge as recurring themes, particularly pronounced in Season 9. Multi-generational family business conversations, interviews with Forbes-recognized innovators, and explicit focus on leadership lessons suggest the industry is grappling with succession planning, generational knowledge transfer, and cultural adaptation. This reflects broader construction sector challenges around attracting talent and modernizing organizational practices.

What this changes in practice

For infrastructure professionals, Pave It Black establishes a credible, accessible source for understanding pavement science beyond proprietary research or technical specifications. The show's research focus legitimizes investment in asphalt innovation and creates industry-wide conversation around emerging technologies—positioning pavement as deserving of the same innovation attention given to other infrastructure materials.

For industry leaders, the podcast models a strategy of industry narrative-building: using sustained, high-quality conversation to reposition a traditional sector as innovation-driven and strategically significant. The show demonstrates that credibility comes from historical grounding, research rigor, and thought leadership interviews—not from marketing messaging or trend-chasing.

For policymakers and infrastructure advocates, the show provides foundational context for infrastructure investment decisions. Understanding how pavement science developed, how research standards emerged, and how the industry is evolving toward new material and sustainability challenges makes evidence-based funding decisions more informed.

Infrastructure narrative shapes policy investment and industry innovation equally. Pave It Black establishes asphalt as a field where research, history, and leadership matter—shifting the sector from commodity perception to strategic capability.
