What GTM Live covers

GTM Live dismantles the broken metrics that dominate B2B marketing and revenue discussions, replacing vanity indicators with unit economics and efficiency measures that actually predict growth. What this podcast really covers

GTM Live focuses on the operational and strategic failures that prevent B2B marketing and revenue teams from achieving aligned, measurable impact. Rather than debating best practices in content or channel selection, the show diagnoses why so many organizations struggle to connect marketing effort to revenue outcomes.

The central premise is clear: the way most B2B companies execute and measure go-to-market is fundamentally broken. Metrics like Marketing Qualified Leads (MQLs), impression share, and vanity engagement rates mask the reality of deal velocity, customer acquisition cost, and lifetime value. Episodes such as "The Rise and Fall of the MQL" and "3 Major GTM Blind Spots Hurting Marketing's Ability to Measure Revenue Impact" directly challenge these frameworks, offering alternative measurement models tied to business outcomes.

The podcast also examines how emerging technologies—especially artificial intelligence—are reshaping the landscape of demand creation, SEO, and brand positioning. "AI Is Exposing a Massive Problem With B2B Marketing Data" and "Brand Is Trust: Gong's Udi Ledergor on Marketing in the AI Era" explore how AI search, recommendation algorithms, and data quality directly influence GTM strategy.

A recurring theme is the gap between marketing capability and revenue operations reality. Episodes like "What to Do When Your RevOps Team Is the Blocker to Your Marketing KPIs" and "Marketing Creates the Demand. But What Actually Builds the Revenue Engine?" expose the structural misalignments that prevent marketing investment from translating into sustainable growth.

This reframes how CEOs and CFOs should evaluate brand investment.

The podcast also exposes organizational friction points: RevOps teams blocking marketing initiatives due to misaligned KPIs, CMOs measured on activities while CFOs demand revenue accountability, and sales teams gaming metrics rather than building sustainable pipeline. Understanding these dynamics is essential for leaders attempting cross-functional transformation.

Guest contributions bring specialized depth: Gaetano DiNardi on B2B SEO and AI search visibility, Aviv Canaani on building revenue engines beyond demand capture, Jon Miller on the MQL lifecycle, and Udi Ledergor on trust and brand in the AI era. What this changes in practice

Listening to GTM Live shifts how revenue leaders approach three critical decisions: measurement frameworks, channel allocation, and organizational alignment.

On measurement, the podcast argues for replacing activity-based KPIs with unit economics: CAC (customer acquisition cost), payback period, net revenue retention, and cohort lifetime value. This means reframing marketing's role not as lead generation but as a contributor to predictable, efficient revenue scaling. CMOs and marketing teams that adopt this language gain credibility with finance and executive leadership.

On channels and tactics, GTM Live provides permission to abandon underperforming playbooks. If your paid search is capturing demand rather than creating it, reduce spend or restructure. If your ABM campaigns lack true intent signals, they will waste resources. The show equips leaders to make these defensible decisions based on business logic, not peer pressure or legacy commitments.

On organizational structure, the podcast makes clear that marketing effectiveness depends on RevOps capability, sales execution, and CFO alignment. A CMO cannot solve a revenue problem alone if RevOps cannot track hand-offs, if sales cannot close, or if finance measures success by marketing costs rather than revenue impact. This insight redirects leaders toward cross-functional transformation rather than isolated marketing improvements.

Modern go-to-market success requires dismantling inherited measurement frameworks and realigning organizational incentives around unit economics and sustainable growth rather than activity metrics and vanity indicators.

The podcast answers these questions

What is the relationship between marketing and revenue engine building in B2B SaaS?

Marketing creates initial demand and awareness, but building a true revenue engine requires alignment between demand creation, sales execution, and revenue operations. The distinction matters because many B2B companies optimize for marketing metrics (leads, MQLs) without connecting them to actual pipeline and closed revenue, creating an illusion of progress while revenue stalls.

Why do account-based marketing campaigns often fail to create pipeline?

ABM failures typically stem from poor targeting fundamentals, lack of true demand (targeting without proof of buyer intent), or misalignment between what marketing considers an 'account' and what sales can actually close. The biggest lie is treating ABM as a tactic rather than a strategy that requires deeper go-to-market changes.

How should B2B companies evaluate paid search performance?

Most B2B companies confuse impression share (competitor visibility metrics) with actual demand creation. Paid search often performs better for demand capture (existing buyer intent) than demand creation (building new awareness). Knowing when to pull the plug requires distinguishing between these two models and measuring against unit economics, not vanity metrics.

What makes a CMO truly elite in modern B2B?

The seven traits of elite CMOs have nothing to do with specific tactics like content marketing or automation. Instead, they focus on business acumen, revenue accountability, team building, and the ability to measure and optimize for what actually matters: customer acquisition cost, lifetime value, and long-term growth sustainability.

How should marketing leaders test whether their contact records are properly connected between marketing automation and CRM?

Pick 10 of last quarter's closed deals and check if you can consistently find those people connected to the opportunity with corresponding records wherever you market. Check if leads are converted to contacts on the opportunity, if there are people missing, or if people have multiple different records that are not merged.
