In this episode

Three practitioners from Euroclear, Société Générale CIB and Borderless.xyz deliver a ground-level assessment of the digital asset market's 2025 inflection point — where tokenized assets have hit $21 billion in market cap, stablecoin volumes have doubled to up to $46 trillion annually, and the shift from proof-of-concept to live institutional deployment is no longer a prediction but a measured reality.

3 key takeaways from this episode

  • The tokenized asset market tripled in two years: from $2 billion in 2023 to $6 billion in 2024 to $21 billion in 2025, driven by the convergence of MiCA regulation, DLT infrastructure maturity and institutional commitment — evidenced by projects such as BlackRock BUIDL and Euroclear's Pythagore initiative with the Banque de France.
  • Payments 3.0 is not an incremental upgrade: it eliminates the multi-hop correspondent banking chain (less than 10% of the world's banks belong to SWIFT, leaving 90% outside the network) by replacing it with a direct two-counterparty settlement layer built on stablecoins and blockchain rails.
  • Liquidity remains the structural bottleneck: despite the technology being production-ready, tokenized asset liquidity is constrained by KYC onboarding, paying agent requirements and withholding tax processes that still demand bilateral connections — a problem Euroclear, DTCC and Canton Network are actively working to solve at the infrastructure level.

Why 2025 marks a genuine inflection point for tokenized assets — and not just another false dawn

The tokenized asset market has been declared "about to take off" for several consecutive years. What makes 2025 structurally different is the simultaneous arrival of three converging forces: a mature regulatory framework in the form of MiCA and the EU's DLT Pilot Regime, institutional-grade infrastructure from established post-trade players, and visible proof of demand from asset managers and corporate treasurers looking for programmable, 24/7 settlement.

The numbers reflect this convergence with unusual clarity. A capitalization of $2 billion in 2023 became $6 billion in 2024 and $21 billion in 2025. Euroclear's Project Pythagore — built in partnership with the Banque de France — is targeting 100% tokenization of the NEU CP market, a short-term debt market exceeding $300 billion in outstanding volume. More than 50% of that market's participants are already actively engaged. Settlement will be executed in CBDC via the European Central Bank's Pontes system, removing the last remaining friction between tokenized securities and central bank money.

Jorgen Ouaknine, Global Head of Innovation and Digital Assets at Euroclear — an infrastructure managing over $40 trillion in assets under custody and processing the equivalent of world GDP every single month — frames the institution's role precisely: not to disrupt, but to translate innovation into something safe, scalable and usable by the largest financial institutions globally. Euroclear's approach is to partner with fintechs rather than compete with them, combining the agility of innovators with the regulatory and compliance robustness that systemic market infrastructure must provide.

What Payments 3.0 actually means: the fundamental rewiring of cross-border settlement infrastructure

Kevin Lehtiniitty, Founder and CEO of Borderless.xyz, draws a precise architectural distinction between the three generations of payment infrastructure. Payments 1.0 is correspondent banking via SWIFT: functional but slow, costly, and structurally limited to the less than 10% of the world's banks that are members of the network. Payments 2.0 — the Airwallex, Nium and similar fintech generation — creates the experience of speed by pre-funding accounts in destination markets. This is not real-time settlement; it is an illusion of settlement built on top of the same correspondent banking rails.

Payments 3.0 eliminates the architecture entirely. By agreeing on a common standard — USDC on a given chain, for example — two counterparties connect directly, with no intermediary hops. The 95-country, 63-currency network that Borderless.xyz has built operationalizes this model for financial institutions, PSPs and fintechs that previously required a separate bilateral integration for every geography in which they operated.

The practical consequences are most visible in two use cases. First, treasury management for multinationals in emerging markets: capital can be deployed and repatriated in near real time, eliminating multi-day FX exposure windows. Second, B2B trade finance: a cargo settlement between Africa and China that takes T+4 via correspondent bank generates several days of port fees. A T+0 stablecoin settlement that costs an additional 10 basis points in FX fees is categorically cheaper. The stablecoin transaction volume data confirms adoption is accelerating: between $33 and $46 trillion in annual transaction volume, double the prior year's figure.

"Payments 3.0 is the era that we're now entering that's being enabled by blockchain and by stablecoins. This is not a new user interface on top of correspondent banking. This is now the fundamental rewiring of the underlying infrastructure."
— Kevin Lehtiniitty, Founder and CEO, Borderless.xyz

Why liquidity in tokenized assets is structurally constrained — and what institutional infrastructure is doing about it

The technology for tokenizing financial assets is production-ready. The liquidity is not, and the reasons are administrative rather than technical. Charles-Antoine Michallet, Director of Digital Assets at Société Générale CIB, identifies the core constraint precisely: all participants in a tokenized asset must be individually onboarded onto the same platform before any transfer can occur. The fact that an asset sits on-chain does not make it automatically transferable. KYC processes, paying agent configurations, withholding tax management and corporate action handling all still require bilateral connections between parties — the same bilateral connections that blockchain was meant to eliminate.

Société Générale has moved faster than most traditional banks in this space. SGForge — SG's dedicated digital assets infrastructure — has issued tokenized bonds on public blockchain and launched euro and USD stablecoins, differentiating the bank from institutions that remain in private-chain or permissioned-only environments. The stablecoin issuance is regulated under MiCA, providing legal certainty that earlier generations of tokenized instruments lacked.

At the network infrastructure level, Euroclear and Jorgen Ouaknine identify privacy as the non-negotiable prerequisite for institutional adoption. Capital markets participants do not disclose their positions to competitors. Any network seeking to host institutional bond or equity transactions must deliver privacy by design — not as a feature added after launch. The Canton Network is cited as one of the rare blockchain networks that has built confidentiality and interoperability natively from its inception. The broader conclusion is structural: there will be no single dominant network. Global interoperability between Canton, DTCC's Kinexis (built with JP Morgan), Fireblocks, and other institutional-grade networks is the actual objective — and the condition under which a genuine secondary market for tokenized assets can emerge.

How digital currencies are classified — and why the distinction between stablecoins, CBDCs and tokenized deposits matters operationally

One of the clearest contributions of this episode is a precise taxonomy of digital currencies, which the market frequently conflates to its operational detriment. Three forms exist, and they are not interchangeable.

Stablecoins — issued by Circle (USDC), SGForge and others — are liabilities of a private issuer, backed by fiat currency or highly liquid short-term assets. They sit off bank balance sheets. CBDCs are a native representation of central bank money on-chain: they carry the same credit quality as physical currency and are what Pontes, the ECB's initiative, provides for institutional settlement. Tokenized deposits are the on-chain representation of customer deposits at a commercial bank but remain on that bank's balance sheet — they are not transferred out of the banking system when moved. This third category preserves deposit insurance and prudential requirements while enabling programmable settlement.

The operational implication is direct: a financial institution choosing between these instruments is making a decision about counterparty risk, regulatory treatment and balance sheet accounting simultaneously. Getting the taxonomy wrong produces mis-priced risk. The episode also contextualizes the broader digital asset universe: cryptocurrencies — Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana — carry a combined market capitalization of $2.6 trillion, a figure that towers over the $21 billion tokenized RWA market and illustrates how much institutional capital has yet to migrate into regulated, yield-bearing tokenized instruments. The competitive pressure from Coinbase and Kraken, both acquiring banking licenses, is not speculative — it is a concrete challenge that Charles-Antoine Michallet names directly when asked whether crypto-native firms are a threat to traditional banks. The answer is unambiguous: yes.

This episode answers these questions

What does the tokenized asset market represent in 2025 and what is its growth trajectory?

In 2025, the tokenized asset market reached a capitalization of $21 billion, up from $6 billion in 2024 and $2 billion in 2023. This acceleration reflects the combined commitment of traditional institutions and crypto-native players, as well as the progressive removal of key regulatory and technological barriers.

What is Payments 3.0 and how does it differ from previous payment models according to Borderless.xyz?

Payments 3.0 is the era of payments enabled by blockchain and stablecoins, representing a fundamental rewiring of the underlying infrastructure rather than a new user interface on top of correspondent banking. Unlike Payments 1.0 (correspondent banking via SWIFT, slow and costly) and Payments 2.0 (fintechs such as Airwallex or Nium creating the illusion of instant payments through pre-funding), Payments 3.0 enables a direct connection between at most two counterparties for every single transfer, by agreeing on a common standard such as USDC on a given chain.

What is the transaction volume of stablecoins and how has it evolved recently?

Stablecoin transaction volume stands between $33 and $46 trillion in total annual transaction volume, double what was observed the previous year. This growth illustrates the growing adoption of stablecoins as settlement infrastructure in cross-border payments.

What is Euroclear's Pythagore project with the Banque de France and what is its ambition?

Project Pythagore is an initiative by Euroclear with the Banque de France aimed at tokenizing 100% of the NEU CP (Negotiable European Commercial Paper) market, the largest short-term debt market at over $300 billion. More than 50% of outstanding amounts and participants are actively engaged in the project, and settlement will be conducted in central bank digital currency (CBDC) via the European Central Bank's Pontes system.

What are the main B2B cross-border stablecoin payment use cases identified by Borderless.xyz?

The two primary use cases are treasury management for multinationals operating in emerging markets — who can deploy and repatriate capital in near real time, avoiding FX risk — and B2B payments related to international trade (import/export). A concrete example is cargo settlement between Africa and China: T+0 settlement via stablecoin versus T+4 via correspondent bank eliminates several days of port fees, making the additional 10 basis points of FX cost highly cost-effective.

Why does liquidity in tokenized assets remain limited despite blockchain technology, according to Société Générale?

According to Charles Michelette of Société Générale, liquidity in tokenized assets remains limited because all participants must first be onboarded onto the same platform, and an asset is not automatically transferable simply because it is on-chain. KYC requirements, paying agent management, withholding tax and other administrative processes still require bilateral connections, which prevents a true liquidity network effect from emerging.

How does Euroclear approach the question of confidentiality and privacy on blockchain networks for institutional capital markets?

Jorgen Ouaknine of Euroclear considers privacy non-negotiable for capital markets and argues that networks must integrate privacy by design natively. He cites Canton Network as one of the very few networks that has built confidentiality and interoperability natively from inception, while emphasizing that there will not be one dominant network — global interoperability across different networks is the key priority.

What is Euroclear's approach to integrating blockchain innovation without replacing existing infrastructure?

Euroclear, which manages over $40 trillion in assets under custody and processes the equivalent of world GDP every month, does not seek to disrupt the system but to translate innovation into something safe, scalable and usable by the world's largest financial institutions. The approach relies on partnerships with fintechs rather than competition, combining fintech agility and innovation with the regulatory robustness and compliance provided by established market infrastructures.

What are the different types of digital assets and how are they classified in the financial ecosystem?

Digital assets divide into several categories: digital currencies (fiat-backed stablecoins, CBDCs as a native representation of central bank money on-chain, and tokenized deposits that remain on bank balance sheets), tokenized RWAs (private equity, private credit, bonds, real estate), cryptocurrencies (Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana) with a combined capitalization of $2.6 trillion, and NFTs representing proof of ownership of a specific asset.

What is the difference between stablecoins, CBDCs and tokenized deposits in the digital currency architecture?

Stablecoins are backed by fiat currencies or highly liquid short-term assets. CBDCs (Central Bank Digital Currency) are a native representation of central bank money directly on-chain. Tokenized deposits are the on-chain representation of customer deposits but remain on the bank's balance sheet, which is a fundamental distinction from the other two forms.
