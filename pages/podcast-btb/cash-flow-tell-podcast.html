What Cash Flow & Tell covers

What this podcast really covers

Cash Flow & Tell is built around four recurring themes that define growth-stage finance: hiring decisions, capital structure, international expansion, and working capital management. The podcast does not offer generic financial advice or investment tips. Instead, it maps the decision trees entrepreneurs face at specific inflection points. When do you hire a CFO instead of using a bookkeeper? When should you raise venture debt versus equity? How do you manage 60- or 90-day payment cycles across borders without running out of cash? These questions have context-dependent answers, and the show provides the reasoning that separates premature hiring from strategic staffing, or aggressive growth from sustainable scaling.

The episodes reveal that most finance challenges in growing companies stem from misalignment between revenue recognition and cash realization. A company can show strong topline growth while facing a cash crisis if customer payment terms, contract structures, or seasonal revenue patterns create gaps between income statement success and actual money in the bank. The podcast addresses this gap directly, with founders and CFOs explaining how they solved it through term negotiations, working capital facilities, or restructured business models.

Who this podcast is essential for

Cash Flow & Tell speaks directly to three audiences: CEOs and founders in the $1M–$50M revenue range who are making their first major hiring or financing decisions; CFOs and finance leaders evaluating capital strategy and team structure at growth companies; and early-stage entrepreneurs asking whether they need professional finance oversight yet. For founders in startup mode, the show clarifies when financial complexity justifies bringing in a CFO or CFO service. For finance leaders, it offers peer insight into scaling team, raising capital, and managing stakeholder expectations across different business models. For investors and board members evaluating a company's readiness for capital, the episodes outline the financial maturity signals that indicate scalability.

The podcast is equally valuable for business owners expanding internationally or entering new customer segments, as it details the working capital and payment term challenges that cross-border growth introduces. Entrepreneurs who have already raised capital but are struggling with cash management will find tactical frameworks for improving cash conversion cycles without sacrificing growth.

What this changes in practice

Listeners who apply Cash Flow & Tell's frameworks typically make three immediate changes. First, they stop conflating revenue with cash and start mapping actual payment cycles into their financial model, often discovering that growth exacerbates cash timing mismatches. Second, they develop a hiring checklist for finance roles that goes beyond "we need someone"—including metrics like revenue size, number of financing rounds, and audit requirements that signal readiness. Third, they restructure capital conversations with investors, board members, or lenders by framing capital as a strategic choice tied to growth strategy, not a generic metric.

The podcast also shifts founder behavior around risk disclosure. Many growth-stage companies hide cash timing challenges from investors or board members out of concern about appearing weak. Cash Flow & Tell demonstrates that transparency about working capital, customer concentration, or payment term risk is a sign of financial maturity, not weakness, and leads to better capital partnerships.

Growing companies face a systematic gap between revenue growth and cash availability. The difference between founders who manage this gap and those who crash lies not in luck, but in understanding capital structure, payment term negotiation, and the timing of finance team expansion. The podcast answers these questions

When should a growing company hire a CFO?

Timing depends on business maturity and complexity. Early-stage companies may outsource CFO functions through fractional or part-time arrangements before justifying a full-time hire. As revenue scales and financing rounds multiply, the demands of forecasting, board reporting, and financial strategy become critical. Waiting too long risks missed growth opportunities or financial mismanagement during critical transitions.

What is the right capital strategy for SMBs?

The optimal capital stack balances debt, equity, and internal cash flow based on growth stage and industry. Early-stage businesses often rely on founder investment and venture capital; growth-stage companies benefit from a mix of venture debt and equity; mature SMBs may leverage bank financing or growth equity. A clear understanding of unit economics and cash conversion cycles guides these decisions.

How do you manage cash flow while scaling internationally?

Cross-border expansion introduces payment delays, currency risk, and higher operational costs. Effective strategies include negotiating favorable payment terms with international customers, using foreign exchange hedging tools, and maintaining adequate working capital reserves. Building relationships with local banks and understanding regional regulations reduces friction and improves cash predictability.

Why do high-growth companies still struggle with cash collection?

Revenue recognition and cash realization diverge in many industries, especially those with long sales cycles or complex contracts. Companies may report strong sales but face extended payment windows, disputed invoices, or credit risk. Establishing clear payment terms upfront, automating collections, and maintaining detailed customer credit profiles prevents cash crises despite healthy-looking financial statements.
