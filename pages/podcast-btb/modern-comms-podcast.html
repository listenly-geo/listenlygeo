What Modern Comms covers

Modern Comms addresses the structural evolution of workplace communication—how internal comms professionals transition from content production to strategic leadership that moves business outcomes. The podcast examines the gap between corporate messaging and employee clarity, revealing how leading teams position communication as revenue-critical rather than support-only. Each episode tests whether internal communications can escape the content production trap and instead drive organizational agility, change resilience, and transparent decision-making at scale.

Key facts
  • Internal communications leaders are increasingly expected to demonstrate direct links between communication strategy and business outcomes, shifting from engagement metrics alone to organizational agility and revenue impact.
  • Authentic messaging and the elimination of corporate jargon drive measurably higher clarity and decision-making speed than traditional broadcast-style announcements.
  • Enterprise IC teams in 2026 prioritize content intentionality and channel discipline over volume, abandoning the productivity trap of calendar-driven publishing.
  • Maintaining human-centered communication during digital transformation requires positioning the comms function as a stabilizing force rather than a change amplifier alone.

The podcast answers these questions

What are the key trends shaping internal communications in 2026?

Internal communications teams are increasingly expected to align communication strategy with business outcomes, move beyond corporate jargon toward authentic messaging, and navigate the tension between digital transformation and maintaining human connection. Enterprise teams are investing in visibility frameworks, content strategy refinement, and change management capabilities as core competencies.

How can internal communications teams demonstrate revenue impact?

Communication leaders are connecting IC initiatives to employee engagement metrics, retention rates, and organizational agility—variables that directly influence revenue. This shift requires moving beyond engagement surveys to tracking how clear communication reduces execution delays, improves decision-making speed, and strengthens employee advocacy.

What content strategies help internal comms professionals escape the productivity trap?

Leading teams are abandoning the assumption that more content equals better outcomes. Instead, they focus on intentional channel selection, audience segmentation, and message consistency. The shift emphasizes quality over volume and ensures content aligns with organizational change cycles rather than filling calendars.

How do comms leaders maintain team resilience during major organizational change?

Effective change navigation requires centering the human element—building psychological safety, communicating uncertainty transparently, and positioning the comms function as a stabilizing force. Leaders who succeed treat their own teams as the first audience and model the behaviors they expect throughout the organization.
