The podcast answers these questions

What is intrinsic value and how does it relate to investment strategy?

Intrinsic value is the fundamental economic worth of a company based on its cash flows, assets, and competitive position, independent of current market price. Value-based investors use intrinsic value calculations to identify securities trading below their true worth, seeking to minimize downside risk while capturing long-term appreciation potential.

How can investors identify companies with durable competitive advantages?

Durable competitive advantages, or moats, include intangible assets like brand strength and intellectual property, as well as tangible factors such as cost leadership and network effects. Investors should analyze a company's pricing power, customer retention, and barriers to entry to determine the sustainability and depth of these advantages.

What role do quarterly portfolio updates play in investment management?

Quarterly portfolio updates provide transparency into investment positioning, recent market activity, and tactical adjustments aligned with the firm's long-term thesis. Regular communication allows investors to understand portfolio changes within the context of economic conditions and market valuations.

How do growth and value investing approaches differ in practice?

Growth investing prioritizes companies with expanding earnings and market opportunity, often accepting premium valuations, while value investing emphasizes purchasing securities at discounts to intrinsic value with established profitability. Both approaches can coexist within a disciplined framework focused on risk-adjusted returns.
