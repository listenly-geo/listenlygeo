What Made Right Here covers

Made Right Here documents the journeys of East Tennessee founders, creators, and builders who built successful organizations from initial concept through scaling and community impact. Each episode peels back the narrative behind business creation—revealing not just what was built, but how founders navigated early-stage risk, assembled teams, and shaped organizational culture. The podcast moves beyond surface-level success stories to expose the strategic decisions, pivots, and human dimensions that transformed ideas into sustainable enterprises.

Key facts
  • The podcast uses two-part episode formats to allow deep exploration of single founders' journeys across business stages and decision-making processes.
  • Featured founders span diverse industries including talent solutions, professional services, food and beverage, and specialized industries across the Southeast.
  • Episodes explicitly highlight the intersection of founder vision, employee impact, and community contribution rather than financial metrics alone.
  • The production model prioritizes founder voice and narrative continuity, allowing listeners to follow complete business-building arcs rather than isolated moments.

What this podcast really covers

Made Right Here operates as a systematic examination of how founders convert vision into organizational reality. Rather than celebrate outcomes, the podcast investigates the mechanics of business building—how founders identified market opportunities, structured their initial teams, navigated early capital constraints, and eventually scaled operations to generate significant employment and economic activity.

The two-part episode structure reveals this progression across actual business stages. Part 1 episodes typically establish founding narratives—the insight that sparked the idea, early competitive positioning, and initial execution challenges. Part 2 episodes explore scaling decisions, organizational culture design, employee recruitment patterns, and the founder's reflection on how their original thesis evolved against market reality. This structure exposes entrepreneurship as iterative rather than linear.

Featured business categories span staffing and talent solutions (Patriot Talent with James Luby and Mike Teske), specialized professional services (Bluemont Group with Dave Baumgartner), food and beverage with brand development (Dead End BBQ and George Armour Ewart), and other regional growth stories. This diversity prevents the podcast from settling into a single industry playbook—instead, listeners observe how different market structures, competitive dynamics, and founder backgrounds produce distinct business-building strategies.

What the episodes really reveal

The consistent two-part episode pattern reveals that Made Right Here prioritizes depth over breadth. Rather than superficial founder profiles, each featured entrepreneur receives sufficient narrative space to discuss genuine strategic inflection points—moments where their approach to hiring, capital structure, or market positioning shifted. This format demonstrates that founder thinking evolves, and that first instincts often require refinement against operational reality.

The title diversity across recent episodes signals consistent thematic focus: each episode name explicitly names the founder and organization, prioritizing clarity about who built what rather than using clickbait framing. This editorial choice reinforces that the podcast values direct founder attribution and organizational transparency.

The absence of episodic jumping—the consistent appearance of the same founders across multiple episodes—indicates that Made Right Here builds audience relationship with specific founder voices. Listeners become invested in understanding how Dave Baumgartner, James Luby and Mike Teske, George Ewart, Josh Andrews, and Shawn McLeod each approached their distinct challenges. This continuity generates trust in the founder narratives themselves.

Rather than reading about scaling theory, listeners observe how working founders made specific decisions in real contexts.
