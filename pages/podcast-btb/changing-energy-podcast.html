What Changing Energy covers

Changing Energy documents the operational and strategic realities of modern energy systems under transition. Episodes reveal how utilities deploy data analytics to optimize performance, guard critical infrastructure against cyber threats, and adapt to climate volatility. The podcast treats energy as a system integration challenge—where technology, policy, and human decisions intersect to determine whether grids can scale renewables while maintaining reliability.

Key facts

  • Energy disaggregation enables utilities to identify consumption patterns at device level, improving demand forecasting and grid balancing in real time.
  • Cybersecurity breaches against power infrastructure have increased 40–60% annually, making digital resilience as critical as physical hardening for grid operators.
  • Hydrogen production, storage, and distribution remain cost-prohibitive at scale without electrolysis innovation and integrated supply-chain infrastructure.
  • Extreme weather events—wildfires, storms, temperature swings—require utilities to invest in predictive analytics and automated failover systems to minimize outage duration.

Across 41 episodes, a consistent pattern emerges: energy transformation requires simultaneous progress in three domains. First, data and analytics—disaggregation, demand forecasting, predictive maintenance—unlock efficiency gains within existing infrastructure. Second, security and resilience—cybersecurity protocols, hardened systems, rapid recovery—become essential as grids digitize and face climate volatility. Third, technology deployment—hydrogen production methods, renewable integration, storage solutions—faces economic and supply-chain barriers that policy and investment can reduce but not eliminate overnight. The show avoids false optimism and recognizes trade-offs, making it a grounded resource for stakeholders managing real transitions rather than pursuing abstract ideals.

The podcast answers these questions

What is energy disaggregation and why does it matter?

Energy disaggregation breaks down consumption data at granular levels, revealing patterns in how electricity is used across different devices and time periods. This visibility enables grid operators and utilities to identify inefficiencies, forecast demand more accurately, and optimize resource allocation in real time. It transforms raw consumption into actionable intelligence for both infrastructure planning and emergency response.

How does cybersecurity impact modern power grids?

As energy grids become increasingly digitized and interconnected, they face rising threats from cyberattacks targeting critical infrastructure. A successful breach can disrupt power distribution, compromise data integrity, or damage physical equipment. This makes cybersecurity an essential layer of grid resilience, requiring coordinated investment in monitoring, authentication, and threat response alongside traditional infrastructure hardening.

What role does hydrogen play in energy transition?

Hydrogen serves as a versatile energy carrier that can store renewable electricity, provide high-temperature heat for industrial processes, and fuel vehicles and ships. It bridges the gap between intermittent renewables and consistent demand, especially in hard-to-decarbonize sectors like heavy manufacturing and aviation. However, scaling hydrogen production sustainably requires advances in electrolysis, distribution infrastructure, and cost reduction.

How do utilities prepare for and respond to climate-driven weather events?

Utilities employ predictive analytics, hardened infrastructure upgrades, and rapid response protocols to mitigate climate risks. Data on temperature extremes, wildfires, and storms informs infrastructure placement and maintenance scheduling. Real-time monitoring and automated failover systems enable faster recovery when outages occur. Collaboration with emergency services and community-based early warning systems strengthens overall resilience.
