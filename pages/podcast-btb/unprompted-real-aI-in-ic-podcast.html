How can micro-experiments help shift mindset and behaviour when used with AI coaching?

Micro-experiments sidestep the common trap of attempting wholesale personality overhauls or operational restructuring. Instead, they involve low-stakes, incremental shifts—such as protecting break time during meetings or approaching a major proposal with conviction rather than hesitation—that compound over weeks , whereas attempting too much at once typically fails.

The power of micro-experiments lies in their psychological accessibility. Kate Hughes discovered that when change feels manageable and non-threatening, it becomes sustainable. Rather than declaring "I will become more confident" or "I will eliminate all stress," a micro-experiment might be as simple as: "This Friday, I will take a ten-minute walk before my leadership meeting." That single, concrete action becomes the testing ground for a new behaviour.

When paired with AI coaching, micro-experiments gain an additional layer of impact. In the Feelings Lounge framework discussed on this episode , every week begins with structured reflection: what energised you, what drained you, what patterns are emerging, and what single micro-experiment will you try this week? AI acts as the thinking partner, identifying patterns across multiple check-ins that humans often miss on their own.

The cumulative effect is profound. Over a series of weeks, these tiny shifts reshape how you approach challenges. One micro-experiment might be: "When I receive critical feedback, I will pause for five seconds before responding instead of defending immediately." Another week: "I will draft that proposal outline today, not wait until it feels perfect." Each small victory builds confidence and creates evidence that change is possible—without the paralysis that comes from aiming for perfection.

That shift in mindset is not a sudden epiphany—it emerges quietly from the evidence you accumulate yourself, week by week, through actions you have chosen and executed.

Why attempts at wholesale change typically backfire

The reason micro-experiments outperform grand resolutions is neurological and psychological. When you attempt to overhaul your entire way of working or thinking, your brain perceives this as a threat. The energy required is overwhelming, the failure rate is statistically high, and after a few setbacks, you abandon the effort entirely.

Micro-experiments bypass this resistance entirely. A change small enough to complete in a single week feels achievable, which means your brain does not trigger the defensive "this is too much" shutdown. You execute, you reflect, you adjust. The full methodology for designing these experiments each week is detailed further in the episode , but the core insight is: incremental action compounds more reliably than heroic ambition.

What prompt engineering techniques help ensure better AI agent outcomes?

Start by asking the AI tool to clarify your requirements before running the full prompt —Rebecca Lindsay emphasizes you don't know what you don't know. Instruct the AI to ask follow-up questions and remember them for future interactions, and be explicit about which knowledge sources the agent should draw from and what outcome you're trying to achieve.

Three foundational principles for stronger prompts

The first step is pre-clarification. Rather than launching straight into a complex request, ask the AI to help you define what you actually need. This upfront conversation prevents misalignment later and forces both you and the agent to name assumptions that might otherwise remain hidden. Second, embed permission for the agent to ask clarifying questions within your initial prompt. This transforms the interaction from one-directional to conversational. Third, be explicit about scope and knowledge boundaries. Define which sources, tools, or data the agent should reference and which it should avoid.
