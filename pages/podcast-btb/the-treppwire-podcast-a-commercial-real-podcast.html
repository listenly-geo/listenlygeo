How have cash-in and cash-out refinancing patterns shifted across commercial real estate cycles?

Cash-out refinancing dominated 2021 and 2022, reaching $7.1 billion and $7.6 billion respectively , while cash-in refinancing totaled only $2.1 billion and $2.2 billion. This pattern reversed sharply in 2023 as interest rates rose, shifting the pressure onto borrowers to inject additional capital into properties rather than extract equity.

How equity extraction flipped from asset class advantage to financing burden

The data comes from analysis of the 15 largest loans in conduit CMBS transactions, which represent 60 to 70% of aggregate principal, providing a reliable window into broader market behavior. In the low-rate, high-property-value environment of 2021–2022, owners were able to harvest accumulated equity through refinancing, extracting capital that exceeded what they were required to reinvest by a ratio of roughly 3-to-1.

This equity extraction strategy reflected a specific moment in the cycle: property valuations had appreciated significantly, and interest rates remained low enough that refinancing at favorable terms was economically rational. Owners could access their gains while still maintaining serviceable debt burdens.

The reversal in 2023 tells the opposite story, one explored at length in the episode . As the Federal Reserve's rate hikes trickled through the market, refinancing became prohibitively expensive, and property values began to normalize downward. Borrowers faced a choice: either accept higher debt service costs or inject additional capital—recapitalize their positions—to maintain loan compliance and avoid default.
