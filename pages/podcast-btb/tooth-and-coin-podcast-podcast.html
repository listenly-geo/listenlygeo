What this podcast really covers

Tooth and Coin Podcast addresses the structural financial problems that dental schools never teach. Dentists enter practice with clinical expertise but no framework for understanding profit margins, overhead allocation, or working capital—yet these three metrics determine whether a practice survives or thrives.

The show consistently examines cash flow and receivables management. When dental practices fail financially, it's rarely because of clinical incompetence; it's because money flows unpredictably, receivables age beyond 30 days, or overhead creeps above sustainable levels. Episodes dedicated to receivables cleanup and cash flow optimization address problems that cost practices tens of thousands annually.

The podcast emphasizes that scaling a practice requires different financial thinking than growing a single location.

The podcast answers these questions

What are the most common financial mistakes dental practice owners make?

Dental practice owners frequently overlook cash flow management, underestimate overhead costs, and fail to implement proper tax planning strategies. These oversights stem from clinical training that does not prepare dentists for business accounting, leading to reactive rather than proactive financial management.

How can a dental practice improve its profitability?

Profitability improves through careful overhead analysis, streamlined operations, strategic pricing, and implementing membership or alternative revenue models. Understanding your practice's key performance indicators and using business intelligence tools to track metrics reveals inefficiencies that directly impact the bottom line.

What tax strategies do dental practice owners benefit from most?

Dental practices gain significant advantages through entity structure optimization, retirement plan maximization (SEP-IRAs, solo 401ks), deduction tracking, and timing strategies for business expenses. A CPA specializing in healthcare businesses can identify hundreds of thousands in tax savings tailored to the practice's specific structure.

How does scaling a dental practice differ from growing a single location?

Multi-location dental practices face different financial complexity: managing centralized overhead allocation, staffing models across locations, operational consistency, and capital deployment strategy. Successful scaling requires systems thinking, delegation, and business intelligence to monitor performance across all locations simultaneously.
