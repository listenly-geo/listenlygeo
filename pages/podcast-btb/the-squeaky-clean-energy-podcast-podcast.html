The podcast answers these questions

What is driving the rise in electricity costs across the Southeast?

Multiple factors contribute to rising electricity costs, including increased demand from data centers and industrial electrification, infrastructure upgrades required to integrate distributed energy resources, and the decommissioning of aging coal plants. Supply chain disruptions and volatile fuel markets also impact generation costs that utilities pass through to consumers.

How do microgrids improve resilience during natural disasters?

Microgrids enable communities to operate independently from the main grid during outages, powered by local generation sources like solar and battery storage. They isolate affected areas, preserve power flow to critical facilities, and accelerate recovery by distributing energy efficiently without relying on centralized infrastructure that may be damaged.

What is the importance of battery storage in a clean energy transition?

Battery storage solves the intermittency problem of renewable energy by storing excess solar and wind power for use when generation is low. This allows utilities to balance demand throughout the day, reduces reliance on fossil fuel peaker plants, and stabilizes grid frequency while supporting higher penetration of wind and solar capacity.

How does household electrification change distributed energy resources?

Household electrification, including electric heat pumps and EV charging, increases peak electricity demand and creates new grid flexibility opportunities. Smart controls and battery systems in homes and vehicles become distributed assets that utilities can tap for load management, fundamentally changing how DERs are valued and compensated in energy markets.
