What WMYT - What Makes YOU Tick covers

WMYT strips away theory to examine how elite tech leaders make decisions that stick. The podcast homes in on patterns across founders, operators, and sales leaders—how they hire for ownership, navigate pressure without burnout, and position their careers as AI transforms work itself. Each conversation moves past surface strategy to the specific failures and reversals that shaped the host's guests, proving that exceptional performance comes from brutal self-awareness, not inherited privilege or luck.

Key facts
  • Tech leaders across companies attribute high performance to clarity about personal motivation and deliberate alignment between decisions and individual values.
  • Sales skill shortages and frontline manager gaps persist because most hiring and development frameworks fail to distinguish between credentials and demonstrated ownership.
  • Leaders who sustain performance under pressure build team redundancy and explicit boundaries, refusing to normalize burnout cycles.
  • Career ladders remain viable in the AI era only when organizations invest in mentorship, cross-functional growth, and judgment-based work that automation cannot yet replace.

What this podcast really covers

WMYT examines four interconnected pillars of tech leadership: hiring and retention, sales effectiveness and team dynamics, career resilience in disruption, and the psychology of decision-making under uncertainty. The episodes reveal that sales skill gaps are not primarily training problems—they stem from misaligned hiring (prioritizing credentials over track record) and underinvestment in frontline manager capability. Similarly, leadership longevity depends less on tenure than on leaders' willingness to invest in their own self-knowledge and to build teams that can absorb pressure without leadership becoming a burnout vector. The podcast insists that in-person selling, mentorship, and real relationship-building remain irreplaceable, even as technology accelerates around them.

Who this podcast is essential for

Founders and early-stage operators who hire first sales leaders and must understand what hiring signals actually predict success. Sales leaders and GTM teams navigating talent shortages and team scaling, seeking frameworks to build sustainable high performance without attrition. Engineering leaders and executives facing AI-driven job transformation, needing concrete strategies to protect and evolve their teams' career prospects. Any leader who has felt pressure mounting and wondered whether burnout is inevitable or a sign of broken systems.

What the episodes really reveal

Recurring themes across WMYT's episode archive show that exceptional tech leaders share visible patterns: they are ruthlessly candid about their own failures, they hire for grit and motivation over pedigree, and they treat frontline managers as a strategic asset, not overhead. The ICP Masterclass episode reveals that sales growth without team expansion is possible only through operational discipline and clear buyer targeting—not more hustle. Episodes on retention and career ladders uncover that AI is not the primary threat to tech careers; rather, the failure to build meaningful growth paths creates vulnerability. When the Cuts Come explores which leaders survive downsizing: those who had already built trust, clarity, and team redundancy. The in-person selling episode acknowledges a generational skill deficit and frames it not as terminal but as an asymmetric advantage for leaders willing to invest in old-school relationship building.

What this changes in practice

Leaders listening to WMYT typically redesign their hiring rubrics first—moving from credential-driven interviews to structured question sets that reveal ownership and intrinsic motivation. Second, they audit their team's growth infrastructure, asking whether their frontline managers have enough support and whether their career ladder looks healthy from the individual contributor's perspective. Third, they reframe pressure and burnout not as leadership taxes but as flags that the team structure is broken and needs redesign. Finally, they reassess their own decision-making: do they know what truly motivates them, or are they operating on inherited assumptions? The podcast's central thesis—that knowing yourself is prerequisite to building strong teams and lasting impact—becomes a practical checkpoint for monthly leadership reflection.

Tech leaders who outperform peers are not distinguished by access to better information or newer tools, but by clarity about their own values and unwillingness to compromise on team health to hit short-term targets.

The podcast answers these questions

What makes tech leaders successful according to this podcast?

Success in tech leadership stems from self-awareness about personal motivation and willingness to learn from failure. The podcast repeatedly demonstrates that high performers attribute their wins to understanding what truly motivates them—their individual 'why'—and aligning decisions with that clarity.

How do sales leaders prevent burnout while performing under pressure?

The podcast reveals that sustainable high performance requires explicit boundaries, delegation to frontline managers, and honest assessment of team capacity. Leaders who survive pressure cycles build redundancy into their teams and refuse to normalize unsustainable sprint cultures.

What hiring practices actually lead to ownership in tech teams?

Ownership emerges from hiring for intrinsic motivation and track record of past initiative, not credentials alone. Structured interviews revealing how candidates have solved problems independently predict ownership better than traditional competency frameworks.

How can tech leaders stay relevant as AI reshapes career paths?

Leaders ensure career growth remains available even as individual contributor tracks shift. This means building mentorship, cross-functional projects, and skill development opportunities that AI cannot fully automate, anchoring human value in judgment and relationship-building.
