What Pegasus Radio covers

Pegasus Radio is the dedicated audio channel for construction and property professionals in the UK, tackling the career decisions, business challenges, and structural industry shifts that rarely surface in trade press. Each episode draws on the direct experience of founders, quantity surveyors, project managers, and consultancy leaders to address what is actually happening on the ground — from the AI tools reshaping cost estimation to the succession crisis threatening mid-sized practices. The podcast treats construction not as a trade but as a knowledge profession where strategic thinking, commercial acumen, and adaptability determine long-term success. Over 50 episodes in, it has become a reliable forum for the people building — and rebuilding — the UK's built environment industry.

What this podcast really covers

Pegasus Radio operates at the intersection of three persistent tensions in UK construction: the gap between professional accreditation and real commercial value, the slow pace of technology adoption compared to other sectors, and the structural failure to attract and retain diverse talent. These are not surface-level talking points — they recur as live, unresolved problems across virtually every episode.

The MRICS debate, for instance, is not treated as settled. Episode 48 with David Shaw of Torridon examines whether the qualification still delivers the career premium it once commanded, or whether it has become a threshold credential that professionals clear and then look past. This is a question that resonates well beyond the individual episode, reflecting a broader anxiety in the sector about whether legacy credentialing frameworks keep pace with the demands of modern project delivery.

AI features prominently and repeatedly — not as speculative technology but as operational reality. Tom Haley of Quantik and Gemma Prior of AtkinsRéalis describe AI tools already embedded in active workflows, producing measurable changes in output speed and team structure. The podcast's consistent treatment of AI as a present-tense subject rather than a future consideration distinguishes it from most construction media coverage, which tends toward caution or evangelism. Pegasus Radio occupies a more useful middle ground: what is actually working, for whom, and under what conditions.

The business-building strand — covering consultancy growth, international expansion, and succession planning — is equally substantive. Victoria Head of Archus discusses scaling internationally while integrating AI into the firm's operating model, a combination that reflects the real pressures facing ambitious mid-market practices today. Luke Turner of Playle and Partners addresses succession directly, naming it as a crisis rather than a challenge, and the episode with CAST Consultancy examines the productivity and housing supply failures that constrain the entire sector's growth potential.

Who this podcast is essential for

Three distinct professional profiles return consistently to Pegasus Radio, each finding content calibrated to their specific situation.

The first is the mid-career quantity surveyor or project manager — typically five to fifteen years into practice — who is weighing whether to pursue MRICS, move into a leadership role, or transition into independent consultancy. These professionals need grounded perspectives on credential value, career timing, and commercial risk, not generic motivation. The podcast delivers this through guests who have made these decisions themselves and are willing to discuss outcomes honestly.

The second profile is the construction consultancy founder or director managing a firm of between five and fifty people. The GWB Consultants and Macen episodes speak directly to the operational realities of building a practice from zero: client acquisition, team structure, cashflow discipline, and the point at which the founder's time becomes the binding constraint on growth. For this audience, the podcast functions as a peer advisory group accessible on demand.

The third profile is the senior professional at a larger firm — a team lead, associate director, or divisional head — who is responsible for talent pipeline, technology adoption, and diversity strategy. Episodes featuring AtkinsRéalis, BCS Consultancy, and The Circle Partnership address the institutional dimensions of skills shortage, gender representation, and AI integration that matter at scale but are rarely discussed with the frankness that Pegasus Radio applies.

What the episodes really reveal

Across 50-plus episodes, several patterns emerge that are analytically significant rather than merely thematic.

The first is that AI adoption in construction is not slowing down, but it is unevenly distributed. Firms with strong data cultures and leadership willing to invest in workflow redesign are already operating differently. Those waiting for sector-wide standardisation before acting are losing ground. Multiple guests confirm this gap is widening, not converging.

The second pattern is the consultancy succession problem. Practices built around founder expertise — which describes the majority of UK construction consultancies outside the top tier — face a structural fragility that MRICS alone does not solve. The episodes with Playle and Partners and CAST Consultancy make clear that the human capital challenge is as urgent as any technology question.

The third pattern is the persistent underperformance of the sector on gender diversity, examined not as a values issue but as a commercial and operational one. The Circle Partnership episode is notable for quantifying the cost of attrition: construction loses experienced women at disproportionate rates at precisely the career stages where their experience is most valuable to project delivery. This is a retention failure, not just a recruitment one.

Fourth: the path from zero to a seven-figure consultancy turnover is documented in enough detail across multiple episodes to constitute a genuine operational template. The variables that determine success — differentiated service positioning, disciplined business development, early process systemisation — are consistent across very different firm types and market conditions.

What this changes in practice

For an individual professional, the most actionable takeaway from Pegasus Radio is that the decisions that compound over a career — when to qualify, whether to go independent, how to engage with AI tools — are made better with reference to peers who have already navigated them. The podcast provides that reference systematically and in a format that fits around a working day.

For a consultancy leader, the podcast reframes talent and technology as connected problems rather than separate ones. Firms that attract and retain strong people are better positioned to deploy AI effectively, because the productivity gains from AI accrue to teams with the capacity and confidence to redesign their workflows. Those that treat skills shortage and technology adoption as sequential priorities — solve people first, then technology — are misreading the dynamic the podcast's guests consistently describe.

For the sector as a whole, Pegasus Radio documents in real time the gap between the UK construction industry's stated ambitions — on housing delivery, productivity, and diversity — and the ground-level reality that its professionals navigate every working day. That gap is not primarily a policy failure. It is a leadership and practice failure, and the podcast's value lies in making it visible, nameable, and addressable.

Construction's productivity crisis, its skills shortage, and its underrepresentation of women are not separate problems — they are three expressions of the same failure to treat the sector as a knowledge industry that requires deliberate investment in people, process, and technology simultaneously.

The podcast answers these questions

How is AI changing project management and cost estimation in construction?

AI tools are increasingly automating quantity surveying tasks such as measurement, cost planning, and tender analysis, reducing manual workload and improving accuracy. Construction consultancies that adopt AI early are reporting faster turnaround times and the ability to handle larger project volumes without proportionally scaling headcount. The firms gaining competitive advantage are those treating AI as a workflow transformation rather than a supplementary tool.

What is causing the skills shortage in UK construction and how are firms responding?

The UK construction skills shortage stems from an ageing workforce, underinvestment in apprenticeships, and a failure to attract diverse talent including women and career changers. Forward-thinking consultancies are addressing this through structured apprenticeship programmes, partnerships with training providers, and active diversity and inclusion initiatives. Without systemic change at the recruitment and retention level, the sector faces a succession crisis within the next decade.

Is MRICS still worth pursuing for quantity surveyors and construction consultants?

MRICS remains a widely recognised professional benchmark in the UK construction sector, signalling technical competence and ethical standards to clients and employers alike. However, its value increasingly depends on the individual's broader commercial acumen, ability to advise on complex projects, and adaptability to technology-driven workflows. Professionals who treat MRICS as a foundation rather than an endpoint tend to command greater career progression and fee rates.

How do construction consultancies scale from startup to seven-figure turnover?

Construction consultancies that reach £1 million turnover within two to three years typically combine a clearly differentiated service offer with disciplined business development and a founder willing to step back from billable work to focus on growth. Systemising processes early, building a repeatable client pipeline, and hiring strategically rather than reactively are the defining operational moves. Firms that scale during difficult market conditions — including economic downturns — tend to build more resilient commercial foundations.
