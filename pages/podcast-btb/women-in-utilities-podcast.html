What Women in Utilities covers

Women in Utilities is a monthly platform dedicated to elevating the professional narratives of women working across energy generation, transmission, distribution, and regulatory affairs. Each episode features candid conversations with executives, engineers, project leaders, and board directors from major utilities including Con Edison, CenterPoint Energy, NorthWestern Energy, and research institutions like EPRI. The podcast explores how women navigate technical careers, lead organizational change, and shape the future of grid modernization and clean energy transitions. Listeners gain direct access to unfiltered professional advice, career strategies, and insights into the systemic challenges and opportunities defining the utilities industry today.

Key facts
  • Monthly episodes feature interviews with women leaders from Fortune 500 utilities and industry research organizations
  • Coverage spans technical roles, executive positions, and board-level responsibilities across the entire energy value chain
  • Guests include professionals from Con Edison, EPRI, CenterPoint Energy, EY, UGI, and Smart Electric Power Alliance

What this podcast really covers

Women in Utilities documents the professional journeys of women operating at every tier of the utilities sector. Episode 16 featuring Vicki Kuo from Con Edison addresses corporate strategy and leadership in one of America's largest utility operators. Episode 15 with Morgan Scott from EPRI—the Electric Power Research Institute—examines research-driven innovation and the role of women shaping future grid technologies. Episode 14 with Ellie Lynch from EN Engineering explores consulting and project delivery within the sector. Episode 13 featuring Hetal Patel from EY covers the financial and strategic advisory work women perform for utility clients. These interviews consistently reveal that women in utilities roles manage complex stakeholder relationships, lead multi-million-dollar infrastructure projects, influence regulatory policy, and drive adoption of distributed energy resources and grid decentralization.

The podcast's scope extends beyond individual career narratives to systemic industry dynamics. Episode 12 with Kate Porter from CenterPoint Energy and Episode 9 with Nancy Conder also from CenterPoint Energy illustrate how women operate across different business lines—operations, customer relations, and corporate development. Episode 11 featuring Bob Rowe from NorthWestern Energy (notably including a male voice, signaling the podcast's broader commitment to examining gender dynamics) and Episode 8 with Britt Ide, a board director at both NorthWestern Energy and ATLIS Motor Vehicles, demonstrate women's movement into governance and emerging energy sectors. Episode 10 with Julia Hamm from SEPA—the Smart Electric Power Alliance—centers the conversation on policy advocacy and industry standardization. Episode 7 with Jenn Hood from UGI addresses gas utility operations and energy infrastructure diversification.

Who this podcast is essential for

Energy utility executives and operations leaders benefit from direct peer intelligence on organizational challenges, succession planning, and competitive talent strategies. Hearing from women at Con Edison, CenterPoint, and NorthWestern provides benchmark data on how peer organizations structure leadership development and diversity initiatives.

Engineers and technical professionals entering or advancing within utilities gain mentorship and tactical career advice from women who have navigated the field's technical rigor and male-dominated culture. Episodes document the specific skills, certifications, and cross-functional experience that accelerate advancement from individual contributor roles to management and director-level positions.

Regulatory, policy, and business development professionals working in energy consulting, investment, or government relations use the podcast to understand women's influence on utilities' strategic direction—from renewable integration priorities to grid modernization investment and workforce planning.

What the episodes really reveal

Across 16 episodes, three consistent themes emerge. First, women in utilities occupy strategic, high-impact roles—they are not concentrated in support functions but lead operations, engineering teams, corporate strategy, research, and board-level governance. Second, mentorship and sponsorship are identified repeatedly as critical accelerators; women advance fastest when paired with executive advocates who actively create opportunities and visibility. Third, the utilities industry is undergoing structural transformation driven by energy transition, and women leaders are disproportionately represented in roles shaping that transition—renewable integration, grid modernization, customer engagement around distributed energy, and workforce strategy for a decarbonized future.

The podcast also reveals the persistence of particular obstacles: women often report being the "only woman in the room" at senior levels, facing skepticism regarding technical competence despite credentials, and navigating workplace cultures that still default to male leadership models. Yet the narrative is not one of victimhood but of agency—each guest discusses how she reframed obstacles into competitive advantage, built her own networks when institutional mentorship was unavailable, and leveraged sector-wide transformation as an opportunity to reshape utilities' culture.

What this changes in practice

Women in Utilities provides utilities, energy consultancies, and equipment manufacturers with actionable intelligence on talent attraction and retention. Executives managing diversity initiatives gain proven strategies—from executive coaching to board placement to cross-company peer learning—that work. For women considering careers in utilities, the podcast removes information asymmetry; they see realistic pathways, understand what stakeholder relationships matter, and learn how successful women navigate technical credibility and organizational politics.

For investors and policy makers, the podcast documents how utilities with higher women's representation in leadership correlate with faster energy transition execution, better regulatory relationships, and stronger innovation. The conversations with EPRI researchers, SEPA advocates, and EY consultants make explicit the connection between diversity and business outcomes—it is no longer an abstract corporate value but a demonstrated competitive factor.

Women in utilities are not simply represented in the industry—they are actively reshaping it. From grid modernization to regulatory strategy to energy transition execution, their leadership directly influences how the sector evolves. The podcast documents this shift with granular clarity, providing both a record of their achievements and a practical map for other women pursuing similar trajectories.

The podcast answers these questions

What skills are most critical for women advancing in energy utilities today?

Technical expertise combined with business acumen and leadership capabilities forms the foundation for advancement. Women in utilities roles increasingly require cross-functional experience spanning operations, finance, regulatory compliance, and strategic planning—competencies that position professionals for executive trajectories within major utility corporations and energy organizations.

How are women leaders reshaping grid modernization and energy transition strategies?

Women executives are driving innovation in renewable integration, smart grid deployment, and decarbonization initiatives. Their participation in strategic decision-making at companies like Con Edison, CenterPoint Energy, and EPRI directly influences investment priorities, technology adoption rates, and the pace at which utilities transition toward sustainable infrastructure.

What mentorship and network opportunities exist for women in utilities and energy?

Industry organizations including SEPA, professional engineering associations, and utility-led diversity initiatives provide formal mentorship frameworks. Peer networks, board participation, and cross-company collaborations enable knowledge transfer and accelerate professional development for women moving into director-level and C-suite positions.

How do regulatory and policy environments affect women's career pathways in energy companies?

Regulatory bodies increasingly mandate diversity reporting and inclusion metrics for utility operators. Policy mandates around clean energy transitions and workforce development create new leadership roles and project opportunities specifically designed to draw diverse talent into engineering, operations, and executive functions.
