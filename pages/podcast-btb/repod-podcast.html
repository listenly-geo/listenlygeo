The podcast answers these questions

What operational challenges does real estate technology face today?

Real estate technology faces critical gaps in data management, system integration, and MLS infrastructure. The industry struggles with disconnected platforms, inadequate data licensing frameworks, and misalignment between what agents need and what systems deliver. MLS organizations must navigate both legacy technology debt and rapid innovation demands while maintaining competitive advantage.

How are MLSs addressing the shift to AI-driven real estate solutions?

MLSs are experimenting with AI integration to convert raw data into actionable insights while maintaining data integrity and agent trust. Key questions emerging include whether MLSs should build proprietary AI solutions or partner with vendors, how to ensure AI recommendations align with agent credibility, and what governance frameworks prevent misuse of listing data.

Why do buyer-focused solutions create gaps for MLS platforms?

Most real estate technology prioritizes agent and broker workflows, but buyer demand has fragmented across unconnected portals and platforms. MLS data, which drives 70% of successful transactions, doesn't reach buyers consistently through modern discovery channels. This structural gap leaves MLSs underlevered in the buyer journey and creates opportunities for non-MLS aggregators to capture buyer data.

What defines relevance vs. survival for an MLS in 2026?

Survival means maintaining data monopoly and transaction fees; relevance means solving real operational problems for agents, brokers, and buyers. An MLS survives by defending legacy business models, but it becomes relevant by enabling faster transactions, better data quality, lower system costs, and genuine trust with the professionals who depend on it daily.
