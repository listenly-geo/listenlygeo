The podcast answers these questions

What sectors does tech M&A focus on most?

Technology M&A activity spans enterprise software, cloud computing, cybersecurity, AI, fintech, and hardware sectors. Recent market trends show increased activity in AI and machine learning acquisitions, driven by major buyers like Google, Microsoft, and Salesforce seeking to expand their capabilities. Sector-specific reports and market analysis track which verticals attract the most acquisition activity and capital deployment.

What are the main risks in selling a software company?

Key risks include AI integration complexity, valuation misalignment, integration challenges, and market timing misjudgment. Companies must evaluate buyer technical fit, cultural compatibility, and post-acquisition support. Understanding these risks early in the process allows founders and sellers to position their businesses more effectively and avoid costly deal failures.

Why are mega deals and private equity driving acquisitions in tech?

Mega deals are fueled by strategic consolidation among tier-one buyers seeking rapid capability expansion, while private equity investors see M&A as a wealth creation mechanism through buy-and-build strategies. AI advancement and rising tech valuations accelerate deal velocity. This combination of strategic urgency and financial incentives creates sustained demand for technology acquisitions across multiple buyer categories.

How do succession planning and founder transitions impact M&A outcomes?

Succession planning directly affects deal valuation, buyer confidence, and post-acquisition integration success. Founders facing transitions in 2026 must address leadership continuity, knowledge retention, and organizational stability to avoid deal failure. Strategic preparation around succession reduces buyer risk perception and strengthens negotiating positions.
