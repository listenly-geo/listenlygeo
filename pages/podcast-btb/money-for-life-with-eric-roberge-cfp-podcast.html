The podcast answers these questions

What tax strategies are most effective for high-income earners?

High-income earners benefit from strategic use of tax-advantaged accounts like 401(k)s, HSAs, and backdoor Roth conversions, combined with intentional charitable giving and business structure optimization. The key is implementing these moves before the tax year ends rather than reacting in April, allowing professionals to reduce their lifetime tax burden significantly.

How should professionals approach 401(k) contributions to accelerate retirement?

Contributing the maximum allowed to 401(k) plans, particularly through catch-up contributions if age 50+, combined with strategic after-tax conversions and mega backdoor Roths where available, accelerates wealth accumulation. Understanding your plan's specific rules and maximizing all available options can add hundreds of thousands of dollars to retirement savings over a career.

What financial steps should expecting parents prioritize?

Expecting parents should focus on establishing emergency reserves, reviewing insurance coverage (particularly life and disability), and understanding education funding options including 529 plans and Section 530A accounts. Setting these foundations before children arrive ensures families are protected and can fund education without derailing other long-term wealth goals.

How can high-earning families reclaim time without sacrificing wealth building?

Strategic delegation through services like professional house management allows high-earning families to outsource time-consuming tasks while earning well above the cost of these services. When hourly earnings exceed the cost of outsourcing, delegating non-core activities preserves both time and wealth-building capacity.

What is the maximum total 401(k) contribution limit per year for any individual across all sources?

The maximum you can put into your 401(k) per year is $61,000 for any individual. This comprises the employee contribution limit of $20,500, employer matching contributions, and after-tax contributions.

How can you access 401(k) funds before age 59 and a half without penalty when retiring early?

You can use 72(t) distributions, which require you to take substantially equal periodic payments from your 401(k) over the period between retirement and five years later, or until age 59 and a half, whichever is longer.

How should couples evaluate whether their cash flow can support having a child?

Rather than asking how much you need to save before having a baby, the right question is what your cash flow can handle. A child is present from age zero through at least age 18, meaning you need 18 or more years of ongoing expenses, not just a one-time cost.
