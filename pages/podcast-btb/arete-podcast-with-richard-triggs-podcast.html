What Arete Podcast with Richard Triggs covers

Arete, derived from the Greek word meaning "fulfillment of one's full potential," guides listeners through candid fireside chats with CEOs, Chairs, and accomplished senior executives. The podcast reveals how successful leaders navigated their careers, the pivotal decisions they made, and the insights that accelerated their rise to the C-suite. Rather than formal interrogation, these relaxed conversations explore the unwritten rules of executive advancement, the role of personal branding and networks, and how leaders approach organizational transformation. For aspiring and incumbent executives seeking to compress their own learning curve, these dialogues offer direct access to wisdom from those who have already walked the path to senior leadership.

What this podcast really covers

Arete Podcast with Richard Triggs zeroes in on the real trajectory of executive careers. Episodes explore how leaders built credibility within their organizations, navigated board dynamics, managed organizational culture during periods of change, and leveraged peer advisory networks like Vistage to accelerate their growth. The podcast reveals the strategic thinking behind major business decisions—from angel investing and diversification strategies to building enterprises with genuine enterprise value. Recent episodes on personal presence, powerful imagery, and showing up authentically as a leader indicate a sustained focus on the intangible yet critical skills that define executive effectiveness. Topics range from AI integration and information governance to the human elements of leadership that no algorithm can replicate.

Who this podcast is essential for

This podcast delivers immediate value to three distinct audiences. First, aspiring executives still climbing toward the C-suite benefit directly from hearing how established leaders solved problems, built networks, and positioned themselves for advancement. Second, incumbent senior executives and board members gain fresh perspectives on sector-specific challenges, emerging technologies like AI, and how peer networks amplify leadership effectiveness. Third, Non-Executive Directors seeking to strengthen their governance contributions listen to discover how executives think about risk, culture alignment, wealth creation, and the long-term sustainability of their organizations.

What the episodes really reveal

Analysis of episode titles reveals consistent patterns in executive preoccupations. A substantial thread addresses the intersection of technology and business—AI governance, information systems, construction technology, and navigating the next era of growth appear across multiple episodes. Personal leadership presence emerges as critical: "Personal Presence, Powerful Images and Showing Up as Yourself" reflects the modern executive's need to build authentic personal brands. Episodes on "Finding Your Tribe" and peer advisory underline that executive progression depends as much on network quality as individual capability. Wealth, values, and diversification topics signal that mature leaders think beyond single enterprises toward portfolio approaches to value creation. Cultural leadership—"Culture, Collaboration and Leading People Through Change"—appears consistently, indicating that executives recognize organizational transformation depends on human factors, not just strategy papers.

What this changes in practice

For listeners, exposure to this podcast shifts how they approach their own executive development. Rather than following conventional career playbooks, listeners gain permission to build diverse skill sets—technical depth in their domain, but also mastery of personal presence, cultural intelligence, and network cultivation. The emphasis on showing up as oneself, rather than adopting a corporate mask, challenges the old hierarchical model of leadership. Aspiring executives learn to view peer networks not as networking obligations but as accelerators of insight and decision-making quality. The focus on building businesses "worth something"—enterprises with real economic moats and culture—provides a counterweight to short-term metrics obsession. For board members and Non-Executive Directors, these conversations reinforce that governance strength depends on understanding how leaders actually think about risk, opportunity, and organizational renewal.

Executive leadership accelerates not through isolated ambition but through sustained access to peer wisdom, authentic self-presentation, and the strategic integration of emerging technologies with human-centered culture.

The podcast answers these questions

How does a fireside chat format differ from traditional executive interviews?

A fireside chat creates a conversational atmosphere that encourages candid discussion about career paths, decision-making, and personal lessons rather than formal Q&A. This format allows listeners to hear authentic narratives about how successful leaders navigated challenges and achieved their position, making the wisdom more relatable and actionable.

What specific career insights do senior executives typically share in these conversations?

Executives discuss their journey to the C-suite, critical career decisions, leadership philosophies, how they handle organizational change, the role of peer networks in advancement, and lessons from both successes and failures. These conversations reveal the unwritten rules of executive progression and the strategic thinking required at senior levels.

How can aspiring leaders apply insights from established CEOs to their own career development?

Listeners gain patterns of executive behavior, strategies for building credibility and networks, approaches to personal branding, and frameworks for making high-stakes decisions. By understanding how experienced leaders solved problems earlier in their careers, aspiring executives can accelerate their own trajectory and avoid preventable pitfalls.

Why would Non-Executive Directors benefit from listening to these conversations?

Non-Executive Directors gain perspective on executive performance, board dynamics, sector-specific challenges, and governance from leaders who have served in both executive and board roles. These conversations provide insight into how effective leaders think about risk, growth, and organizational culture from multiple vantage points.
