What this changes in practice

Operations teams listening to Supply Chain Moves will rethink how they measure supplier performance. Treating on-time delivery as a leading indicator—monitored daily or weekly rather than monthly—converts vendor data into early warning system that prevents stockouts rather than explaining them retroactively.

Inventory analysis shifts from blended "turns" calculations to SKU-level turns and cash conversion cycles. This granularity reveals which products trap working capital and which generate rapid cash recovery, allowing operations leaders to rightsize production forecasts and reallocate manufacturing capacity toward high-velocity SKUs.

Sourcing strategy for founders changes when tariff exposure is quantified upfront rather than absorbed at landed-cost reconciliation. Understanding tariff rates, duty structures, and trade agreement impacts before factory commitments allows founders to negotiate pricing that reflects true landed cost and protects margin across policy changes.

Cross-border expansion planning moves earlier in the growth trajectory. Waiting until demand reaches a scaling inflection point to design international fulfillment architecture forces expensive retroactive logistics restructuring. Building region-specific supplier networks and fulfillment pathways before volume demand creates operational flexibility and margin protection.

Supply chain excellence in DTC operations is not execution against a fixed plan—it is real-time decision-making grounded in granular KPIs, supplier performance visibility, and inventory economics understood at the SKU level. The difference between founders who protect margin during scaling and those who discover margin destruction too late is measurement depth and decision velocity.

The podcast answers these questions

What are the most expensive mistakes in DTC supply chain management?

Stockout costs and inventory turn misalignment represent the largest hidden profit drains in DTC operations. Poor supplier on-time delivery visibility, new product launch miscalculations, and tariff exposure compound operational risk exponentially. The cost of discovering these issues at batch three rather than batch one often exceeds six figures.

How does supplier on-time delivery performance prevent stockouts?

Supplier on-time delivery is a leading indicator that reveals supply chain fragility weeks before inventory runs dry. Tracking this KPI across all suppliers creates early warning systems that allow operations teams to reforecast demand, adjust safety stock levels, and negotiate alternative sourcing before customer-facing shortages occur.

What scaling strategies protect profitability during international expansion?

Cross-border supply chain design requires separating logistics operations by geography and establishing region-specific supplier networks before demand spikes. Successful scaling preserves margin by building flexibility into sourcing strategy, negotiating tariff hedges upfront, and maintaining clear visibility into landed costs at every fulfillment step.

How do inventory turns by SKU reveal cash flow problems?

Inventory turns by individual SKU expose slow-moving stock that traps working capital and masks profitability issues hidden in aggregate metrics. SKU-level analysis uncovers which products generate cash quickly and which create dead inventory, allowing operations teams to rightsize production runs and free cash for growth investments.
