What Pegasus Radio covers

Pegasus Radio examines the systemic pressures reshaping UK construction: an acute skills shortage, the rapid integration of AI into consultancy workflows, and the chronic underrepresentation of women in the sector. Each episode draws on practitioners running live businesses — from quantity surveying firms crossing the £1M milestone to international consultancies navigating post-Brexit positioning. The podcast operates at the intersection of professional development and industry strategy, making it a practical resource for anyone building a career or a business in construction and property.

Key facts

  • Pegasus Radio has published over 50 episodes dedicated exclusively to construction and property professionals, with guests drawn from active UK-based firms and consultancies.
  • AI adoption in construction is a recurring editorial focus, with dedicated episodes covering how tools are changing quantity surveying, cost consultancy, and project management workflows.
  • The podcast has documented at least two consultancies that scaled to significant revenue milestones within their first two years — including one reaching £1M turnover — providing rare longitudinal insight into early-stage growth in the sector.
  • Gender equity in construction is treated as a structural business issue, not a peripheral topic: episodes with The Circle Partnership and AtkinsRéalis address both cultural barriers and practical retention strategies for women at senior levels.

What this podcast really covers

Pegasus Radio is not a general business podcast that occasionally mentions construction. Every episode addresses a challenge specific to professionals working in the built environment — and the editorial choices reflect a deliberate intent to cover the full career arc, from apprentice entry points to senior consultancy leadership and firm succession.

The thematic architecture of the show breaks into three distinct pillars. The first is professional credentialing and career navigation: episodes interrogate the real-world value of MRICS, the calculus of launching an independent consultancy during economic disruption (including COVID-era decisions), and the conditions under which established firms begin to face succession crises. The second pillar is structural industry reform: episodes on the UK housing crisis, construction productivity stagnation, and the skills shortage frame individual career decisions within a wider systemic context. The third pillar is technology adoption, with a concentration on AI — not as a speculative future topic, but as a present operational reality being negotiated by quantity surveyors, project managers, and firm principals right now.

The show consistently features guests who hold operational responsibility in their organisations — a managing director, a director leading 52 quantity surveyors, a founder two years into building a consultancy. This grounds the content in decisions that have already been made, not hypothetical frameworks.

Who this podcast is essential for

Three distinct professional profiles extract disproportionate value from Pegasus Radio's episode catalogue.

Quantity surveyors and cost consultants at mid-career inflection points will find the show particularly relevant. Episodes on MRICS, AI tools reshaping the QS role, and the experience of senior women leading large surveying teams at firms like AtkinsRéalis directly address the strategic decisions this cohort faces — whether to pursue chartership, how to position against AI-augmented competitors, and how to evaluate a move into independent practice.

Construction consultancy founders and directors — especially those running sub-50-person firms — will find the growth and succession episodes unusually candid. Guests discuss real revenue figures, the difficulty of hiring in a tight labour market, the challenge of internationalising a UK-based consultancy, and the generational knowledge transfer problem that threatens many established practices.

HR and talent leaders in the built environment will find the show's sustained focus on gender equity, apprenticeship pipelines, and the skills shortage directly applicable. The BCS Consultancy episode on athlete-to-apprentice pathways, for instance, represents an unconventional workforce pipeline strategy that few HR teams in construction have yet evaluated.

What the episodes really reveal

Across fifty-plus episodes, several patterns emerge that go beyond the individual guest's story. The first is the consistency of the AI anxiety-to-adoption trajectory: nearly every technical or consultancy guest acknowledges that AI is arriving faster than anticipated, but the response varies sharply between firms that have experimented with tools in live projects and those still treating AI as a distant strategic concern. The Quantik episode and the Archus international expansion episode both illustrate firms that moved early; the contrast with more cautious voices in the succession and credentialing episodes is instructive.

The second pattern is the gender gap as a retention crisis, not merely a recruitment problem. The Circle Partnership episode and the AtkinsRéalis episode both frame the loss of women from mid-career construction roles as an economic problem for firms — not just a representation metric. This reframing is notable and positions the issue within the commercial logic that firm principals actually respond to.

The third pattern is the loneliness of the construction founder. Guests who launched consultancies — during COVID, during market downturns, from scratch — consistently describe the absence of structured peer support as a material challenge. The podcast itself appears to function partly as a community infrastructure for this cohort, providing reference points and benchmarks that founders would otherwise lack.

In UK construction, the firms navigating the next decade successfully are those treating AI adoption, gender retention, and skills pipeline development not as separate HR and tech agendas, but as a single integrated productivity strategy.

What this changes in practice

The cumulative effect of the Pegasus Radio episode catalogue is to make visible the strategic decisions that individual construction professionals and firm leaders tend to make in isolation. Listening to the show does not provide a universal playbook, but it does provide something arguably more valuable: a set of comparable cases drawn from practitioners operating under genuinely similar constraints.

For the quantity surveyor evaluating MRICS, the David Shaw episode offers a practitioner's account that moves past the official RICS narrative. For the consultancy founder wondering whether their growth rate is normal, the Nick Wdowczyk and Ben Elsey episodes provide real benchmarks. For the firm director asking whether AI will hollow out their technical team, the Tom Haley and Victoria Head episodes present both the threat and the early evidence of adaptation.

The show's consistent editorial discipline — keeping every episode anchored to someone currently running something, not just commenting on the industry — means the actionable density per episode is high. That is the functional distinction between Pegasus Radio and broader business podcasts that occasionally address construction: the specificity of the professional context is never sacrificed for general audience accessibility.

The podcast answers these questions

What are the main barriers to women progressing in construction careers in the UK?

Women in UK construction face structural barriers including limited mentorship networks, male-dominated site cultures, and a lack of flexible progression pathways. Initiatives like The Circle Partnership are working to build peer support structures and visibility for women across professional roles in the built environment.

How is AI changing the role of quantity surveyors and construction consultants?

AI tools are beginning to automate cost estimation, document analysis, and productivity benchmarking tasks traditionally handled by quantity surveyors and consultants. Firms that integrate AI early are reporting faster turnaround on reports and the ability to serve more clients without proportionally increasing headcount.

Is MRICS still worth pursuing for construction professionals in the current market?

MRICS remains a credible professional benchmark that signals rigour and technical competency to clients, particularly in high-value consultancy and project management roles. Its value depends on individual career trajectory — for those running independent consultancies or advising on complex schemes, the designation continues to command fee premiums and client trust.

How can a construction consultancy reach £1M turnover within its first two years?

Reaching £1M turnover quickly in construction consultancy typically requires a combination of an established professional network inherited from prior employment, a clear niche service offering, and disciplined pipeline management from day one. Pricing decisions — avoiding undercharging in early client relationships — are consistently cited as a critical factor in sustainable early growth.
