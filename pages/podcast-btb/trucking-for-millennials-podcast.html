What this podcast really covers

Faith and Fortitude: How Dana Gardner Built His Trucking Business

How did Dana Gardner start his trucking business with his first truck investment?

Dana Gardner bought his first truck, a 1999 International Eagle 9400 model, for $9,500. He leased it onto a company where a coworker helped him get work hauling containers out of port. He ran that truck for about eight months, investing all his earnings back into maintenance before eventually trading it in.

What is liquid bulk transportation and how does Dana Gardner Trucking operate in this niche?

Liquid bulk transportation involves moving hazardous or non-hazardous liquids in stainless steel chemical tanks, including herbicides, petroleum products, and other liquids. Dana Gardner Trucking currently operates five trucks specializing in this field, which requires drivers to manage liquid surge and motion control carefully to operate vehicles safely.

How did Dana Gardner get his Commercial Driver's License and what challenges did he face?

Dana Gardner attended truck driving school in Indiana and was sent home twice—once due to a ticket on his license that wasn't caught initially, and again for another unspecified reason. After returning to Louisiana, he worked at Kentwood Springs as a yard driver, using their truck and trailer to pay $65 and take his CDL test in December 2012.

What advice does Dana Gardner give to aspiring truck drivers based on his own journey?

Dana Gardner advises aspiring drivers to attend proper truck driving school rather than trying to get a license cheaply by paying someone directly. He emphasizes that formal training is essential because without proper education, drivers end up stuck without knowing much about the profession.

What triggered Dana Gardner's interest in trucking as a career?

While working as a scaffold builder in the chemical plant, Dana Gardner saw an 18-wheeler Peterbilt with eight-inch stacks drive by, and he was impressed by how clean and loud the truck was. That moment inspired him to tell his mother he wanted to drive trucks and eventually pursue truck driving school.

Why did Trucking for Millennials podcast go on indefinite break after 290 episodes?

The Trucking for Millennials podcast announced an indefinite break after 290 episodes due to the host needing to focus on certain business opportunities and priorities that were calling for his attention, though he expressed hope that the show had positively impacted listeners' careers in the trucking industry.
