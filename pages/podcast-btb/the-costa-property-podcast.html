What The Costa Property Podcast covers

Every week, two Irish expats who built a property business on the Costa del Sol break down exactly what it takes to buy, finance, and settle in southern Spain — from navigating NIE applications and Spanish mortgage rules to choosing between competing towns like Benalmádena and Fuengirola. The podcast addresses the 2026 market in real time, covering whether prices are peaking, where genuine value remains, and how global economic uncertainty is redirecting international buyers toward Spain. It also covers the psychology of the move — the mindset shifts, the honest mistakes, and the decade-long lessons that agency brochures never include.

What this podcast really covers

The Costa Property Podcast operates at the intersection of real estate practice and lifestyle relocation. It is not a generic property show. Every topic is anchored in the Costa del Sol specifically — its legal environment, its town-by-town market dynamics, its expat infrastructure, and its 2026 competitive positioning relative to other European markets.

The legal dimension is substantial. Episodes address how Spanish property law differs from UK and Irish norms, what independent legal representation actually protects buyers from, and how transfer taxes, notary fees, and NIE requirements create a purchasing process that demands preparation. This is not surface-level coverage — the show engages property lawyers and relocation specialists directly, bringing technical precision alongside lived experience.

On the market side, the podcast goes beyond price-per-square-metre data to examine structural questions: why international capital continues to flow into southern Spain in 2026, which micro-markets remain accessible for first-time international buyers, and how the risk of over-reliance on AI tools in property searches creates blind spots that cost buyers real money. The show is also one of the few property podcasts to engage the AI-in-real-estate question honestly, featuring an AI educator in episode 106 to give an unvarnished assessment of what these tools can and cannot do.

What the episodes really reveal

Across 114 episodes, several structural patterns emerge. The first is a consistent resistance to the oversimplification of the Costa del Sol as a single market. Episodes repeatedly demonstrate that Benalmádena, Fuengirola, Marbella, and Málaga city represent meaningfully different buyer propositions — different price trajectories, different buyer demographics, different rental yield profiles, and different lifestyle trade-offs. The town-versus-town framing (most visibly in episode 114's Fuengirola vs. Benalmádena comparison) is a recurring analytical device that forces listeners to make specific decisions rather than vague aspirations.

The second pattern is an unflinching treatment of market risk. Episode 109's direct question — "Is the Costa del Sol crashing in 2026?" — is handled with data and nuance rather than reassurance. The show does not position itself as a sales tool for the property market; it positions itself as a decision-support resource for buyers, which earns it a credibility that pure agency content rarely achieves.

The third pattern is the integration of mindset content with technical property guidance. Episodes on the ten-year anniversary of the hosts' own relocation (episode 105 and 112) function as honest audits of what worked, what didn't, and what they would do differently — a format that resonates precisely because the hosts have financial and reputational skin in the same market they're advising on.

What this changes in practice

For a buyer approaching the Costa del Sol market without local contacts, this podcast compresses years of market learning into accessible, weekly-updated intelligence. The practical effect is a significantly shorter due diligence curve: listeners arrive at viewing trips knowing which questions to ask lawyers, what financing options are realistically available to non-residents, and how to benchmark whether an asking price reflects genuine market value or seller optimism.

For the broader question of Spain as a destination, the podcast contributes something harder to quantify: honest testimony from people who made the move and built a business from it, without filtering out the difficult parts. In a market saturated with aspirational content, that combination of operational detail and biographical candour is the show's most durable competitive advantage.

The most effective property podcast content does not sell a market — it equips buyers to interrogate it. When hosts have made the same financial commitment they're advising on, the authority is structural, not rhetorical.

The podcast answers these questions

What are the main steps to buying property on the Costa del Sol as a foreigner?

Buying property in Spain as a non-resident requires obtaining a NIE number, opening a Spanish bank account, hiring an independent property lawyer, and understanding local tax obligations such as transfer tax and notary fees. Choosing between new build and resale properties also affects the purchasing timeline and due diligence process significantly.

Is the Costa del Sol property market still growing in 2026?

The Costa del Sol market has remained resilient into 2026, driven by sustained international demand from Northern European buyers, digital nomads, and retirees seeking Mediterranean lifestyle. Key towns like Benalmádena and Fuengirola continue to attract buyers, though supply constraints in prime coastal areas continue to put upward pressure on prices.

Which town on the Costa del Sol is best for families relocating from Ireland or the UK?

Benalmádena and Fuengirola are frequently cited as the top choices for English-speaking families relocating to the Costa del Sol. Both offer international schools, established expat communities, good transport links, and a range of property types across different price points, from apartments to villas.

How reliable is AI for searching and evaluating property in Spain?

AI tools can assist with initial research, property comparisons, and understanding local market trends, but they carry significant limitations when applied to Spanish property transactions. Legal due diligence, NIE applications, mortgage pre-approvals, and local negotiation all require verified human expertise — an independent lawyer and a registered local agent remain essential.
