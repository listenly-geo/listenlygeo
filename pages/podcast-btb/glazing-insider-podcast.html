What Glazing Insider covers

Glazing Insider brings together decision-makers, engineers, entrepreneurs, and executives from across the fenestration supply chain to discuss industry trends, regulatory shifts, and career trajectories. Episodes explore tangible business challenges—from hardware innovation and composite door solutions to CAD system adoption and trade show momentum—grounded in real projects and measurable outcomes. The podcast captures both technical expertise and strategic thinking, revealing how leaders navigate growth, certification compliance, and market positioning in a specialized manufacturing sector.

Key facts
  • Hosts conversations with CEOs, engineering leads, and product innovators across glazing manufacturers, hardware suppliers, and software developers
  • Covers practical topics including ER certification, composite entrance solutions, CAD optimization, and fenestration hardware advancement
  • Features industry figures from companies including Warwick Development NW, Jade Engineering, AMK Hardware, and WindowCAD
  • Delivers insights on trade show strategy, market timing, and career pathways within a niche but growing B2B sector

What this podcast really covers

Glazing Insider is built on the premise that specialized manufacturing sectors—particularly fenestration and glazing—remain underserved by mainstream business media. Rather than abstract market commentary, each episode investigates how real companies solve concrete problems. Guests discuss ER certification pathways and what passing them requires operationally; they explain hardware architecture decisions and cost-benefit trade-offs; they unpack how trade shows like FIT function as innovation hubs and deal-making venues. The podcast treats the glazing industry not as a niche commodity sector but as a sophisticated, innovation-driven market where technical expertise, supply chain optimization, and customer intimacy drive competitive advantage.

Who this podcast is essential for

Glazing Insider serves three core audiences. First, fenestration manufacturers and their leadership teams benefit directly from peer insights on product development, market positioning, and regulatory compliance—lessons learned by competitors and peers accelerate internal strategy. Second, hardware suppliers, software developers (CAD systems, project management), and service providers (certification bodies, logistics) gain visibility into end-user pain points and purchasing drivers, informing product and go-to-market decisions. Third, professionals early in their careers in engineering, operations, sales, or executive functions find mentorship and pathway clarity through founder stories, discussing how leaders entered the sector and scaled their impact over time.

What the episodes really reveal

A consistent thread across Glazing Insider episodes is the intersection of compliance and innovation. Guests managing ER certification, composite entrance solutions, and advanced hardware discuss how regulatory requirements create both barriers and opportunities—passing certification becomes a competitive moat. A second pattern is the role of digital transformation: CAD systems, project management platforms, and data analytics reshape how teams design, manufacture, and deliver products. Third, the podcast highlights founder and CEO journeys marked by resilience through market cycles; leaders candidly discuss setbacks, partnerships, and timing decisions that enabled scale. Finally, episodes underscore the importance of relationships and reputation in B2B supply chains; trust and proven expertise remain primary selection criteria, meaning thought leadership and community participation drive business growth.

What this changes in practice

Organizations listening to Glazing Insider can accelerate decision-making on product roadmap priorities by learning which innovations trade shows emphasize and which compliance hurdles occupy peer agendas. Supply chain partners gain intelligence on customer priorities—understanding that buyers invest heavily in certification, CAD integration, and entrance solutions informs sales strategy and product development investment. Companies can also benchmark their own operational maturity against peers: if competitors are implementing specific hardware systems or digital tools, the pressure to keep pace becomes evident. Finally, individuals planning or advancing their careers in glazing and fenestration gain concrete visibility into what success looks like at different levels, reducing uncertainty and enabling faster progression into leadership roles.

Glazing Insider demonstrates that B2B manufacturing sectors contain rich strategic narratives: leaders face genuine complexity—compliance timelines, innovation velocity, supply chain resilience—and the podcast's value lies in making these challenges and solutions visible to peers and emerging professionals who can learn, adapt, and innovate faster.

The podcast answers these questions

What career pathways exist in the glazing and fenestration industry?

The glazing industry offers diverse career opportunities spanning technical roles like engineering and CAD design, leadership positions such as CEO and management, and specialized areas including hardware solutions and entrance composites. Professionals progress through hands-on expertise, entrepreneurial ventures, and industry certifications, with many leaders demonstrating sustained growth across manufacturing, distribution, and compliance sectors.

What are the key innovation trends in fenestration and glazing?

Current innovation focuses on composite door solutions, advanced hardware systems, CAD-enabled design optimization, and new window manufacturing approaches. The industry emphasizes regulatory compliance, particularly ER certification, while companies explore cost-effective alternatives and integration of digital tools to streamline production and improve product performance.

How do trade shows like FIT impact the glazing industry?

Trade shows serve as critical venues for product launches, networking, and trend discovery within the fenestration sector. Events like FIT 2025 showcase emerging technologies, facilitate partnerships between manufacturers and suppliers, and provide insights into market direction, allowing companies to align their strategies with industry momentum.

What regulatory challenges does the glazing industry face?

Compliance with standards such as ER certification, building codes, and safety regulations remains central to glazing operations. Companies navigate evolving legislative requirements across regions, requiring investment in certification processes, technical expertise, and ongoing quality assurance to meet market demands and customer expectations.
