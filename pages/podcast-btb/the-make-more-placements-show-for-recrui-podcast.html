What The Make More Placements Show covers

The hosts dissect the practical systems—from prospect calling discipline to client advocacy—that separate six-figure recruiters from those perpetually fighting for placements and racing to the bottom on fees.

What this podcast really covers

The Make More Placements Show is built on a single, uncompromising premise: recruitment and staffing businesses fail not because the market is saturated, but because owners and consultants lack fundamental discipline in execution and psychology. The podcast strips away motivational clichés and exposes the concrete reasons consultants lose deals, leave fees on the table, and burn out.

Each episode tackles one barrier standing between a recruitment business and sustainable profitability. The show does not sell you systems or shortcuts. Instead, it diagnoses where your current practice—whether it's your calling habits, your relationship mindset, your pricing posture, or your visible presence—is costing you placements and margin. This is brutally practical content for owners who have already attempted the mainstream recruitment coaching circuit and are ready for uncensored, results-focused insight.

What the episodes really reveal

Across 400+ episodes, The Make More Placements Show reveals recurring patterns that define successful recruitment businesses. The first is the centrality of discipline: not the mythical self-discipline of willpower, but the structured discipline of making quality calls, following up systematically, and refusing to negotiate from weakness. Episodes like "The Calls You're Not Making Are Costing You Business" and "The Discipline Lie That's Quietly Killing Your Recruitment Firm" attack the gap between intention and action directly.

The second pattern is the psychological shift from transactional to advocacy-based relationships. "Why Needy Recruiters Lose Good Deals" and "Stop Chasing the Deal: Detachment and Advocacy for Recruitment Business Owners" establish that desperation is visible to clients and kills commissions. Successful recruiters have learned to detach emotionally from individual placements while remaining deeply committed to client outcomes. This is not cold indifference; it is professional maturity.

The third is visibility and confidence-building in advance of market proof. "Invisible to Inevitable: The Mindset to Show Up On Video When No One's Clapping Yet" and "Confidence" address the psychological barrier that prevents consultants from building personal brand and public presence before they have obvious credentials. The podcast argues that this sequencing is backwards—visibility and reputation must precede the deals that validate them.

What this changes in practice

Listening to The Make More Placements Show fundamentally reshapes how recruitment owners approach three operational levers: call discipline, fee architecture, and personal market positioning. Owners recognize that the cost of staying the same—the staff turnover, the lost placements, the commodity pricing, the burnout—exceeds any investment in systems, visibility, or coaching. This realization is the gateway to action.

On the consultant level, the podcast permission-grants authenticity and detachment simultaneously. Recruiters stop treating every interaction as a desperation play and instead focus on candidate quality and client fit. This single shift—from hunting to serving—changes conversation tone, negotiating power, and deal closure rates measurably. The show also normalizes the early-stage visibility journey, removing shame from showing up on video or in content before you have a massive track record to lean on.

Recruitment business owners who embrace systematic discipline, client advocacy over deal chasing, and early-stage visibility building exit the commodity trap entirely. The competitive advantage is not luck or market conditions—it is execution consistency and psychological maturity in how you position your value to prospects and clients.

The podcast answers these questions

What prevents recruitment consultants from reaching their income potential?

Recruitment consultants often fail to execute core business fundamentals consistently: making quality calls, building genuine advocate relationships with clients, and maintaining discipline during market shifts. Most consultants lack the strategic detachment needed to prospect effectively without desperation, which clients immediately sense and reject.

How do staffing business owners increase placement fees without losing clients?

Premium fees come from genuine value delivery and market positioning, not negotiation tactics. Owners must focus on building recruitment discipline, generating quality candidates, and demonstrating measurable business impact to justify higher rates. The cost of staying the same—losing deals, staff turnover, burnout—exceeds the investment in strategic visibility and client advocacy.

Why do most recruitment businesses struggle with sustainability?

Two-thirds of recruitment consultants fail because they treat sales as transactional chasing instead of strategic relationship building. Successful owners shift mindset from neediness to advocacy, build systems that don't depend entirely on their personal effort, and invest in visibility—including video and content—before they have external validation.

How can recruitment firm owners build business visibility from zero audience?

The transformation from invisible to inevitable requires consistent, patient content creation and video presence even when clapping is absent. Owners must develop the psychological discipline to show up authentically, share hard-won insights, and build confidence through repetition rather than waiting for permission or external proof of success.

There are 27 questions in the triage call script designed to establish that the hiring manager has budget, authority, need and timing (BANT).
