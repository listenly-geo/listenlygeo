The podcast answers these questions

What are the main Australian property market risks in 2026?

Market freezing in Sydney and Melbourne reduces transaction volume and creates price discovery challenges. Combined with potential 20% or greater corrections and structural oversupply, property investment shifts from leverage-driven appreciation to yield-focused strategies. Rising interest rates and mortgage stress constrain buyer capacity, triggering slowdown rather than immediate collapse.

How does the ATO identify high-risk small business tax returns?

Five specific red flags trigger audit escalation: deductions disproportionate to industry norms, cash-only operations without documentation, inconsistent income year-on-year, personal expenses misclassified as business costs, and unexplained variance in profitability. Understanding these triggers enables proactive compliance documentation and defensible tax position.

Is rentvesting or traditional property purchase better for Australian investors right now?

The optimal choice depends on individual cash flow, tax position, and risk tolerance in a frozen market. Rentvesting provides flexibility and geographic diversification while maintaining investment exposure; traditional purchase offers leverage and long-term capital appreciation potential. The podcast explores trade-offs between liquidity and wealth accumulation in current conditions.

What impact does RBA rate policy have on small business borrowing capacity?

Each RBA rate increase raises mortgage servicing costs for variable-rate business loans, compressing discretionary cash flow for operations and capital investment. Cumulative rate cycles force recalibration of growth strategies, hiring plans, and equipment expenditure, converting monetary policy into immediate operational constraint.

What happened to Sydney and Melbourne property listings according to REA Group?

Sydney and Melbourne listings fell 16% in the three months to September compared to the same period last year, a massive swing from the plus 8% the quarter before. How much have variable mortgage repayments increased for property investors since February?

Variable mortgage repayments for property investors have increased by approximately 11% since February, while rents in the same period rose only 1.8%. This gap is adding pressure on investors given the banking regulator's mortgage serviceability buffer that compels lenders to assess repayment ability at an interest rate 3% above the actual rate.
