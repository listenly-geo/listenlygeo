The podcast answers these questions

What is digital asset investing and how does it differ from traditional finance?

Digital asset investing involves purchasing and managing cryptocurrencies, blockchain-based tokens, and other emerging digital financial instruments. Unlike traditional finance, digital assets operate on decentralized networks, offer 24/7 trading, and carry distinct risk models tied to technology adoption and regulatory evolution rather than corporate earnings or economic cycles.

How should financial advisors approach crypto and digital assets in client portfolios?

Financial advisors should integrate digital assets through education on risk-adjusted positioning, understanding both technological fundamentals and market cycles. The key is applying traditional portfolio principles—diversification, risk modeling, and tax efficiency—while acknowledging that digital assets represent an emerging asset class with higher volatility and unique liquidity characteristics.

What are the primary tax considerations for cryptocurrency investors in 2023 and beyond?

Cryptocurrency tax treatment involves capital gains reporting on every transaction, wash-sale rules complexity, staking income classification, and evolving regulatory requirements across jurisdictions. Advisors and investors must track cost basis meticulously, understand jurisdiction-specific rulings, and plan for potential tax optimization strategies while maintaining compliance as regulations continue to crystallize.

What should investors understand about risk modeling in digital assets?

Risk modeling in digital assets requires analysis of volatility clustering, correlation patterns with traditional markets, regulatory event risk, and technology-dependent drawdowns. Effective risk frameworks measure historical Value-at-Risk, stress-test against market dislocations, and account for the longer-tail risk profile that distinguishes digital assets from mature equity markets.

Tokenized securities are currently trading on Alternative Trading Systems (ATSs) under SEC jurisdiction, which requires approval and a license.
