What this changes in practice

Systematic M&A preparation begins 5-7 years before any sale, not when a buyer calls. Advisors should map employment agreements backward from acquisition close, ensuring enforceability, restrictive covenant legality, and retention alignment with buyer expectations. Entity structure should be audited annually—S-corp to C-corp conversions, partnership restructuring, and subsidiary creation can each shift post-sale proceeds by 15-25% depending on buyer structure and tax treatment.

Compensation design must include acquisition contingency planning from year one. Equity grants should vest over 5-7 years with acceleration only if specific retention milestones are met during buyer transitions. Bonus structures should reward client retention and AUM stability, not just revenue growth—these metrics directly impact earnout achievement and buyer satisfaction post-close.

PE evaluation requires advisors to model three scenarios: organic growth, strategic buyer acquisition, and PE partnership. Each scenario produces vastly different five-year outcomes and tax consequences. The decision should be systematic, not emotional—The Fine Print provides frameworks rather than prescriptions, because firm-specific dynamics (team depth, client concentration, organic growth capacity) ultimately determine which path maximizes long-term value.

The podcast answers these questions

What are the key risks independent advisors face during M&A transactions?

Acquisition risk encompasses pricing uncertainty, deal terms misalignment, and valuation contingencies. Buyers and sellers must balance competitive pricing against earn-out protections, employment agreement enforceability, and post-closing client retention guarantees. The episode reveals that most advisors underestimate execution risk and contingency planning requirements.

Should independent advisors pursue private equity partnerships?

PE offers capital, growth infrastructure, and exit liquidity, but requires surrendering equity control and accepting operational constraints. The decision hinges on your growth stage, capital needs, and operational independence preferences. Advisors should evaluate PE terms against organic growth timelines and valuations from traditional buyers.

What compensation strategies work best for advisory teams?

Effective compensation balances retention, performance incentives, and equity alignment. Models range from fixed salary plus bonuses to revenue-sharing and equity vesting schedules. The structure must reflect your firm's growth stage, profitability margins, and long-term succession planning to avoid talent exodus and valuation compression.

How does entity structure impact M&A outcomes?

Entity structure determines tax efficiency, liability protection, and acquisition attractiveness. S-corps, C-corps, LLCs, and partnership structures create vastly different post-sale proceeds and employment contingencies. Buyers evaluate structure upfront, making it the backbone of valuation, earnout terms, and post-closing employment agreements.
